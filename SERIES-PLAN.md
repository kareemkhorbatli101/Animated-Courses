# Wayfinder English — Series Authoring Plan
### An original six-level course built on the Step Forward lesson architecture, authored for international learners

**Status:** **Books 1 and 2 authorized** (22 Nov 2026). Books 3–6 planned, not authorized.
**Audience:** international adult learners, weighted by level — see §3.0.
**Convention:** American English base, switchable British layer — see §4.1.
**Working series name:** *Wayfinder English — Communication for Study, Work and Life*
(alternates held in reserve: *Crosspoint English*, *Portmere English*. The name needs a
trade-mark clearance search before any commercial release; see §9.)

## 0. The five open questions from the research brief, now closed

| # | Question | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|

## 2. The unit and lesson architecture (the cloned 88%)

Every unit, every level, no exceptions:

| Lesson | Pages (Starter–2 / 3–5) | Name | Core output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opener + L1 | 2 / 2 | **Vocabulary** — opener carries a *UNIT GOALS* banner with 3 goals | Topic lexis, two lexical sets |
| L2 | 2 / 3 | **Real-life Reading** | Article + authentic-style document or data graphic |
| L3 | 3 / 3 | **Grammar** | 2–3 grammar charts, inductive entry |
| L4 | 3 / 3 | **Everyday Conversation** + pronunciation + *On the Job* box | Functional dialogue |
| L5 | 2 / 3 | **Writing** — *Real Lives* model text | Model → plan → write → revise |
| Review | 2 / 2–3 | **Team Up & Review** + *Think It Through* case | Team task, project, case study |

Every lesson ends with a **Check Yourself**. Lesson 2 carries the **Take It Outside** out-of-class
task, which is harvested in Team Up & Review — making the unit a loop rather than a list.

**The one structural deviation, and why.** Step Forward's signature order is
Vocabulary → Writing → Grammar → Conversation → Reading. Wayfinder runs
**Vocabulary → Reading → Grammar → Conversation → Writing**. Two reasons:

1. *Legal.* The research brief's own competitor analysis flags that lesson order as Step Forward's
   signature arrangement and advises changing the secondary order. Keeping the five lesson types,
   their page budgets and their internal slot functions while reordering two of them is the cheapest
   available separation from the one element most likely to read as copied arrangement.
2. *Pedagogical.* It is the better sequence. Input first (vocabulary, then a reading that seeds the
   target structure), then focus on form, then spoken production, then writing as the unit's
   culminating output rather than its second lesson. Writing at L5 can draw on everything the unit
   has built, and the Take It Outside task set in L2 has four lessons to run in before it is reported.

**Confirmed.** The reordering is approved, with one binding condition attached to it: moving the
writing lesson to the end of the unit must not cost the course any of its talk. Hence §2.1.

### 2.1 The speaking spine — a binding condition on the reorder

The risk in putting Writing last is that a unit drifts into four lessons of intake before anybody
says anything. Step Forward's real strength is that learners are talking from the second slot of
Lesson 1 onward, and that strength is not negotiable. So the speaking load is specified as a floor,
per lesson, and audited as a quality gate.

**Floor: every lesson contains at least three oral slots, one of which is open-ended** — meaning it
has no single right answer and the learner has to decide something, rank something, disagree with
something or tell the class about their own life.

| Lesson | Guaranteed oral slots | Of which open-ended |
|---|---|---|
| **L1 Vocabulary** | 1E pair Q&A from a model exchange · 2B say-and-check · 2D *Think about it* discussion (2 questions) · AUG-a task is argued in pairs before it is answered | 2D, and the AUG-a judgment call |
| **L2 Reading** | 1 prediction poll — spoken, hands up, reasons given · 6 the document questions are asked partner-to-partner, not read silently · *Talk it over* graph discussion (L3+) · Take It Outside is briefed aloud and reported aloud | the poll, *Talk it over*, the report-back |
| **L3 Grammar** | *Remember?* strip done aloud in pairs · 1D pair oral practice from the chart · 2B listen and repeat · 3 picture-cued pair questions · 4 personalized questions → partner → class | 4, which is about the learner's own life and ends in front of the class |
| **L4 Conversation** | the whole lesson: 2 pronunciation pair practice · 3 dialogue read aloud · 5 realia pair task · 6 gap-dialogue performed to another pair · On the Job exchanges practiced · Culture Check discussion · CHECK role-play from two role cards | 5, the Culture Check, the role-play |
| **L5 Writing** | 1E compare your answers with a partner and say where you differ · 2 the plan is role-played before it is written · 3 peer feedback is spoken, against the checklist, before anything is revised · CHECK self-assessment said aloud to a partner | the peer feedback conversation |
| **Team Up & Review** | A–C team discussion of a scene or chart · D–F team project with assigned roles, class survey, reporting back · *Think It Through* case argued in groups | nearly all of it |

**Three further guarantees, series-wide:**

1. **No silent lesson opener.** Every lesson's first slot is spoken or has a spoken step before
   anyone writes. A learner's first act in a lesson is to say something.
2. **Every open-ended question is authored, not generated at the point of use.** Discussion prompts
   are written into the Student Book with a *Need help?* phrase bank beside them at Starter–Level 2,
   because "discuss in pairs" with no language given is how a speaking task dies in a mixed class.
   The phrase bank is withdrawn across Levels 3–5 as learners can carry the turn themselves.
3. **Teacher notes carry a stage timing for talk.** The communicative-practice stage of every lesson
   is budgeted at no less than a third of lesson time, and the notes name what the room should sound
   like at that point.

**Gate 13** (§8) counts the oral slots in every lesson mechanically and fails the unit if any lesson
falls under three, or has no open-ended one.

### Slot schema

Each slot carries: `slot_id, function, rubric, items, interaction, audio(Y/N + script), art_brief(Y/N), answer_key(Y/N)`.

**L1 Vocabulary.** 1A activate — circle the words you know (6–8) · 1B gist listening with a scene
· 1C listen and repeat · 1D gap-fill (6–8, first done as an example) · 1E pair Q&A from a model
exchange · 2A match words to pictures (8–9) · 2B listen and check · 2C paragraph completion (5–6
blanks) · 2D *Think about it* discussion (2 questions) · **CHECK** two-column sort (6 items)
· **AUG-a** digital & AI literacy micro-task. From Level 3: a team research task with sentence frames.

**L2 Real-life Reading.** 1 pre-reading vocabulary (4) + a prediction poll · 2 the article (word
budget per §4; 2–3 footnoted glosses; a *Reader's Tip* strategy; a cited real public source)
· 3 audio read-along · 4 multiple choice, exam format (4) · 5 word gap-fill (4) · 6 an
authentic-style document or data graphic with 4 questions plus one inference question
· **TAKE IT OUTSIDE** real-world data task. From Level 3: True/False/Not Given with line numbers
(5–6), a word-study affix chart (5), and a *Talk it over* graph discussion.

**L3 Grammar.** **AUG-b** *Remember?* spaced-retrieval strip (5 items recycling units n−1 and n−3)
· 1A context text or dialogue + an opinion question · 1B notice and underline the forms · 1C chart
plus *Grammar Note* · 1D pair oral practice · 1E one sentence per rule · 2A–2C second structure:
chart, listen and repeat, controlled completion (4–6) · 3 picture-cued pair questions, then
notebook sentences · 4 personalized questions → partner → class · **CHECK** write 3 sentences about
a partner. From Level 3: a *Language connection* rule-completion box and a team error-correction
task (4 items).

**L4 Everyday Conversation.** 1 realia prompt with 5 questions, then gist listening and
note completion · 2 pronunciation: model, identify (4), check, pair practice · 3 model dialogue
(8–10 lines) + a/b comprehension (4) · 4 think about the grammar (2) + mini-chart + listen and
repeat · 5 pair task from a realia stimulus · 6 *Make conversation* gap-dialogue, then present to
another pair · **ON THE JOB** box: 3 short workplace exchanges, practice, 3 discussion questions
with a *Need help?* phrase bank · **AUG-c** minimal-pair micro-drill · **AUG-e** *Culture Check*
· **CHECK** role-play with two role cards. From Level 3: focused listening for detail, a group
ranking or discussion task, and a participation self-check.
*Anti-pattern rule:* in odd-numbered units the On the Job box precedes the gap-dialogue; in
even-numbered units it follows it.

**L5 Writing.** 1A realia warm-up (3–4) · 1B picture story, listen · 1C the *Real Lives* model
text, one named character, first person, word budget per §4.2, with a *Writer's Tip* · 1D check
understanding, circle the correct word (4) · 1E second speaker, circle the details (4), compare with
a partner · 2 plan: a form or checklist, then a role-play · 3 write: a frame text with 6–8 blanks
(Starter–Level 2) or free writing with a 4-item editing checklist and peer feedback (Levels 3–5)
· **CHECK** self-assessment: what I can do now / what I need more help with.

**Team Up & Review.** A–C team discussion of a scene or chart, then a team summary from a model
· D–F team project or survey with assigned roles (from Level 3: manager, writer, editor, presenter),
class survey and reporting · **THINK IT THROUGH** a 40–140-word case about one named adult, audio,
with 3 questions: what is the problem, why did it happen, what are two or three things she could do
· **AUG-d** *My Progress* log: one goal, one strategy, one can-do rating · harvest of the unit's
Take It Outside findings.

### Teaching cycle and multilevel tiers

Teacher notes stage every lesson as Warm-up and review (books closed) → Introduction → Presentation
→ Guided practice → Communicative practice and application → Evaluation, each with a timing of
5–25 minutes and a multilevel note. This is the standard adult-ESL staging model, documented in the
public literature; it is not anyone's property.

Every lesson states three objectives — **Pre-level / On-level / Higher-level** — differing by verb,
not by topic: pre-level learners *identify* and *complete*, on-level learners *use* and *produce*,
higher-level learners *extend* and *explain*. Tactics are fixed across the series: extra time and a
reduced item set for pre-level; extra original sentences for higher-level learners who finish early;
oral answers for pre-level where on-level learners write.

## 3. Scope and sequence — all six books

The grammar spiral is inherited wholesale, because a grammar sequence is a method, not expression.
The themes, titles, situations, documents and every word of text are new.

### 3.0 Audience, weighted by level

The series serves international adult learners, and the weighting shifts as the levels climb:

| Books | Weighting | What that means on the page |
|---|---|---|
| **Starter, 1, 2** (A1–A2) | **Settling and working.** ~75% everyday life and first employment, ~25% study. | Arriving, housing, shopping, health, transport, a first job, registering an address. The study content is *learning how to study* — a timetable, a deadline, asking a question in class — not academic content. |
| **3, 4** (B1–B1+) | **Balanced, tilting academic.** ~50/50 at Level 3, ~40/60 at Level 4. | Both strands run in every unit: how a university works alongside how a workplace works; a reading list alongside a staff handbook; an essay plan alongside an incident report. |
| **5** (B2) | **Academic and professional readiness.** ~70% study and career, ~30% life. | Argument, summary, evidence evaluation, presenting, extended writing, reported speech in meetings and minutes. The level a learner needs for university entry or professional registration. |

This is how learners actually move — survival first, ambition later — and it widens the market
without diluting either end. A Starter unit never pretends its learner is writing essays; a Level 5
unit never pretends they still need to ask where the post counter is.

### 3.1 The world and the cast — the series' largest upgrade

Step Forward deliberately has **no recurring characters**: fresh first names every unit. Wayfinder
takes the opposite decision, and it is the main reason the finished books should read above the
source rather than level with it.

**Portmere** is a mid-size coastal city: a university on the hill, a teaching hospital, a port
and logistics park, a market quarter, a tech campus, and the bus routes between them. Learners
arrive in Portmere and build a life there. The city is deliberately unplaced — no country named,
no currency symbol tied to one state, no national institution — so the book travels. The name is
deliberately spelling-neutral: the variant layer of §4.1 never touches a proper noun, so a city called
Portmere reads the same in both editions, where a Portmere/Harborline would read as a typo in one
of them.

Each band follows one cohort, and the cohort ages across the series:

- **Starter / Level 1 — arriving.** Nadia (language-course student, newly arrived), Tomás (works
  mornings in the market café), Mei (nursing student in a shared flat), Yusuf (night shift at the
  logistics park), Mrs Oyelaran (the front desk at Portmere Language Center), Dr Haddad (the
  walk-in clinic), Priya (Mei's housemate), Mr Caruso (the landlord).
- **Level 2 / 3 — settling.** The same people one and two years on: Nadia now has a part-time
  contract, Tomás is a shift supervisor, Mei is on placement, Yusuf is studying logistics at night.
  New arrivals join: Oksana, Adeel, Linh, Samira.
- **Level 4 / 5 — progressing.** Careers, degrees, a tenants' association, a community radio slot,
  a start-up that nearly works. Nadia is interviewing for jobs she would not have applied for in
  Book 1 — and Book 5's Unit 1 narrative lets a B2 learner retell a story the A1 learner lived.

Why this is worth it: a returning cast gives continuity learners actually care about, it makes the
series feel authored rather than assembled, it lets grammar recycle inside a *story* rather than a
grid, and it cuts art cost by an order of magnitude because character construction is reused rather
than re-briefed 78 times.

**Casting rules.** Ages 18–60. No character is defined by their nationality, none is comic relief,
none is a victim, and nobody's problem is solved for them by an institution. Women hold technical
and supervisory roles at the same rate as men. The person who does not understand something is as
often a local as a newcomer.

### 3.8 Recycling law

A structure is introduced as statements, then as questions, then contrasted with a neighboring
structure inside the same unit. Every structure reappears in at least two later units — once as
*Remember?* retrieval (AUG-b) and once embedded in a task that does not announce it. Each book's
*Starting Point* reviews the previous book's tenses. This is checked mechanically; see §8.

## 4. Word budgets, vocabulary and readability

### 4.1 American base, switchable British layer

The authored base is **American English** — spelling, lexis, date format and grammatical preference.
A **variant layer** is authored alongside it so a British edition can be generated rather than
rewritten.

The layer is not a spell-checker swap. Each entry is a keyed token recorded in `VARIANTS.json` with
the slot that uses it, so audio scripts, answer keys, art labels and realia all switch together:

| Divergence class | Base (US) | Variant (UK) | Where it bites |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spelling | color, center, realize, traveled, enroll, program, meter | colour, centre, realise, travelled, enrol, programme, metre | everywhere, including art labels, realia and audio scripts |
| Everyday lexis | apartment, line (queue), subway, cell phone, résumé, pharmacy, trash, elevator, fall (season), check (bill) | flat, queue, underground, mobile, CV, chemist, rubbish, lift, autumn, bill | Starter U10, U5, U8; Level 1 U4, U7, U10 — the housing, shopping and job-hunt units carry most of it |
| Dates | March 14 / 3-14 | 14 March / 14-03 | Starter U4 is **built on** this divergence and teaches both formats explicitly |
| Grammar preference | *Do you have…*, past simple with *just/already*, *on the weekend* | *Have you got…*, present perfect with *just/already*, *at the weekend* | Level 1 U3, Level 2 U8, Level 3 U5 |
| Quantity and measure | metric with an imperial gloss | metric, imperial gloss retained | Starter U7, Level 2 U7 |
| Realia | US-style form field order and phone/address shape, but **no US institution named** | UK-style equivalents | every document asset |

**Rules.** No slot's *answer* may depend on which variant is active — an item whose key changes with
the layer is rewritten until it does not, except where the divergence is the teaching point itself
(Starter U4 dates, Level 1 U7 money words), which is flagged `variant_is_content: true` and keyed
twice on purpose. Audio is scripted once with a variant note per affected line, so a British record
re-cuts only the flagged lines. Gate 14 (§8) checks that every `VARIANTS.json` token resolves in both
editions and that no un-flagged answer key diverges.

The base is American but the *content* is not: §4's internationalization rules still hold. No US
institution, agency, document, brand or emergency number appears in either edition. American here is
a language convention, not a setting — Portmere is still unplaced.

### 4.2 Budgets

| Level | *Real Lives* model | Reading article | *Think It Through* case | Dialogue | New words/unit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | 40–60 | 60–90 | 40–60 | 8 lines | 16–20 |
| 1 | 60–105 † | 100–140 | 60–80 | 8–10 lines | 20–24 |
| 2 | 90–120 | 150–200 | 70–100 | 10 lines | 24–28 |
| 3 | 120–170 | 220–300 | 90–120 | 10–12 lines | 28–34 |
| 4 | 170–250 | 300–400 | 100–140 | 12–14 lines | 34–40 + academic set |
| 5 | 250–350 | 400–550 | 140–180 | 14–16 lines | 40–48 + academic set |

† **Amended after Book 1 and Book 2 were written.** The Level 1 model texts came out at 81–104 words against an original budget of 60–90. Seven of the twelve exceeded it. The budget was the wrong number, not the prose: Book 2 spans A1+ to A2, and the plan's own Level 2 budget is 90–120, so texts in the eighties and nineties sit exactly between the two published bands. The band is widened to 60–105 and the reason recorded here rather than the texts cut to fit a figure estimated before any of them existed.

Target readability: roughly grade 2–3 at Starter, 3–4 at Levels 1–2, 5–7 at Level 3, 7–9 at Level 4,
9–11 at Level 5, measured on the article text only.

**Vocabulary selection is justified item by item against public frequency data** — NGSL, the Oxford
3000/5000, CEFR-J and COCA bands — never against anyone's published word list. Bands: A1 for
Starter, A1–A2 for Level 1, A2 for Level 2, B1 for Level 3, B1–B2 for Level 4, B2 for Level 5. The
topic-dictated core stays (a housing unit must teach *rent*, *deposit*, *leak*), because that core is
dictated by the topic rather than chosen by a publisher. Every `META` file records, per item: the
word, its band, its source list, and the slot that introduces it.

**Internationalization rules.** No US-only institution, document, brand or emergency number — the
language convention is American (§4.1), the setting is not. No currency symbol in body text; prices
use a neutral unit written as a number plus the word. Measurements are metric with an imperial gloss
where a learner would meet both. Names, faces and places are plural and unmarked. Dates are written
out in full at Starter and Level 1 before any numeric format is taught, and then **both** numeric
formats are taught explicitly, because a learner will meet both and guessing between 3-14 and 14-03
is a real-world failure with real-world consequences.

## 5. The five augmentation strands (~12%, in fixed positions)

| Code | Strand | Position | Size | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a | **Digital & AI literacy** | end of L1 | ½ page | Check a machine translation against the original; spot the phishing message in three; work out what a form will do with your data; write a prompt that gets a usable answer and then verify it. Current, concrete, and tested by a task rather than described. |
| b | **Remember?** | head of L3 | ⅓ page | Five spaced-retrieval items drawn from units n−1 and n−3. Retrieval practice, not revision reading. |
| c | **Say it clearly** | end of L4 | ⅓ page | Minimal-pair micro-drill, audio-linked, built on the pronunciation point the lesson taught. |
| d | **My Progress** | Team Up & Review | ⅓ page | One goal, one strategy, one can-do rating. The learner's own record, not the teacher's. |
| e | **Culture Check** | in L4, after the On the Job box | ½ page | Intercultural pragmatics: how direct is too direct here, when silence means disagreement, how to decline an invitation twice, what a manager means by "when you get a chance". The single largest gap for international learners and the thing a domestic competency series has no reason to teach. |

Together these add roughly 2 pages per unit against a 14–16 page unit: about 12%. Nothing is removed
to make room.

## 6. Visual production specification

This is where "same caliber or higher" is won or lost. The source series uses flat spot illustration
and photographed realia. Wayfinder authors everything as vector art in one system, which means it
can be animated, themed, translated and made accessible — none of which a photograph can do.

### 6.1 Per-unit art inventory

| # | Asset type | Count/unit | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Unit opener scene | 1 | Large establishing illustration of the unit's Portmere setting, 6–12 labeled affordances |
| 2 | Vocabulary set A images | 8–12 | Objects or actions, consistent scale and lighting |
| 3 | Vocabulary set B images | 8–9 | Second lexical set, for the match task |
| 4 | *Real Lives* story panels | 2–3 | Before/after sequence for the writing model |
| 5 | Grammar picture cues | 6–10 | A single diorama rendered in two states (now vs earlier) for tense contrast |
| 6 | Realia documents | 3–5 | Form, timetable, label, listing, payslip, agreement, app screen, invoice, evaluation |
| 7 | Data graphics | 1–2 | Bar, line, pie or scatter, for the *Talk it over* discussion |
| 8 | Explanatory diagrams | 1–2 | Process, pathway, cycle, decision tree, comparison matrix |
| 9 | Conversation scene | 1 + 3 poses | The dialogue's setting, with character poses for the turns |
| 10 | Pronunciation visual | 1 | Articulation close-up or a stress-pattern figure |
| 11 | *Think It Through* case scene | 1 | One moment, one problem, no resolution shown |
| | **Total** | **~28–35** | **× 78 units ≈ 2,200–2,700 assets** |

### 6.2 Style system

- **One illustration grammar across the series:** flat fills, a single consistent line weight for
  structure and a lighter weight for detail, no gradients except a two-stop ambient wash, geometric
  character construction with a fixed head-to-body ratio per age band.
- **Six level palettes** sharing one neutral spine, so a Starter page and a Level 5 page are
  recognizably one series and instantly distinguishable by level.
- **Every asset exists in light and dark theme**, defined in tokens, not duplicated by hand.
- **Diagrams carry their meaning in layout, not in color.** Color is never the only channel:
  every encoded distinction also has a shape, a label or a position. Checked against WCAG AA
  contrast in both themes.
- **Every image has authored alt text** written as a teaching sentence, not a caption — because a
  learner using a screen reader is doing the same exercise.
- **Realia is designed, not photographed.** Each document is a real layout with invented data:
  invented names, invented addresses, invented companies, plausible numbers that add up. Documents
  are typeset so that the *reading skill* the slot teaches — finding a total, a date, a condition —
  is genuinely exercised by the layout.
- **Characters are reused.** A fixed turnaround and expression sheet per character, authored once per
  band. This is what makes 2,400 assets achievable and what makes the books feel written.

### 6.3 Diagram taxonomy (so the diagrams teach rather than decorate)

Each unit's diagram is chosen from a fixed set according to what the content *is*: a **process**
(numbered steps with a clear entry and exit), a **pathway** (branching routes with conditions on the
branches), a **cycle** (a loop whose repetition is the point), a **comparison matrix** (two or three
options against three or four criteria), a **timeline** (for tense contrast and for life events), a
**part-whole** (for count/noncount, for budgets, for composition), or a **ranked bar** (for survey
and poll data the class generates itself). A diagram that does not match one of these is a decoration
and does not ship.

## 8. Quality gates — every unit passes all of them or does not ship

1. **Band check.** ≥95% of running tokens inside the level band + 1, measured against `WORDLIST.json`.
2. **Readability** inside the §4.2 target range, article text only.
3. **Recycling check.** Every grammar point from unit n appears in ≥2 later units; every *Remember?*
   strip resolves to real earlier items.
4. **Key coverage.** 100% of closed items keyed, variants included.
5. **Audio completeness.** Every audio slot has a script; every script has a track id, speaker and
   duration estimate; total tracks per unit inside 15–22.
6. **Descriptor completeness.** Every lesson carries CEFR can-do descriptors. No CASAS, CCRS or ELPS
   codes anywhere.
7. **Clean-room scan.** No 6-word-or-longer string shared with any Step Forward source lawfully held
   by the project. Unit titles, box names and character names all checked against the source list.
8. **Internationalization scan.** No US-only institution, brand, document or emergency number; no
   cross-reference to any external picture dictionary; no currency symbol in body text.
9. **Word-count check.** Every text inside its §4.2 budget.
10. **Art completeness.** Every `ART` id referenced by a slot and every slot needing art has an id;
    every asset has alt text; every diagram matches a §6.3 type; contrast passes in both themes.
11. **Answer sanity.** Every item solvable from the material presented, with exactly one defensible
    answer unless the slot is explicitly open.
12. **Source check.** Every cited source is real, public, reachable, and dated 2024 or later unless
    the point is historical.
13. **Speaking floor.** Every lesson has ≥3 oral slots and ≥1 open-ended one, per §2.1, counted from
    the slot schema's `interaction` field. No lesson opens silently. A lesson that fails this is
    rewritten, not waived.
14. **Variant integrity.** Every `VARIANTS.json` token resolves in both editions; no answer key
    diverges between editions unless flagged `variant_is_content: true`; every affected audio line
    carries its variant note.

Gates 1–5, 7–10 and 12–14 are automated. Gates 6 and 11 are a human read of every unit.

## 9. Legal posture

*General information, not legal advice. Have your own IP counsel review before commercial release.*

The plan rests on the distinction US law draws in 17 U.S.C. §102(b), and in *Baker v. Selden*,
between a method or system — free to use — and the expression of it, which is protected. What
Wayfinder takes is method: the number and order of lesson types, page budgets, slot functions and
item counts, the grammar sequence, the multilevel tiering, the staged teaching cycle, the practice of
correlating to published standards. All of that is either generic adult-ESL convention documented in
the public literature, or an unprotectable system.

What Wayfinder does not take, and the rules that keep it that way:

1. **Clean room.** No Step Forward text is opened, pasted or paraphrased during authoring. Authors
   work from this plan only.
2. **No shared strings.** No rubric, sentence or dialogue line shares a six-word run with any source.
   Scanned mechanically (gate 7).
3. **All expression is new:** every character, name, story, situation, document, number, address,
   company, article and image. Sources cited are our own and real.
4. **All names differ.** Every unit title differs from the source's. Every named box is renamed:
   *UNIT GOALS*, *Check Yourself*, *Take It Outside*, *On the Job*, *Team Up & Review*,
   *Writer's Tip*, *Reader's Tip*, *Real Lives*, *Think It Through*, *Starting Point*.
5. **Arrangement is varied where it is most distinctive:** the lesson order is changed (§2), and the
   within-unit order of secondary boxes alternates by unit parity.
6. **Vocabulary is justified by frequency band**, line by line, never by anyone's list.
7. **Distinct trade dress.** Own name, own palettes, own logo, own grid, own illustration system. No
   third-party trade marks and no cross-reference to any picture dictionary.
8. **Trade-mark clearance** on the final series name before release.
