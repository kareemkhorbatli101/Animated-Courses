# Unit 8 · The Transit Obligation
%%SUB A host state's duties are in an agreement, not in courtesy — and the agreement says what happens when the parties disagree%%
%%GOALS
Use impersonal structures to say whose duty something is
Describe an obligation that arises from an agreement rather than from goodwill
Say what a dispute settlement clause provides for
%%

People describe a host state's duties towards an international organization as hospitality. They are
in a written agreement, they concern access and transit for people with business at the organization,
and the agreement contains a clause about what happens when the two sides disagree. Hospitality has
no arbitration clause.

> **Reader's Tip** — Find the dispute settlement clause. An instrument that provides for arbitration
> is an instrument whose other clauses are obligations, and the presence of that clause will tell you
> more about the agreement's character than its preamble will.

%%FIG j08-f5 | A one-page internal note: a heading, a short table listing three things the agreement does, a boxed paragraph headed the clause that tells you the others are obligations, a paragraph headed two framings, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

> ## Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Expatriates · protocol note 8
> *Protocol Department and Legal Affairs · a headquarters agreement · page 1 of 1*
>
> The **agreement of 1947** between the Organization and the state in which its headquarters are
> situated does three things that matter to a protocol department.
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | **1** | it **defines the headquarters district** — the area in which the premises lie |
> | **2** | it provides for **access to that district and transit through the host state's territory** for persons having business with the Organization, including representatives of members, officials and persons invited by the Organization |
> | **3** | it provides for **disputes between the Organization and the host state to be settled by arbitration** |
>
> **The third clause is the one to notice.** An instrument that provides a procedure for resolving
> disagreements is an instrument whose other provisions are **obligations**. Nobody drafts an
> arbitration clause for a hospitality arrangement.
>
> **Two framings of the same facts.**
>
> | Framing | What follows |
> |---|---|
> | **Hospitality** | nothing follows. There is no remedy for the withdrawal of a favor, and no forum in which to raise it |
> | **Obligation under an agreement** | **it falls to the Organization** to raise a difficulty; **it is open to either party** to invoke the dispute settlement clause; and the question is justiciable |
>
> **Why this matters to a sending state.** When one of our representatives has difficulty reaching a
> meeting, our first question is not whether the host state has been helpful. It is **what the
> agreement provides, to whom the duty is owed, and whose duty it is to raise it** — which, in the
> ordinary case, is the Organization's and not ours.
>
> **What to write.** *The obligation arises under the headquarters agreement.* **Not** *the host
> state has generously facilitated…*
>
> *Source: the agreement of 1947 between the United Nations and the state in which its Headquarters
> are situated, which defines the headquarters district, provides for access and transit for persons
> having business with the Organization, and provides for the settlement of disputes between the
> parties by arbitration.*

<sub>**the headquarters district** — the defined area in which the premises lie · **transit** —
traveling through a state to reach somewhere else · **to arise from** — to be created by, as a legal

### 1 · Impersonal structures

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP4-08-06%%

> **Layla:** Your note says *we should complain about this*.
> **Omar:** Our representative could not get to the meeting.
> **Layla:** I know, and the question is whose complaint it is. **It falls to the Organization** to
> raise a difficulty of this kind with the host state, because the obligation is owed to the
> Organization under its agreement.
> **Omar:** We are the state whose representative was delayed.
> **Layla:** We are, and **it is open to us** to ask the Organization to take it up, and **it is open
> to us** to make our own representations. What is not open to us is to treat the agreement as though
> we were a party to it.
> **Omar:** So what do I write?
> **Layla:** **There is no obligation on the host state towards us under that agreement.** **It is
> for the Organization to raise the matter**, and we have asked it to. Three impersonal sentences
> and nobody's feelings in any of them.

*Which of Layla's structures says that something is permitted, and which that it is a duty?*

**1B  Underline the five impersonal structures in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Structure | Means |
|---|---|
| **It falls to** X **to** + verb | it is X's duty |
| **It is for** X **to** + verb | it is X's decision or role |
| **It is open to** X **to** + verb | X may, if X wishes |
| **There is no obligation on** X **to** + verb | X need not |
| **It is not open to** X **to** + verb | X may not |
| **It would be for** X **to** + verb | hypothetically, X's role |

> **Grammar Note** — These structures move the agent out of the subject position without hiding it:
> *it falls to the Organization* still says who. That is the difference between an impersonal
> structure and a bare passive, and it is why this register prefers them. *The matter should be
> raised* says nothing about who must raise it, and in a note about an obligation that silence is the
> whole question.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Host state arrangements are the part of multilateral life where gratitude and obligation are hardest
to keep apart, and both sides have an interest in blurring them. A host state that describes its
duties as hospitality collects credit for performing them. Delegations that describe them as
hospitality have, in effect, agreed that there is no remedy when they are not performed. The
professional position is unsentimental and compatible with genuine good manners: thank a host state
for what it does beyond its obligations, in a speech, warmly; and in a note, write what the agreement
provides, to whom the duty is owed, and whose it is to raise a difficulty. The two registers do
different jobs and there is no reason an officer cannot use both in the same week, provided the
documents do not get mixed up.

**Discuss:** What does your ministry do for other states beyond its obligations? Does anybody write
it down?
