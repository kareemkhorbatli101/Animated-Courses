*Which of Layla's forms carries a suggestion that something did not happen?*

**1B  Underline the three future-in-the-past forms in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Form | Example | What it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| **was / were to** + verb | The treaty **was to enter** into force when… | the plan at the time |
| **was / were to have** + participle | It **was to have entered** into force by then. | and it did not |
| **was due to** + verb | The conference **was due to convene** in 1999. | scheduled; outcome neutral |
| **was going to** + verb | Ratification **was going to follow** quickly. | expected at the time |
| **would** + verb | Everybody thought it **would be** in force by 2000. | reported expectation |
| **would have been** | It **would have been** in force under any other clause. | a counterfactual |

> **Grammar Note** — *Was to have entered into force* is the form that quietly says *and it did not*.
> That is a strong implication to put in a note without evidence, and here the evidence is thirty
> years of a status page. Use the plainer *was to enter into force* when you are only describing the
> design.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Treaties with conditional entry-into-force clauses are the hardest instruments to describe neutrally,
because every available adjective takes a side. *Close* implies it will happen; *stalled* implies it
will not; *moribund* is a verdict. The only language that holds in every room is the arithmetic of
the clause: this many named states, this many outstanding, and then this many days. It is dry, and
dryness here is a professional asset rather than a failure of style. There is also a discipline about
reasons. A state's decision not to ratify is published, debated and defended in its own parliament,
and none of that is a foreign ministry's to summarize in a legal note. Officers who keep clause and
politics in separate documents are the ones whose legal notes get believed, and whose political
cables get read.

**Discuss:** Which adjective does your own ministry use about this kind of treaty? What does it
imply?

> have been** more than sufficient. Under this clause **the number of ratifications is not the
> question**; the completeness of the annex is. **The Treaty was to have entered into force once the
> annex was complete.**
>
> **One withdrawal, both halves.** One state **withdrew its ratification in 2023** and **remains a
> signatory**. **A signatory is obliged to refrain from acts which would defeat the object and
> purpose of a treaty** until it makes clear that it does not intend to become a party.
>
> **What this note does not do.** It uses **no adjective** about the likelihood of entry into force.
> It **names no state** and **characterizes no state's reasons**. Those are published, and they
> belong in a political reporting cable written with the clearances that this note does not carry.
>
> *Source: the Treaty's entry-into-force provision; published ratification figures as at March 2024;
> a published fact sheet of January 2025; published accounts of the 2023 withdrawal of ratification
> with the signature remaining.*

> **Writer's Tip** — *It uses no adjective about the likelihood of entry into force* is a sentence
> about your own drafting, inside the draft. It looks strange the first time. It is the line that
> stops a later reader inserting one.
