# Unit 11 · Three Instruments, One Subject
%%SUB The unit with no figure in it — and the sentence a drafter writes instead%%
%%GOALS

One subject, cultural property in armed conflict, and three instruments: a convention of 1954, a
First Protocol of the same year, and a Second Protocol of 1999. Each has its own list of parties. Add
the lists together and you have invented a number. This unit is the only one in this book that prints
no count at all. It prints where the counts live, and the sentence you write when you have not yet
been there.

### 1 · Passive causatives

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP4-11-06%%

> **Hala:** Your draft says *I checked the three lists*. Did you?
> **Omar:** The reference section checked them. I asked them to.
> **Hala:** Then write what happened. **We had the three lists checked by the reference section.**
> You did not do the work and you should not take the credit, and more to the point, a reader needs to
> know whose check it was.
> **Omar:** Isn't *we had them checked* a little indirect?
> **Hala:** It is exact. *I checked* claims a competence you were not exercising. *We had them
> checked* says somebody competent did it at our request. And **we had the printouts dated** says the
> other thing a reader needs, which is when.
> **Omar:** And the one list that has not come back?
> **Hala:** **It has not been verified**, and the cell stays empty. Do not get it filled in by
> somebody's recollection to make the table look finished.

*What is the difference between what Omar wrote and what Hala wants him to write?*

**1B  Underline the four causative or passive structures in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| **have + object + past participle** | We **had the list checked**. |
| **have + object + pp + by** | We **had it checked by** the reference section. |
| **get + object + past participle** *(informal)* | Don't **get the cell filled in** from memory. |
| **has not been + pp** | The third figure **has not been verified**. |
| **is being + pp** | The figures **are being verified**. |
| **must be + pp** | Each list **must be confirmed** separately. |

> **Grammar Note** — *I checked* and *we had it checked* are both honest and they are not the same
> claim. Use the first only for work your own hands did. In a ministry most checking is had done, and
> saying so tells the reader which section to ask when the figure turns out to be wrong.

**1D  Ask and answer with a partner. Three each.**

3. What has not been confirmed yet?

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

The pressure to merge lists is not laziness, it is kindness. A director with eleven minutes wants one
number, and the officer who supplies three numbers and a sentence about overlapping instruments feels
obstructive. This is the point in a career at which habits set. The officers who go on to be trusted
are the ones who learned to say "that question has three answers" without apology and without a
lecture — and who then make the three answers easy to use, with a date beside each and one line of
explanation. The others become fluent. Fluency is what produces a tidy figure for "the Hague regime"
at five o'clock, and a correction eighteen months later that nobody can trace, because the note did
not say who had checked what or on which day. Being useful and being exact are not in tension in this
trade. They are in tension for about forty seconds, which is how long it takes to say the longer
sentence.

**Discuss:** When were you last obstructive in the useful sense? What did it cost you that day?
