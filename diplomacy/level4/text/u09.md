# Unit 9 · A Count Nobody Can State Officially
%%SUB One disputed ratification, and a number that cannot be given rather than one that is wrong%%
%%GOALS
Use qualifying markers to limit a statement precisely
Report a figure that is unavailable rather than wrong
Say what a page does not allow you to state
%%

A commentary published alongside a secretariat report once recorded that a dispute about a single
ratification meant the figure could not officially be counted as one hundred and twenty-three. Not
that it was one hundred and twenty-two, and not that it was wrong. That it could not officially be
counted. That sentence is the purest thing in this book.

### 1 · Qualifying markers

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP4-09-06%%

> **Layla:** Your sentence says *the membership has grown by nineteen since 2012*.
> **Omar:** A hundred and forty-one now, a hundred and twenty-two then.
> **Layla:** **Insofar as** the two figures are counted on the same basis, yes. Are they?
> **Omar:** I do not know.
> **Layla:** Then that is the sentence. *Insofar as the two figures are counted on the same basis,
> the membership has grown by nineteen since 2012.* The marker is not decoration; it is the limit of
> what you have checked.
> **Omar:** And the disputed ratification?
> **Layla:** **Save that** a dispute concerning one ratification meant the 2012 figure could not
> officially be stated as a hundred and twenty-three. One marker, one exception, in its own clause.
> **Omar:** It sounds lawyerly.
> **Layla:** It is lawyerly, and it is also the difference between a sentence that is true and a
> sentence that happens to be true today.

*Which of Layla's markers limits the extent, and which excepts one thing?*

**1B  Underline the two qualifying markers in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Marker | Example |
|---|---|
| **insofar as** | **Insofar as** the figures are counted on the same basis, the total has grown. |
| **to the extent that** | **To the extent that** the entries are comparable, the trend holds. |
| **save that** | …**save that** one ratification was disputed. |
| **subject to** | The position is as stated, **subject to** any later accession. |
| **without prejudice to** | **Without prejudice to** our position on that entry… |
| **for the purposes of** | **For the purposes of** this note, the lower figure is used. |

> **Grammar Note** — These markers are not synonyms. *Insofar as* and *to the extent that* limit how
> far a statement holds. *Save that* takes one thing out of it. *Subject to* makes it conditional on
> something future. *Without prejudice to* reserves a position without arguing it now. Using the
> wrong one is worse than using none, because each one tells a trained reader something specific.

**1D  Ask and answer with a partner. Three each.**

1. What is true in your work insofar as something else holds?
2. What is true save that one exception?
3. What is true subject to a decision you do not control?

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Officials are trained to supply numbers and a request for one feels like a request that must be met.
This unit is about the rarest and most valuable exception. Where a publisher has declined to state a
total because stating it would decide a contested question, a ministry that supplies a figure anyway
has done two things: it has taken a position the publisher refused to take, and it has done so by
arithmetic rather than by decision, which means nobody authorized it and nobody will remember that it
happened. The professional response is to report the absence precisely, in the publisher's own terms,
and to add the current figure if there is one. It is less satisfying than an answer and it is more
useful than a wrong one, and officers who can do it calmly are the ones trusted with files where the
numbers themselves are the dispute.

**Discuss:** Has your office ever supplied a figure that somebody else had declined to state?
