### 1 · Formulaic requests

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP4-03-06%%

> **Hala:** Read me your request.
> **Omar:** "Please send us the list of reservations."
> **Hala:** To a foreign ministry? **The Ministry would be grateful if a copy of the declarations and
> reservations could be transmitted.**
> **Omar:** That is twice as long and says the same thing.
> **Hala:** It says the same thing and it does something else as well. **Would be grateful if**
> leaves them free to say no. **Please send** does not, and a ministry that has been given no way of
> refusing politely will find a way of refusing impolitely.
> **Omar:** And if we have been instructed to ask?
> **Hala:** Then you say so: **the Embassy is instructed to request**. That form tells the reader
> that the request comes from a capital and that the officer signing it has no discretion, which is
> sometimes exactly what you want them to know.
> **Omar:** And *ventures to suggest*?
> **Hala:** Is for when you are proposing something they may dislike, and it is the politest form in
> the language. Use it twice a year.

*Which of Hala's forms leaves the other ministry room to refuse?*

**1B  Underline the four formulaic request forms in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Form | What it signals |
|---|---|
| **has the honour to request** | a formal request, neutral |
| **would be grateful if** + passive | a request that leaves room to refuse |
| **would be obliged for** + noun | the same, slightly warmer |
| **is instructed to request** | the request comes from the capital |
| **ventures to suggest** | a proposal they may dislike |
| **is not in a position to** | a refusal that is not a quarrel |
| **avails itself of the opportunity to recall** | a reminder without an accusation |

> **Grammar Note** — *Would be grateful if* takes a past form after it — *if a copy could be
> transmitted* — and almost always a passive. The passive is doing real work: it asks for the thing
> to be done without naming the person who must do it, which in a note between ministries is the
> whole point of the construction.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Privileges and immunities is the subject where a protocol officer is most often asked for an answer
on the telephone, and where the right answer is almost always *I will read the page and call you
back*. Two habits make the difference. The first is treating the published total as a starting point
rather than an answer: in this field the total is the least useful number on the page, because every
practical question is about one state and the page answers it state by state, in footnotes. The
second is knowing which convention you are in. The 1946 and 1947 conventions are routinely merged in
conversation, in briefings and in drafts, and the merge is invisible until somebody asserts an
immunity that the relevant instrument does not provide. Neither habit is difficult. Both are
unglamorous, and both are why some officers are telephoned about hard cases and others are not.

**Discuss:** What question does your own office answer on the telephone that it should answer in
writing?

> and Immunities of the United Nations of 1946**, which entered into force on **17 September 1946**.

> Government of Astrand in respect of that Convention could be transmitted** at the Government's
> convenience.
>
> The Ministry **ventures to add** that its inquiry does not extend to the **Convention on the
> Privileges and Immunities of the Specialized Agencies of 21 November 1947**, which the Ministry
> understands to be a **separate instrument with its own list of parties**, and in respect of which
> the Ministry **would make a separate inquiry** should one be required.
>
> The Ministry **avails itself of this opportunity to renew** to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of

>
> **Internal note, not part of the above.** On the depositary's record the 1946 Convention has **162
> parties out of 193 member states — 31 are not parties — and 23 parties accepted it subject to
> reservations**. Published figures give about **131 parties** to the 1947 Convention. **Both figures
> carry the date of the page they were taken from.** **No statement about the position of any
