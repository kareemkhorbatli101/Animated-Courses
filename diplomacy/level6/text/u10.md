# Unit 10 · Subsequent Practice
%%SUB What your ministry did for nine years, read back to you as what the treaty means%%
%%GOALS
Use the language of evidential weight
Say when silence may constitute acceptance
Distinguish practice that establishes agreement from practice that does not
%%

A treaty is interpreted in the light of its text, its context, its object and purpose — and of what
the parties have since **done**. Subsequent practice in the application of a treaty which
**establishes the agreement of the parties** regarding its interpretation is to be **taken into
account** in interpreting it. Conduct may consist of acts **or omissions**, and **silence may
constitute acceptance** where the circumstances call for a reaction. Everything your ministry has
done for nine years is evidence of what it thinks the treaty means, and the file is open.

%%FIG l10-f5 | A one-page internal note: a heading, a boxed paragraph on status, a boxed quotation of a treaty-interpretation provision with one phrase underlined, a two-column box distinguishing the two kinds of practice, a boxed paragraph on silence, a four-row box on weight, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

> ## Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Expatriates · legal note 10
> *Legal Affairs with Dr S. Mallah · subsequent practice · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The texts, and their status.**
>
> > The rule is in the **Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties** of **23 May 1969**, Articles 31
> > and 32 — a treaty, binding on its parties. The **Conclusions on subsequent agreements and
> > subsequent practice in relation to the interpretation of treaties** were adopted by the
> > **International Law Commission in 2018** with commentaries; the General Assembly **took note of
> > them** and **brought them to the attention of States**. **The Conclusions are not a treaty.**
>
> **The rule.**
>
> > There shall be **taken into account**, together with the context:
> > **(a)** any **subsequent agreement** between the parties regarding the interpretation of the
> > treaty or the application of its provisions;
> > **(b)** any **subsequent practice** in the application of the treaty <u>**which establishes the
> > agreement of the parties**</u> regarding its interpretation.
> >
> > Recourse may be had to **supplementary means** of interpretation, which include **other
> > subsequent practice** — conduct by **one or more parties** in the application of the treaty that
> > does **not** establish the agreement of all of them.
>
> **Two categories, and the phrase that separates them.**
>
> | | What it is | What it does |
> |---|---|---|
> | **subsequent practice establishing agreement** | conduct in applying the treaty that **establishes the agreement of the parties** | is **taken into account** in interpreting the treaty, as an authentic means |
> | **other subsequent practice** | conduct by **one or more** parties that does not establish that agreement | may be used as a **supplementary means**; it is evidence, and not authority |
>
> **Conduct includes omissions, and silence may count.**
>
> > Conduct may consist of **acts or omissions**, including, in certain circumstances, **inaction**.
> > **Silence may constitute acceptance** of a practice **where the circumstances call for a
> > reaction**. Whether they do is a question of fact: what was circulated, to whom, how visibly, and
> > whether a party had a real opportunity to respond.
>
> **What gives practice weight.**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | **clarity and specificity** | a practice that answers the exact question counts for more than a general tendency |
> | **repetition** | conduct repeated over years counts for more than a single instance |
> | **visibility** | practice the other parties could see counts; practice buried in a file does not |
> | **consistency** | a practice contradicted by the same ministry's own conduct elsewhere is worth little |
>
> **Why this unit exists, for us.** Because every form this Ministry files, every figure it reports,
> every statement it makes at a conference and **every note it declines to answer** is conduct in the
> application of a treaty, and all of it is evidence of what we take the treaty to mean. **We make
> interpretative arguments every working day, mostly by accident, and we are the only party in the
> room who has not read them.**
>
> **The two working rules.** **First, know our own practice before we argue about a provision** —
> compile the log, nine years if there are nine years, before the first meeting and not after the
> other side has quoted it. **Second, answer notes that call for an answer.** Where the
> circumstances call for a reaction, silence may be acceptance, and a note left unanswered for two
> years is not neutral.
>
> **What to write.** *Our practice since 2017 has been to [X]. That practice is recorded in the
> annual returns at Annex B and has been circulated to all parties. We are not aware of any
> objection.* **What not to write:** *the provision clearly means [X]*, with nothing about what we
> have been doing, which invites the other side to tell our own history to us.
>
> *Source: the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties of 23 May 1969, Articles 31(3) and 32; and
> the Conclusions on subsequent agreements and subsequent practice in relation to the interpretation
> of treaties, adopted by the International Law Commission in 2018 with commentaries, of which the
> General Assembly took note and which it brought to the attention of States. The Conclusions are not
> a treaty.*

> **Suhair:** You can say that **silence may constitute acceptance where the circumstances call for

> circumstances are the argument. The silence on its own is nothing.**
> **Jamil:** And if only we have done it this way?

> authority. Say so yourself, in your own paper, before anybody says it to you. **A paper that states

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Form | Example | The mistake it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| **our practice since [year] has been to** | the fact, with a date | *it clearly means* |

> **Grammar Note** — In this subject the strong form is always the factual one. *We have done X since
> 2017, visibly, and nobody has objected* is stronger than any adverb, and it cannot be contradicted
> except by facts, which is exactly the argument you want.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

This is the unit that changes how officers see their own filing cabinets. Every ministry contains
thousands of small acts performed in the application of treaties — returns filed, forms designed,
notifications sent, questions answered, notes ignored — and almost none of them were decided as
interpretative positions. They were decided by a competent officer in 2017 who had an afternoon and a
reasonable view. Nine years later that afternoon is the practice of a state, visible to every other
party, specific to the exact question, and repeated annually with the regularity of a metronome. The
professional response is not anxiety. It is curiosity: find out what your ministry has been saying,
in conduct, before somebody else tells you. The ministries that lose arguments in this field are not
the ones whose practice is awkward; every ministry's practice is awkward in two places. They are the
ones who learn their own practice from the other side's paper.

**Discuss:** What would you find if you compiled your own office's practice for nine years? Who
decided it?

### 🗣 Group ranking

*In fours.* A meeting next month will discuss a provision your ministry has applied consistently for
nine years, with two inconsistent earlier years. Rank these, best to worst. You must agree.

a. Compile the log, state the practice factually, and explain the two years ourselves
b. State the practice factually and say nothing about the two years
c. Assert the meaning of the provision and rely on the text
d. Say nothing at the meeting and preserve our position
e. Propose that the conference adopt an agreed interpretation

Report your order, and say which one depends on nobody looking.

> ### Paper · interpretation of the threshold provision: our practice and its weight
> *Legal Affairs · for the delegation to the meeting of 14 October · 29 September*
>
> **1 Our practice. Since 2017 this Ministry has applied the provision as treating the threshold as
> net.** The practice is recorded in the **annual returns for 2017 to 2025**, nine in number, which
> are at **Annex B**. **The practice is specific to the question now raised and has been repeated in
> each of those years.**
>
> **2 Visibility. Each return was circulated to all parties**, by the secretariat, on the dates at
> **Annex B, column 3**. **The treatment of the threshold appears on the face of each return** and
> required no inquiry to discover.
>
> **3 Objections. No objection has been received, so far as the Ministry is aware.** **Silence may
> constitute acceptance of a practice where the circumstances call for a reaction**; the
> circumstances are those set out at paragraphs 1 and 2, and **the Ministry invites the meeting to
> draw its own conclusion** rather than asserting acquiescence.
>
> **4 Two earlier returns. The returns for 2015 and 2016 treated the threshold differently.** They
> were completed by a section reorganized in 2016, on a form superseded in the same year. **The
> Ministry draws attention to them**; their weight is a matter for the meeting, and in the
> Ministry's submission they are outweighed by nine consistent and more specific returns.
>
> **5 Legal characterization, with status. Subsequent practice in the application of a treaty which
> establishes the agreement of the parties regarding its interpretation is to be taken into
> account** under **Article 31(3)(b)** of the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties. **Practice of
> one party alone is a supplementary means** under Article 32: **evidence, and not authority**. The
> Ministry's submission is that the practice at paragraphs 1 to 3, taken with the practice of other
> parties at **Annex C**, falls within the former; **if the meeting takes a different view, it falls
> within the latter.**
>
> *The Conclusions of the International Law Commission of 2018 on this subject, of which the General
> Assembly took note, are not a treaty and are cited for their statement of the matter.*

> material, with the explanation and the submission in the same paragraph, converts an ambush into a
> detail — and nobody who reads it will believe you have hidden anything else.

### 3 · Write

Write **170 to 200 words**: a paper on your own office's practice in applying a rule.

**State the practice with a start date, its record and its specificity. Show its visibility, with
dates. Deal with objections and with silence, stating the circumstances and inviting a conclusion

and your submission in the same paragraph. End with the legal characterization, and state the status
of any non-binding text you rely on.**
