# Unit 7 · Express, and Again for Execution
%%SUB Two waivers, not one — and the waiver is the sending state's to give%%
%%GOALS
Use *express*, *shall not be held to imply* and *a separate waiver*
Say who may waive an immunity, and who may not
Distinguish jurisdiction from execution
%%

Immunity from jurisdiction can be waived. It is waived by **the sending state**, not by the person
who enjoys it, and the waiver **must always be express**. And a waiver in respect of civil
proceedings **shall not be held to imply** a waiver in respect of the execution of the judgment, for
which **a separate waiver shall be necessary**. Four sentences, one convention article, and more
embarrassment avoided in a careless ministry than any other provision in this book.

> ## Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Expatriates · legal note 7
> *Legal Affairs with the Protocol Department · waiver of immunity · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The instrument.** The **Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations**, done at Vienna on **18 April
> 1961**, entered into force on **24 April 1964**. It has about **193 parties** on the depositary's
> record, which carries the date of that page — **one of the most widely ratified treaties in
> existence**, which is why its text is quoted and not paraphrased in this Ministry.
>
> **The article, in four paragraphs.**
>
> > **1.** The immunity from jurisdiction of diplomatic agents and of persons enjoying immunity
> > under article 37 **may be waived by the sending State**.
> >
> > **2. Waiver must always be express.**
> >
> > **3.** The **initiation of proceedings** by a diplomatic agent or by a person enjoying immunity
> > from jurisdiction under article 37 **shall preclude him from invoking immunity from
> > jurisdiction** in respect of any **counterclaim directly connected with the principal claim**.
> >
> > **4.** <u>Waiver of immunity from jurisdiction in respect of civil or administrative proceedings
> > **shall not be held to imply waiver of immunity in respect of the execution of the judgment**,
> > for which **a separate waiver shall be necessary**.</u>
>
> **Four rules, and each one catches somebody.**
>
> | | The rule | Who it catches |
> |---|---|---|
> | **1** | the waiver is **the sending State's** to give | anybody who asks the individual |
> | **2** | it **must always be express** | anybody relying on conduct, cooperation or silence |
> | **3** | a person who **sues** cannot claim immunity against a **connected counterclaim** | a diplomatic agent who begins a case lightly |
> | **4** | a waiver for the **proceedings** is **not** a waiver for **execution** | the successful claimant at the door with a judgment |
>
> **Who may waive.** The sending State. **Not the diplomatic agent**, however senior, and **not the
> head of mission on their own authority** unless the sending State's own law or instructions so
> provide. A waiver communicated by the mission is accepted as the act of the sending State; **a
> waiver offered by the individual is not a waiver at all.**
>
> **Jurisdiction and execution are two different things, and the second is the one that bites.**
>
> > Immunity from **jurisdiction** concerns whether a court may **hear** the case. Immunity from
> > **execution** concerns whether a judgment may be **enforced** — against a bank account, against
> > property, by a bailiff at a door. **A ministry that waives the first and not the second has
> > allowed a case to be heard and nothing to be collected**, which is sometimes exactly what it
> > intends and must never be done by accident.
>
> **What a waiver note must say.** Four things, and a note missing any of them will be read against
> us: **who** is waiving (the sending State, in terms); **whose** immunity; **in respect of which
> proceedings**, identified by court and number; and **whether execution is included**. **A note
> that is silent on execution has not waived it**, which is the correct default and should
> nonetheless be stated, because silence is read as a decision only by people who know this article.
>
> **What to write.** *The sending State hereby expressly waives the immunity from jurisdiction of [X]
> in respect of the proceedings [identified]. This waiver does not extend to immunity from execution
> of any judgment, for which a separate waiver would be necessary.* **What not to write:** *the
> Embassy confirms that [X] is willing to appear*, which waives nothing and reads as though it
> does.
>
> *Source: the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations of 18 April 1961, in force 24 April 1964,
> Article 32; and the depositary's published figure of about 193 parties, which carries the date of
> that page.*

### 1 · Express terms and implication

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP6-07-06%%

> **Layla:** Your draft note says the Embassy confirms that the Counselor is willing to appear
> before the court.
> **Jamil:** That is what the Embassy told us.
> **Layla:** And it waives nothing. **Waiver must always be express**, and willingness to appear is
> not an express waiver by the sending State; it is a statement about a person's attitude. A court
> that proceeded on it would be proceeding on nothing, and when the point was taken — and it would
> be, on appeal — the whole proceeding would be wasted.
> **Jamil:** So the note must say *waives*.
> **Layla:** The note must say that **the sending State expressly waives the immunity from
> jurisdiction of the named person in respect of identified proceedings**. Who, whose, which, and in
> terms. And then the paragraph everybody forgets.
> **Jamil:** Execution.
> **Layla:** **A waiver of immunity from jurisdiction in civil or administrative proceedings shall
> not be held to imply waiver of immunity in respect of execution of the judgment, for which a
> separate waiver shall be necessary.** So our note must say what it does about execution — and
> whichever way we decide, it must say it, because a note that is silent on execution has not waived
> it and will be read by somebody who does not know that.
> **Jamil:** Could we waive both at once?
> **Layla:** We could, in two express sentences in one note, and the decision on the second is a
> different decision taken by different people, because it puts the mission's accounts and property
> within reach. **Never let the two decisions arrive in the building as one.**

*Why does Layla say a willingness to appear waives nothing?*

**1B  Underline the four express-and-implication structures in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Form | Example | The mistake it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| **expressly waives** | the sending State **expressly waives** | relying on conduct or cooperation |
| **must always be express** | the Convention's own rule | an implied waiver |
| **shall not be held to imply** | the second-step rule | assuming one waiver covers two |
| **a separate waiver shall be necessary** | what must be done instead | silence read as inclusion |
| **does not extend to execution** | the sentence for our note | ambiguity about the second step |
| **two decisions, taken separately** | the internal rule | one signature covering both |

> **Grammar Note** — *Shall not be held to imply* is a drafter's warning to a future reader: the
> inference you are about to draw is the wrong one. Whenever you meet it, somebody once drew that
> inference and it ended badly.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

The second waiver is the part of this article that looks like a trick and is in fact the reason the
first waiver can ever be given. Consider a sending state deciding whether to let one of its
diplomats be sued. If waiving immunity for the hearing automatically opened the mission's bank
accounts and the diplomat's apartment to a bailiff, no state would waive anything, ever, and every
private dispute involving a diplomatic agent would end in a dead silence. By separating the two
decisions the Convention makes the first one affordable: a state can allow a court to decide a
dispute on its merits while keeping control of whether an officer of another state enters premises
and removes property. That is why waivers for jurisdiction are given with some regularity and
waivers for execution are rare, and why the honest advice to a private claimant is to find out,
early, whether the second waiver is likely — because a judgment nobody can enforce has cost
somebody two years.

**Discuss:** Would you advise a private claimant to begin proceedings without knowing whether the
second waiver would come? What would you want to know first?

> ### Note No. 77/4 · waiver of immunity from jurisdiction
>
> The Embassy of Astrand **presents its compliments** to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and
> Expatriates and **has the honour to** communicate the following.
>
> **1 The waiver. The Government of Astrand, as sending State, expressly waives the immunity from
> jurisdiction** of **Mr Oskar Rehn**, Counselor of this Embassy, **in respect of the proceedings
> pending before the Commercial Court of Damascus, case number 1182 of this year**, and in respect of
> those proceedings only.
>
> **2 Scope.** The waiver extends to **the hearing and determination of the said proceedings,
> including any appeal**. It extends to **no other proceedings**, present or future.
>
> **3 Execution. This waiver does not extend to immunity from execution of any judgment given in the
> said proceedings.** As **Article 32(4)** of the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations provides,
> waiver of immunity from jurisdiction in respect of civil proceedings **shall not be held to imply
> waiver of immunity in respect of the execution of the judgment, for which a separate waiver shall
> be necessary**. **No such waiver is given by this note.**
>
> **4 Communication.** The Embassy requests that a copy of this note be **transmitted to the
> Court**.
>
> The Embassy **avails itself of this opportunity to renew** to the Ministry **the assurances of its
> highest consideration.**
>
> *Damascus, 4 March · [seal]*

> **Writer's Tip** — Paragraph 3 would be legally unnecessary, since silence waives nothing. Write it
> anyway. It converts a rule that only specialists know into a fact that the judge, the registrar and
> the plaintiff's lawyer all have in front of them on the day, and it prevents a year of
> correspondence.
