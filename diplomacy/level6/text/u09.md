# Unit 9 · Without Delay
%%SUB Three immediacies, one condition, and a right the detained person holds too%%
%%GOALS
Use *without delay*, *if he so requests* and *shall refrain from*
Distinguish the state's rights from the individual's
Explain a right that is exercised subject to local law
%%

One article of the law of consular relations contains the words **without delay** three times, a
condition that is constantly forgotten — the consular post is informed **if he so requests** — and a
sentence that stops a ministry in its tracks: a consular officer **shall refrain from taking action**
on behalf of a detained national **if he expressly opposes such action**. The rights in the article
are the sending state's and the national's at the same time, and this unit is about keeping those two
things in one sentence.

### 2 · Read

> **Reader's Tip** — Count the obligations in an article before you argue about one of them. This
> article contains at least four, they fall on different people, and ministries routinely complain
> about the wrong one.

%%FIG l09-f5 | A one-page internal note: a heading, a line giving the instrument with its dates and parties, a boxed quotation of one subparagraph with three phrases underlined, a four-row table of obligations with who owes each, a boxed quotation on refraining from action, a paragraph headed the proviso, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

> ## Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Expatriates · legal note 9
> *Legal Affairs with the Consular Department · consular access to detained nationals · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The instrument.** The **Vienna Convention on Consular Relations**, done at Vienna on **24 April
> 1963**, entered into force on **19 March 1967**. It has about **182 parties** on the depositary's
> record, which carries the date of that page.
>
> **The subparagraph, in its own words.**
>
> > If **he so requests**, the competent authorities of the receiving State shall, <u>**without
> > delay**</u>, **inform the consular post** of the sending State if, within its consular district, a
> > national of that State is arrested or committed to prison or to custody pending trial or is
> > detained in any other manner. **Any communication addressed to the consular post** by the person
> > arrested, in prison, custody or detention **shall be forwarded** by the said authorities
> > <u>**without delay**</u>. **The said authorities shall inform the person concerned** <u>**without
> > delay**</u> **of his rights** under this subparagraph.
>
> **Four obligations, and they fall on different people.**
>
> | | The obligation | Owed by | Triggered by |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **1** | inform **the person** of his rights under the subparagraph | the receiving State's authorities | **the detention itself** |
> | **2** | inform **the consular post** of the detention, without delay | the receiving State's authorities | **the person's request** |
> | **3** | **forward** any communication he addresses to the post, without delay | the receiving State's authorities | **his writing it** |
> | **4** | **refrain** from action on his behalf if he expressly opposes it | **the consular officer** | **his express opposition** |
>
> **Obligation 1 is the one that matters most and is complained about least.** A detained person who
> is never told that he may ask will not ask, and the obligation to notify the post will never be
> triggered. **So the first question in any case of late notification is not *when were we told?* but
> *when was he told?*** — and ministries that ask the second question get further.
>
> **The rights of the consular officer.** Consular officers **shall have the right to visit** a
> national of the sending State who is in prison, custody or detention, **to converse and correspond
> with him** and **to arrange for his legal representation**; and to visit a national serving a
> sentence in their district.
>
> **And the sentence that stops us.**
>
> > Nevertheless, consular officers **shall refrain from taking action** on behalf of a national who
> > is in prison, custody or detention **if he expressly opposes such action**.
>
> **Whose rights are these?** Both. The article confers rights on the sending State, exercised
> through its consular officers, and it requires that **the detained person be informed of his
> rights** — and his express opposition stops the State acting on his behalf. **A ministry that
> treats this as its own right alone will one day act for somebody who has asked it not to.**
>
> **The proviso.** The rights in this paragraph **shall be exercised in conformity with the laws and
> regulations of the receiving State**, **subject to the proviso** that those laws and regulations
> **must enable full effect to be given to the purposes for which the rights are intended**. **Both
> halves travel together.** The first half alone is what a receiving State quotes; the second half
> alone is what a sending State quotes; and a serious note quotes the sentence entire.
>
> **What to write.** *Our national was detained on 12 March. The Ministry has asked on what date and
> at what hour he was informed of his rights under Article 36(1)(b), and on what date he requested
> that this post be informed.* **What not to write:** *the authorities failed to notify us*, which may
> be untrue and which asks about the second obligation rather than the first.
>
> *Source: the Vienna Convention on Consular Relations of 24 April 1963, in force 19 March 1967,
> Article 36; and the depositary's published figure of about 182 parties, which carries the date of
> that page.*

### 1 · Immediacy and the trigger

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP6-09-06%%

> **Layla:** Your draft note protests that the authorities failed to notify this post for three days.
> **Jamil:** They did. He was detained on Tuesday and we heard on Friday.
> **Layla:** And when did he request that we be told?
> **Jamil:** I do not know.
> **Layla:** Then we do not yet know whether the obligation to notify us was ever triggered, because
> it arises **if he so requests**. If he requested it on Thursday evening and they told us on Friday
> morning, there may be no breach of that obligation at all.
> **Jamil:** That seems like a loophole.
> **Layla:** It is a condition, and there is a separate obligation that no condition protects: **the
> authorities shall inform the person concerned, without delay, of his rights** under that
> subparagraph. Ask about that one. **When was he told that he could ask for us?**
> **Jamil:** And if he was never told?
> **Layla:** Then the failure is at the beginning of the chain and everything downstream follows from
> it. That is a far stronger note than ours, and it is a note about him rather than about our
> convenience, which is also how it should read.
> **Jamil:** So the first paragraph asks about him.
> **Layla:** The first paragraph asks two dates and two hours: when he was informed of his rights,
> and when he made a request. **You will notice that both questions can be answered from the
> receiving State's own register, and that is why we ask for dates and hours rather than for an
> explanation.**

*Why does Layla say the note about the first obligation is stronger?*

**1B  Underline the three immediacy and trigger expressions in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Form | Example | The mistake it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| **without delay** | the standard, quoted | inventing a number of hours |
| **if he so requests** | the trigger named | protesting an untriggered obligation |
| **when was he informed of his rights?** | the first question | asking about our own convenience |
| **on what date and at what hour** | the form of the question | inviting an explanation |
| **shall be forwarded without delay** | the third immediacy | forgetting his letters |
| **shall refrain from … if he expressly opposes** | his veto | acting for somebody who refused |

> **Grammar Note** — *Without delay* is deliberately not a number. Do not convert it into
> forty-eight hours in a note, however convenient: you will be quoted, and the next case will be the
> one where thirty hours was plainly a delay.

> the tenth day, our note will be answered in a single line and we shall have spent credit we need.
> **Jamil:** What do I ask instead?
> **Layla:** Two dates and two hours, and the first one is about him. **On what date and at what hour
> was he informed of his rights under Article 36(1)(b)?** That obligation has no condition. It falls
> on the authorities the moment he is detained, and it is the obligation whose breach explains every
> other failure, because a man who has not been told that he can ask for his consulate does not ask.
> **Jamil:** And if they answer that he was told on the first morning?
> **Layla:** Then we have learned something important and unwelcome, and we write a different note —

> shall refrain from taking action on behalf of a national in detention if he expressly opposes such
> action.** We do not know his wishes. So we ask him, we record the answer, and if he says he does

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

This article is the one place in these six books where the law speaks directly to a person rather
than to a state, and it is worth noticing how it does it. The receiving state's duty is to tell the
detained person his rights — not to tell his embassy, first, but to tell him. Everything else in the
chain depends on that sentence, and the sentence exists because the drafters understood that a
foreigner in a cell has no idea what he may ask for and nobody to ask. The second half of the same
insight is the one ministries resist: if he does not want his consulate involved, his consulate must
refrain. Officers who have done consular work for a decade come to value that sentence, usually after
the case where they acted energetically for somebody who had begged them not to, for reasons they
learned about afterwards. Our rights in that article exist for him. When the two diverge, the
article has already decided which way it goes.

**Discuss:** Can you think of a reason a detained person might not want their consulate told? Does
that change what you would do?

### 3 · Write

Write a **note of five or six short paragraphs** about a failure you cannot yet establish.

**State the facts you have, with dates and hours. Ask for the dates and hours you do not have, in
numbered subparagraphs. Make your request. Provide for the wishes of the person concerned, including
their opposition. Quote the applicable standard in full, both halves. End by saying what you are not
alleging and why, and reserve your position.**

**Read your note aloud to your partner first.** If any sentence alleges something your facts do not
support, move it into the paragraph of questions. Then check your own against the list, and swap.
