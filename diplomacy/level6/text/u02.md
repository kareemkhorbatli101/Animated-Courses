# Unit 2 · Treaty or Not
%%SUB *Whatever its particular designation* — four words that decide what a document is%%
%%GOALS
Use definitions and characterize a document by what it does
Read the drafting indicators that distinguish a treaty from a memorandum
Say what a non-binding instrument still does
%%

The definition of a treaty in the law of treaties ends with four words that undo every attempt to
decide the question by the title page: **whatever its particular designation**. A document headed
*Memorandum of Understanding* may be a treaty. A document headed *Treaty* may not be one. What
decides is whether the agreement is **governed by international law**, and that turns on what the
parties intended — which is read off the drafting, clause by clause, by somebody who knows the
indicators.

> **Reader's Tip** — A definition in a convention is drafted *for the purposes of that convention*.
> Read the opening words of the definition as carefully as its content, because they tell you how
> far the definition reaches.

%%FIG l02-f5 | A one-page internal note: a heading, a boxed quotation of a definition with four words underlined, a six-row table of drafting indicators with two columns, a boxed paragraph on what a non-binding instrument still does, a paragraph headed the one question to ask, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

> ## Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Expatriates · legal note 2
> *Legal Affairs with the Treaty Section · characterizing an instrument · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The definition.** The Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties of **23 May 1969** provides, **for
> the purposes of that Convention**:
>
> > *Treaty* means an **international agreement** concluded **between States** in **written form**
> > and **governed by international law**, whether embodied in a single instrument or in two or more
> > related instruments and <u>**whatever its particular designation**</u>.
>
> **Four elements, and one of them does the work.** An agreement; between States; in writing;
> **governed by international law**. The first three are usually obvious. **The fourth is the
> question**, and it turns on **what the parties intended** — which, in practice, is read off the
> drafting.
>
> **Six indicators, as widely used in drafting practice.**
>
> | A treaty tends to use | A non-binding instrument tends to use |
> |---|---|
> | **shall** | **will** |
> | **the Parties** | **the Participants** or **the Signatories** |
> | **enter into force** | **come into operation** / **come into effect** |
> | a **dispute settlement** clause | no such clause |
> | provision for **registration** | none |
> | **done at**, with formal signature blocks | **signed at**, with names and titles only |
>
> **These are indicators and not a code.** A single *shall* does not make a treaty, and a document
> that mixes the sets is a document to be read whole, and usually a document somebody drafted from
> two precedents at eleven at night. **The one thing that never decides the question is the title.**
>
> **What the four words prevent.** Without *whatever its particular designation*, a ministry could
> place itself outside the law of treaties by choosing a cover for its document. **The phrase is the
> reason the question is answerable at all** — and it cuts both ways: it also stops the other side
> claiming that your memorandum was a treaty because they would now prefer it to be one.
>
> **What a non-binding instrument still does.** A great deal, and officers who conclude that it does
> nothing are as wrong as the officers who think it binds.
>
> > It records what was agreed, in terms both sides can quote. It commits the **signatories
> > politically**, and political commitments are kept by states that wish to be trusted. It
> > establishes **practice**, and practice has evidential value, which is the subject of Unit 10 of
> > this book. And it may create expectations that cost real money to disappoint.
>
> **The one question to ask.** Not *what is it called* and not *is it binding*, which invites a yes
> or a no. **Ask: *is this agreement governed by international law, and what in the drafting shows
> it?*** The second half of that question is what a legal adviser is for.
>
> *Source: the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties of 23 May 1969, Article 2(1)(a), defining
> *treaty* for the purposes of that Convention and including the words *whatever its particular
> designation*; and its Article 3, which provides that the Convention's non-application to certain
> agreements does not affect their legal force. The drafting indicators in the table reflect widely
> published practice and are not provisions of the Convention.*

### 1 · Characterizing a document

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP6-02-06%%

> **Layla:** Your minute says the document is not a treaty because it is a memorandum of
> understanding.
> **Jamil:** That is what it is called.
> **Layla:** And the definition says *whatever its particular designation*, which means the title is
> the one thing in the document that proves nothing. Characterize it by reference to its terms.
> **Jamil:** It says *the Parties shall*, twice.
> **Layla:** Then say that, and say what it shows. **The instrument uses *shall* throughout and
> refers to the signatories as the Parties; it contains an entry-into-force provision and a dispute
> settlement clause. Those terms are consistent with an intention that the agreement be governed by
> international law.** You will notice that I have not said it is a treaty.
> **Jamil:** Why not? It plainly is.
> **Layla:** Because *characterization turns on the intention of the parties*, and the other side
> has not yet told us theirs. Our minute sets out the indicators and reaches a provisional view. When
> we write to them we ask, and we ask in a way that does not oblige anybody to lose an argument:
> *the Ministry would welcome confirmation that the instrument is intended to be governed by
> international law.*
> **Jamil:** And if they say it is not?
> **Layla:** Then we have a document with treaty language that both sides say is not a treaty, which
> is the worst of the three available outcomes and the one we are trying to avoid before signature
> rather than after.

*Why does Layla stop short of saying the instrument is a treaty?*

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

The memorandum of understanding is the most useful instrument in modern practice and the least
understood inside ministries. Two states that want to cooperate without engaging their legislatures,
their treaty registers or their dispute-settlement systems can write down exactly what they intend to
do, sign it, and perform it faithfully for twenty years. That is a real achievement of diplomatic
technique, and the states that use it well are not avoiding commitment: they are choosing a form of
commitment that can be made quickly and changed quickly. What goes wrong is never the choice. It is
the drafting: a ministry that wants a political instrument and signs one full of *shalls* has got
the worst of both forms, and a ministry that treats every memorandum as beneath clearance will one
day find an arbitration clause in one. The rule for a career is short. Choose the form deliberately,
draft it consistently with the choice, and never let the title do the thinking.

**Discuss:** Which form would you choose for a cooperation arrangement in your own area, and why?

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Minute · character of the proposed instrument with the Ministry of Astrand
> *Legal Affairs · for the Regional Section · 22 May*
>
> **1 The test. The character of an instrument does not depend on its designation.** A treaty is an
> international agreement concluded between States in written form and **governed by international
> law**, **whatever its particular designation**. The question therefore **turns on whether this
> agreement is intended to be governed by international law**, which is **evidenced by its terms**.
>
> **2 The terms of this instrument.**
>
> | Indicator | This instrument |
> |---|---|
> | obligatory language | **shall**, in paragraphs 2, 3 and 6 |
> | the signatories | **the Parties** |
> | commencement | an article headed **Entry into force** |
> | dispute settlement | **paragraph 9: arbitration** |
> | registration | not provided for |
> | title | *Memorandum of Understanding* — **not an indicator** |
>
> **3 Provisional view. The terms are consistent with an intention that the agreement be governed by
> international law.** This is a provisional view: **characterization turns on the intention of both
> Parties**, and the intention of the Ministry of Astrand has not been ascertained.
>
> **4 What we propose to ask. That the Ministry would welcome confirmation that the instrument is
> intended to be governed by international law.** The question is put in that form because it obliges
> nobody to abandon a position.
>
> **5 What follows on each answer.**
>
> | If the answer is | Then |
> |---|---|
> | **yes** | the instrument is cleared as a treaty: capacity under Article 7, registration, and any domestic procedure |
> | **no** | we invite the amendment of paragraphs 2, 3, 6 and 9 to language consistent with a political instrument |
> | **no answer** | we do not recommend signature |
>
> *We do not recommend signature before paragraph 4 has been answered. An instrument whose character
> is unsettled is the most expensive kind.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Paragraph 5 is what makes this a minute rather than an opinion. Setting out what
> follows on each possible answer lets a section act without coming back to you, and it is the single
> most useful habit a legal adviser can acquire.

**1D  Circle the correct word.**

1. The character (depends / does not depend) on the designation.
2. The view in paragraph 3 is (final / provisional).
3. The title (is / is not) an indicator.
4. Signature is recommended (now / after the question is answered).

### 3 · Write

Write **160 to 190 words**: a minute characterizing an instrument in your own area.

**State the test, with the four words that exclude the title. Tabulate the indicators and what this
instrument does, and mark the title as not an indicator. State a provisional view in terms exactly as
strong as the evidence. Say what you propose to ask the other side, and in what form. End with what
follows on each possible answer, including no answer.**

**Read your minute aloud to your partner first.** If your view sounds stronger than your table, soften
the view or strengthen the table. Then check your own against the list, and swap.

|---|---|

| Is the view marked as provisional? | |

**Peer feedback.** Read your partner's minute as a section with a delegation leaving on Friday. Do you
know what to do?

**Then talk it over.** *In pairs, out loud.* Did either of you state a conclusion the table did not support?
Why do you think a view drifts ahead of its evidence? What would you check before signing a minute?

> The question went to the Ministry of Astrand in May and came back in four lines in June. They had
> intended a political instrument throughout.
>
> Paragraphs 2, 3, 6 and 9 were redrafted: *will* for *shall*, *Participants* for *Parties*, *come
> into operation* for *entry into force*, and the arbitration clause replaced by a paragraph on
> consultations. It was signed in July, and it has been performed since without a single question.
>
> The three earlier instruments were pulled from the files in August. Two were political on their
> terms. One was not, and has an arbitration clause in it, and is now registered.
>
> Nobody has ever had to use the arbitration clause, and nobody now relies on anybody's memory about
> what the document was.
>
> Layla's note on the file is two lines. *Four verbs and a title, and the whole character of a
> relationship. The cheapest hour this department spent all year was the one in May.*

2. What was found when the three earlier instruments were examined?
3. Do you agree that the hour in May was the cheapest? What makes that hour so hard to find?
