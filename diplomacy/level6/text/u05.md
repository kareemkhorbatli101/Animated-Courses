# Unit 5 · What You Call It Does Not Decide What It Is
%%SUB *However phrased or named* — and the one word that settles the question: *purports*%%
%%GOALS
Characterize a statement by the effect it purports to produce
Use *purports to*, *however phrased or named* and *provides an indication of*
Distinguish a reservation from an interpretative declaration
%%

Two definitions in this field begin in almost the same words and end in entirely different places.
A **reservation** is a unilateral statement, **however phrased or named**, whereby a state purports
to **exclude or modify the legal effect** of provisions of a treaty for itself. An **interpretative
declaration** is a unilateral statement, **however phrased or named**, whereby a state purports to
**clarify the meaning or scope** of a treaty. What a state calls its statement is an indication and
nothing more. What decides is the effect the author purports to produce.

> **Reader's Tip** — When two definitions share an opening, the difference is in the verb. Underline

%%FIG l05-f5 | A one-page internal note: a heading, a boxed paragraph on status, two boxed definitions side by side with their verbs underlined, a boxed line giving the test, a four-row table of worked examples, a paragraph headed what follows from the characterization, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

> ## Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Expatriates · legal note 5
> *Legal Affairs with the Treaty Section · characterizing a statement · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The texts, and their status.**
>
> > The definition of a **reservation** is in the **Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties** of
> > **23 May 1969**, Article 2(1)(d) — a treaty provision, binding on its parties. The definition of
> > an **interpretative declaration**, and the method of distinguishing the two, are in the **Guide
> > to Practice on Reservations to Treaties**, adopted by the **International Law Commission in
> > 2011** with commentaries, of which the General Assembly **took note** and which it **brought to
> > the attention of States**. **The Guide is not a treaty.**
>
> **The two definitions, with their verbs underlined.**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | **Reservation** | a unilateral statement, **however phrased or named**, made by a State when signing, ratifying, accepting, approving or acceding to a treaty, whereby it purports to <u>**exclude or modify the legal effect**</u> of certain provisions of the treaty in their application to that State |
> | **Interpretative declaration** | a unilateral statement, **however phrased or named**, whereby a State purports to <u>**clarify the meaning or scope**</u> of a treaty or of certain of its provisions |
>
> **The test.**
>
> > The character of a unilateral statement as a reservation or as an interpretative declaration **is
> > determined by the legal effect that its author purports to produce**.
>
> **And the name?** The phrasing or name given to a statement **provides an indication of** the
> purported legal effect. **An indication**: it is evidence of intention and it is not the test. A
> statement is to be interpreted **in good faith**, in accordance with its terms, in the light of the
> treaty to which it relates.
>
> **Four worked examples.**
>
> | The statement | What it purports to do | What it is |
> |---|---|---|
> | *X understands article 6 to require notification within a reasonable time.* | clarify a term the article uses | **an interpretative declaration** |
> | *X understands article 6 as not applying to vessels under fifty meters.* | remove a category the article does not distinguish | **a reservation** |
> | *X declares that it will apply article 9 only in accordance with its constitution.* | modify how the provision applies to it | **a reservation** |
> | *X declares that nothing in the treaty affects its position on the status of the Sable Isles.* | neither of the two; it reserves a position outside the treaty's subject | **neither**, and a reply is still needed |
>
> **A third category, worth knowing. A conditional interpretative declaration** is one by which a
> State makes its **consent to be bound subject to** the interpretation stated. **It is to be treated
> with the seriousness of a reservation**, because the State has not consented except on that
> footing.
>
> **What follows from the characterization.** Everything procedural. A reservation may be prohibited
> by the treaty, may be incompatible with its object and purpose, and attracts a published
> twelve-month practice for objections; an interpretative declaration attracts none of that, and the
> appropriate response is a statement of our own interpretation if we disagree. **Characterize
> first, reply second. A ministry that replies first will reply in the wrong form.**
>
> **What to write.** *The statement, however named, purports to exclude the application of article 6
> to a category of vessel not distinguished by that article. It is accordingly to be characterized as
> a reservation.* **What not to write:** *the statement is headed an interpretative declaration and
> is therefore not a reservation.*
>
> *Source: the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties of 23 May 1969, Article 2(1)(d); and the
> Guide to Practice on Reservations to Treaties, adopted by the International Law Commission in 2011
> with commentaries, of which the General Assembly took note and which it brought to the attention of
> States — guidelines on the definition of an interpretative declaration, on the method of
> distinguishing the two, on the indicative value of the phrasing or name, and on conditional
> interpretative declarations. The Guide is not a treaty.*

### 5 · Word study — characterization

**5A  Complete the chart.**

%%FIG l05-f6 | A three-column chart headed the form, what it does and an example, with rows for purports to, however phrased or named, is determined by, provides an indication of, and two empty rows.%%

| Form | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **purports to** | reports what a statement sets out to do | the statement **purports to** exclude article 6 |
| **however phrased or named** | excludes the label from the test | a unilateral statement, **however phrased or named** |
| **is determined by** | states the test | the character **is determined by** the legal effect purported |
| **provides an indication of** | gives the label its exact weight | the name **provides an indication of** the effect intended |
| **is to be characterized as** | states the conclusion | the statement **is to be characterized as** a reservation |
| **falls to be treated as** | the same, more cautious | |

> **Grammar Note** — *Purports to* is the most precisely weighted verb in legal English. It reports
> what a text sets out to achieve without conceding that it achieves it, and it attributes no bad
> faith whatever. Officers who learn it stop needing the word *allegedly*.

### 1 · Characterization

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP6-05-06%%

> **Layla:** Your minute says Astrand has made an interpretative declaration.
> **Jamil:** That is what the instrument calls it.
> **Layla:** Then you have reported Astrand's characterization and not reached ours. What does the
> statement say?
> **Jamil:** That Astrand understands article 6 as not applying to vessels under fifty meters.
> **Layla:** And does article 6 distinguish by length?
> **Jamil:** No. It applies to all vessels.
> **Layla:** So the statement does not clarify the meaning of article 6; there is nothing in article 6
> to clarify about length. **It purports to exclude the application of article 6 to a category the
> article does not distinguish.** That is the definition of a reservation, however the statement is
> phrased or named.
> **Jamil:** May I say Astrand mischaracterized it?
> **Layla:** You may say what the statement purports to do and what follows. Do not say
> *mischaracterized*, and do not say *disguised*. **The verb *purports* does all the work and
> attributes nothing to anybody.** Astrand's legal advisers may well have a reasoned view; we have
> a different one; and our minute records ours in a form they could read without offense.
> **Jamil:** And the name?
> **Layla:** **Provides an indication of the purported effect** — which cuts both ways and is worth
> stating. It is evidence that Astrand intended something less than a reservation, and it is
> outweighed here by what the words actually do.

*Why does Layla refuse the words* mischaracterized *and* disguised?

**1B  Underline the four characterization structures in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Form | Example | The risk it avoids |
|---|---|---|
| **purports to** | the statement **purports to** exclude article 6 | asserting bad faith |
| **however phrased or named** | the label excluded by name | arguing about the heading |
| **is to be characterized as** | our conclusion, stated as ours | reporting the author's view as the answer |
| **provides an indication of** | the label's exact weight | treating the label as nothing, which is also wrong |
| **there is nothing in article 6 to clarify about length** | the reasoning in one line | a paragraph of adjectives |
| **we take a different view** | disagreement without accusation | *mischaracterized* |

> **Grammar Note** — Characterization is a conclusion you reach, not a fact you report. Write *the
> statement is to be characterized as a reservation* and the reader knows whose view it is; write
> *the statement is a reservation* and you have asserted something you cannot prove and nobody can
> check.

**3A  Listen and read.** %%AUD DIP6-05-12%%

> **Layla:** Read me article 6.
> **Jamil:** *Each Party shall require vessels flying its flag to carry the equipment specified in
> Annex II.*
> **Layla:** And the statement.
> **Jamil:** *The Government of Astrand declares its understanding that article 6 does not apply to
> vessels of less than fifty meters in length.*
> **Layla:** Now hold the two side by side and tell me what article 6 says about length.
> **Jamil:** Nothing.
> **Layla:** So there is nothing in article 6 to be clarified about length. A statement cannot clarify
> a distinction the provision does not draw. **What it does is exclude the application of article 6 to
> a category of vessel**, which is the definition of a reservation, **however phrased or named**.
> **Jamil:** Astrand's advisers presumably see it differently.
> **Layla:** They may, and they may have an argument I have not thought of, and the way to leave room
> for that is the verb. **The statement purports to exclude**, we **characterize** it as a
> reservation, and we **take a different view** from the Government of Astrand. Three expressions,
> none of which accuses anybody of anything. If their reply persuades us, we can move without having
> called them dishonest first.
> **Jamil:** What does the characterization actually change?
> **Layla:** The form of everything that follows. If it is an interpretative declaration, the
> appropriate response is a statement of our own interpretation, which can be made at any time and
> costs us nothing. If it is a reservation, there is a published practice by which an objection is
> formulated within twelve months of circulation, and circulation was the fourth of March. **The
> label does not change the law. It changes which calendar you are on**, and the calendar is the only
> thing in this subject that cannot be argued about afterwards.
> **Jamil:** Fadi's section had one like this.
> **Layla:** They had one headed *declaration of understanding* which excluded two articles from one
> province, and they treated it as a declaration for eighteen months because of its heading. By the
> time anybody characterized it, the period had run. Nothing dramatic followed — these things rarely
> produce a dramatic consequence — but the ministry had lost an option it could have kept for the
> price of reading one verb.
> **Jamil:** So the rule is read the verb.
> **Layla:** The rule is read the verb and then read the provision, because the verb only tells you
> what the author claims and the provision tells you whether the claim is possible. **A statement can
> only clarify what is there to be clarified.** That sentence is the whole of this unit, and it took
> the Commission a hundred pages of commentary to earn it.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

There is a reason both definitions in this unit carry the words *however phrased or named*, and it is
not suspicion of bad faith. Ministries name their statements for domestic reasons. A government that
has to explain to its own legislature why it is joining a treaty may find *interpretative
declaration* an easier phrase than *reservation*, and the choice may be made by people who never see
the legal advice. Nobody is being cunning; a word has simply been chosen for an audience at home,
and it then travels abroad and lands on your desk, where it has no domestic context at all. The
drafters of these definitions understood that perfectly, which is why they built a test that does
not depend on anybody's label and does not require anybody to be accused of anything. Your side of
the bargain is to use the verb *purports*, state your own characterization as your own, and leave the
other ministry a way to agree with you later without having conceded that it was hiding something.

**Discuss:** Have you ever named something for a domestic audience? What happened when it traveled?

### 🗣 Group ranking

*In fours.* A statement headed *interpretative declaration* purports to exclude an article. Rank
these, best to worst. You must agree.

a. Characterize it as a reservation, record the view, and consider an objection within the period
b. Ask the author what effect was intended, and characterize after the answer
c. Treat it as a declaration and state our own interpretation
d. Characterize it as a reservation and object immediately
e. Take no action and note the heading

Report your order, and say which one keeps the most options open.
