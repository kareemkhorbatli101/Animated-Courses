# Unit 8 · A Third State's Protection
%%SUB Representing interests that are not yours — and the two consents that make it lawful%%
%%GOALS
Use conditions precedent accurately
Distinguish custody of premises from protection of interests
Say what a protecting power is not
%%

When relations between two states are broken off, or a mission is withdrawn, the sending state may
entrust the custody of its premises, property and archives, and the protection of its interests and
of its nationals, **to a third state acceptable to the receiving state**. Separately, a state may
**with the prior consent of a receiving state**, and **at the request of a third state not
represented there**, undertake the **temporary** protection of that state's interests. Two
arrangements, two consents, and a great deal that neither of them is.

> **Reader's Tip** — Where two consecutive articles cover similar ground, draw the distinction before
> you read either of them closely. Here one article is about a mission that existed and one is about
> a State that was never represented.

%%FIG l08-f5 | A one-page internal note: a heading, a boxed quotation of one article in three lettered paragraphs, a boxed quotation of the following article, a two-column box distinguishing the two situations, a box headed what protection of interests is not, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

> ## Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Expatriates · legal note 8
> *Legal Affairs with the Consular Department · protection of interests · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The instrument.** The **Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations** of **18 April 1961**, in force
> **24 April 1964**, with about **193 parties** on the depositary's record. Two consecutive articles
> are in point and they are not alternatives: **they cover different situations.**
>
> **The first: where relations are broken off or a mission is withdrawn.**
>
> > If diplomatic relations are broken off between two States, or if a mission is **permanently or
> > temporarily recalled**:
> >
> > **(a)** the receiving State must, **even in case of armed conflict**, **respect and protect the
> > premises of the mission, together with its property and archives**;
> >
> > **(b)** the sending State may **entrust the custody** of the premises of the mission, together
> > with its property and archives, **to a third State acceptable to the receiving State**;
> >
> > **(c)** the sending State may **entrust the protection of its interests and those of its
> > nationals** to a **third State acceptable to the receiving State**.
>
> **The second: where a State is not represented at all.**
>
> > A sending State may, **with the prior consent of a receiving State**, and **at the request of a
> > third State not represented in the receiving State**, undertake the **temporary protection of
> > the interests** of that third State and of its nationals.
>
> **The two situations, side by side.**
>
> | | The first article | The second article |
> |---|---|---|
> | **When** | relations broken off, or mission recalled | the third State **never represented** there |
> | **Who asks** | the **sending State** entrusts | the **third State requests** |
> | **The condition** | the third State must be **acceptable to the receiving State** | the **prior consent** of the receiving State |
> | **What may be entrusted** | **custody of premises, property and archives**; and **protection of interests and nationals** | **temporary protection of interests** and of nationals |
>
> **Note paragraph (a), which is not about a third State at all.** The receiving State's own duty to
> respect and protect the premises, property and archives continues **even in case of armed
> conflict**, and it exists whether or not any third State is involved. **A ministry negotiating a
> custody arrangement should never allow the impression that the receiving State's own obligation
> depends on it.**
>
> **What protection of interests is not.**
>
> > It is **not the establishment of diplomatic relations** between the receiving State and the third
> > State. It does **not** make the protecting mission the third State's mission. It confers **no
> > additional privileges or immunities** beyond those the protecting mission already has. And under
> > the second article it is **temporary**, in terms.
>
> **The order of operations, which cannot be varied. Consent first.** The words are *acceptable to*
> and *prior consent*, and both are conditions precedent. **Work begun before consent is work done
> without a legal basis**, and it is also discourteous in a way that is remembered for years.
>
> **What to write.** *The Ministry would be willing to undertake the temporary protection of the
> interests of Astrand and of its nationals, subject to the prior consent of the receiving State,
> which is being sought.* **What not to write:** *the Ministry has agreed to represent Astrand*,
> which claims something neither article permits.
>
> *Source: the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations of 18 April 1961, in force 24 April 1964,
> Articles 45 and 46; and the depositary's published figure of about 193 parties, which carries the
> date of that page.*

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP6-08-06%%

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Form | Example | The mistake it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| **subject to the prior consent of** | the condition named | claiming an arrangement exists |
| **which is being sought** | the condition's present state | silence read as satisfaction |

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Protecting another state's interests is one of the oldest acts of friendship between governments and
one of the least understood by the people it serves. A family whose own embassy has closed arrives
at a stranger's counter expecting their embassy, and what they find is a mission of another country
doing what it can within a narrow mandate, with no power to issue their documents and no authority to
speak for their government on anything. The disappointment is real and it is nobody's fault: the
arrangement was never designed to replace a mission, only to make sure that premises are not looted,
archives are not lost, and nationals are not without anybody at all. What makes it work is the thing
officers find hardest — the two consents, obtained in advance, in writing, before a single window
opens. A protecting power that begins work on goodwill will be tolerated for a fortnight and
remembered for a decade.

**Discuss:** What would you want the first notice at that counter to say? Write it in three
sentences.

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Note No. 211/6 · protection of the interests of Astrand in Perenne
>
> The Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Expatriates **presents its compliments** to the Ministry of
> Foreign Affairs of Astrand and, **with reference to** its note of 14 June, **has the honour to**
> state the following.
>
> **1 Our position. The Ministry would be willing to undertake the temporary protection of the
> interests of Astrand and of its nationals in Perenne**, through its Embassy at [city].
>
> **2 The condition. Such an undertaking requires the prior consent of the Government of Perenne.**
> That consent **is being sought** by note of today's date. **No arrangement exists until it is
> received.**
>
> **3 On receipt of consent.** The Ministry will **inform the Ministry of Astrand on the same day**;
> an **inventory** will be taken where premises, property or archives are involved; and the Embassy
> will **open a counter** for the nationals of Astrand, on a date to be notified.
>
> **4 Meanwhile.** The Embassy **will** note the names and contact details of nationals of Astrand
> who present themselves, and **will** inform them that an arrangement is being sought. The Embassy
> **will not** accept documents, open files, charge fees or display any notice, **since each would be
> an act performed in a capacity not yet held**.
>
> **5 What the arrangement would and would not be.** It would be the temporary protection of
> interests under **Article 46** of the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations. It would **not**
> constitute the establishment of diplomatic relations between Perenne and Astrand; the Embassy would
> **not** become a mission of Astrand; **no additional privileges or immunities** would be conferred
> on anybody; and the arrangement would be **temporary**.
>
> **6 A request.** The Ministry **would be grateful if** nationals of Astrand were **not advised to
> attend** the Embassy until the arrangement is in force, **so that no one travels in vain**.
>
> The Ministry **avails itself of this opportunity to renew** the assurances of its highest
> consideration.

> **Writer's Tip** — Paragraph 6 is the one that comes from experience rather than from the
> Convention. The courteous request not to send people yet is worth more to the families than
> anything else in the note, and it can only be made before somebody has already sent them.

**1D  Circle the correct word.**

1. The Ministry (has agreed / would be willing) to undertake protection.
2. Consent (has been / is being) sought.
3. The Embassy (will / will not) accept documents meanwhile.
4. The arrangement would be (permanent / temporary).

### 3 · Write

Write a **note of five or six short paragraphs** on an arrangement in your own area that requires a
third party's prior consent.

**State your willingness, not your agreement. Name the condition, say that it is being sought, and
say that nothing exists until it is received. Say what will happen on receipt, with a time. Say what
may and may not be done meanwhile, and why. List what the arrangement would not be. End with one
courteous request that prevents somebody acting too early.**

**Read your note aloud to your partner first.** If any sentence could be read as an arrangement
already in force, add the condition to it. Then check your own against the list, and swap.
