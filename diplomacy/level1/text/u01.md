> **Reader's Tip** — A treaty has numbered articles. When you talk about a rule, say the number.
> *Article 22* is shorter than nine words of explanation, and the other person can look it up.

> *For new entrants · Protocol Office · page 1 of 1*
>
> Every diplomat in this building works inside one treaty. It is the **Vienna Convention on
> Diplomatic Relations**. It was signed at Vienna on **18 April 1961**. There are **193 states
> parties** to it.
>
> | Article | What it says, in plain words |
> |---|---|
> | **Article 1** | It says who is a *member of the mission*, who is a *member of the staff*, and who is a *private servant*. These are not the same. |

### 1 · *be*: am, is, are

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP1-01-06%%

> **Omar:** Excuse me. Is this the Registry?
> **Hana:** No, this is the post room. The Registry is downstairs.

**1B  Underline every form of *be* in 1A. How many are short forms?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Long | Short |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | I am new. | I**'m** new. |
| **You** | You are Omar. | You**'re** Omar. |
| **He / she / it** | It is open. | It**'s** open. |
| **We** | We are on the second floor. | We**'re** on the second floor. |
| **They** | They are in a meeting. | They**'re** in a meeting. |
| **Negative** | It is not open. | It **isn't** open. |
| **Question** | Is this the Registry? | — |

> **Grammar Note** — In a question, *be* comes first: **Is** this the Registry? Not *This is the
> Registry?* with a rising voice. The word order is the question, not the voice.

### 2 · *There is* and *there are*

**2A  Study the chart.**

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **There is** + one thing | **There is** a meeting room on this floor. |
| **There are** + more than one | **There are** nine floors. |
| **Negative** | **There isn't** a lift in this part of the building. |
| **Negative, plural** | **There aren't** any chairs in room 306. |
| **Question** | **Is there** a telephone on this desk? |
| **Question, plural** | **How many** floors **are there**? |

> **Grammar Note** — *There is* and *it is* are not the same. **There is** a meeting at four = a

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

The first minute at a reception desk is not the same everywhere. In some countries a visitor gives
their family name first and expects to be called by it with a title; in others the first name comes
first and the title is dropped quickly. Some ministries ask every visitor for an identity document
and photograph them; others do not ask for anything and would find the request insulting. There are
differences in how you show somebody the way, too: in some places a member of staff walks the
visitor to the door of the office, and letting a guest find their own way is rude, while elsewhere
pointing and saying *second floor* is normal and walking them there looks like you do not trust
them.

**Discuss:** What happens at your reception desk? What would surprise a visitor from another
country?
