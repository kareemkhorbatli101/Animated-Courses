### 1 · The present continuous

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP1-05-06%%

> **Yusuf:** Good morning, Ministry of Foreign Affairs.
> **Caller:** Good morning. Could I speak to Mrs Shaheen?
> **Yusuf:** I'm afraid she's in a meeting at the moment. She's with the Head of Department.
> **Caller:** When is she free?
> **Yusuf:** The meeting is finishing at eleven. Can I take a message?
> **Caller:** Yes. Tell her Astrand is sending the list today.
> **Yusuf:** Astrand is sending the list today. Could I have your name, please?

*Yusuf uses four present continuous verbs. Why not the present simple?*

**1B  Underline every present continuous verb in 1A. What does each one tell you about time?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Example | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **now** | She **is speaking** to a visitor. | happening at this moment |
| **today, this week** | We **are preparing** the visit program. | around now |
| **an arrangement** | The meeting **is finishing** at eleven. | arranged, in the near future |
| **Negative** | He **isn't answering**. | |
| **Question** | **Is** she **working** today? | |

> **Grammar Note** — Use the present simple for the routine and the present continuous for now:
> *She **reads** the telegrams at eight* (every day) and *She **is reading** the telegrams* (at this
> moment). On the telephone, almost everything is *now*, which is why the continuous is the tense of
> the switchboard.

> **Grammar Note** — Repeating back is not being slow. It is the only way to find a mistake while

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Telephone manners travel badly. In some countries the person who answers gives their own name
first, and not doing so is impolite; in others they give the organization and never their name. The
length of an opening is different too: a short *yes?* is normal and efficient in some places and
sounds abrupt elsewhere. Putting somebody on hold in silence is rude in most places and normal in
some. And there are real differences in how *urgent* is used: in some working cultures it means
today, in others it means now, and a caller who says *urgent* may be asking for a transfer rather
than a message. The safe habit everywhere is the same one: write six lines, and read five of them
back.

**Discuss:** What do you say when you answer the telephone at work? Does it include your name?
