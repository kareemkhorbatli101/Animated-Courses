### 2 · Read

> **Reader's Tip** — Two dates matter for every country on a treaty list: the day it **joined** and
> the day the treaty **started** for it. They are not the same day, and the gap can be months.

%%FIG g04-f5 | A one-page counter note: a heading, a line in bold giving a number of contracting parties, a table with three countries and two date columns, a short boxed paragraph headed what to say at the counter, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

> *Consular Affairs · certified documents · page 1 of 1*
>
> The **Apostille Convention** of **5 October 1961** makes long legalisation unnecessary. Instead of
> three or four stamps, a document gets **one certificate**, called an apostille.
>
> There are **130 contracting parties**.
>
> Now the part that matters at the counter. **Joining is not the same as starting.**
>
> | Country | Date it joined | Date it starts for that country |
> |---|---|---|
> | Algeria | — | **9 July 2026** |
> | Viet Nam | — | **11 September 2026** |
> | **Thailand** | **deposited 30 June 2026** | **28 February 2027** |

### 1 · Imperatives

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP1-04-06%%

> **Samira:** Sit down. Take a pen — a black one. Write your family name in box one.
> **Visitor:** In capitals?
> **Samira:** Block capitals, yes. Don't write in box fourteen. That one is ours.
> **Visitor:** And the date?
> **Samira:** Write the day first, then the month, then the year. Don't use numbers only. Write the
> month in letters: the fourth of March, M-A-R-C-H.
> **Visitor:** Why?
> **Samira:** Because 03/04 means two different days in two different countries, and the Registry
> keeps this form for thirty years.

*Samira gives nine instructions in six lines. Does it sound rude? Why not?*

**1B  Underline every imperative in 1A. How many are negative?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **Positive** | **Write** your name in box one. **Sign** at the bottom. |
| **Negative** | **Don't write** in box fourteen. **Don't use** numbers only. |
| **Softer** | **Please** write your name in box one. |
| **Softer still** | **Could you** write your name in box one? |
| **With a reason** | **Write** the month in letters, **because** 03/04 means two days. |

> **Grammar Note** — An imperative has no subject and no *to*: *write*, not *you write* or *to
> write*. It is not rude in instructions — it is the normal English of forms, signs and recipes.
> What makes it polite is *please*, a reason, or a friendly voice. What makes it rude is saying it
> to somebody who did not ask.

### 2 · Question forms

**2A  Study the chart.**

| Question word | Example |
|---|---|
| **What** | **What** do I write in box seven? |
| **Which** | **Which** box is the family name? |
| **Where** | **Where** do I sign? |
| **When** | **When** do I have to bring it back? |
| **Who** | **Who** signs box fourteen? |
| **How many** | **How many** copies do you need? |
| **Do I …?** | **Do I** have to use black ink? |

> **Grammar Note** — In a question with *do*, the main verb has no *-s* and no *to*: *What do I
> **write**?* And the word order is fixed: question word, then *do*, then the person, then the verb.
> *What I write here?* is the commonest question-form mistake in any ministry, in any language.

### 3 · Saying the date so it cannot be misread

**3A  Study the chart.**

| Risky | Safe |
|---|---|
| 03/04/27 | the **third of April** 2027 |
| 4/3/27 | the **fourth of March** 2027 |
| 03.04 | 3 **April** |
| next Friday | Friday **the 10th** |

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Forms carry assumptions about names, and the assumptions are not the same everywhere. Some systems
expect one family name and one first name; others expect a father's name in the middle, or two
family names, or a name that is written differently in two scripts. A form with two boxes forces
every visitor into a shape that may not fit, and the officer at the counter is the person who has to
solve it. Dates differ too: day-month-year, month-day-year and year-month-day are all normal
somewhere. And the idea of a certified copy is not universal — in some countries a lawyer certifies
copies, in others a notary, in others any official with a stamp, and a visitor who has done it
correctly at home may have done it wrongly for us.

**Discuss:** How many name boxes does your form have? What do you do when a name does not fit?

>
> One month does not usually matter. It mattered here, because a second document in the same file
> was dated the twentieth of March, and on the other country's reading the second document was
> signed *after* the form it was supposed to support.
>
> Hana found the original application register, which has the date in letters, and the question was
> answered in four days. The form had been filled in at the counter by an officer in a hurry, and
> the instruction sheet that says *write the month in letters* had been handed to the applicant and
