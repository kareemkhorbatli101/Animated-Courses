**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **I / you / we / they** | I **start** at eight. They **read** the telegrams. |
| **He / she / it** | She **reads** them at eight. He **finishes** at four. |
| **Negative** | I **don't** work on Saturdays. He **doesn't** read them. |
| **Question** | **Do** you start at eight? **Does** she read them? |
| **Short answer** | Yes, I **do**. No, she **doesn't**. |

> **Grammar Note** — The *-s* goes on *he*, *she* and *it* only. After *does*, the *-s* goes away:
> *Does she **read** them?* — not *Does she reads them?* This is the commonest mistake at this level
> and it never stops being heard.

### 2 · How often: adverbs of frequency

**2A  Study the chart.**

| Word | How often | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| always | every time | I **always** check the in-tray first. |
| usually | most times | I **usually** finish at four. |
| often | many times | She **often** works late. |
| sometimes | some times | We **sometimes** work on Saturday. |
| rarely | almost never | He **rarely** takes a break. |
| never | no times | I **never** take work home. |

> **Grammar Note** — The adverb goes **before** the main verb and **after** *be*. *I **always**
> check* but *I **am** always late*. And *never* already means *not*, so do not add another
> negative: *I never work on Sundays*, not *I don't never work*.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Working hours and the shape of the week are not the same everywhere, and this is one of the first
things to get wrong with a counterpart abroad. The working week begins on a different day in
different countries, the weekend falls on different days, and a Thursday deadline can mean three
different things depending on who set it. Lunch is an hour in some ministries and twenty minutes in
others. There are differences in how people answer a request for speed, too: in some places *I will
try* means yes, and in others it means no, politely. And the language of a reply can carry meaning
of its own — replying in a language the other side did not write in is read as neutral in some
capitals and as a signal in others.

**Discuss:** When does your working week begin and end? What would surprise a counterpart in another
country?
