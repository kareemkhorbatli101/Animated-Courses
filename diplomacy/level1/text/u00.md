# Starting Point · Four Habits for the First Week
%%SUB Before Unit 1: the four things nobody tells you%%
%%GOALS
Review *be*, simple questions and numbers
Say a name and a number so somebody else can write them
Say *I don't know yet* in a professional way
%%

Omar started three weeks ago. Nobody gave him a list. This is the list.

### 1 · What you already have

**1A  Work with a partner. Say one sentence of your own in each row, out loud.**
%%AUD DIP1-00-01%%

%%FIG g00-f1 | A single review sheet with six boxes. Each box has a heading — the verb be, questions with be, wh-questions, numbers, dates, and the alphabet for spelling — and one short example sentence from a working day at a ministry.%%

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| ***be*** | I **am** a third secretary. She **is** in the Registry. |
| **Questions with *be*** | **Is** this the Protocol Office? **Are** you Mr Rifai? |
| **Wh-questions** | **Where** is the Treaty Section? **Who** is the duty officer? |
| **Numbers** | Extension **four two one**. Room **three oh six**. |
| **Dates** | The **fourth** of March. The **twenty-first** of June. |
| **Spelling** | S for Sugar, A for Alpha, B for Bravo. |

**1B  Ask and answer with a partner.** One question from each row. Then change roles.

### 2 · Habit one — say it so the other person can write it

**2A  Listen and repeat.** %%AUD DIP1-00-02%%

%%FIG g00-f2 | A desk with a telephone, a notepad and a pen. On the pad, a name written in capital letters, then the same name spelled out letter by letter underneath, then a number written in digits and in words. A hand holds the pen above the pad.%%

> A name is not information until the other person has written it correctly.
>
> **Say the name. Then spell it. Then say it again.**
> *Sabbagh. S, A, B, B, A, G, H. Sabbagh.*
>
> **Say a number in small pieces, and stop between them.**
> *Four two one. Pause. Three oh six.* Not *four hundred and twenty-one thousand three hundred and six.*
>
> **Then ask for it back.** *Could you read that back to me, please?*

**2B  Say these to your partner. Your partner writes. Then check.**

1. Your family name.
2. Your extension number, or a telephone number.
3. Today's date.
4. The name of your street.

### 3 · Habits two, three and four

**3A  Listen and read.** %%AUD DIP1-00-03%%

%%FIG g00-f3 | A card taped inside the cover of a notebook, printed with four short rules: say it so they can write it; write down who asked and when; say what you do not know; and never guess a rule out loud. Under each rule there is one example sentence in smaller type.%%

> **Two — write down who asked, what they asked, and when.** Four words and a time. *Mrs Moretti,
> Consulate of Perenne, asked about the sealed copy, 10:20.* In three weeks somebody will ask you
> who called, and the note is the only answer you will have.
>
> **Three — say what you do not know.** *I don't know yet. I will find out and call you back before
> four o'clock.* Two sentences. They are better than any guess, and the second one is the half that
> matters, because it has a time in it.
>
> **Four — never guess a rule out loud.** If you are not sure whether a document needs a stamp, do
> not say *I think so*. Say *let me check that*. A wrong rule said politely is still a wrong rule,
> and the visitor will repeat it to somebody else as *the ministry says*.

**3B  Say it both ways. With a partner, give the weak version and then the strong one.**

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| *I think it's fine.* | *Let me check that. I'll call you back by __________ .* |
| *Somebody called for you.* | *__________ called at __________ about __________ .* |
| *My name is Sabbagh.* | *Sabbagh. __________ . Sabbagh.* |

### 4 · One question, three answers

**4A  Read. Then answer the three questions with a partner.**

%%FIG g00-f4 | Three speech bubbles above a reception desk. The first is short and confident, the second is short and uncertain, the third is two sentences long with a clock drawn beside it showing four o'clock.%%

A visitor asks Omar: *Do I need a stamp on this copy?* Omar has three answers available.

> **One.** *Yes.* — Fast. Helpful. He is not sure, so it may be wrong.
>
> **Two.** *I don't know.* — True, and the visitor now has nothing and will ask somebody else.
>
> **Three.** *I don't know yet. Let me check with the Legal Department. I'll call you before four.*
> — Longer, and the only one that ends with the visitor knowing what happens next.

1. Which answer is the most polite?
2. Which answer is the most useful?
3. What does answer three have that answer two does not?

**4B  Think about it.** *Talk with a partner.* When did somebody last tell you *I will find out and
come back to you*? Did they come back?
