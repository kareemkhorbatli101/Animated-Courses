### 2 · Read

> **Reader's Tip** — Before you use a percentage, find out which **scale** it comes from. The same
> country has two different percentages in two different scales, and both are correct.

%%FIG g08-f5 | A one-page internal note: a heading, a short table with three rows of percentages, a boxed paragraph headed two scales, not one, a line giving a resolution number and a date, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

> ## Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Expatriates · induction note 8
> *For new entrants · Multilateral Affairs · page 1 of 1*
>
> Every member state pays a share of the United Nations **regular budget**. The shares are set out in
> a **scale of assessments**, agreed for three years at a time. The scale for **2025 to 2027** was
> adopted in resolution **79/249** on **31 December 2024**.
>
> | | Rate |
> |---|---|
> | **Minimum** for any member state | **0.001 per cent** |
> | **Maximum** for any member state | **22 per cent** |
> | **Maximum for a least developed country** | **0.01 per cent** |
>
> Say the minimum carefully. **Nought point nought nought one per cent.** Three noughts after the
> point. It is one thousandth of one per cent.
>
> **Two scales, not one.** There is a second scale, for **peacekeeping**. It starts from the regular
> budget scale and then applies further adjustments. So a member state has **two** percentages:
>
> 1. one in the **regular budget** scale, and
> 2. a different one in the **peacekeeping** scale.
>

> arithmetic mistake in multilateral work. **Always say which scale.**
>
> *Source: UN General Assembly resolution 79/249 of 31 December 2024, scale of assessments for the

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Numbers are written differently in different places, and this causes real errors in international
work. The decimal separator is a point in some countries and a comma in others, so 1,029 can mean a
thousand and twenty-nine or one point nought two nine. Thousands are grouped with commas, with
points, with spaces or not at all. Large numbers have different names: a *billion* has meant two
different quantities in different places within living memory. And some systems count in units that
do not exist elsewhere, such as the lakh and the crore. The safe habit is the one in this unit: say
the figure, say what it means in words, and say what it is a percentage of.

**Discuss:** How do you write a decimal? Have you ever had a figure misread because of it?

> The error was found in nine days, by the other ministry, politely. The difference was small in

> Nadia's view is that the note did its job and a reader ignored it. Omar's view is that he should
