**1B  Underline the four reporting verbs in 1A and write what each one adds.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Verb | What it signals |
|---|---|
| **stated**, **said** | neutral; no view either way |
| **claimed**, **asserted** | the writer is not vouching for it |
| **insisted**, **maintained** | repeated against resistance |
| **denied**, **rejected** | contradicted something put to them |
| **pointed out**, **noted** | the point is accurate, and was being missed |
| **acknowledged**, **conceded** | agreed to something against their own interest |
| **argued** | advanced a position with reasons |

> **Grammar Note** — These verbs are not synonyms with different flavors; they are different claims
> made by **you**, the writer. *Pointed out* says the point was right. *Claimed* says you are not
> standing behind it. Using them carelessly is how a neutral cable acquires a position its author
> never meant to take.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Multilateral arithmetic is a field where almost every number is correct and almost every comparison
is wrong, and the pressure on a desk officer runs in a predictable direction. The lower figure is
always more interesting, the gap is always more quotable, and the clause that explains the difference
is always in smaller type than the table. There is also a professional courtesy worth naming. When a
delegation corrects itself in public, the cable that records it plainly — *acknowledged*, no adverb,
no satisfaction — is doing something for the next delegation that has to do the same thing. A
ministry that is known for recording concessions gracefully gets told things early. A ministry known
for enjoying them gets told things late, and only once they are unavoidable.

**Discuss:** How does your ministry record it when somebody else corrects themselves? How does it
record it when you do?
