# Foreign Service English 2
%%SUB English for Foreign Ministry Staff · Syrian Edition · CEFR B1%%

## How to use this book

This is the third of six books. Book Foundation was the building and Book 1 was the consular
section. This one is **the desk officer's chair**: the chair where somebody reads a draft resolution
at eight in the morning, writes four hundred words about it by eleven, and watches a minister quote
those four hundred words at six in the evening.

**Twelve units**, sixteen pages each, with the same six lessons in the same order every time.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Sixteen words, a second set of six, and a team research task |
| **2 · Reading** | A real instrument or published record, with its figure, its date and its caveat |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures, with the mistake each one causes |
| **4 · At Work** | Clear English, a long dialogue, a culture check and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A model, then your own |
| **Team Up & Review** | A picture, a team project, a survey, and one problem with no easy answer |

**The audio.** 🔊 marks a track. 195 tracks, numbered `DIP3-unit-track`.

## What is different about this book

**The reading texts are the documents a desk officer actually reads.** A Charter article on voting. A
Court's Statute. A resolution that created a body. A status page that says in its own small print
that it is unofficial. Twelve source bodies, none of them used in Books Foundation or 1, and not one
of them simplified.

**Every unit turns on a sentence that is true and is usually reported wrongly.** An abstention is not
a veto, and the reason is a practice rather than a text. *Present and voting* means the denominator
moves. Credentials are not recognition. A judgment binds the parties, in that case, and nobody else.
Concluding observations are not judgments. A hundred and sixty-eight plans for a hundred and
ninety-five parties is not a gap, because one plan is filed for twenty-eight of them. An
international trademark registration is a bundle of national rights. A Main Committee recommends and
the plenary decides.

**The grammar is the grammar of reporting.** At B1 the structures that matter are the ones that let
you say exactly how certain you are and exactly who said what: past perfect for what had already
happened, modals of deduction, the passive with reporting verbs, reporting verbs that carry an
attitude, hedging, concession, and cleft sentences for the one thing in your paragraph that has to
be emphasized. Nothing here is grammar for its own sake, because a desk officer who cannot hedge
accurately will either overstate or disappear.

**Four sentences you will practice until they are automatic.** *That is the practice, not the text.*
*Two-thirds of those present and voting, which is not two-thirds of the membership.* *It appears to,
on the material we have.* And *the committee has agreed a draft; the Assembly has not adopted
anything*, which is the sentence that keeps a cable out of trouble.

## The people

The same ministry. **Omar Sabbagh** has moved up one floor to the Europe Department and now writes
for other people to sign, which is a different job from any he has had before. **Nadia Shaheen** is
his head of section and marks his drafts in pencil, which is worse than ink. **Layla Bahri** in
Legal Affairs answers the questions that begin *can we say that*. **Samira Barakat** still runs the
consular front office and still knows more than anybody on the fifth floor. **Hana Darwish** keeps
the Registry. **Rashid Nasr** is at our consulate in Perenne. **Dalia Khoury** is the court liaison
officer.

New in this book: **Fadi Antoun**, who has been a desk officer for twenty-two years and has never
once been quoted inaccurately, and **Rana Sayegh**, newly arrived from the Information Department,
who asks the question nobody else asks because she has not yet learned not to.

Outside the building: **Karin Lindholm** and **Oskar Rehn** at the Embassy of Astrand, **Lucia
Moretti** at the Consulate of Perenne, **Linh Tran** at the Embassy of Marovia, and **Marco
Caruso**, who by Unit 11 has been to this ministry four times and still does not have what he came
for.

Every one of them is invented. Astrand, Perenne, Marovia, Valmora, Kesteland and the Sable Isles are
invented countries, and they are invented on purpose: in a role-play about a vote, a draft or a
dispute, nobody should have to put a real government's name to a position it has never taken.

## Nothing here is official

This is an independent teaching draft. It is not an official publication of any ministry and it
carries no official status. Every person, office, cable, draft, vote and meeting in it was written
for this book. The instruments and records quoted in the reading texts are real, and each text gives
the instrument or page, the date, the published figure and the base it is counted from — because
these figures move, and because two correct figures for the same thing are more common in this field
than one.
