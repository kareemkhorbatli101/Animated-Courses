### 1 · Cleft sentences

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP3-11-06%%

> **Nadia:** Your paragraph has four figures in it and I cannot tell which one you think matters.
> **Omar:** Fifty-three.
> **Nadia:** Then say so with the grammar, not just the order. **It is the Danger List, not the main
> List, that tells you what inscription does not do.**
> **Omar:** That is longer than *the Danger List tells you*.
> **Nadia:** Six words longer, and it does something those six words buy. **What a cleft sentence
> does is force one element to the front** and make everything else subordinate to it. A reader
> skimming four figures will stop at the one you have put in that position.
> **Omar:** And if I use it twice in a paragraph?
> **Nadia:** Then you have emphasized two things, which means you have emphasized nothing. One cleft
> a paragraph. **It is the discipline, not the construction, that is difficult.**

*Which element does each of Nadia's cleft sentences push to the front?*

**1B  Underline the three cleft sentences in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **It is … that / who** | **It is** the Danger List **that** tells you. |
| **It was … that** | **It was** the abstention **that** mattered. |
| **What … is** | **What matters is** the base. |
| **What … does is** | **What a cleft does is** force one element forward. |
| **The thing that … is** | **The thing that changed is** the periodicity. |
| negative contrast inside the cleft | **It is** X, **not** Y, **that**… |

> **Grammar Note** — A cleft sentence spends words to buy emphasis. One a paragraph is a tool; two is
> a tic; three makes the reader feel they are being managed. And the strongest form is the one with
> the contrast built in: *it is X, not Y, that…*, because it fixes the emphasis and corrects a likely
> misreading in the same clause.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Heritage is one of the few multilateral subjects where a ministry's figures reach a general audience,
and that changes the drafting problem. The pressure is not towards caution but towards pride: the
big figure is cheerful, the listing is an achievement, and nobody at a cultural event wants to hear
about a danger list. The case for saying it anyway is not gloom. It is that the fifty-three is the
only figure that explains why conservation work, reporting cycles and assistance exist at all, and a
minister who says it is giving an audience a reason to care rather than a statistic to admire. There
is also a professional point about sovereignty. Audiences often assume that an international listing
brings international control, and a ministry that leaves that assumption standing is storing up a
difficult conversation for the day a monitoring mission is announced.

**Discuss:** Which figure does your own ministry quote with pride? What does it leave out?
