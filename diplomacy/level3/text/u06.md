> **The cycle, in four steps.**

### 1 · The third conditional

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP3-06-06%%

> would have been much harder.**
> **Nadia:** So the damage was done in March.
> **Omar:** The damage was done in February, when nobody asked for an extension. **If we had asked,
> we would probably have got one.** That is the sentence I am going to put in the note, and it is not
> going to be popular.

*Which of Omar's conditionals is about something that was within the ministry's control?*

**1B  Underline every third conditional in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **if** + past perfect, **would have** + participle | **If** the report **had gone** in March, they **would have had** our figures. |
| negative | **If** it **hadn't answered**, paragraph nine **would have been** harder. |
| **might have** / **could have** | They **might still have** written something. |
| **would probably have** | We **would probably have** got one. |
| passive | **If** it **had been sent** in March, it **would have been** considered. |

> **Grammar Note** — The third conditional is about a past that did not happen, and it carries blame
> whether you intend it or not. *If we had asked, we would have got one* is a sentence about
> somebody's failure. That is sometimes exactly the right sentence to write, and you should know you
> are writing it.

| | Example | What it mixes |
|---|---|---|

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

How a ministry handles a document that is authoritative and not binding says more about it than any
statement it issues. The two failure modes are mirror images. One is to treat the observations as a
verdict, go quiet, and produce nothing — which leaves the field to everybody else's summary of them.
The other is to seize on *not binding*, say so publicly, and do nothing — which is read everywhere as
an answer in itself, and a poor one. The professional path is unglamorous: a grid with owners and
dates, a one-sentence position on each recommendation including the ones you disagree with, and a
submission on time next cycle. There is also a quieter discipline about the lessons section. Writing
*nobody asked for an extension in February* costs somebody something, and leaving it out costs the
next officer the same mistake.

**Discuss:** In your own ministry, what happens to a document that nobody is obliged to act on?
