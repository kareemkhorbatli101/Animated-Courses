# Grammar reference

Every structure in this book, in unit order. The second column is the mistake it causes, which is
the column worth reading twice.

## Starting Point · Attribution, hedging, emphasis

| | |
|---|---|
| **Attribution** | without it, a draft becomes the ministry's own view by accident |
| **Hedging** | without it, a draft is either overconfident or silent |
| **Emphasis** | without it, the most important sentence is the one the reader skips |
| **Write for the fourth reader** | the one who finds the file in two years and was not there |

## Unit 1 · Past perfect and past simple

| | |
|---|---|
| **past perfect for what had already happened** | the nine days before the eleven minutes |
| **past simple for the event reported** | *the resolution was adopted at 11:40* |
| **no past perfect where the order is already clear** | it starts to sound heavy |
| **the text, then the practice, in two sentences** | never *under Article 27, an abstention is not a veto* |

## Unit 2 · Present perfect continuous

| | |
|---|---|
| **have been + -ing** for work still going on | *I have been counting since Tuesday* |
| **have + participle** for the finished result | *I have counted a hundred and ten* |
| **state verbs stay simple** | *we have been knowing* |
| **a fraction travels; its base does not** | say *two-thirds of those present and voting* in one breath |

## Unit 3 · Modals of deduction

| | |
|---|---|
| ***must have*** = the only explanation that fits | and it reads like a finding, so use it rarely |
| **the negative is *can't have*** | not *mustn't have* |
| ***might have***, ***could well be*** | possible, likely, not certain |
| **two decisions, two sentences** | credentials settle participation; recognition is for each state |

## Unit 4 · The passive with reporting verbs

| | |
|---|---|
| ***is said to be*** / ***is said to have been*** | now, and earlier |
| ***it is reported that*** + clause | heavier, very formal |
| **attribute the statement, not the speaker** | never *a source close to the delegation* |
| **a judgment binds the parties, in that case** | Article 59, quoted rather than paraphrased |

## Unit 5 · Relative clauses, including reduced

| | |
|---|---|
| **no commas = defining; commas = non-defining** | the commas are a factual claim, not a style choice |
| **reduced: *the resolution, adopted in 2006*** | buys four or five words a sentence |
| ***whose***, ***where*** | *a body whose members are states* |
| **fix the category in the first mention** | *47 member states*, not *a panel of experts* |

## Unit 6 · Third and mixed conditionals

| | |
|---|---|
| **if + past perfect, would have + participle** | *if the report had gone in March…* |
| **mixed: a past condition, a present result** | *…we would not be writing this note now* |
| **conditionals carry blame** | write them passive and name nobody |
| **authoritative and not binding** | both halves, same tone of voice |

## Unit 7 · Reporting verbs with attitude

| | |
|---|---|
| ***stated*** neutral · ***claimed*** not vouched for | the verb is your own claim, not a flavor |
| ***pointed out*** = the point was right | ***acknowledged*** = conceded against interest |
| ***insisted***, ***denied***, ***argued*** | each one grades the speaker for the reader |
| **a count of documents is not a count of parties** | 168 and 195 are both right |

## Unit 8 · Nominalization and register

| | |
|---|---|
| **each noun built from a verb removes a doer** | *the treatment of* does not say who treats |
| **use it where the doer is irrelevant** | avoid it where the doer is the news |
| **four elements of a citation** | figure, base, date, the source's own description |
| **put the disclaimer last** | it is the part a listener keeps |

## Unit 9 · Hedging and modality

| | |
|---|---|
| **three dimensions** | how likely · how much · on what basis |
| ***is likely to*** / ***may well*** / ***appears to*** | and *on the material we have* |
| **hedge the basis as well as the likelihood** | otherwise you imply you have seen everything |
| **questions, not predictions** | *what I would want to know, in your position, is…* |

## Unit 10 · *whereas*, *by contrast*, *in contrast to*

| | |
|---|---|
| ***whereas*** + clause, inside one sentence | *a committee recommends, whereas a plenary decides* |
| ***by contrast*** starts a new sentence | with a comma after it |
| ***in contrast to*** takes a noun | not a clause |
| **two sentences, not one with a comma** | the second sentence survives summarizing |

## Unit 11 · Cleft sentences

| | |
|---|---|
| ***It is X that…*** / ***What matters is…*** | spends words to buy emphasis |
| **one cleft a paragraph** | two is a tic; three manages the reader |
| **the strongest form has the contrast inside it** | *it is X, not Y, that…* |
| **do the subtraction for the reader** | *so 26 have none* |

## Unit 12 · Cohesion and summary

| | |
|---|---|
| ***this***, ***the***, ***those***, ***that is why*** | order is not cohesion |
| **repeat the key noun rather than risk a pronoun** | clumsy and unambiguous beats elegant and wrong |
| **four sentences** | what happened · the roles · the dispute · the action, with a time |
| **name the role in the second sentence** | never at the end |

# The British layer

This book is written in American English, and the series carries a switchable British layer: a fixed
list of words is respelled at build time. The list lives with the series rather than in this book.

What the layer does not touch:

1. **The date.** Write the month in letters.
2. **The floor.** Say *ground floor*, or give the room number.
3. **A quoted instrument keeps its own spelling.** Every treaty article and resolution quoted in this
   book is quoted as published, inside quotation marks, even where its spelling differs from the
   surrounding text. Correcting an instrument inside a quotation is an alteration.
4. **A document symbol, a resolution number and a treaty article are never translated or
   reformatted.** `A/RES/79/249` is that, in both variants and in every language version of your
   draft.

**Twelve honesty notes, printed in the units as well as here.**

- **Unit 1.** Article 27 requires nine affirmative votes including the concurring votes of the
  permanent members. The reading of a voluntary abstention as not blocking adoption is a long
  practice, accepted in conduct; the Article has never been amended, and a draft that attributes the
  practice to the Article will be marked.
- **Unit 2.** *Present and voting* excludes abstentions, so two-thirds is two-thirds of a smaller
  number than the membership, and the base changes while the vote is taken.
- **Unit 3.** A credentials decision concerns participation in a body's work. Recognition of a
  government is taken by each state. The two may be connected politically and are not the same
  decision.
- **Unit 4.** A judgment binds the parties, in that case. The reasoning is read everywhere and
  relied on by states that were not parties, and influence is not binding force.
- **Unit 5.** The Council's members are states, elected by the Assembly. The review's periodicity has
  been set and reset by later decisions, so the unit prints only the published first-cycle figure.
- **Unit 6.** Concluding observations are adopted by eighteen independent experts and are
  authoritative. They are not binding. Both halves have to be said in the same breath.
- **Unit 7.** 195 at adoption; 194 on the secretariat's page in January 2026. And 168 contributions
  for 195 Parties is not a compliance gap: one contribution is filed jointly for 28 Parties.
- **Unit 8.** The status page the field relies on calls itself unofficial quick reference, carries a
  totals row dated 2019, and sits beside two published party counts that differ over a regional
  organization.
- **Unit 9.** One filing and one number produce as many separate national or regional rights as there
  are designations, each governed by its own law. *International trademark* is a phrase that costs
  companies money.
- **Unit 10.** Agreement in a Main Committee is a recommendation. Until the plenary acts, nothing has
  been adopted, and *taken note of* is not an adoption at all.
- **Unit 11.** 1,248 properties, 170 States Parties with any property inscribed, and 196 States
  Parties answer three different questions — and 53 properties are on the Danger List, which is the
  figure that explains what inscription does not do.
- **Unit 12.** A membership figure of 176 Member States plus three Associate Members is not a number
  of countries, and the three roles a maritime state plays attract different obligations on the same
  day.

**Everything else in this book is invented.** Omar Sabbagh, Nadia Shaheen, Layla Bahri, Samira
Barakat, Hana Darwish, Rashid Nasr, Dalia Khoury, Fadi Antoun and Rana Sayegh are not real people.
Astrand, Perenne, Marovia, Valmora, Kesteland and the Sable Isles are not real states, and they
exist in these pages precisely so that no real government is given a position it has not taken.
Every cable, draft, vote, meeting, case and telephone call in this book was written for this book.
Nothing here is an official publication of any ministry, and nothing here carries official status.

# Closing

You began this book able to report what happened. You end it able to say what had already happened
before it, to give a threshold with the base it is calculated from, to say how certain you are and on
what material, to attribute a statement without identifying the person who made it, to describe a
body in six accurate words, to write a lessons paragraph that accuses nobody, to choose a reporting
verb on purpose, to cite a figure in four elements, to hedge on three dimensions at once, to report a
stage as a stage, to put the emphasis on the one figure that matters, and to say which role your
state is acting in before anybody asks.

That is the whole of the desk officer's craft, and almost none of it is about English.

Four sentences to take with you.

From Unit 1: **that is the practice, not the text.**

From Unit 2: **two-thirds of those present and voting, which is not two-thirds of the membership.**

From Unit 10: **the committee has agreed a draft; the Assembly has not adopted anything.**

And from Unit 12, which is where this book ends: **everything in this job is somebody else's
sentence arriving at the wrong moment, and the only part I control is whether mine arrives when I
said it would.**

Protocol, and a motorcade that is nine minutes late, in Book 3.
