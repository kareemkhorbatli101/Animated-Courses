### 1 · Cohesion: making a paragraph hold together

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP3-12-06%%

> **Fadi:** Your four sentences are all true and the paragraph does not hold together.
> **Omar:** They are in the right order.
> **Fadi:** Order is not cohesion. Each sentence has to reach back to the one before it. **This** ship
> was detained on Tuesday. **The detention** was on three deficiencies. **Two of those** concerned
> certificates issued by the flag State. **That** is why the flag State's position matters here and
> did not matter last month.
> **Omar:** You are using *this*, *the*, *those* and *that* to tie them together.
> **Fadi:** And one of them is doing more work than the others. *That is why* tells the reader the
> paragraph has an argument, not just four facts. Without it they have to build the argument
> themselves, and they will build a different one.

*Which word in Fadi's paragraph carries the argument?*

**1B  Underline the four linking words in Fadi's paragraph.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Device | Example |
|---|---|
| **this** / **these** + noun | **This detention** was on three deficiencies. |
| **the** for something already introduced | **The** detention was on Tuesday. |
| **those** / **two of those** | **Two of those** concerned certificates. |
| **that is why** | **That is why** the flag State's position matters. |
| **which** for the whole clause | …, **which** is why last month's line does not apply. |
| repeating the key noun on purpose | **the flag State**… **the flag State**… |

> **Grammar Note** — Pronouns are cheaper than nouns and riskier. In a paragraph with three states
> in it, *it* and *they* become unreadable. Repeat *the flag State* and *the port State* in full as
> often as you need to, even when it feels clumsy, because clumsy and unambiguous beats elegant and
> wrong.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Technical fields are where a ministry's consistency is tested, because the same state takes opposite
positions in different roles as a matter of routine, and critics do not always distinguish. The
defense is not to soften either position but to name the role every time, in the second sentence,
before anybody asks. There is a second, subtler point about membership lists. Almost every technical
organization has categories — associate members, observers, participating organizations — and almost
every ministry has at some point written *X countries have agreed* on the strength of a total that
includes one of them. The correction, when it comes, is always public and always from somebody who
has the list open. Eleven extra words in your own draft is a cheap insurance premium against being
that story, and this is the last thing this book has to teach you: the extra clause is never the
expensive part.

**Discuss:** Which membership figure does your ministry quote? What categories does it contain?
