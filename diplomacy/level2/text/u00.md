# Starting Point · Six Habits for a Consular Desk
%%SUB Before Unit 1: the six sentences that hold a consular desk together%%

Book Foundation gave you four habits for the first week. This book adds two, and sharpens the other
four, because a consular desk is different from a reception desk in one way: almost everybody who
telephones it wants you to promise something.

### 1 · What you already have

%%FIG h00-f1 | A single review sheet with six boxes. Each box has a heading — the past simple, the present perfect, modals of obligation, reported speech, the passive, and conditionals — and one short example sentence from a consular working day.%%

You finished Book Foundation able to say *there is*, *there are*, *can*, *could*, *have to*, *going
to*, *will*, *some*, *any*, the present perfect and the past simple. Everything in this book is
built on those. Nothing is replaced.

**1A  Say one sentence from each box to your partner.** Then say which box you use most.

### 2 · The six habits

%%FIG h00-f2 | A card taped inside the cover of a notebook, printed with six short rules, each with one example sentence under it in smaller type. The sixth rule is in bold.%%

> ### Six habits
>
> **1  Say it so the other person can write it.**
> *"Nasr. N, A, S, R. Extension two one nine."*
>
> **2  Write down who asked, what they asked for, and when.**
> *"Ms Lindholm, Embassy of Astrand, 11:40, about the request of 2 March."*
>
> **3  Say what you do not know, and when you will know it.**
> *"I don't know. I'll find out and call you back by four."*
>
> **4  Never guess a rule out loud.**
> A guessed rule is quoted back to you as the ministry's position.
>
> **5  Name the instrument before you name the answer.**
> *"Under the 1980 Convention — not under our own law — the question is return."*
>
> **6  Separate what a rule says from what a court will decide.**
> *"I can tell you what the Article says. I cannot tell you what a judge will do with it."*

**2B  Which two habits are new in this book?** *Talk with a partner.*

**2C  Habit 6 is the longest.** Why do you think a consular section needs it more than a protocol

### 3 · Naming the instrument

%%FIG h00-f3 | Two speech bubbles above a telephone. The first says the case will go back and nothing else. The second names a convention, an article and a year, and then says what the convention decides. Under the second bubble, three small boxes read the instrument, the article, and what it decides.%%

Compare these two answers to the same question.

| Risky | Safe |
|---|---|
| The child will be sent back. | **Under the 1980 Convention**, the question is **return**, not custody. The court there decides. |
| They have to arrest him. | **A Red Notice is not an arrest warrant.** Each country decides what to do with it. |
| They are party to that convention. | They are on the status page as a **Contracting Party**. For them it is **not yet in force**. |
| Everybody has signed it. | **196 states are parties.** One state **signed in 1995** and has not ratified. |

> **Habit 5, in one line.** The instrument, the article, the date. Then the answer. In that order,
> because an answer with no instrument in front of it becomes *the ministry said*.

**3B  Cover the right-hand column.** Your partner reads a risky sentence. You make it safe.

### 4 · Four sentences to learn now

%%FIG h00-f4 | Four speech bubbles of different sizes around a desk telephone and a notepad. Each bubble holds one sentence. Beside the smallest bubble, a small clock shows four o'clock.%%

> **1** *That is not what that document does.*
> **2** *I can tell you what the rule says. I cannot tell you what a court will decide.*
> **3** *Those two figures are both published. They count different things.*
> **4** *I would rather check than tell you something that turns out to be wrong.*

**4A  Listen.** %%AUD DIP2-00-03%%

**4B  Which of the four is hardest to say to somebody who is frightened?** *Talk with a partner.
Why?*

**4C  Say each one aloud twice.** Then put the book down and say all four from memory.

> **One thing before Unit 1.** Nobody on the telephone wants the caveat. Every one of them needs it,
> and the ones who are angry about it today are the ones who are grateful for it in six weeks. Say
> the caveat early, say it in one sentence, and then help.
