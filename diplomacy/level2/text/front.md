# Foreign Service English 1
%%SUB English for Foreign Ministry Staff · Syrian Edition · CEFR A2+ to B1%%

## How to use this book

This is the second of six books. Book Foundation was about the building. This one is about
**the consular section**: the request that arrives from another state, the parent on the telephone,
the person with no nationality, the file that has to be sent somewhere and come back, and the twelve
published instruments that decide what you may say in reply.

**Twelve units**, fourteen pages each, with the same six lessons in the same order every time.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Sixteen words, a second set of six, and a team research task |
| **2 · Reading** | A real instrument, with its party count, its date and its caveat |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures, with the mistake each one causes |
| **4 · At Work** | Clear English, a long dialogue, a culture check and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A model, then your own |
| **Team Up & Review** | A picture, a team project, a survey, and one problem with no easy answer |

**The audio.** 🔊 marks a track. 195 tracks, numbered `DIP2-unit-track`.

## What is different about this book

**Every reading text is a real instrument, and the text names it.** A convention, the date it was
signed, the number of parties on a published status page, and the date that page was published. Not
one of the twelve was used in Book Foundation, and none of them is simplified. A figure is either
right, with its date, or it is not in the book.

**Every text prints the caveat that comes with the figure.** One hundred and seventy-two parties and
one hundred and sixty-nine parties are both correct, and the difference is three states that are not
members of the United Nations. One hundred and forty-nine states are party to a convention *and/or*
its protocol, which is not the same as being party to the convention. A Red Notice is not an arrest
warrant. A return order is not a custody order. A signature is not a ratification, and it is not
nothing either.

**The grammar does the work the content needs.** You report what a caller said, so Unit 4 is
reported speech. You explain what was decided by somebody else, so Unit 5 is the passive. You say
what will happen if the other ministry does not reply, so Unit 7 is conditionals. Nothing here is
grammar for its own sake.

**You will practice saying four hard things.** *That is not what that document does.* *I can tell
you what the rule says; I cannot tell you what a court will decide.* *Those two figures are both
published and they count different things.* And *I would rather check than tell you something that
turns out to be wrong*, which is the sentence that keeps a consular section out of the newspapers.

## The people

The same ministry, the same nine floors. **Omar Sabbagh** is six months older than he was in Book
Foundation and has been moved to the consular section, where the telephone rings before nine.
**Samira Barakat** still runs the front office and is the person he asks. **Layla Bahri** in Legal
Affairs answers the questions that start *can we*. **Hana Darwish** keeps the Registry.
**Nadia Shaheen** is on the Europe desk. **Ahmad Rifai** is on reception. **Yusuf Mansour** is on
the switchboard.

New in this book: **Rashid Nasr**, a consular officer at our own consulate in Perenne, who is at the
other end of most of the long telephone calls; and **Dalia Khoury**, a court liaison officer who
arrives in Unit 3 and spends the rest of the book explaining what a judge can and cannot be asked.

Outside the building: **Karin Lindholm** and **Oskar Rehn** at the Embassy of Astrand, **Lucia
Moretti** at the Consulate of Perenne, **Linh Tran** at the Embassy of Marovia, and **Ida Pedersen**
and **Marco Caruso**, who are still in the waiting room.

Every one of them is invented. Astrand, Perenne, Marovia, Valmora, Kesteland and the Sable Isles are
invented countries, and they are invented on purpose: in a role-play about a difficult request,
nobody should have to put a real government's name to a position it has never taken.

## Nothing here is official

This is an independent teaching draft. It is not an official publication of any ministry and it
carries no official status. Every person, office, case, form, file and telephone call in it was
written for this book. The instruments quoted in the reading texts are real, and each text gives the
instrument, the date, the published figure and the date that figure was published — because these
figures move, and a number without a date is not a fact. If you need a current count, the text tells
you whose page to look at.
