> *Consular Affairs · police cooperation · page 1 of 1*

> **A Red Notice is not an arrest warrant.** The organization says so itself, on its own pages, and

>
> | | What it is | Who makes it |
> |---|---|---|
> | **The warrant** | an order to arrest | a **judicial authority** in one country |

*Which verbs are about the background, and which are about the single events?*

**1B  Underline four past continuous verbs and four past simple verbs in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Example | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **past simple** | Two officers **came** over. | one finished event |
| **past continuous** | He **was waiting** at the gate. | the background it happened inside |
| both together | He **was reading** it when they **took** it back. | the long one is interrupted by the short one |
| **while** + continuous | **While** he **was waiting**, they checked the system. | |
| **when** + simple | He was reading it **when** they **took** it. | |
| state verbs | He **knew** the name. *(not* was knowing *)* | |

> **Grammar Note** — The past continuous sets the scene and the past simple moves the story. In a
> case note that order matters: the background goes in one sentence and each event goes in its own,
> with a time. A reader who cannot tell which is which will not be able to put the day in order.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Police cooperation is the part of a foreign ministry's work where the gap between what a document
says and what people believe is widest, and the belief is usually stronger than the document. Part
of the reason is translation: in several languages the ordinary word for this kind of alert is the
same word used for a warrant, and a newspaper in a hurry will choose the shorter one. Part of it is
that families are frightened, and a frightened listener hears the worst version of any sentence. And
part of it is that the honest answer — *that is the other country's decision, taken on the day* —
sounds in every language like an official refusing to help. It is not. It is the only answer that is
true in all 196 countries.

**Discuss:** Which word in your own working language is used for two different documents? What
trouble does that cause?

**Put the background in one paragraph with no times. Number the events and give each one a time.
Name the document and say what it is not. End with two separate lines: what you told them, and what
you did not say. Give the next action a named hour.**

**Read your note aloud to your partner first.** Reading it out is how you hear a promise you did not
mean to make. Then check your own against the list, and swap.

**Then talk it over.** *In pairs, out loud.* Did either of you write *we will look into it*? What
