### 1 · The first conditional

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP2-07-06%%

> **Counterpart:** We have twenty-four hours and the laboratory result is not back.
> **Omar:** **If the result comes back today**, your twenty-four hours start today. **If it does
> not**, the clock has not started.
> **Counterpart:** And if we notify now, before the assessment is finished?
> **Omar:** **If you notify early, nothing bad happens to you** — but the message will be
> incomplete, and the organization will come back with questions.
> **Counterpart:** **Unless** we have the laboratory confirmation, we cannot answer them.
> **Omar:** Exactly. So the sequence protects you. **When the determination is made**, the clock
> starts, and not before.
> **Counterpart:** What **would** you do if you were us?
> **Omar:** **If I were in your position, I would** finish the assessment today and notify tonight.
> But I am not, and it is your determination to make.

*Two of these conditionals are about something likely, and one is about an imagined position. Which
is which?*

**1B  Underline every *if*, *unless* and *when* clause in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **if** + present, **will** | **If** the result **comes** back today, the clock **will** start today. |
| **if** + present, present *(a rule)* | **If** an event **is** notifiable, a state **notifies** within 24 hours. |
| **unless** = if not | **Unless** you have the confirmation, you cannot answer. |
| **when**, not **if**, for certainty | **When** the determination is made, the clock starts. |
| **if** + present, **may / might** | If you notify early, they **may** come back with questions. |

> **Grammar Note** — No *will* in the *if* clause: *if the result will come back* is wrong. And
> *when* and *if* are a real difference in this work: *when the determination is made* says it is
> coming; *if the determination is made* says it may not be. Choose on purpose, because the other
> side will read it closely.

| | Example |

> **Grammar Note** — *If I were you, I would…* is the politest way in English to give advice to

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Health notifications are where a foreign ministry's instinct and a health ministry's instinct pull
in opposite directions. A diplomat's instinct is to say as little as possible until the picture is
clear. A public health official's instinct is to tell everybody immediately. The Regulations contain
both instincts on purpose: an assessment period that protects against noise, and a short
notification period that protects against silence. Two things are worth remembering when you are
between them. First, a state that notifies is complying, not confessing, and an officer who treats a
notification as an admission teaches their counterparts to delay. Second, keep measures out of the
notification. The moment a notification letter mentions an airport, every reader joins the message to
the measure, the connection appears in print, and the state that was being careful looks as though it
was closing something.

**Discuss:** In your own ministry, who would want to wait and who would want to tell? How is that
decided?

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Internal note · health notification query
> *Consular Affairs · 4 March, 07:40 · O. Sabbagh*
>
> **The query.** A counterpart health ministry believes it is within twenty-four hours of a
> notification deadline and has asked us to pass material on.
>
> **The two clocks, with their starting points.**
> | Step | Period | Runs from |
> |---|---|---|
> | Assessment, using the Regulations' decision instrument | **48 hours** | **detection** of the event |
> | Notification through the National IHR Focal Point | **24 hours** | the **determination** that the event is notifiable |
>
> **Which clock is running.** On the facts as given to us at 06:00, the laboratory result is
> outstanding and **the determination has not been made**. **The 24-hour clock has therefore not
> started.** They are inside the assessment period, not outside the notification period.
>
> **What a notification is not.**
> 1. It is **not** a closure of a border, a cancellation of a flight or a quarantine.
> 2. It is **not** an admission of anything. A state that notifies is complying.
> 3. It is **not** a request to us. The channel is their **own focal point**, not this ministry.
>
> **What cannot be predicted.** We cannot say what recommendations the organization will issue,
> because recommendations are issued on the facts as notified and those facts are incomplete. I have
> said so to the consulate in those words.
>
> **Recommended reply.** One question — has the determination been made? — and one sentence: the 24
> hours run from that point. Offer help with the channel only.

> **Writer's Tip** — Put the starting point in the same table cell as the period, never in a
> sentence further down. A period and its start get separated the moment a note is summarized, and
> the summary is what the minister reads.
