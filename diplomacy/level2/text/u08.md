%%SUB Two articles, two requests, and the one word that decides which you are making%%
%%GOALS

A prosecutor sends you a two-page letter and asks you to forward it. Halfway down the second page it
asks for bank records and for the man himself. Those are two requests under two articles, they go to
two different places, and a letter that asks for both at once usually comes back with neither.

### 1 · Articles: *a*, *the* and no article

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP2-08-06%%

> **Prosecutor:** I need **the** records and **the** man.
> **Omar:** Then you need **a** request for assistance and **a** separate request for extradition.
> **Prosecutor:** Can you send **the** request today?
> **Omar:** I can transmit **a** request today if it is complete. **Evidence** requests go to **the**
> central authority; **extradition** goes by **a** different route in that state.
> **Prosecutor:** Which is faster?
> **Omar:** **Assistance** is usually faster. **The** extradition request you are describing would
> involve **a** court, and courts take **time**.
> **Prosecutor:** Send both in one envelope.
> **Omar:** I will put both in one envelope if you want. They are still two requests, and
> **the** one that is ready should not wait for **the** one that is not.

*Which nouns have no article at all, and why?*

**1B  Circle every *a* and *the* in 1A. Underline the nouns with no article.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **a / an** — one of several, first mention | We will send **a** request. |
| **the** — the one we both mean | **The** request arrived on Tuesday. |
| **no article** — uncountable, in general | **Evidence** takes time. · **Assistance** is faster. |
| **no article** — plural, in general | **Courts** take time. |
| **the** + singular, a category | **The** central authority is in the justice ministry. |
| first **a**, then **the** | We received **a** letter. **The** letter asked for two things. |

> **Grammar Note** — Uncountable nouns take no article when you mean the thing in general:
> *assistance is faster*, *evidence takes time*, *cooperation improved*. They take *the* when you
> mean a particular instance: *the assistance they requested*, *the evidence in this file*. This is
> the commonest small error in formal drafting and it changes how precise a sentence sounds.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Requests between states are read as much for their care as for their content. An official in a
central authority receives more requests than they can execute quickly and has to decide, informally
and often in a few seconds, which file looks as though it will be easy and which looks as though it
will generate three rounds of correspondence. Everything visible contributes to that judgment: the
article cited correctly, the names spelled consistently across every page, the account numbers
written out, the translation certified rather than promised. None of it is law. All of it is why one
request is executed in six weeks and an identical one takes eight months. The lesson is not that
form matters more than substance. It is that a reader who has to choose will choose the file that
respects their time.

**Discuss:** When you receive a request, what makes you deal with it sooner? Be honest.

### 3 · Write

Write **110 to 140 words**: a covering note on a two-part request.

**Number the two requests and put the article beside each. Give each a route and a timescale, and do

where you can. Say what you propose and why. End with what you cannot do.**

**Read your note aloud to your partner first.** Reading it out is how you hear two requests wearing
one coat. Then check your own against the list, and swap.
