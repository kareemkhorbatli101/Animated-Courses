**1C  Study the chart.**

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| **Has** the note been delivered? | Could you tell me **whether** the note **has been** delivered? |
| **How long do** they take? | Do you know **how long they take**? |
| **Who signs** it? | Would you mind telling me **who signs** it? |
| **Is** there a reference number? | May I ask **whether there is** a reference number? |
| **Where did** you send it? | Could you tell me **where you sent** it? |

> **Grammar Note** — Two things change. The auxiliary stops inverting: *how long do they take*
> becomes *how long they take*. And a yes-or-no question needs *whether* or *if*. Keeping the
> inversion — *could you tell me how long do they take* — is the error that marks out somebody who
> learned the polite phrase without the grammar behind it.

### 3 · Requesting without assuming

**3A  Study the chart.**

| Risky | Safe |
|---|---|
| We're flying through on Thursday. | We **are seeking** permission to overfly on Thursday and will not file until it is granted. |
| We've filed the flight plan, so we're clear. | The flight plan is filed. **The permissions are a separate matter** and two are outstanding. |
| They always say yes. | They have granted similar requests. **Each one is a fresh decision**, and I will not plan on the answer. |
| Can you hurry them up? | **Could you tell me** whether anything in our note is holding it up? |

> **Grammar Note** — Never write a sentence that presumes the answer. *We will be overflying on
> Thursday* reads, to the state being asked, as an announcement rather than a request — and the one
> thing Article 1 means is that it is theirs to decide.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Overflight and landing requests are the clearest illustration in consular work of what sovereignty
means in practice. Nothing about them is a formality, and the state being asked is doing a favor
even when the request is humanitarian and even when the answer has been yes every previous time.
Three habits mark out the officers whose requests get answered. They ask what is needed before they
ask how long it will take. They never write a sentence that presumes the grant, because a note that
announces a flight invites a refusal on principle rather than on the merits. And they say what they
will do if the answer is late — move the flight, not go without permission — which converts an urgent
request into a credible one. The underlying point is older than any of us: the airspace belongs to
somebody, completely and exclusively, and the whole of the courtesy follows from that.

**Discuss:** What does your own ministry grant to other states? How do you feel about requests that
assume the answer?

### 3 · Write

Write **120 to 150 words**: a note requesting a permission.

**Request, do not announce. Number the details. Offer a second window. State the purpose and say what
is not being sought. Give a contact available through the night. Ask for the decision in writing with
a reference. End with what will happen if the answer is late.**

**Read your note aloud to your partner first.** Reading it out is how you hear a sentence that
presumes the answer. Then check your own against the list, and swap.
