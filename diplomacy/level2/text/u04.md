> *Consular Affairs · protection terminology · page 1 of 1*

> The protection agency's own page says that **149 states are parties to the 1951 Convention
> *and/or* its 1967 Protocol**.
>
> That is a **combined** figure. Published per-instrument counts are different:
>
> | Instrument | Parties |
> |---|---|
> | the **1951** Convention | **146** |
> | the **1967** Protocol | **147** |
> | **either or both** | **149** |
>
> So a state can be party to one and not the other, and three of the 149 are parties to only one of
> the two. Quoting 149 as "parties to the Convention" is wrong by three, and quoting it without the
> words *and/or* is wrong in a way the reader cannot detect.
>
> **The original time limit has not vanished everywhere.** The 1951 Convention as drafted covered
> events occurring before 1951. The 1967 Protocol removed that limit. According to the agency's own
> material, the limitation still applies for **Saint Kitts and Nevis** and **Madagascar**.
>

> **refugee** has been recognized. The obligation of **non-refoulement** — not returning a person to

> difference between the two words is a matter of procedure and not of protection.
>
> *Source: the protection agency's own published pages on the 1951 Convention and the 1967 Protocol,

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In protection work the vocabulary is the law, and people use it loosely for understandable reasons.
*Refugee* is the word with dignity in it; *asylum-seeker* sounds provisional, which is exactly what
it is and exactly why nobody wants to be called it. A consular officer is not there to correct a
frightened man's self-description, and is also not free to adopt it, because the word determines
which office acts and under what obligation. The way out of that is not to argue on the telephone.
It is to write both, label both, and let the right authority decide. The same discipline matters in
the other direction too: an officer who writes *economic migrant* because that is their impression
has made a determination they have no power to make, and has put it in a file where somebody will
later quote it.

**Discuss:** Which word in your own work carries dignity for the speaker and a technical meaning for
the office? How do you handle the gap?
