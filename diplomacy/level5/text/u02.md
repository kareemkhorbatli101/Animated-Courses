# Unit 2 · Provisional Since 1946
%%SUB Rules that have been applied for eighty years and have never been made final%%
%%GOALS
Use the perfect passive to report what has and has not been done
Cite a provisional rule as a provisional rule
Distinguish a meeting under the rules from a consultation outside them
%%

The Charter says the Council shall adopt its own rules of procedure. At its first meeting, in
January 1946, it adopted them **provisionally**. They have been amended several times since, and the
word *provisional* has never been removed. Eighty years of decisions have been taken under rules that
their own title describes as temporary, and much of the Council's actual working day happens outside
them altogether.

### 1 · The perfect passive

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP5-02-06%%

> **Layla:** Your note says *the Council's rules of procedure provide*. Which rules?
> **Omar:** The ones in the booklet.
> **Layla:** The booklet is called *Provisional Rules of Procedure*, and the word is load-bearing.
> Write it: **the Council's provisional rules of procedure**. And when you explain their status, the
> perfect passive does all the work in one sentence: **they have been amended several times and have
> never been adopted in final form.**
> **Omar:** Why the passive there?
> **Layla:** Because the agent is either obvious or unhelpful. *The Council has amended them* invites
> the question *which Council?* — the membership has turned over many times. *They have been amended*
> reports the state of the text, which is what a reader needs.
> **Omar:** And *have never been adopted* rather than *are still provisional*?
> **Layla:** Use both, in that order. The perfect passive says what has not been done; *still
> provisional* says what follows from that. One is the history and the other is the status, and a
> good note gives the history in nine words and then stops.

*Why does Layla prefer the passive to naming the agent?*

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| **have been + pp** | The rules **have been amended** on several occasions. |
| **have never been + pp** | They **have never been adopted** in final form. |
| **has not yet been + pp** | The question **has not yet been settled** by the membership. |
| **had been + pp, before** | They **had been applied** for thirty-six years **before** the revision. |

> **Grammar Note** — The perfect passive is the natural tense of institutional history, because
> institutions outlive the people who act in them. *They have been amended* is not evasion: it is
> accuracy about a body whose membership in 1946 and in 2025 has nobody in common.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

There is a style of commentary that treats the provisionality of these rules as an embarrassment —
eighty years and they have not finished the paperwork. Officers who have worked in the building tend
to see it differently. A provisional text can be adjusted by a body whose composition changes every
two years, without anybody having to concede a principle, and that is worth more than tidiness. The
same instinct explains the second room: a gathering that is not a meeting has no record, and a
discussion with no record is a discussion in which a delegation can change its mind without
explaining why. Both arrangements buy flexibility and both are paid for in transparency, and the
bill is paid by people outside the room. There is no settled view about whether the trade is worth
it, and you will meet serious people on both sides for the rest of your career. What is not in
dispute is the drafting consequence: write what the document is called, and name the room you were
in.

**Discuss:** Is a discussion with no record a good thing or a bad thing? Who benefits, and who pays?

> | A record is kept | yes | no |
> | The same fifteen delegations | yes | yes |
>
> **A gathering that is not a meeting is not to be described as one**, however it was described to
> you in the corridor.
>
> **4 What may be cited for a day of consultations.** What was issued, and nothing else: a
> resolution, a presidential statement, a note by the President. **If nothing was issued, the
> sentence is that nothing was issued** — which is a reportable fact and frequently the most useful
> one in the cable.
>
> **5 Two invitations worth knowing.** Under the provisional rules, a Member of the United Nations
> which is not a member of the Council **may be invited to participate** in the discussion of a
> question when its interests are specially affected; and the Council may **invite members of the
> Secretariat or other persons** to supply information. **Both are invitations, taken by decision.
> Neither is a right**, and a draft that describes either as one will be marked.

> **Writer's Tip** — Paragraph 4 is the paragraph to steal. Any guidance note that tells a drafter
> what to write when nothing happened will be used; one that only tells them what to write when
> something did will sit in a drawer.
