# Unit 12 · The Silence Procedure
%%SUB The last reading in this book is a body writing down how it will decide when it cannot meet%%
%%GOALS
Write the report of a meeting
Report a decision taken without a meeting
Review the structures of Units 1 to 11

In March 2020 an assembly of a hundred and ninety-three members could not meet. It adopted a
decision describing how it would take decisions in the meantime: the President would circulate a
draft to every member under a **silence procedure** of at least seventy-two hours, and if the silence
was not broken the draft would be considered adopted. Eleven units of this book have been about what
counts as agreement. This one ends the book with an agreement that consists of nobody speaking for
three days.

>
> **The decision.** On **27 March 2020**, the General Assembly adopted a decision establishing a
> **procedure for taking decisions during the coronavirus disease pandemic**, the Assembly being
> unable to meet in person.
>
> **The procedure, in outline.**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | **1** | the President of the General Assembly **circulates** a draft decision to **all Member States** |
> | **2** | the circulation states a **silence procedure** of **at least 72 hours** |
> | **3** | a Member State may **break silence** within that period |
> | **4** | if **no** Member State breaks silence, the draft is **considered adopted** |
> | **5** | if silence **is** broken, the draft is **not adopted under that procedure** |
>
> **What it is, and what it is not.**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | **it is** a decision of the Assembly, taken under a procedure **the Assembly itself adopted** | and it is recorded as such |
> | **it is not** a vote | no votes are cast and no voting record exists |
> | **it is not** consensus as the rules of procedure use the word | nobody is present, and presence is part of that test |
>
> **The sentence that most often goes wrong. Silence broken is not rejection.** A draft whose silence
> is broken has not been adopted under the procedure. It has not been voted down, no position has
> been taken on its substance by anybody except the delegation that spoke, and it may be taken up when
> the body can meet. **A cable reporting that a proposal was rejected because silence was broken has
> reported an outcome that did not occur.**
>
> **Why the procedure was written down at all.** Because a body that cannot meet discovers that it
> has been relying on things it never recorded: that a decision is taken in a room, at a time, in the
> presence of people who can raise a hand. **Take away the room and every one of those assumptions has
> to be replaced by a sentence.** The decision of 27 March 2020 is three paragraphs long and each
> paragraph replaces something nobody had previously had to write.
>
> **It was temporary, and that matters too.** The procedure was adopted for an exceptional situation
> and was described in those terms. **A procedure adopted as exceptional is cited as exceptional**,
> with its date, and is not described as the Assembly's practice.
>
> **Why this is the last reading in this book.** Eleven units have asked what counts as agreement: no
> Member present objecting; rules applied but never adopted; a rule never agreed at all; a threshold
> whose denominator was argued for sixteen years; a listing that consensus prevented; two classes of
> party; a model nobody is obliged to follow; a year's notice; a relationship that exists unless
> either side says otherwise; a date with a condition; and a report you are not allowed to read. **This
> one is the limiting case: agreement constituted by nobody speaking for three days.** If you can
> report that accurately, you can report anything in this field.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

The silence procedure is the clearest window in this book onto what a procedure actually is. For
seventy-five years the Assembly had decided things in a room, and the room did an enormous amount of
work that nobody had written down: it established who was present, when the moment to speak had
arrived, what counted as having spoken, and how long anybody had to decide. Remove the room and all
of it has to be replaced in prose, in a hurry, by a body that cannot convene to discuss how to
convene. Three paragraphs were enough, which is remarkable, and the three paragraphs are made
entirely of the things this book has been about: a period, a trigger, a defined act, two outcomes and
no third. If you want to understand any institution, find the document it wrote when it could not do
the thing it normally does. That document will tell you what the institution thought was essential,
because it was written at the one moment when nobody had time to include anything else.

**Discuss:** What does your own ministry do that has never been written down? What would you have to
write if you could not do it?

**1B  Listen. What did the Director ask about the first report?** %%AUD DIP5-12-14%%

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Report of proceedings · draft decision circulated 9 November
> *Delegation · drafted 20:40, 11 November*
>
> **1 What was before the body.** A draft decision circulated by the President of the General
> Assembly to all Member States on **9 November at 15:00**, under a **silence procedure of
> seventy-two hours** established by the Assembly's decision of **27 March 2020**, adopted for the
> exceptional circumstances then prevailing.
>
> **2 What was decided, and under what procedure. Silence was broken at 18:22 on 11 November** by
> **one** Member State, by letter to the President. **The draft was accordingly not adopted under the
> procedure.** It **was not rejected**: no vote was taken and **nothing has been decided on the
> substance**. It **may be taken up at a formal meeting**.
>
> **3 Statements.** The letter breaking silence has been **circulated to all Member States** and is
> **attached in full**. **This report does not summarize it.** No other Member State communicated
> anything within the period.
>
> **4 What did not happen. No vote was requested** and none could have been taken under this
> procedure. **No meeting was convened**, and there is accordingly **no meeting record** for these
> proceedings. **No deadline is prescribed** by the procedure for a draft whose silence has been
> broken.
>
> **5 Follow-up, with a time.** We will report **on the day** any revised draft is circulated or any
> meeting is convened. Our own instructions on the substance **remain as given on 4 November** and
> are unaffected by this outcome.
>
> *This report characterizes no delegation's position. The letter at paragraph 3 is in the words of
> the Member State that sent it.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Paragraph 4 is the one to copy into every report you ever write. *No meeting
> was convened, and there is accordingly no meeting record* is the sentence that stops somebody in
> two years concluding that your ministry's files are incomplete.

**1D  Circle the correct word.**

1. The period was (forty-eight / seventy-two) hours.
2. The draft was (rejected / not adopted under the procedure).
3. The report (summarizes / attaches) the letter.
4. There (is / is no) meeting record.

2. It (uses / avoids) the word *rejected*.

### 3 · Write

Write **160 to 190 words**: a report of proceedings, for a meeting or for a decision taken without
one.

**Say what was before the body, with the document and the time. Say what was decided and under what
rule or procedure, in the procedure's own words. Attach statements rather than summarizing them.
Report what did not happen, including the absence of any record. End with follow-up and a time, and
say whether your instructions are affected.**

**Read your report aloud to your partner first.** Any verdict word you hear is a word to cut. Then
check your own against the list, and swap.

| | ✓ |
|---|---|
| Is the rule or procedure named with its date? | |
| Is there a paragraph on what did not happen? | |
| Are statements attached rather than summarized? | |

**Peer feedback.** Read your partner's report as somebody in two years looking for a record that does
not exist. Does the report tell you it does not exist?

**Then talk it over.** *In pairs, out loud.* Did either of you supply a verdict? Which word did it?
Why do you think the habit is so strong, and what would you read differently to weaken it?
