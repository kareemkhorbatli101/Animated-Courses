# Unit 8 · A Year's Notice
%%SUB What a withdrawal ends, when it ends it, and what it leaves exactly where it was%%
%%GOALS
Use temporal precision in formal writing
Report a withdrawal with its effective date
Say what a withdrawal does not discharge
%%

A state that wishes to leave a treaty of this kind sends a written notification to the depositary.
The withdrawal takes effect **one year after the date of receipt** — not on the date of the
announcement, not on the date of the notification, and not on the date the newspapers report it. And
when it does take effect, it leaves behind everything that had already accrued, every obligation
incurred while the state was a party, and every proceeding already commenced. This unit is about
dates, and about what a date does not undo.

> **Reader's Tip** — In any withdrawal clause, read the second paragraph before the first. The first
> tells you how to leave; the second tells you what you cannot leave behind, and the second is where
> the questions come from.

> | What | Why |
> |---|---|

> | **financial obligations already accrued** | named expressly, to end an obvious argument |
> | **cooperation in proceedings commenced before the effective date** | the clause preserves it |
> | **matters already under consideration by the Court** | their continued consideration is not prejudiced |

>
> | | |

### 1 · Temporal precision

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP5-08-06%%

> **Layla:** Your note says the state left the Statute in the year of the announcement.
> **Omar:** That is when they said they were leaving.
> **Layla:** Which is not a date in the clause. The clause says the withdrawal takes effect **one
> year after the date of receipt of the notification**. So you need the date the depositary received
> the written notification, and then you add a year, and that is the date you print.
> **Omar:** And if the notification is dated earlier than the receipt?
> **Layla:** Then the date on the document is irrelevant and you ignore it. The clause chose receipt
> deliberately, because receipt is the one date the depositary can prove.
> **Omar:** My sentence also says they are no longer bound by anything.
> **Layla:** The second paragraph of the clause says the opposite, in four separate ways, and you
> must use its words. **The State is not discharged from the obligations arising while it was a
> Party, including financial obligations which may have accrued.** And the withdrawal **does not
> affect cooperation in proceedings commenced prior to the date on which it became effective.**
> **Omar:** *Prior to* rather than *before*?
> **Layla:** In a note that may be read against the clause, use the clause's own words. *Before* is
> better English and *prior to the date on which* is the phrase a reader can match to the text in
> front of them. Match the instrument where the instrument will be consulted; write plainly
> everywhere else.

*Why does the clause use the date of receipt rather than the date of the notification?*

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| **one year after the date of receipt** | the interval, with its trigger named |
| **with effect from [date]** | **with effect from** 29 March, the State is not a Party |
| **prior to the date on which** | commenced **prior to the date on which** the withdrawal took effect |
| **while it was a Party** | obligations arising **while it was a Party** |
| **unless … specifies a later date** | the exception the clause itself provides |
| **on the date of, and not before** | emphatic, for a date somebody keeps moving |

> **Grammar Note** — A date in a formal note should always carry its derivation when the derivation
> is a rule: *29 March, being one year after the date of receipt of the notification*. The reader can
> then check you, which is the only reason to print a date at all.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

A withdrawal clause is the part of a treaty that gets drafted last and read first. Nobody negotiating
an institution wants to spend the final afternoon on how to leave it, and yet the clause decides how
much the institution can be trusted: a treaty you can leave on a week's notice, taking your
liabilities with you, is a treaty nobody will build anything on. The year's notice does two things at
once. It gives the leaving state a real exit, which is what makes joining possible in the first
place, and it gives everybody else twelve months to arrange their affairs, which is what makes the
institution durable. The carve-outs do the rest: a state cannot use the exit to erase what it already
owes or to end a case already under way. The result is an exit that is genuine and expensive, which
is the only kind worth drafting. Officers who have worked on institutional treaties often say the
withdrawal clause is where you can see what the drafters really thought of each other.

**Discuss:** Should it be easy or hard to leave an international institution? What does each choice
protect?

> notification are **not** the operative dates.
>
> **2 What ends. With effect from that date**, the State is **not a Party** to the Statute, and
> obligations arising **after** that date do not apply to it.
>
> **3 What does not end. The State is not discharged, by reason of its withdrawal, from the
> obligations arising from the Statute while it was a Party**, including **financial obligations
> which may have accrued**. **The withdrawal does not affect cooperation** with the Court in
> connection with investigations and proceedings **commenced prior to the date on which the
> withdrawal became effective**, and **does not prejudice the continued consideration** of matters
> already before the Court.
>
> **4 What this Ministry does not do.** This line does not **name** the States that have withdrawn
> under this provision, and does not **characterize** any State's reasons. **Both are on the
> depositary's published record**, to which inquiries may be directed.
>
> **5 If pressed on a particular date.** Whether a duty existed on a particular day is a question of
> the effective date and of when the relevant proceeding was commenced. **We will answer it in
> writing, from the record, and not from memory.**
>
> *Figures for States Parties carry the date of the Court's page.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Paragraph 5 is the one that keeps a spokesperson out of trouble. Any line that
> tells a journalist exactly which questions will be answered in writing is a line that can be read
> out at speed.
