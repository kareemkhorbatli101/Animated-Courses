# Unit 9 · Unless Either State Notifies
%%SUB Two states can both be parties and the Convention can apply between neither of them%%
%%GOALS
Use negative conditions precisely
Check a treaty relationship pair by pair
Explain why being a party is not enough
%%

Almost every status question in this book has had one axis: is this state a party? This unit adds a
second. Under the convention studied here, the treaty has effect **between two Contracting States
only if neither of them has notified the depositary** about the other within the period allowed. Two
states may both have ratified, both appear on the party list, and have no treaty relationship with
each other at all. A party list cannot show you that, and a draft built on one will be wrong without
being inaccurate.

>

> > notified the depositary regarding the other**. Where no such notification is made, the Convention
> > has effect between the two States from a date calculated from the end of the period during which
> > a notification could have been made.
>
> **So there are two questions, not one.**
>
> | | The question | Where the answer is |
> |---|---|---|
> | **1** | Is each State a Contracting State? | the party list |
> | **2** | Has either State notified the depositary regarding the other? | the depositary's record of notifications |
>
> **A list cannot answer the second question.** Two States may each appear on the party list and have
> **no treaty relationship with each other**. The Convention continues to have effect between every
> other pair. **The picture is a grid, and the party list is one edge of it.**

> of confidence in another State's courts that a State may be willing to extend to some and not

> about a particular pair.

### 1 · Negative conditions

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP5-09-06%%

> **Layla:** Your advice says the Convention applies between the two States because both are parties.
> **Omar:** Both are on the list. I checked.
> **Layla:** You checked one axis. The article says the Convention has effect between two Contracting
> States **only if neither of them has notified the depositary regarding the other**. Read that
> condition again and notice what it asks you to establish.
> **Omar:** That neither has notified. A negative, about two states.
> **Layla:** A negative about two states, which means two searches of the depositary's record, and a
> sentence that cannot be built out of a list. And notice the form: *only if neither*. Not *unless
> both*, which would mean something else entirely.
> **Omar:** What would *unless both* mean?
> **Layla:** That the Convention applies unless they have both notified — so one notification would
> leave it in effect. The clause says the opposite: one notification is enough to prevent the
> relationship. **In a negative condition the quantifier does all the work, and getting it wrong
> reverses the rule.**
> **Omar:** So how do I write it?
> **Layla:** Two sentences and a search. **Both States are Contracting States.** **The Convention has
> effect between them only in the absence of a notification by either regarding the other, which I
> have checked on the depositary's record as at today's date.** And if you have not checked, you say
> so.

*What is the difference between* only if neither *and* unless both?

**1B  Underline the three negative conditions in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Form | Means | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| **only if neither X nor Y** | both must have refrained | the commonest correct form here |
| **unless either X or Y** | the same thing, stated the other way round | easy to write and easy to misread |
| **unless both** | **not** the same thing: one act leaves it in force | reverses the rule |
| **in the absence of a notification by either** | neutral, and the shortest safe form | — |
| **provided that no notification has been made** | a proviso, slightly heavier | — |
| **save where a notification has been made** | the exception stated positively | useful in a second sentence |

> **Grammar Note** — When a condition is negative and has two subjects, write it twice: once with
> *neither* and once, in the next sentence, as the positive exception — *the Convention does not have
> effect between them where either has notified*. Readers get negatives wrong at speed, and two
> formulations of the same rule cost ten words and prevent a mistake.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Bilateralization is one of those devices that looks like bureaucratic obstruction and turns out to be
the reason the instrument exists at all. A convention on the recognition of judgments asks a state to
let another state's courts have practical effect inside its own legal system, and that is a larger
request than it sounds: it touches the independence of the judiciary, the protection of citizens'
property and the state's confidence in a system it does not control. No state will extend that to
every other state on the strength of a single signature. The mechanism lets each state build its own
network inside a common framework, which means the framework can be joined by states that disagree
profoundly about each other's courts. The cost is borne entirely by the people who have to answer
questions about particular pairs, which is to say by legal advisers and registries, who are never
consulted when the mechanism is chosen. That is a pattern worth noticing: the complexity of
international law tends to be pushed downwards onto whoever answers the telephone.

**Discuss:** Who should bear the complexity of a system — the negotiators or the officers who apply
it? Is there a choice?

> ### Advice note · enforcement of a judgment of the courts of Astrand
> *Legal Affairs · for the Ministry of Justice, court registry · 11 October*
>
> **1 The instrument.** The **Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Judgments in
> Civil or Commercial Matters**, concluded at The Hague on **2 July 2019**, entered into force on **1

>
> **2 Two questions, not one.**
>
> | | The question | The source |
> |---|---|---|

> depositary regarding the other.**
>

> Convention accordingly **has effect as between them**.
>

> application. **This note states the treaty position between the two States and nothing further.**
>
