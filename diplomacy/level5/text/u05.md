# Unit 5 · The Listing That Never Happened
%%SUB When consensus is a veto — and what a refusal to list does not mean%%
%%GOALS
Use result and purpose clauses accurately
Report a proposal that has been recommended and not adopted
Say what a non-decision does not establish
%%

A convention set up a procedure so that a chemical in international trade could not be exported
without the importing state's informed consent. The procedure applies only to the chemicals in one
annex, and adding a chemical to that annex requires a decision of the parties **by consensus**. A
scientific committee recommended one substance for listing, and the parties have considered it at
session after session since 2006 without reaching consensus. The substance is still not listed. This
unit is about what that does and does not mean.

> | **Rotterdam Convention** on the prior informed consent procedure for certain hazardous chemicals and pesticides in international trade | adopted **10 September 1998** |
> | **Entered into force** | **24 February 2004** |
> | **Parties** | about **165**, on the secretariat's page, which carries the date of that page |
>
> **What listing does.** A chemical **listed in Annex III** is subject to the prior informed consent
> procedure. In outline: an exporting party must ensure that export does not take place contrary to
> the importing party's decision, and the importing party's decision — to consent, to consent subject
> to conditions, or to withhold consent — is published and circulated. **The procedure is about
> information and consent. It is not a prohibition**, and listing a chemical does not ban it.
>
> **How the annex is amended.** A chemical is added to Annex III by a **decision of the Conference of
> the Parties**, and the Convention requires that decision to be taken **by consensus**. There is no
> majority route. **One party present and objecting is therefore sufficient to prevent a listing**,
> and that is not a defect in the procedure: it is the procedure.
>
> **The recommendation, and the non-decision.** The Convention's **Chemical Review Committee**
> examines proposals against the Convention's own criteria and makes recommendations. It
> **recommended** that **chrysotile asbestos** be listed in Annex III. The Conference of the Parties
> has considered that recommendation at **session after session since 2006** and **has not reached
> consensus**. **The substance remains unlisted.**
>
> **What non-listing does not establish.**
>
> | It does not mean | Because |
> |---|---|
> | that the substance is safe | the Conference took no decision about its properties |
> | that the Committee was wrong | the recommendation stands, published, unamended |
> | that the substance is unregulated | every party regulates it, or does not, under its own law |
> | that the question is closed | the item returns to the agenda |

### 5 · Word study — result and purpose clauses

**5A  Complete the chart.**

%%FIG k05-f6 | A three-column chart headed the form, result or purpose, and an example, with rows for so that, in order that, with the result that, thereby, so as to, and two empty rows.%%

| Form | Result or purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **so that** | either, from context | The procedure exists **so that** the importing state can decide. |
| **in order that** | purpose, formal | **In order that** consent may be informed, information is circulated. |
| **with the result that** | result only, neutral | Consensus was not reached, **with the result that** the substance is not listed. |
| **thereby** | result, attached to a participle | failing to reach consensus and **thereby** leaving the annex unchanged |
| **so as to** | purpose, with an infinitive | |
| **such that** | result, formal and rather heavy | |

> **Grammar Note** — *With the result that* is the safest connector in this subject, because it
> reports a consequence without attributing an intention. *So that* can be read as purpose, and in a
> sentence about why a listing failed, implying purpose is implying bad faith.

### 1 · Result and purpose

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP5-05-06%%

> **Layla:** Your sentence says the parties blocked the listing so that trade could continue.
> **Omar:** That is what happened.
> **Layla:** It may be why it happened, and you cannot show it, and *so that* asserts it. Write the
> result and leave the purpose to the people who stated one. **Consensus was not reached, with the
> result that the substance is not listed.** That sentence is unanswerable.
> **Omar:** Is that not evasive?
> **Layla:** It is precise. There were parties that explained their position and parties that did
> not, and the explanations are in the record where anybody can read them. Your note is not the place
> where motives are assigned, because your note will be quoted by somebody who does not have the
> record.
> **Omar:** And where may I use purpose?
> **Layla:** About the procedure itself, where the purpose is in the text. **The procedure exists in
> order that the importing state may consent on the basis of disclosed information.** That is not a
> guess about anybody's motive; it is what the Convention says it is for.
> **Omar:** So purpose for the instrument, result for the room.
> **Layla:** Purpose for the instrument, result for the room. Write that on a card.

*Why does Layla object to* so that *in Omar's sentence?*

**1B  Underline the result clause and the purpose clause in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Form | Example | The risk it avoids |
|---|---|---|
| **with the result that** | Consensus was not reached, **with the result that** the annex is unchanged. | attributing purpose |
| **thereby** | leaving the item on the agenda and **thereby** keeping the question open | a second sentence |
| **in order that** | circulated **in order that** consent may be informed | vagueness about the aim of a procedure |
| **so as to** | drafted **so as to** require consensus | the same, with an infinitive |
| **for the purpose of** | listed **for the purpose of** the consent procedure, and no other | implying a ban |
| **the effect of which is** | a rule **the effect of which is** that one objection suffices | editorializing |

> **Grammar Note** — Result connectors are about the world; purpose connectors are about minds. In a
> report of a negotiation you have evidence about the first and almost never about the second.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Consensus rules have two reputations and both are earned. In a conference where everything must be
agreed by everybody, the party with the least to gain sets the pace, and a scientific recommendation
can sit on an agenda for two decades while people who were not born when it was made grow up and
join delegations. That is a real cost and it falls on people who never attend the meetings. The
other reputation is harder to see and just as real: a procedure that can impose an obligation on a
state by majority vote is a procedure that state will think twice about joining, and a convention
with a hundred and sixty-five parties and a slow annex may protect more people than a convention
with forty parties and a fast one. Officers who have spent a career in this work rarely hold either
view purely. What they share is a refusal to describe the outcome in words the record will not
support, because the argument about whether consensus is right is a legitimate argument and
misreporting a non-decision is not a contribution to it.

**Discuss:** Would you trade a faster procedure for fewer parties? Who pays for each choice?

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Press line · listing of chemicals under the Convention
> *Legal Affairs with Health · cleared for use, 21 May*
>
> **1 The recommendation. The Convention's Chemical Review Committee has recommended** that the
> substance be listed in Annex III, having examined it against the Convention's criteria. **That
> recommendation stands and has not been amended or withdrawn.**
>
> **2 What the Conference has done.** The Conference of the Parties has considered the recommendation
> at **successive meetings since 2006** and **has not reached consensus**. Amendment of that annex
> requires a decision **taken by consensus**.
>
> **3 The result. With the result that** the substance **is not listed** in Annex III and **the prior
> informed consent procedure does not apply to it**.
>
> **4 The limit of that outcome. No decision has been taken by the Conference as to the properties
> of the substance.** The absence of a listing is not a finding about the substance; it is the
> absence of an amendment to a list.
>
> **5 Where the next question goes.** What may lawfully be imported into or used in this country is a
> matter of **our own law**, on which the **competent ministry** speaks. This Ministry speaks about
> the Convention and the proceedings of its Conference.
>
> *This line characterizes no delegation's position. Positions stated at the sessions are on the
> record of those sessions.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Paragraph 5 is the one that saves a spokesperson's afternoon. A press line that
> says which ministry answers the next question is a press line nobody has to improvise.
