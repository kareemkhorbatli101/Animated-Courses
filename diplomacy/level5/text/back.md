# Grammar reference

Every structure in this book, in unit order. The second column is the mistake it causes, which is
the column worth reading twice.

## Starting Point · What a procedure is for

| | |
|---|---|
| **A procedure produces a decision nobody can reopen** | that is its purpose, and the reason it looks less important than it is |
| **Three rules** | who must agree · how objection is made · how the result is written |
| **Four sentences a week** | nobody present objected · the rules remain provisional · the figure carries the date of its page · being a party is not enough |
| **The three questions** | under what rule · what counted as agreement · what is the record, and who keeps it |

## Unit 1 · *Deemed* and *taken to be*

| | |
|---|---|
| ***is deemed to have*** decided by consensus | a rule treating silence as a decision; the gap is the point |
| ***does not amount to*** a formal objection | disappointment, a reservation of position and absence are not objections |
| **no Member present formally objected** | the rule's own test, and not *unanimously* |
| **report the decision, then the speech, then the absences** | in that order, or the reader cannot tell what was decided |

## Unit 2 · The perfect passive

| | |
|---|---|
| **the rules *have been amended*** | the state of the text, without naming a Council that has turned over many times |
| **they *have never been adopted*** in final form | the history in nine words; then the status |
| **provisional rule 37** | the word is part of the citation |
| **the members held informal consultations** | a consultation is not a meeting; no number, no record |

## Unit 3 · Exceptive structures

| | |
|---|---|
| ***with the exception of*** draft rule 42 | the exception first, because the exception is the point |
| ***save for***, ***other than***, ***except as provided in*** | three registers for the same carve-out |
| ***in the absence of*** an agreed voting rule | consensus is what remains; no rule provides for it |
| ***notwithstanding*** | overrides a rule that exists — unavailable where none does |

## Unit 4 · Presenting two readings

| | |
|---|---|
| ***On one reading … On the other*** | parallel structures, or you argue a case by rhythm |
| ***turns on whether*** | names the point in dispute without taking it |
| ***was resolved by … in favor of*** | decided, **not** discovered — the decision could have gone the other way |
| **in force *between the parties that have accepted it*** | *in force* alone is incomplete for an amendment |

## Unit 5 · Result and purpose clauses

| | |
|---|---|
| ***with the result that*** | a consequence with no intention attributed |
| ***in order that***, ***so as to*** | purpose, for the instrument, where the text states it |
| **has not reached consensus** | not *declined*, not *rejected*, not *defeated* |
| **no decision was taken as to the properties** | the limit of a non-decision, said to anybody outside the ministry |

## Unit 6 · Apposition

| | |
|---|---|
| **the consultative parties, *that is, those entitled to participate*** | the definition beside the term, not in brackets or a footnote |
| **bare appositives and *namely*** | the same job, in two other registers |
| **a party, and a party entitled to decide** | the unqualified plural always means the larger group |
| **the Treaty neither recognizes nor denies any claim** | a clause may preserve a disagreement on purpose |

## Unit 7 · Degrees of conformity

| | |
|---|---|
| ***based on*** / ***closely modeled on*** / ***in terms identical to*** | each phrase is a claim about what you did, not only about the texts |
| ***in substance, with modifications*** · ***departing from*** | when you have read it and can name the difference |
| **a model law has no parties** | *party*, *ratify*, *in force* and *depositary* are all unavailable |
| ***it has not been established whether*** | the sentence that protects a case in a year |

## Unit 8 · Temporal precision

| | |
|---|---|
| **one year after *the date of receipt*** | the trigger is receipt; the announcement and the notification are not |
| ***with effect from*** | the date something begins, for one party |
| ***prior to the date on which*** | the clause's own words, where the clause will be consulted |
| ***is not discharged from*** obligations arising ***while it was a Party*** | a withdrawal is forwards; almost nothing about it is backwards |

## Unit 9 · Negative conditions

| | |
|---|---|
| ***only if neither*** has notified | both must have refrained; *unless both* reverses the rule |
| ***in the absence of*** a notification by either | the shortest safe form |
| ***as between*** X and Y | the relation of two particular states, not the convention generally |
| **the word *so*** | between two status facts it asserts an inference that almost never holds |

## Unit 10 · Ordinal thresholds and conditional dates

| | |
|---|---|
| **on 1 January 2019, *provided that* twenty instruments had been deposited** | the date is the event; the threshold is a proviso |
| ***upon the deposit of the twentieth instrument*** | a different clause, with a different date to print |
| **entered into force / bound from** | one date for the instrument, a different date for most parties |
| ***universal ratification*** | true of one instrument in this series; fence it in the next sentence |

## Unit 11 · Reporting absence

| | |
|---|---|
| ***recorded as not having submitted*** | a fact about a record, which stays true if the record is wrong |
| ***has not been made public*** | about publication, not existence — and publication is not the obligation |
| ***we are not in a position to state*** | neither ignorance nor accusation |
| **three figures and no total** | adding two of them creates a category the publisher never created |

## Unit 12 · Review, and the report of a meeting

| | |
|---|---|
| ***was considered adopted***, no delegation having broken silence | the mechanism named in the sentence that reports it |
| **silence having been broken, *not adopted under the procedure*** | not *rejected*; nothing has been decided on the substance |
| **the five parts** | what was before the body · what was decided and under what rule · statements · what did not happen · follow-up with a time |
| **what did not happen** | *no meeting was convened, and there is accordingly no meeting record* |

# The British layer

This book is written in American English, and the series carries a switchable British layer: a fixed
list of words is respelled at build time. The list lives with the series rather than in this book.

What the layer does not touch:

1. **The date.** Write the month in letters.
2. **A quoted instrument keeps its own spelling.** Every agreement, rule of procedure and decision
   quoted in this book is quoted as published, inside quotation marks, even where its spelling
   differs from the surrounding text. Correcting an instrument inside a quotation is an alteration.
3. **A defined term keeps the spelling of the instrument that defines it.** *Contracting State*,
   *High Contracting Party* and *States Parties* are capitalized as their instruments capitalize
   them.
4. **A decision number, a document symbol and a rule number are never translated or reformatted**,
   and a provisional rule is cited as provisional in both variants.
5. **The five parts of a meeting report keep their order** in both variants, because the order is
   what makes the report usable.

# Sources

Twelve source bodies carry the twelve reading texts. Not one appears in Books Foundation, 1, 2 or 3,
or anywhere in the two sister series, and no figure is used twice. The series note is in
`SOURCES.md`.

| Unit | Source | What the text prints |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Marrakesh Agreement Establishing the World Trade Organization**, Article IX:1 and its footnote | The Agreement continues the practice of **decision-making by consensus**; the footnote deems a body **to have decided by consensus if no Member present at the meeting formally objects**. **166 Members** on the organization's own page, the most recent two accessions having taken effect in **August 2024**. **Absence is not objection; unhappiness is not objection; one Member present and objecting is enough** |
| 2 | **Charter of the United Nations, Article 30**; the Security Council's **Provisional Rules of Procedure** | Article 30: the Council **shall adopt its own rules of procedure**. The rules were adopted **provisionally** at the Council's **first meeting in January 1946**, **amended** on several occasions, issued as a numbered revision in **1982**, and **never adopted in final form**. The provisional rules provide for the **invitation** of non-member States and of other persons. **Informal consultations of the whole are not meetings** under the rules: no rule number, no meeting number, no record |
| 3 | The decision of the **Conference of the Parties to the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change at its first session, 1995**, and the published **draft rules of procedure** | The Conference **was unable to adopt** rules of procedure and decided to **apply the draft rules with the exception of draft rule 42**, on **voting**, whose text stands in **square brackets** with more than one alternative. The item has been on the agenda of **every session since 1995** and **deferred every time**. **In the absence of an agreed voting rule, decisions are taken by consensus** — the default is an absence, not a provision |
| 4 | **Basel Convention** of **22 March 1989** (in force **5 May 1992**, about **191 parties**); the **Ban Amendment** of **1995**; the decision of the Parties of **2011** | The amendment article requires instruments from **at least three-fourths of the Parties who accepted** an amendment, **without stating the date at which they are counted**. On the **fixed-time** reading the denominator is settled in 1995; on the **current-time** reading it rises with every accession. **Unresolved for sixteen years**; **resolved by decision of the Parties in 2011** in favor of the fixed-time approach; the Ban Amendment **entered into force 5 December 2019**, **between the parties that have accepted it** |
| 5 | **Rotterdam Convention** of **10 September 1998** (in force **24 February 2004**, about **165 parties**); the recommendation of its **Chemical Review Committee** on **chrysotile asbestos** | Listing in **Annex III** subjects a chemical to the **prior informed consent procedure** and requires a decision of the Conference of the Parties **taken by consensus**. The Committee **recommended** listing; the Conference has considered the recommendation at **session after session since 2006** and **has not reached consensus**. **Non-listing is not a finding that the substance is safe**, and proposals to allow listing by qualified majority have themselves not been adopted |
| 6 | **Antarctic Treaty**, done at Washington **1 December 1959**, signed by **twelve** states, in force **23 June 1961** | About **57 parties** on the Secretariat's published list, of which **29 are Consultative Parties** — the original signatories and parties demonstrating their interest by **conducting substantial scientific research activity**. **Measures become effective when approved by all parties entitled to participate.** The article on claims provides that nothing is to be read as a **renunciation**, that no party's position on recognition is prejudiced, and that **no new claim may be asserted while the Treaty is in force** |
| 7 | **UNCITRAL Model Law on International Commercial Arbitration**, **1985**, amended **2006** | A **legislative text recommended to States**: **no parties, no entry into force, no depositary**. The published list records jurisdictions that have **enacted legislation based on** it — on the order of **ninety States** and a larger number of jurisdictions, **as at the date of the page**. **Based on** covers a faithful copy, an enactment in substance with modifications, a structure-only enactment, and an enactment of the **pre-2006** text |
| 8 | **Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court**, adopted **17 July 1998**, in force **1 July 2002**, Article 127 | Withdrawal is by **written notification** and **takes effect one year after the date of receipt**, unless a later date is specified. A State **is not discharged** from obligations arising **while it was a Party**, including **financial obligations which may have accrued**; the withdrawal **does not affect cooperation** in proceedings **commenced prior to the effective date**, nor **prejudice the continued consideration** of matters already before the Court. **Two withdrawals have taken effect; the unit does not name the States** |
| 9 | **Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Judgments in Civil or Commercial Matters**, The Hague, **2 July 2019**, in force **1 September 2023** | The Convention **has effect between two Contracting States only if neither has notified the depositary regarding the other**. So there are **two questions**: is each a Contracting State (the party list), and has either notified about the other (the depositary's record). **Two States may both be parties and have no treaty relationship with each other.** The picture is a **grid**, and the party list is one edge of it |
| 10 | **Montreal Protocol**, adopted **16 September 1987**, in force **1 January 1989**; its **five amendments**; the **Kigali Amendment** of **15 October 2016** | **198 parties** to the Protocol on the secretariat's page: **every United Nations member State** and further entities — **the one genuinely universal instrument in this series**. Each of the five amendments has **its own, smaller membership**. The Kigali Amendment's clause fixed **1 January 2019 provided that at least twenty instruments had been deposited**, with a fallback of **ninety days** after the condition was met; the condition was met. **A state ratifying later is bound from its own date** |
| 11 | **Arms Trade Treaty**, adopted **2 April 2013**, opened for signature **3 June 2013**, in force **24 December 2014**, Article 13; the Secretariat's published reporting record | An **initial report** within the first year on implementation measures, and an **annual report by 31 May** on authorized or actual exports and imports, which **may exclude commercially sensitive or national security information**. The published record distinguishes **three** states of affairs: **submitted and public**, **submitted and not made public**, and **not submitted** — and the first two are **compliance**. **Three figures and no total**, each carrying the date of the record |
| 12 | The decision of the **General Assembly of 27 March 2020** establishing a procedure for taking decisions during the COVID-19 pandemic | The President **circulates** a draft decision to **all Member States** under a **silence procedure of at least 72 hours**; if **no Member State breaks silence**, the draft is **considered adopted**; if silence **is** broken, the draft is **not adopted under that procedure** — which **is not a rejection**, no vote having been taken and nothing decided on the substance. Adopted for the **exceptional** circumstances then prevailing, and cited as exceptional. **The last reading text in this book is a body writing down how it will decide when it cannot meet** |

**Twelve honesty notes, printed in the units as well as here.**

- **Unit 1.** Consensus in that Agreement is defined by the absence of a formal objection by a Member
  present. It tells you almost nothing about support, and a cable that calls such a decision
  unanimous has reported a different meeting from the one that happened.
- **Unit 2.** Eighty years of decisions have been taken under rules whose own title says
  *provisional*, and much of the Council's working day takes place in consultations that the rules do
  not govern and of which no record exists.
- **Unit 3.** One rule out of about fifty has never been agreed, and it is the rule on how to decide.
  No provision requires consensus; consensus is what remains in the absence of an agreed voting rule,
  and the item has been deferred at every session for thirty years.
- **Unit 4.** A threshold expressed as a fraction of an undated set produced sixteen years of
  argument between serious legal advisers. It was settled by a decision of the parties, not by
  insight, and the decision could have gone the other way.
- **Unit 5.** A scientific recommendation stands, unamended, and the substance is not listed, because
  listing requires consensus. Non-listing establishes nothing about the substance, and the same
  consensus rule means nothing is ever added over the objection of a party that would have to apply
  it.
- **Unit 6.** Twenty-nine of about fifty-seven parties approve the measures. The right to decide
  follows scientific activity rather than accession, and the Treaty's article on claims resolves the
  central question of the region by declining to resolve it.
- **Unit 7.** Ninety enactments based on one model are ninety statutes. *Based on* covers a faithful
  copy and a distant relative, and it does not tell you which version of the model was used.
- **Unit 8.** A withdrawal operates forwards. It discharges nothing that accrued, ends no proceeding
  already commenced, and takes effect one year after receipt — not in the year of the announcement,
  which is the year everybody quotes.
- **Unit 9.** Two states can both be parties to the same convention and have no treaty relationship
  with each other. No party list can show that, and the word *so* between two status facts is the
  most expensive word in this subject.
- **Unit 10.** This is the one place in these five books where a superlative is literally true, and
  the way it goes wrong is always the same: the universality of the parent instrument is extended to
  the amendments that carry the obligations.
- **Unit 11.** A blank in a compliance table can mean *submitted and not published* or *not
  submitted*, and only the second is an absence. A compliance rate requires a choice of categories
  that belongs to the Conference of States Parties and not to a desk officer with a spreadsheet.
- **Unit 12.** A broken silence is not a rejection. And a body that cannot meet discovers how much
  the room had been doing for it: presence, timing, the moment to speak, and what counts as having
  spoken all had to be replaced by three paragraphs in a week.

# Closing

You began this book able to report a decision. You end it able to say what counted as agreement:
nobody present objecting; rules applied and never adopted; a rule never agreed at all; a denominator
argued for sixteen years; a listing that one objection prevented; two classes of party; a text nobody
is obliged to follow; a year's notice that erases nothing; a relationship that exists unless either
side says otherwise; a date with a condition attached; a report you may not read; and three days of
silence.

Twelve mechanisms, and one habit: name the rule, and write down the basis.

Four sentences to take with you.

From Unit 1: **no Member present at the meeting formally objected.**

From Unit 3: **in the absence of an agreed voting rule, decisions are taken by consensus.**

From Unit 11: **three figures, and no total.**

And from Unit 12, which is where this book ends: **everything you will be asked in twenty years is a
question about what was decided and on what basis — write the basis down every time, and the twenty
years take care of themselves.**
