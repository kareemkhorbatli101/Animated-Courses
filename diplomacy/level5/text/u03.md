# Unit 3 · The Rule That Was Never Adopted
%%SUB Thirty years of conferences under draft rules, with one rule excepted%%
%%GOALS
Use exceptive structures to carve one thing out of a general statement
Report an agenda item that has been deferred at every session
Explain what follows when there is no agreed voting rule

A conference of nearly two hundred parties has met every year since 1995. At its first session it
could not adopt its rules of procedure, so it decided to **apply the draft rules** — **with the
exception of one rule**, the rule on voting. That rule has been on the agenda of every session since
and has never been agreed. Everything the conference has decided in thirty years has therefore been
decided without an agreed rule on how to decide.

> | | |
> |---|---|

>
> **The rule that was excepted.** Draft rule 42 appears in the draft with its text **in square
> brackets**, and in more than one version: broadly, one alternative would allow decisions on
> substantive matters to be taken by a **majority** where consensus cannot be reached, and another
> would require **consensus**. **The disagreement is not about drafting. It is about whether a
> majority of parties can bind a minority**, which is the central question of the entire regime and
> was never going to be settled by a rules committee.
>
> **What follows.** Three consequences, and all three matter to a delegation.
>
> **First, decisions are taken by consensus in practice.** Not because a rule says so, but because
> there is no agreed rule under which a vote could be taken. **The default is an absence, not a
> provision.**
>
> **Second, a single party's objection has great weight.** With no voting rule, there is no route
> around an objection except persuasion, the chair's management of the room, and time.
>
> **Third, the arrangement is not an oversight.** It has been examined at every session for thirty
> years by delegations who understand it better than any commentator, and it has survived every
> examination. **A thing that has been reconsidered thirty times and left alone is a decision, even
> if no decision text records it.**
>

### 1 · Exceptive structures

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP5-03-06%%

> **Layla:** Your paragraph says *the Conference's rules of procedure provide*. There are no rules of
> procedure.
> **Omar:** There is a fifty-page document called the rules of procedure.
> **Layla:** There is a fifty-page document called the **draft** rules of procedure, which the
> Conference decided in 1995 to **apply**, **with the exception of** draft rule 42. Three facts, one
> sentence, and the exception is the sentence's whole point.
> **Omar:** Could I write *the draft rules apply, apart from rule 42*?
> **Layla:** In conversation. In a note, use the formal exceptive: **with the exception of**, or
> **save for** if you want it shorter. *Apart from* reads as an afterthought, and this exception is
> not an afterthought — it is the only part of the document anybody has ever fought about.
> **Omar:** And what fills the gap?
> **Layla:** Nothing fills it, and your sentence must say so without sounding dramatic. **Save for
> draft rule 42, on voting, which has not been agreed, the draft rules are applied.** Then: **in the
> absence of an agreed voting rule, decisions are taken by consensus.** The second sentence is the
> one a reader will quote.
> **Omar:** May I say *notwithstanding* anywhere?
> **Layla:** Not here. *Notwithstanding* overrides a rule that exists. There is no rule to override,
> and an exceptive is not an override.

*What distinction does Layla draw between* with the exception of *and* notwithstanding?

**1B  Underline the four exceptive structures in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Form | Example | The mistake it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| **with the exception of** | applied **with the exception of** rule 42 | reporting the exception as an aside |
| **save for** | **save for** rule 42, the draft rules apply | length, with no loss of formality |
| **other than** | all matters **other than** voting | listing everything that is included |
| **except as otherwise provided** | **except as otherwise provided**, decisions require consensus | forgetting the carve-out exists |
| **in the absence of** | **in the absence of** an agreed voting rule | implying a rule provides for consensus |
| **notwithstanding** | **notwithstanding** rule 16, the Chair proceeded | using it where there is no rule to override |

> **Grammar Note** — An exceptive placed at the front of a sentence (*Save for rule 42, …*) tells the
> reader that something is being carved out before they read the general statement. Placed at the
> end, it reads as a qualification remembered late. In a note where the exception is the point, put
> it first.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Officers coming to this subject for the first time almost always want the rule to be agreed. It
offends a tidy mind that a conference of nearly two hundred parties has met annually for three
decades under rules it never adopted, with the central question of how it decides left in square
brackets. But consider what agreeing the rule would involve. Every party would have to say, in
advance and in the abstract, whether it is prepared to be outvoted on a question it cannot foresee,
by a majority it cannot predict, on a subject that touches its economy. No ministry on earth would
sign that blank check, and the delegations that keep the item open are not being obstructive: they
are declining to answer a question that cannot be answered in advance. The cost is real — an
objection by one party carries weight it would not carry under a voting rule — and so is the
benefit, which is that everyone is still in the room thirty years later. Whether that trade is right
is one of the genuinely open questions in this field.

**Discuss:** Would you sign a rule letting a majority bind you on a subject you cannot foresee? What
would you want in exchange?

> ### Procedural note · decision-making at the Conference of the Parties
> *Legal Affairs · for the delegation · 9 October*
>
> **1 Status. The Conference applies draft rules of procedure.** They were not adopted. At its first
> session, in **1995**, the Conference was unable to adopt rules of procedure and decided to apply
> the draft rules.
>
> **2 The exception. The draft rules are applied save for draft rule 42, on voting**, which **has
> not been agreed**. The draft text of that rule stands in square brackets, with more than one
> alternative.
>
> **3 The history, in one sentence.** The adoption of the rules of procedure **has remained on the
> agenda of every session since 1995**; informal consultations have been held at each session and
> **no agreement has been reached**.
>
> **4 What follows. In the absence of an agreed voting rule, decisions are taken by consensus.** No
> rule provides for consensus; consensus is what remains where no procedure for deciding otherwise
> has been agreed. **A single party's objection therefore carries weight that it would not carry
> under a voting rule.**
>
> **5 How to answer the question we will be asked.** If a delegation asks whether a matter could be
> put to the vote, the accurate answer is that **there is no agreed procedure under which a vote
> would be taken, and that the question has been open since 1995.** The answer is **not** that
> voting is prohibited.
>
> **6 What this note does not do.** It does not state the position of any other party on draft rule
> 42. Those positions are on the record of the sessions and are for the delegations concerned to
> state.

> **Writer's Tip** — Paragraph 5 is the paragraph with a use. A procedural note that anticipates the
> one question the delegation will actually be asked, and supplies the sentence, is worth more than
> four pages of accurate background.
