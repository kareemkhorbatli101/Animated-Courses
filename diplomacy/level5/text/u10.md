# Unit 10 · The Twentieth Instrument
%%SUB A date with a condition attached, under a protocol everybody has joined%%
%%GOALS
Use ordinal thresholds and conditional dates accurately
Distinguish a protocol's membership from its amendments'
Report universality without extending it
%%

One protocol has been ratified by every state in the world and then some. Five amendments have been
made to it, each with its own membership, and the memberships differ. The most recent was adopted in
2016 and provided that it would enter into force on a **named date**, the first of January 2019,
**provided that at least twenty instruments had been deposited by then** — a fixed date with a
condition attached, which is a third kind of entry-into-force clause and the cleanest of the three.

### 1 · Ordinal thresholds and conditional dates

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP5-10-06%%

> **Layla:** Your paragraph says the Amendment entered into force when twenty states had ratified it.
> **Omar:** Twenty was the threshold.
> **Layla:** Twenty was the condition, and the clause named a date. Read it as it is built: **the
> Amendment shall enter into force on 1 January 2019, provided that at least twenty instruments have
> been deposited.** The date is the operative part and the threshold is the proviso.
> **Omar:** Does the order matter?
> **Layla:** It changes what you can say. If the clause had said *upon the deposit of the twentieth
> instrument*, the twentieth deposit would have been the event, and you would report the date of that
> deposit. As drafted, the event is the first of January and the twentieth deposit is a condition
> precedent that happened to be satisfied. **You report the named date, and the condition in a
> subordinate clause.**
> **Omar:** And if the condition had failed?
> **Layla:** Then the fallback: **on the ninetieth day following the date on which it was
> fulfilled.** Three mechanisms in one clause, and a reader needs all three only if the second
> happened — but you must know which one applied, because the date you print depends on it.
> **Omar:** My draft also says the Amendment entered into force for all parties on that date.
> **Layla:** For the parties that had ratified by then. **Every other party is bound from its own
> date**, and in a note about a particular state that is the only date that matters. *Entered into
> force* is a fact about an instrument. *Bound from* is a fact about a state, and the two are usually
> different dates.

*What is the difference between the clause as drafted and a clause saying* upon the deposit of the
twentieth instrument?

**1B  Underline the three dating mechanisms in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| **on [date], provided that [condition]** | the date is operative; the condition is a proviso |
| **upon the deposit of the [ordinal] instrument** | the deposit is the event; its date is the date |
| **in the event that … on the ninetieth day following** | the fallback, when the condition fails |
| **entered into force on** | a fact about the instrument |
| **bound from** | a fact about one state |
| **for each Party, from the date of its own deposit** | the general rule for latecomers |

> **Grammar Note** — Report the clause in the order the clause has, and put the condition in a
> subordinate clause. A reader who sees *when twenty states had ratified* will look for the date of
> the twentieth ratification and will not find it in your note, because it is not the date you used.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

This is the one place in these five books where a superlative is literally true, and it is worth
pausing on. Every state in the world is a party to one instrument. Nothing else in this series comes
close: the counts have been 38, 34, 162, 128, 178 and 193, each with its own caveat, and here the
number is everybody. The temptation to lean on that fact is enormous, and the way it goes wrong is
always the same — the universality of the parent instrument is quietly extended to the amendments
that carry the actual obligations, which are newer, narrower and still being ratified. The
professional habit is to say the universal thing with pride and fence it in the very next sentence.
There is no virtue in understating a genuine achievement of international law, and there is no safety
in letting a true sentence do the work of a false one. Both halves belong in the same paragraph.

**Discuss:** Why do you think this instrument achieved what no other has? What made it different?

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Briefing · ozone instruments: memberships and obligations
> *Legal Affairs with Environment · for the Director · 11 April*
>
> **1 The Protocol.** The **Montreal Protocol**, adopted **16 September 1987** and in force from **1
> January 1989**, has **198 parties** on the secretariat's page **as at 9 April**. **That is universal
> ratification: every United Nations member State is a party**, together with further entities.
>
> **2 The amendments have their own memberships.** The Protocol has been amended on **five**
> occasions. **Each amendment is a separate instrument with its own list of parties**, and the
> figures differ; **the most recent amendment has the lowest**. The figure for any amendment is on the
> same page, **with the date of that page**.
>
> **3 The most recent amendment and its date.** The **Kigali Amendment** was adopted **15 October
> 2016** and **entered into force on 1 January 2019**, the clause having fixed that date **provided
> that at least twenty instruments had been deposited** — a condition that was met. **Had it not been
> met**, entry into force would have fallen on the ninetieth day after it was.
>
> **4 Dates for individual parties. 1 January 2019 is the date the Amendment entered into force for
> the States that had ratified it by then.** Any State ratifying afterwards is **bound from its own
> date.**
>
> **5 The question you asked. Astrand is a party to the Protocol.** On the secretariat's page **as at
> 9 April**, **Astrand is also a party to the 2016 Amendment**, with effect from its own date of
> deposit. **The obligations you asked about arise under the Amendment and not under the Protocol
> alone.**
>
> *All figures carry the date of the secretariat's page and should be re-checked before external use.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Paragraph 5 is the briefing. Paragraphs 1 to 4 are the apparatus that makes
> paragraph 5 defensible, and a director who is short of time will read 5 and 1 and nothing else.
> Write 5 first and then build the apparatus behind it.
