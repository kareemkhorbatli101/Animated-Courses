# Unit 7 · A Model Law Is Not a Treaty
%%SUB Ninety states, no parties, and the words *based on* doing a great deal of work%%
%%GOALS

Some of the most widely used texts in international law are not treaties. A model law is a text that
a body recommends to states for enactment in their own legislation. It has no parties, no entry into
force and no depositary. Nothing obliges any state to enact it, every state that does so enacts its
own version, and the published list of states says their legislation is *based on* it — three words
that cover everything from a faithful copy to a distant relative.

### 1 · Degrees of conformity

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP5-07-06%%

> **Layla:** Your note says Astrand's arbitration law *is* the Model Law.
> **Omar:** The list says their legislation is based on it.
> **Layla:** Then write what the list says and nothing more. **Astrand has enacted legislation based
> on the Model Law.** If you have read the statute and it follows the model closely, you may write
> **closely modeled on**. If you have compared it clause by clause and it is the same text, you may
> write **in terms identical to**. You have done none of those things, so you write what the list
> says.
> **Omar:** Is the difference practical?
> **Layla:** It decides cases. Suppose the question is whether an interim measure ordered by a
> tribunal is enforceable in Astrand. That turns on provisions introduced by the 2006 amendments. If
> Astrand enacted the 1985 text and never revised it, its law may contain nothing on the point, and
> the list will still say *based on the Model Law*.
> **Omar:** So I should say which version.
> **Layla:** Where it matters, yes, and you say how you know: **the enacted text, as published by
> Astrand, reproduces the 2006 provisions on interim measures**. Or, if you have not looked:
> **whether the enacted text reproduces the 2006 amendments has not been established.**
> **Omar:** That second sentence is an admission.
> **Layla:** It is a statement of what you know, which is the only thing a legal note is for. An
> admission that costs nothing today is cheaper than an assumption that costs a case in a year.

*Which three phrases does Layla say Omar may use, and on what condition each?*

**1B  Underline the four conformity phrases in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Form | When you may use it |
|---|---|
| **based on** | when a published list says so and you have not read the text |
| **closely modeled on** | when you have read the text and it follows the model closely |
| **in terms identical to** | when you have compared it and it is the same text |
| **in substance, with modifications** | when you have read it and it differs in detail |
| **departing from X in [respect]** | when you can name the difference |
| **it has not been established whether** | when you have not looked, and the point matters |

> **Grammar Note** — Every phrase on this scale is a claim about what *you* have done, not only about
> the texts. Choose the one your own work supports. This is the single most useful habit in legal
> drafting and it takes a career to acquire.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

The model law is one of the quiet successes of international legal work, and it succeeds for reasons
that look like weaknesses. Because nothing obliges a state to enact it, no state has to negotiate
over whether to be bound, which removes the whole apparatus of reservations, thresholds and entry
into force that occupies the rest of this book. Because every state enacts its own version, the text
spreads into places a convention could never reach, including states that would never ratify
anything in the field. What is lost is uniformity, and the loss is real: ninety statutes based on one
model are ninety statutes, and the practitioner who assumes otherwise will be surprised in a
hearing. The professional attitude is neither to oversell the harmonization nor to dismiss it.
Something genuinely did spread to ninety states in forty years, which is more than most conventions
achieve, and the three words that made it possible are *based on*.

**Discuss:** Would you rather have forty states bound by one text or ninety states with laws based on
it? What does the answer depend on?

> ### Advice note · foreign arbitration legislation and interim measures
> *Legal Affairs · for the Ministry of Justice · 18 April*
>
> **1 What the instrument is.** The **UNCITRAL Model Law on International Commercial Arbitration**
> is a **legislative text recommended to States** for enactment in their own law. **It has no
> parties, no entry into force and no depositary.** The adopting body's recommendation is the whole
> of its legal force.
>
> **2 What the published list says.** The adopting body publishes a list of jurisdictions that have
> **enacted legislation based on** the Model Law — on the order of **ninety States**, and a larger
> number of jurisdictions, **as at the date of the page**. **Astrand appears on that list.**
>
> **3 Which version matters here.** The Model Law was **adopted in 1985** and **amended in 2006**,
> the amendments including the provisions on **interim measures** and on the **form of the
> arbitration agreement**. **Your question turns on the 2006 provisions.**
>
> **4 What has not been established. Whether Astrand's enacted legislation reproduces the 2006
> provisions has not been established.** The published list does not record which version an
> enactment was based on, nor what was modified in enactment. **The list is accurate; it does not
> answer your question.**
>
> **5 What we propose. We will obtain the enacted text of Astrand's legislation, compare it with the
> Model Law as amended, and advise within two working days** of receiving a translation. We do not
> recommend advising on the basis of the list alone.
>
> *Nothing in this note states the content of Astrand's law. Figures for the list carry the date of
> the page.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Paragraph 4 is the paragraph that builds a reputation. A note that says, in
> terms, *this source is accurate and does not answer your question* is worth more to the reader than
> any amount of correct background, and it is the sentence most officers are afraid to write.

### 3 · Write

Write **150 to 180 words**: an advice note on a question where the available published source does
not answer it.

**Say what the instrument or source is, in its own terms. Say what the published source does record.
Name the detail the question actually turns on. Say what has not been established, and say that the
source is accurate and does not answer the question. End with what you propose to do, and by when.**

**Read your note aloud to your partner first.** Any sentence that sounds like an answer but rests on
the list is the sentence to move into paragraph 4. Then check your own against the list, and swap.
