# Unit 1 · No Member Present Objects
%%SUB Consensus, defined in a footnote — and why silence is the operative fact%%
%%GOALS
Use *deemed*, *taken to be* and *presumed* accurately
Report a decision taken without a vote
Distinguish consensus from unanimity and from agreement
%%

Most people believe that consensus means everybody agreed. In the organization this unit is about,
consensus is defined in the agreement itself, and the definition does not mention agreement at all.
It mentions objection: a body is deemed to have decided by consensus if **no Member present at the
meeting formally objects**. That is a different thing, and the difference is the subject of this
unit and of a good deal of the rest of your career.

### 2 · Read

> **Reader's Tip** — When an agreement defines one of its own words, read the definition before the
> article that uses it. A defined term is a machine part, not an English word, and the definition is
> often in smaller type than everything it governs.

%%FIG k01-f5 | A one-page internal note: a heading, a boxed quotation of a provision with its footnote printed beneath in smaller type, a short table distinguishing three words, a paragraph headed what a cable must say, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

> ## Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Expatriates · delegation note 1
> *Economic Affairs and Legal Affairs · decision-making by consensus · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The provision, in the Agreement's own words.** The Marrakesh Agreement Establishing the World
> Trade Organization provides, in **Article IX:1**, that the organization **shall continue the
> practice of decision-making by consensus** followed under the earlier arrangements, and that where
> a decision cannot be arrived at by consensus, the matter at issue shall be decided by voting,
> except as otherwise provided.
>
> **The definition is in a footnote to that paragraph**, and it is the most consequential footnote in
> the trading system:
>
> > The body concerned **shall be deemed to have decided by consensus** on a matter submitted for
> > its consideration, **if no Member, present at the meeting when the decision is taken, formally
> > objects** to the proposed decision.
>
> **Three words that are not synonyms.**
>
> | | What it requires | What it tells you about support |
> |---|---|---|
> | **unanimity** | every Member positively agreeing | a great deal |
> | **consensus**, as defined | no Member present formally objecting | **almost nothing** |
> | **agreement** | whatever the parties say it means | nothing until defined |
>
> **What follows from the definition.** Three things, and all three are counter-intuitive.
>
> **First, absence is not objection.** A Member not in the room when the decision is taken cannot
> formally object, and the decision is taken by consensus whether that Member would have objected or
> not.
>
> **Second, unhappiness is not objection.** A delegation may state that it is disappointed, that it
> accepts the text with difficulty, or that it reserves its position, and none of those is a formal
> objection. **The decision is taken.**
>
> **Third, one delegation is enough.** A single Member present and formally objecting prevents
> consensus. Where the matter can be put to a vote it may be, but in practice much of this system
> runs on the fact that it usually is not.
>
> **The membership, for the record. 166 Members**, on the organization's own page. That figure
> carries the date of the page it was taken from and should be re-checked on the day it is needed;
> the most recent two accessions took effect in **August 2024**.
>
> **What a cable must say.** *No Member present at the meeting formally objected, and the decision
> was accordingly taken by consensus.* Then, separately, what delegations said before and after. **A
> cable that says *unanimously* has reported a different meeting from the one that happened.**
>
> *Source: the Marrakesh Agreement Establishing the World Trade Organization, Article IX:1 and its
> footnote defining consensus; and the organization's own published membership figure of 166
> Members, the most recent two accessions having taken effect in August 2024. Membership figures
> carry the date of the page.*

### 1 · *Deemed* and *taken to be*

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP5-01-06%%

> **Nadia:** Your cable says the decision was unanimous. Were you in the room?
> **Omar:** I was. Nobody spoke against it.
> **Nadia:** Then write what the footnote says. **The body is deemed to have decided by consensus if
> no Member present formally objects.** *Deemed* is doing real work there: it tells you the rule is
> treating silence as a decision, and that it is a rule and not a fact.
> **Omar:** Is *deemed* not rather heavy for a cable?
> **Nadia:** It is heavy, and it is exact, and a cable that reports a procedural fact has to be
> exact before it is readable. You may also write *was accordingly taken by consensus*, which is
> lighter and carries the same claim.
> **Omar:** And the four delegations that were unhappy?
> **Nadia:** A separate sentence. What they said, when they said it, and whether any of it was on the
> record. **None of it is deemed to be anything.** It is reported, as speech, with attribution.
> **Omar:** And the delegation that was absent?
> **Nadia:** Also a separate sentence, and the most useful one in your cable, because somebody in
> this building will ask about that delegation on Thursday.

*What does Nadia say the word* deemed *tells a reader?*

**1B  Underline the three deeming structures in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Form | Example | The mistake it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| **is deemed to have** | The body **is deemed to have** decided by consensus. | reporting a rule as a fact |
| **is taken to be** | Silence **is taken to be** acceptance. | the same, in plainer register |
| **was accordingly taken by** | The decision **was accordingly taken by** consensus. | heaviness, with no loss of accuracy |
| **is presumed to**, **unless** | A notification **is presumed to** have arrived, **unless** the contrary is shown. | treating a presumption as conclusive |
| **does not amount to** | Disappointment **does not amount to** a formal objection. | treating unhappiness as objection |

> **Grammar Note** — *Deemed* always carries a hidden *although it may not be so*. That is its whole
> function. If you use it where there is no gap between the rule and the facts, you are being
> pompous; if you leave it out where there is a gap, you are being inaccurate, which is worse.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Consensus of this kind is often described as a weakness of international organizations, and the
description is usually made by people who have never had to produce a decision that two hundred
ministries will implement. The function of a silence rule is not to discover agreement. It is to
produce a decision that nobody can later claim was taken over their objection, because the rule gave
them a moment in which to object and they did not use it. That is a lower standard than agreement
and a far higher one than majority: it means every decision in the system carries, by construction,
the fact that no one present was prepared to say no on the record. What it costs is speed, and what
it buys is durability. The officers who understand this stop describing consensus as weak and start
noticing which delegations are reliably in the room at a quarter to seven in the evening — which is
the real skill, and which cannot be taught in a book.

**Discuss:** Is a decision nobody blocked better or worse than a decision most people voted for?
Which would you rather implement?
