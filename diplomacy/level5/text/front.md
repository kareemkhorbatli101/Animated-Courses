# Foreign Service English 4
%%SUB English for Foreign Ministry Staff · Syrian Edition · CEFR B2%%

## How to use this book

This is the fifth of six books. Book Foundation was the building, Book 1 the consular section, Book 2
the desk officer's chair and Book 3 protocol and the mission. This one is **negotiating and
reporting**: the room in which a text is agreed, the rule under which it is agreed, the silence that
counts as agreement, and the report that says what happened.

**Twelve units**, sixteen pages each, with the same six lessons in the same order every time.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Sixteen words, a second set of six, and a team research task |
| **2 · Reading** | A real instrument, rule of procedure or published record, with its dates and its caveats |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures of negotiation and record, with the mistake each one causes |
| **4 · At Work** | Clear English, a long dialogue, a culture check and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A model, then your own |
| **Team Up & Review** | A picture, a team project, a survey, and one problem with no easy answer |

**The audio.** 🔊 marks a track. 195 tracks, numbered `DIP5-unit-track`.

## What is different about this book

**One subject runs through all twelve units: how a decision is actually taken.** Not the policy —
the procedure. Who has to agree, what counts as agreement, what happens when nobody objects, what
happens when one delegation does, and how the thing that happened is written down afterwards by
somebody who was in the room for eleven minutes of it.

**Twelve procedures, and what each one really requires.** A trade organization in which a decision
is taken by consensus, defined as nobody present formally objecting. A Security Council that has
operated under **provisional** rules of procedure since 1946. A climate conference that has applied
its draft rules for thirty years with one rule excepted, because that rule has never been agreed. An
amendment whose entry into force turned on which three-quarters was meant. A chemical that a review
committee recommended for listing and that is still not listed, because listing requires consensus.
A treaty with two classes of party, only one of which decides. A model law that is not a treaty and
that no two states have enacted identically. A withdrawal that takes effect in a year and does not
erase what came before it. A convention that takes effect between two parties only if neither of them
says otherwise. An amendment that entered into force on the twentieth instrument, under a protocol
with a hundred and ninety-eight parties. A reporting obligation whose reports may be withheld from
publication. And a procedure in which a decision is adopted because nobody spoke.

**The grammar is the grammar of the record.** *Deemed* and *taken to be*; the perfect passive;
exceptive and concessive structures; presenting two readings of one clause; result and purpose
clauses; apposition; degrees of conformity; temporal precision; negative conditions; ordinal
thresholds; reporting an absence; and, at the end, the report of a meeting.

**Four sentences you will write until they are automatic.** *No delegation present formally
objected.* *The rules remain provisional.* *The figure is on the publisher's page and carries the
date of that page.* And *the Convention takes effect between two Contracting States only if neither
has notified otherwise* — the sentence that catches the largest number of otherwise careful drafts.

## The people

The same ministry, now seen from the conference room. **Omar Sabbagh** has been attached to a
delegation for the first time and has discovered that most of a negotiation is waiting. **Nadia
Shaheen** leads the delegation and marks his drafts in pencil, as she has since Book 2. **Layla
Bahri** in Legal Affairs writes the rules-of-procedure notes that nobody reads until the second week.
**Hala Mounzer** is in Protocol and appears here twice, both times about seating. **Fadi Antoun** in
the Treaty Section answers the telephone when a threshold has to be counted. **Samira Barakat**,
**Hana Darwish**, **Ahmad Rifai** and **Yusuf Mansour** are where they were.

New in this book: **Rania Haddad**, the ministry's conference officer, who has sat behind more
delegations than anybody in the building and has never once spoken in a meeting.

Outside the building: **Karin Lindholm** and **Oskar Rehn** of Astrand, **Lucia Moretti** of Perenne,
**Linh Tran** of Marovia, **Ida Pedersen**, and **Marco Caruso**, who in Unit 11 asks the question
this whole book is about.

Every one of them is invented. Astrand, Perenne, Marovia, Valmora, Kesteland and the Sable Isles are
invented countries, and they are invented on purpose: in a role-play about consensus, objection or
withdrawal, nobody should have to put a real government's name to a position it has never taken.

## Nothing here is official

This is an independent teaching draft. It is not an official publication of any ministry and it
carries no official status. Every person, office, meeting, delegation, report and note in it was
written for this book. The instruments, rules of procedure and published records quoted in the
reading texts are real, and each text gives the instrument, its date, the figure, the page the figure
came from and the date of that page — because in this field a number without a page and a date is
not information.
