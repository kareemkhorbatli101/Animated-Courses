# Unit 6 · Twenty-Nine of Fifty-Seven
%%SUB Two classes of party, and a clause that settles nothing on purpose%%
%%GOALS

A treaty of 1959 has about fifty-seven parties. Twenty-nine of them take the decisions. The other
twenty-eight are bound by the treaty, attend the meetings, and do not approve the measures. This is
not an irregularity: it is in the treaty, in the article that says who meets. And the same treaty
contains a clause whose purpose is to leave the largest question in the region exactly where it was.

> | | What it may do |

> that wishes to join the deciding group does so by conducting substantial scientific research
> activity in Antarctica — in practice by operating a station or mounting an expedition and
> publishing the work. This is unusual and deliberate: in most bodies the right to decide follows
> accession, and here it follows activity.
>
> **The other clause, the one that settles nothing.** The Treaty provides that nothing in it shall be
> interpreted as **a renunciation** by any party of previously asserted rights or claims to
> territorial sovereignty, nor as **prejudicing the position** of any party as regards its

> > up.

### 1 · Apposition

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD DIP5-06-06%%

> **Layla:** Your sentence says *the parties approved the measure*. Which parties?
> **Omar:** The ones at the meeting.
> **Layla:** Then say so inside the sentence. **The measure was approved by the consultative parties,
> that is, the parties entitled to participate in the meeting.** The definition sits beside the term
> and the reader never has to go anywhere.
> **Omar:** Could I define it in the previous sentence instead?
> **Layla:** You could, and your reader will have forgotten by the time they reach the term, because
> they are reading at speed with four other files open. Apposition puts the definition where it is
> needed. It is the cheapest clarity in formal English: two commas and six words.
> **Omar:** Is *that is* the only way in?
> **Layla:** No. You can use a bare appositive — **the consultative parties, the twenty-nine entitled
> to approve measures, met in May** — or *namely*, or a dash if the sentence is already busy. What
> you cannot do is put the appositive in brackets, which tells the reader it is optional, when it is
> the only reason the sentence is accurate.
> **Omar:** And when everybody in the room knows the term?
> **Layla:** Define it anyway, once, in the first sentence that uses it. The person who does not know
> the term is never in the room; they are reading the file in two years, and they are the reason you
> are writing.

*What does Layla say is wrong with putting an appositive in brackets?*

**1B  Underline the three appositives in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| **X, that is, Y** | the consultative parties, **that is,** the parties entitled to participate |
| **X, namely Y** | two classes, **namely** consultative and non-consultative parties |
| bare appositive | the consultative parties, **the twenty-nine entitled to approve measures**, met in May |
| **X — Y —** | the twelve original signatories **— the parties named in the preamble —** |
| **X, which is to say Y** | approval by all of them, **which is to say** unanimity among that group |
| **or** (naming) | substantial scientific research activity, **or** a station and published work |

> **Grammar Note** — An appositive is punctuated with commas when it is extra information and with
> commas just the same when it is essential, which is why you must get the content right: the
> punctuation will not help your reader. If the sentence is false without the appositive, make the
> appositive short and put it immediately after the term.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Two things in this treaty offend a modern instinct and have both worked for sixty-five years. The
first is that the right to decide follows activity rather than membership: a state that operates a
station and publishes science decides, and a state that signs and stays home does not. Stated
plainly it sounds like a club, and it is a club; stated more carefully, it means that the states
carrying the cost and the risk of being present are the states that make the rules for being present,
and nobody has yet proposed a better rule that the present states would accept. The second is the
claims article, which resolves the central legal question of the continent by declining to resolve
it. Both arrangements are compromises that a drafter would be embarrassed to defend in the abstract,
and both have produced an outcome — a demilitarized continent given over to science, with its
sovereignty disputes neither settled nor pursued — that almost nobody in 1959 expected to last a
decade. Officers who work on this treaty tend to become suspicious of elegance.

**Discuss:** Should the right to decide follow membership or activity? What would you accept if you
were outside the deciding group?

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Brief · Antarctic Treaty: our position and who decides
> *Legal Affairs · for the Director · 7 June*
>
> **1 The figures.** The Treaty, done at Washington on **1 December 1959** and in force since **23
> June 1961**, has about **57 parties** on the Secretariat's published list. **29** of them are
> **consultative parties**.
>
> **2 The two classes.** A **consultative party** — **that is, a party entitled to participate in the
> consultative meetings** — may attend, speak and **approve measures**; a measure becomes effective
> when approved by **all** the parties entitled to participate. **Any other party is bound by the
> Treaty and does not approve measures.**
>
> **3 Our own position. This Ministry's state is a party and is not a consultative party.** Our
> approval of the measure adopted in May was **not sought and was not required**. We are nonetheless
> bound by it, as every party is.
>
> **4 How that could change.** A party may become a consultative party **by demonstrating its
> interest in Antarctica through conducting substantial scientific research activity there** — in
> practice by operating a station or mounting an expedition and publishing the work. **The cost and
> the policy of doing so are matters for the competent ministry**, not for this note.
>
> **5 On territorial claims, if asked.** The Treaty **neither recognizes nor denies** any claim.
> Nothing in it is to be interpreted as a **renunciation** of previously asserted claims or as
> prejudicing any party's position on recognition, and **no new claim may be asserted while it is in
> force.**
>
> *The figures in paragraph 1 carry the date of the Secretariat's page and should be re-checked before
> external use.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Paragraph 3 is the one to write first and the one most briefs omit. A note that
> says what the published figures mean *for us* turns a page of background into advice.
