# Starting Point · What a Procedure Is For
%%SUB Four structures you have, and the three questions before any decision is reported%%
%%GOALS
Review the structures of Books Foundation to 3
Say what a procedure protects
Ask the three questions before reporting a decision
%%

A procedure is a machine for producing a decision that nobody can reopen. That is its whole purpose,
and it is why procedure is the part of this work that looks least important and takes the longest to
learn. This book is about the machines: consensus, provisional rules, thresholds, notifications,
reports and silence.

### 1 · What you already have

%%FIG k00-f1 | A single review sheet with six boxes. Each box has a heading — attribution, hedging, inversion, participle clauses, impersonal structures and qualifying markers — and one short example sentence from a delegation's working day.%%

**1A  Say one sentence from each box to your partner.** Then say which box your own drafts use least.

### 2 · What a procedure protects

%%FIG k00-f2 | Three numbered boxes in a column. Each has a procedure named in bold, a line reading what it produces, and a line reading what it prevents, with an arrow at the foot pointing to a box reading a decision nobody can reopen.%%

| | What it produces | What it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| **A rule on who must agree** | a decision with a known basis | an argument afterwards about whether it was taken |
| **A rule on how objection is made** | a record of who objected, and when | a delegation discovering in March that it agreed in January |
| **A rule on how the result is written** | one text everybody can quote | six accounts of the same meeting |

**2A  Listen.** %%AUD DIP5-00-01%%

**2B  Which of the three would you miss first if it were taken away?** *Talk with a partner, out
loud.*

### 3 · Four sentences a delegation officer writes every week

%%FIG k00-f3 | A card taped inside a folder, printed with four sentences in bold, each with one line of smaller type under it explaining when to use it.%%

> **1  No delegation present formally objected.**
> *When the chair's gavel has fallen and you are writing the cable.*
>
> **2  The rules remain provisional.**
> *Before anybody cites a rule number as settled law.*
>
> **3  The figure is on the publisher's page and carries the date of that page.**
> *Every time a number leaves the building.*
>
> **4  Being a party is not enough: the Convention takes effect between two States only if neither
> has notified otherwise.**
> *The sentence that catches the largest number of otherwise careful drafts.*

**3A  Listen and repeat.** %%AUD DIP5-00-02%%

**3B  Cover the explanations.** Your partner reads a situation; you say which sentence fits. Take
turns.

### 4 · The three questions before any decision is reported

%%FIG k00-f4 | Three numbered boxes in a column, each with a question in bold and two short lines under it, and an arrow at the foot pointing to a fourth box reading only now may you report it.%%

> **1  Under what rule was it taken?**
> Name the rule and whether that rule is in force or applied provisionally.
>
> **2  What counted as agreement?**
> Consensus, a majority, a notification, or nobody speaking.
>
> **3  What is the record, and who keeps it?**
> A decision text, a report, a summary record, or a depositary notification.

**4A  Listen.** %%AUD DIP5-00-03%%

**4B  Think about it.** *Talk with a partner.*

1. Which of the three questions is hardest to answer in a hurry?
2. Which one does a cable most often leave out?
3. What would you add to the three?
