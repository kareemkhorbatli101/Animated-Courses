## 2. Two timed papers and a speed tracker

**Reading paper** — Academic, three passages, 40 questions, 60 minutes, 2,618
words, with its own key and all forty explanations. The passages are the
standardisation of the shipping container, how a lost script is deciphered,
and what the replication crisis actually showed. Built to the arithmetic unit
50 teaches: 7½ minutes orienting and loading, 15 minutes of text at 170–185
wpm, **37 minutes for 40 questions — about 55 seconds each**, not the 90 you
get by dividing 60 by 40.

Note what has happened across the three volumes you now have. The texts got
longer and denser (2,196 → 2,400 → 2,618 words) and the time available per
question went *up* (about 50 → 40 → 55 seconds), because the reading speed the
book trains rose faster than the text did. That is the speed ladder paying
out, and the unit 50 technique page says so to the student explicitly.

**Listening paper** — four parts, 40 questions, 1,520 words of script, each
part written to the volume's own band, with its own key and forty
explanations. The rules page carries the Volume 5 addition: at this level a
speaker corrects themselves mid-sentence and the answer is the second figure.
Part 1 of the paper does exactly that with a room capacity.

**Reading-speed tracker** — a page of method, a table the student fills in for
units 41–50, and a chart with this volume's **170–185 wpm** band shaded.

Defects 3, 5 and 6 were invisible to every gate that reads the Python, and 3
would have printed a chart with a missing bar next to a passage that discusses
that bar at length. They came out of rendering the file and looking at it,
which is the same argument the two previous reports made and the reason I did
it again.

Defect 8 deserves a line of its own, because it is a gate that was lying.
Volume 5's own result was sound — it was compared against Volumes 3 and 4
correctly — but Volume 4's documented reproduce command had started emitting
spurious failures, and a gate that cries wolf is on its way to being ignored.
It is fixed, and all three volumes now pass their own command cleanly.

### On defect 2, in full

The register gap was real and small, and the way I closed it matters more than
the number. First a mechanical pass over the gated paragraphs only, trading a
handful of 1k–3k forms for higher-register equivalents. I inspected every rule
in **every instance it fired**, and dropped the ones that misfired anywhere
rather than patching them — `requires → necessitates` produced "necessitates
anybody to want", `many → numerous` produced "a great numerous people",
`happens → occurs` produced "whatever its absolute level occurs to be". Ten
rules survived that inspection; seven did not. Two bugs in my own tooling
turned up on the way: offsets reported in UTF-8 bytes were being used as
character indices, which silently merged two paragraphs of unit 45, and a
chained rule turned "problem" into "intractability".

The mechanical pass got about two thirds of the way. The rest is nineteen
paragraphs in the nine lowest-scoring passages, rewritten by hand, where no
rule could have done it safely.

Question items, answers, explanations, listening scripts, speaking sets,
writing models, grammar tables and every non-gated text are byte-identical
through all of this; I verified that structurally rather than by eye.

- **The Listening recordings are scripts, not audio.** Nothing in this volume
  produces sound.
- **Speaking and Writing are rated, not marked.** A student working alone gets
  less from those two strands than from Reading and Listening.
- ~~**The syntax window**~~ **Closed on 17 November.** `density.py` now gates
  the two syntactic numbers against a laddered window, and `density.py --ladder`
  checks the eight books together. Volume 5 measures **23.3 words a sentence**
  (window 22.3-24.3), longest sentence **56**, nominalisation **4.9%** — all in
  window. The gate failed six of the eight books when it was written, and about
  275 sentences across the series were split or merged to pass it; the worst
  failure was that the B1 book had the longest sentences in the series and the
  C1 book did not. The full account is in `IELTS_Syntax_Ladder_Report.md`,
  including what the gate still does not prove.
- **One Reading paper and one Listening paper.** No timed Writing or Speaking
  paper at this level.
- **Volumes 3 and 4 would benefit from a rebuild.** Fifteen outline frames in
  those two books are set small to fit a thesis on one line. They are *not*
  clipped — `gate6` passed on both — but the fix in defect 4 would set them at
  full size. The files you already have are correct as they stand; say the
  word and I will rebuild them.
