### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

A technique that produces a single number will beat a better technique that produces a diagram, in
any country and in any industry, because a number survives being put in a board pack and a diagram
needs somebody standing beside it. That is not a failing of the people involved; it is what reports
do to knowledge. A standard can list every technique ever devised, print the limitations beside each
one and decline to recommend — and most organizations will still use the one that fits on a page.
The way through is not to abandon the quick technique: screening sixty-one rows is a real job and
the matrix does it well. It is to accept that a screening tool cannot make a decision, and to buy
one afternoon a quarter in which five risks get looked at by a method that can count what stands
between you and the event.

> **I am asking for 4,000 for a risk this register ranks fourth, and here is why the rank is not the
> argument.**
>
> | Technique | What it gave us |
> |---|---|
> | **matrix** | likelihood **3**, consequence **3**, score **9**, **rank 4 of 61** |
> | **bow tie** | **one barrier** — a weekly telephone call — and **no recovery control** |
> | **scenario** | racking in May &rarr; fit-out stands down **3 weeks** &rarr; handover to **November** &rarr; client's engineer unavailable until **January** |
> | **two options priced** | second supplier **4,000** &middot; doing nothing, exposure near **60,000** |
>
> **The matrix is not wrong.** It ranks by likelihood and consequence and it did that correctly.
> **Whereas** it asks two questions, the bow tie asks a third: **what stands between us and the
> event?** On this risk the answer is one telephone call, and **unlike** the other four risks in our
> top five there is **nothing at all on the consequence side**.
>
> **Why we have used one technique for eleven years.** Because it **fits on a page and produces a
> number**, and a number can be put in a board pack **whereas** a bow tie needs somebody standing
> next to it. **That is an organizational reason, not a technical one**, and it belongs in this note
> rather than in a conversation afterwards.
>
> **What I want.** The **4,000**, and **one afternoon a quarter** to draw a bow tie for the top five.
> **Forty minutes each.**
>
> **What I am not claiming.** The standard **describes** these techniques, prints the **limitations**
> beside each, lists the **data and expertise** each requires, and **declines to recommend any of
> them**. Several need specialists we do not employ. And it says plainly that **risk assessment
> supports a decision and does not make one** — so this note ends with a request and not with a
> conclusion.
>
> *Source: IEC 31010:2019, Risk management &mdash; Risk assessment techniques, second edition,
> replacing ISO/IEC 31010:2009. It describes techniques and their attributes, strengths and
> limitations without prescribing which to use; many require data or expertise that may not be
> available; it is guidance rather than requirements and not intended for certification; and it
> states that risk assessment supports decision-making rather than replacing it.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Say what the weaker technique is **for**. *The matrix cannot count controls,
> and that is not what it is for* protects the colleague who built the register, and the note gets
> read instead of defended against.
