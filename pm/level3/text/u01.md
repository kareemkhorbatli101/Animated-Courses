Earned value needs three numbers and gives you two. The arithmetic is trivial, the conclusion is
usually unwelcome, and the entire thing rests on a fourth number that nobody checks: how the work
was measured.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Earned value is the most portable project language in the world: three numbers and two ratios that
mean the same thing in every country, which is exactly why it travels into rooms where nobody asks
the fourth question. The decimal looks objective because it is arithmetic, and the arithmetic is
flawless; everything uncertain has already happened by the time the division is done. Somebody
decided how to measure physical progress, and in most organizations that somebody is the person
whose work is being measured — not from dishonesty, but because a person who has solved the hard
part of a job is genuinely further through it than a count of meters suggests. The repair is not
suspicion. It is a column on the report saying how each package was measured, and a number somebody
else can count.

**Discuss:** Who at your company could check a progress figure without asking the person who wrote
it?

> **In sentences, because the decimals are useless on their own.** **For every 100 we spend we are
> getting 78 of work.** **For every 100 of work we planned to have finished by now, we have finished
> 79.**
>
> **The forecast.** Budget **divided by** the cost index: **about 3.48 million**. **This assumes
> today's cost index continues for the rest of the project**, which is an assumption and not a
> prediction. For reference, last year's optimism-bias paper, by a completely different route,
> produced **3.46 million**. **I have drawn no conclusion from that and would rather be asked about
> it.**
>
> **How the 1.11 was measured.** **19** of **38** packages by **quantity**; **5** on a
> **fifty-fifty** rule; **14** by a **percentage written in by the person doing the work**. Where
> both existed, **the self-scored figure averaged 12 points above the quantity count**. **This is
> not an accusation.** A person who has solved the difficult eighty percent of a job is genuinely
> at ninety percent of the thinking, and some of those fourteen packages — a written procedure, for
> instance — **cannot be counted in meters at all**.
>
> **What I am changing.** From next month, a **countable proxy** on the six largest self-scored
> packages: pages issued, loops tested, drawings approved. **Not perfect. Countable by somebody who
> is not doing the work.**
>
> **One thing the figures cannot tell you.** The schedule index is in **money**. At completion it
> returns to **1.00** whether a project is early or eleven weeks late. **Weeks will be in Oskar's
> note on Thursday.**
>
> *Source: ISO 21508:2018, Earned value management in project and programme management. It is
> guidance and is not intended for certification: it prescribes no thresholds for action, no method
> for measuring physical progress and no reporting frequency, and its results depend entirely on
> the quality of the performance measurement baseline.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Write the index as a sentence and the forecast with its assumption attached.
> A decimal with no sentence gets nodded at; a forecast with no assumption gets quoted for two
> years.
