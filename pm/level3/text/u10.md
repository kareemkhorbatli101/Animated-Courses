### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

A continuity plan is one of the few documents an organization writes hoping never to use, which is
exactly why it gets reviewed and not tested. A review reads a document and finds it well written; an
exercise asks the document to be true, and it only takes six people, one table, two hours and
somebody willing to say *and then what* after every line. The second pattern in this unit is the one
worth carrying between countries and contracts: two organizations each held a correct, approved
number, and the two numbers differed by a factor of twenty-four because nobody's job was to read
them side by side. That is not carelessness and no amount of diligence inside either organization
would have found it. It takes one table, and the only thing that reliably puts two documents on one
table is a standard that insists on a date.

**Discuss:** Which two numbers at your company have never been read side by side?

> twenty-four.**
>
> **The exercise.** Not a changeover. **Six people, one table, two hours**, the plan read aloud step
> by step with somebody asking *and then what* after every line.
>
> | | Finding | Owner | By |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **1** | the standby pump can be running **within 40 minutes** — better than expected | A. Saleh | — |
> | **2** | **the isolation valve on the old main is seized; last operated 2016** | A. Saleh | **14 April** |
> | **3** | the operator who knows the manual start sequence is **not on the call-out list** | H. Virtanen | **within 5 working days** |
>
> **Finding 2 makes finding 1 irrelevant.** Until the valve is freed, the forty minutes cannot
> begin. Freeing it is about **six hours** with two people; if it will not free, replacement is
> **three days** and a road closure.
>
> **The commitment.** Valve freed and operated **no later than 14 April**, and **at least once a
> year** thereafter with a written report. Changeover in **two stages**, each restoring supply
> **within two hours**. **Cost: about 11,000 and one additional weekend.** **Next exercise before the
> end of April.**
>
> **Why this was not found in a review.** The plan has existed since **2019** and has been reviewed
> three times. **It reads perfectly.** It says the valve *will be closed when the plan is invoked* —
> **which is a sentence, not a maintenance task**. **A review reads a document; an exercise asks the
> document to be true.**
>
> **What the standard does and does not give us.** It **requires** plans to be exercised, **and that
> requirement is the only reason this report exists**. It **does not set** the two-hour objective —
> that is the client's figure from their own analysis — and it **prescribes no solution**. **Our
> certificate proves the system exists and is operated. It does not prove that any plan works**: ours
> read perfectly for six years with a seized valve in the middle of it.
>
> *Source: ISO 22301:2019, Security and resilience &mdash; Business continuity management systems
> &mdash; Requirements, second edition, replacing the 2012 edition. It specifies requirements for a
> management system and may be certified against; it does not set recovery time objectives or
> prescribe continuity solutions, which follow from the organization's own business impact analysis;
> it does not address project delivery risk; and certification concerns the existence and operation
> of the system rather than the effectiveness of any individual plan.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Report the good finding first and then say it is irrelevant. *The standby runs
> within forty minutes* is true, deserves saying, and makes the second finding land as a fact rather
> than as a complaint about the site team.
