Everything in this book fits on one page a month: what has been earned, how many weeks behind, what
stands between us and the top risk, which boxes are empty, who will measure the benefit, and what
the second number says. The last source is the standard whose certificate hangs in the corridor, and
reading it was a surprise.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

The most surprising thing in this book is not in any of the project documents. It is that a clause
two pages long, in a standard a company is proud of and pays to be audited against, can go unread
for eleven years — not through negligence, and not through any failure by the auditors, who checked
exactly what they were there to check. A review was held. It had minutes. The actions were tracked.
Twenty minutes is a review. What nobody had done was open the clause and count the items it says a
review shall consider, which takes four minutes and is available to anybody in the building. The
same is true of most of the sources in these three books: the limits are printed in the documents, by
their own authors, in the first pages, and the work is reading them rather than quoting the title.

**Discuss:** Which clause at your company has never been read past its heading?

> **Ninety seconds, read aloud, every month. Two lines carry an asterisk because they are weak, and

> **One thing we found this month that is not about this project.** The standard our certificate is
> issued against lists **twelve inputs** a management review **shall consider**. **Ours considered
> three, for eleven years.** The audits were correct: a review was held, it had minutes, actions were
> tracked. **Twenty minutes is a review.**
>
> **This page was not built for that clause** — it was built over eleven months because we could not

> certification concerns the existence and operation of that system.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Mark the weak lines with an asterisk and say why. A page on which everything
