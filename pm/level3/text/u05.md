# Unit 5 · The Column Nobody Fills In
%%SUB Four columns, four rows, and an assumptions box containing the words *stakeholders remain supportive*%%
%%GOALS
Talk about cause and effect
Say what each column of a logical framework is for
Say why an assumption with no name is a formality

There is a one-page table that holds a whole project: what you will do, what will change, how
anybody will know, and what has to be true for it to work. Three of the four columns get filled in
carefully. The fourth is where the projects fail.

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD TL3-05-06%%

> **Tunde:** How did you find it?
> **Chidi:** **By rewriting the fourth column.** It said *stakeholders remain supportive* and three
> more like it. **Nine lines now, each with a name and a date**, and that one came back false in a

> **Tunde:** The old column was not wrong.

> cost nothing to write and told nobody anything.

> It is their main, their purpose and their money, and **due to** the lead time on that size of pipe

**1B  Underline four cause-and-effect structures in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **leads to** | The station **leads to** the pressure. |
| **results in** | Old mains **result in** a failed purpose. |
| **as a result** | **As a result**, the purpose row does not happen. |
| **because of** + noun | **Because of** two sections of old main. |
| **due to** + noun | **Due to** the lead time on that pipe. |
| **so that** + clause | We tell them in March **so that** it can be paid for. |

> **Grammar Note** — *Because of* and *due to* take a **noun**; *because* takes a **clause**.
> *Because of the mains cannot take the pressure* is the error of this unit: write *because the
> mains cannot take the pressure* or *because of the condition of the mains*.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

The fourth column is the same everywhere, in every language, in every funding system: it is filled
in last, by one person, at speed, with sentences that cannot be false. *Stakeholders remain
supportive* costs nothing to write, satisfies the form and warns nobody. The authors of the 2004
guidelines saw this coming and said so in the guidelines themselves: a matrix completed to please a
funder stops being a way of thinking and becomes a requirement to be met. What makes the difference
is not a better template but the same discipline as a plan's second page — a name and a date beside
every assumption, so that each one is a question somebody can be asked rather than a sentence in a
document. Six of nine came back confirmed in a fortnight. One came back false, and it was the one
the whole purpose rested on.

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Logical framework · Ridgeway pressure · fourth column rewritten
> *From C. Nkemelu · 12 March · for S. Marchetti and E. Okonjo · one page*
>
> **The first three columns are unchanged. The fourth is rewritten, and one line of it came back
> false.**
>
> | | Assumption | Who confirms | By | State |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | purpose | **the estate's existing mains can carry the increased pressure** | client's network engineer | 9 Mar | **FALSE** |
> | purpose | the twelve-month measurement is taken from the client's own log | S. Marchetti | 9 Mar | confirmed |
> | result | the client's commissioning engineer is on site from week 30 | S. Marchetti | 9 Mar | confirmed |
> | result | no more than four tide windows are lost in October | harbour log | &mdash; | **open** |
> | activity | the racking arrives by 14 March | O. Nasser | 4 Mar | confirmed |
>
> **Nine lines in all; six confirmed within a fortnight, two open, one false.**
>
> **What the false one means.** The station **leads to** pressure at the estate **only if** the
> mains between them can carry it. **Two sections cannot.** Replacing them is about **90,000** with
> an **eight-week** lead time on that pipe size, and **it is not in our scope**. **As a result, we
> can deliver every indicator in the result row and the purpose row will still fail** — and the
> first person to discover that would have been a resident in **November**.
>
> **What the old fourth column said.** *Stakeholders remain supportive. Weather conditions are
> normal. Funding continues. Suppliers deliver as agreed.* **All four were true and none of them
> could have been false in a way anybody would have noticed.** They cost nothing to write and
> warned nobody.
>
> **Two indicators also rewritten.** *Improved water supply* has become **pressure above the
> contract minimum at Ridgeway, monthly, read from the client's network log**. **An indicator with
> no source of verification is an opinion with a number in it.**
>
> **What we are borrowing, and what we are not.** These guidelines were written in **2004** for the
> Commission's **external assistance** programmes, not for engineering contracts. They give **no
> test** of whether an indicator is genuinely verifiable, and the matrix shows **one logic** and
> hides the options we rejected. **The guidelines themselves warn** that a matrix completed for a
> funder becomes a bureaucratic requirement rather than a way of thinking — **which is exactly what
> ours had been for two years.**
>
> *Source: European Commission, Aid Delivery Methods, Volume 1: Project Cycle Management
> Guidelines, March 2004. Written for the Commission's external assistance programmes rather than
> commercial projects; the guidelines state that the logical framework is an aid to thinking and
> warn against mechanical or bureaucratic application; they require indicators to be objectively
> verifiable without providing a test of verifiability; and the matrix presents one chosen logic
> without the alternatives considered.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Put the false assumption in the first row of the table, not the last. A reader
> who finds it on line nine has already decided the document is a formality.

### 3 · Write

Write **110 to 130 words**: a rewritten fourth column.

**Give at least four assumptions, each with a name and a date and a state. Put the most important one
first. Explain what happens to the purpose if it fails, using *leads to* and *as a result*. Rewrite
one vague indicator so that it names its source of verification. End with one sentence on what the
guidelines were written for and what they warn against.**

> A project's logical framework had carried the same four assumptions since its funding application:
> stakeholders remain supportive, weather conditions are normal, funding continues, suppliers
> deliver as agreed. All four were true. None could have been shown false before the project

>
> Rewritten with a name and a date against each, there were nine. Six were confirmed within a

> its purpose, and the first person to notice would have been a resident in November.

2. What would have happened without the rewrite?
3. Do you agree that a document completed for a funder stops being a way of thinking?
