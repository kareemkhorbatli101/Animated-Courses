# Unit 9 · What a Measure Is Made Of
%%SUB Fourteen numbers on one report, nine that can be traced, two that cannot%%
%%GOALS
Use defining and non-defining relative clauses
Take a reported number apart
Say why a standard about measurement prescribes no measures

Every number on a report is made of smaller numbers, collected by somebody, from something, by a
method. There is a standard that sets out that chain. Running one real report through it takes an
afternoon and is the most uncomfortable afternoon in this book.

> **Grammar Note** — A **defining** clause tells you which one and takes **no comma**: *the numbers
> that nobody can trace*. A **non-defining** clause adds information and takes **commas**: *the
> indicator, which the board reads, is 0.78*. In a report the difference changes the meaning: *the
> figures, which are estimates* says all of them are; *the figures that are estimates* says only
> some.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

A number gets onto a report because somebody asked a question in a meeting once, and it stays there
for ever, because removing it looks like hiding something. That is true in every country and in
every reporting culture, and it is why most monthly packs contain at least one figure whose origin
nobody can name. The chain in this standard is a cheap way of finding them: an afternoon, a wall,
and one question asked downwards until you reach a person collecting something. The second finding
is usually more uncomfortable than the first. A figure with no method is not simply ignored — it is
read as a fact by at least one of the people who matter, and the fact is not true. And the honest
response to a weak method is often not to change it but to print it beside the number, so that
nobody mistakes a judgment for a count.

> ### Measurement note · the monthly report, taken apart
> *From H. Virtanen · 21 November · for E. Okonjo, copied to G. Mensah · one page*
>
> **Fourteen numbers. One afternoon. Nine of them are stronger than I expected and two are coming
> off.**
>
> | | Count |
> |---|---|
> | numbers **that** trace to base measures somebody collects deliberately | **9** |
> | numbers **that** rest on a base measure **which** is itself an estimate | **3** |
> | numbers **that** trace to nothing anybody can name | **2** |
>
> **The nine.** Hours booked, invoices received, meters laid, drawings issued, valves installed,
> tests passed, days elapsed, orders placed, defects raised. **Each has a method, a unit and a named
> collector**, and nobody had ever written that chain down. **This is the strong part of the report
> and it deserves saying first.**
>
> **The three.** All rest on **percentage complete**, which on **14** packages is collected by the
> method *ask the person doing the work*. **I am not changing the method** — a written procedure
> cannot be counted in meters. **I am printing the method beside the number**, so that a judgment
> stops looking like the same kind of thing as *valves installed*.
>
> **The two, and why they go.** *Team morale: amber.* *Stakeholder confidence: green.* **On the
> report every month since 2021.** No information need anybody can state, no base measure, no
> method, no scale, no collector; **the color has been set each month by whoever assembled the
> pack, which for three years has been me.**
>
> I asked **six** readers what **amber morale** would make them do. **All six said nothing. One said
> he had assumed it meant somebody had resigned.** **So the figure is not ignored; it is misread by
> at least one of the six people it is written for.**
>
> **Six numbers I am keeping, for now.** Nobody could state their information need either — they are
> on the report because they were on last month's. **A question is now written above each one. If it
> is still blank in March, they go as well.**
>
> **What the standard gives us.** It defines the process and the chain and **prescribes no measures
> at all**: it will not tell us what to count, it does not say which questions matter, and **it
> cannot tell us whether a number means what we think it means**. It also asks for the **measurement
> process itself to be evaluated** — **which nobody here has done in eleven years, and which took one
> afternoon.**
>
> *Source: ISO/IEC/IEEE 15939:2017, Systems and software engineering &mdash; Measurement process.
> It defines a measurement process and an information model relating information needs to
> indicators, derived measures and base measures; it prescribes no particular measures and does not
> determine which information needs an organization should have; it cannot establish whether a
> measure validly represents the concept of interest; and it is written for systems and software
> engineering.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Report the strong part first and in detail. Nine traceable numbers named one by
> one buys you the two deletions; starting with the deletions makes the whole note read as an
> attack on the pack.
