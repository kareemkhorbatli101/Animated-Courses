# Starting Point · Four Numbers That Are Not Progress
%%SUB What a percentage complete usually means, and the three questions it cannot answer%%
%%GOALS

Separate three different kinds of number
%%

Ask how a project is going and you will be given a percentage. It is almost always one of four
things, and only one of them is progress.

### 1 · Six things you brought from Book 2

%%FIG pc00-f1 | A single review sheet with six boxes. Each box has a heading — cannot start until, at worst, per cent higher than, increase by and to, the more the more, and none of — and one short example sentence from a progress meeting.%%

**1A  Read the six examples aloud with a partner. Which two did you use at work this month?**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dependency** | Commissioning **cannot start until** the test **passes**. |
| **Ranges** | Seven weeks, **at worst** eleven. |
| **Comparatives with data** | Twenty-two per cent **higher than** the gate figure. |
| ***By* and *to*** | It rose **by** 44 per cent, **to** 3.46 million. |
| ***The more … the more*** | **The more** people, **the more** links. |
| **Quantifiers** | **None of** the three **was** stopped on purpose. |

### 2 · Four numbers that get called progress

%%FIG pc00-f2 | Four stacked boxes, each with a heading and one line of smaller type, under a single figure reading forty-one per cent; an arrow at the foot points to a line reading only one of these is progress.%%

**2A  Read the four. Then say which one your last report meant.**

| What *41 per cent* might mean | What it actually measures |
|---|---|
| **1 Time has passed** | the calendar, and nothing else |
| **2 Money has been spent** | the account, and nothing else |
| **3 Tasks have been ticked** | how many boxes, not how big they were |
| **4 Work has been completed** | the only one of the four that is progress |

Most reports mean the first or the second and are read as the fourth. **Nobody is lying. The number
simply does not say which one it is.**

### 3 · Three questions a percentage cannot answer

%%FIG pc00-f3 | A card taped inside a notebook, printed with three questions in bold, each with one line of smaller type under it saying who needs the answer.%%

**3A  Read the three questions. Then ask your partner all three about their own project.**

> **1  Per cent of what?**
> *Of the money, the time, the tasks, or the work?*
>
> **2  Measured how, and by whom?**
> *The person who measures it is usually the person being measured.*
>
> **3  Compared with what was planned by now?**
> *Forty-one per cent is good news or bad news, and the figure alone cannot say which.*

**The third question is the whole of Unit 1.** A number with no plan beside it is a fact without a
verdict.

### 4 · Two ways of reporting the same month

%%FIG pc00-f4 | Two report extracts, one above the other. The first gives one percentage and a color. The second gives work completed against work planned, money spent, and one sentence saying what that combination means.%%

**4A  Read the two reports. Which one could you argue with?**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Report 1** | "Forty-one per cent complete. Amber." |
| **Report 2** | "Work completed: 41 per cent. Planned by now: 52. Spent: 53. We are behind and overspending, and the two have different causes." |

Report 2 is three times as long and can be checked line by line. **Report 1 cannot be wrong, which
is the same problem the last unit of Book 2 ended on.**
