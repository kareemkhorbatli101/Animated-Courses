## How to use this book

This is the third book of six. Book 1 taught the parts of a project and Book 2 taught the English of
estimating. This one is about the sentence after that: **how do you know where you are, and what do
you do about it?** Every unit is built on one real published document, named with its edition and
its date.

**Twelve units**, with the same six lessons in the same order every time.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Sixteen words, a second set of six, and a team research task |
| **2 · Reading** | One real standard, paper or published method, with its date and its limits |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures, with the mistake each one causes |
| **4 · At Work** | Clear English, a dialogue, a culture check and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A model, then your own |
| **Team Up & Review** | A picture, a team project, a survey, and one problem with no easy answer |

**The audio.** 🔊 marks a track. 195 tracks, numbered `TL3-unit-track`.

## What is different about this book

**It is full of arithmetic, and the arithmetic is the easy part.** Earned value will tell you, in two
numbers, that a project is forty-one per cent through its work and has spent fifty-three per cent of
its money. It will not tell you whether the forty-one per cent is true, and the whole of Lesson 4 in
every unit is about the person who has to produce that figure.

**Three of the twelve units are about what a measure does to the people being measured.** A measure
that nobody is judged on gets ignored. A measure that somebody is judged on gets managed. There is
no third option, and the most famous sentence about it is usually credited to the wrong person — as
Unit 11 shows.

**Every unit still prints what its document does not cover.** A standard for earned value gives no
thresholds. An annex full of risk assessment techniques declines to say which one you should use. A
logical framework makes your assumptions visible and has no view on whether they are true.

**Money has no symbol in this series.** Every figure is written without a currency sign — *a budget
of 2.4 million*, *a variance of 180,000* — because the unit is whatever currency your project uses.
