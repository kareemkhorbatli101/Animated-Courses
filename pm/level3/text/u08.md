> ## Lindhurst · project office note 8
> *Project office · what we have learned · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The standard.** **ISO 30401**, *Knowledge management systems — Requirements*, dated **2018**. It
> is a **requirements standard** and can be **certified against**. It covers how knowledge is
> **developed**, **conveyed** and **retained**, the **enablers** — people, process, technology,
> governance and **culture** — and the requirement to **demonstrate the value** of the whole thing.
>
> **The sentence that matters most is early on.**
>
> > **Knowledge management is not the same as information management or document management.**
> >
> > A document store holds documents. **Knowledge is what somebody can do with them**, in a
> > context, which is why a log of two hundred and twelve sentences is not a knowledge management
> > system. **It is a document.**
>
> **Our own figures.**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | lessons recorded since 2019 | **212** |
> | times anybody has opened a lesson | **9** |
> | lessons that have changed a document | **0** |
>
> **Nobody has been lazy.** Two hundred and twelve lessons were written by people who had just
> finished something difficult and wanted the next person to be spared it. **The log was the wrong
> destination, not the wrong intention.**
>
> **Where a lesson should go instead.**
>
> | The lesson, as written in the log | Where it belongs |
> |---|---|
> | *the valve lead time runs from approval, not from order* | the **estimating template**, as a field |
> | *the drawing register should be reconciled weekly* | the **project process record**, as line 3 |
> | *self-scored progress runs about twelve points high* | the **progress report**, as a required column |
> | *the matrix cannot count controls* | the **risk procedure**, as a quarterly bow-tie step |
>
> **Four lessons were rewritten as changes to templates in one afternoon.** Each had been in the log
> for between two and five years. **Each took under ten minutes.**
>
> **The part that cannot be written down easily.**
>
> > **Karin's spreadsheet is thirty years of finished projects**, and the useful part is not the
> > numbers. It is which of the nineteen stations was unusual and why, which supplier's quotations
> > have historically been optimistic, and which kind of client question means something has already
> > gone wrong.
> >
> > **None of that is in the file. All of it is in her.** **She retires in 2027.**
> >
> > The standard calls this **retention** and asks for it to be managed. **It does not say how**, and
> > we have asked her politely once a quarter for eleven years, which is not a method.
>
> **What the standard does not do.** It specifies a **system** and prescribes **no tools, no
> taxonomy and no method**. It does not say **what knowledge** your organization should keep. A
> **certificate** proves the system exists and is operated, **not** that anybody knows anything. And
> it can be satisfied, in principle, by an organization whose log has been read nine times in six
> years, **provided the system around the log is documented** — which is a thought worth keeping.
>
> *Source: ISO 30401:2018, Knowledge management systems &mdash; Requirements. It specifies
> requirements for a knowledge management system and may be certified against; it states that
> knowledge management is distinct from information and document management; it prescribes no tools,
> taxonomies or methods and does not determine which knowledge an organization should retain; and
> certification concerns the existence and operation of the system rather than the organization's

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **have / has** + participle | We **have recorded** 212 lessons. |

> **Grammar Note** — The present perfect links the past to now: *we have learned that three times*

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Lessons logs fail the same way in every country, and the reason is structural rather than cultural. A
lesson is written by somebody who has just finished something difficult, at the moment they are most
motivated and least able to decide where it belongs; the log accepts anything, so everything goes
there; and nobody reads it because reading it is nobody's job and the entries are not addressed to
any particular task. The repair is not a better log or a reminder to read it. It is a rule about the
destination: a lesson is closed when a document changes — a template field, a line in a checklist, a
required column on a report — so that the next person uses it without knowing it exists. The second
half is harder. The most valuable knowledge in most companies is a judgment in one person's head,
with a retirement date attached, and no standard will tell you how to get it out.

**Discuss:** Whose head holds something your company could not replace?

> ### Lessons note · the destination rule
> *From H. Virtanen · 14 November · for N. Rahimi and E. Okonjo · one page*
>
> **We have recorded 212 lessons since 2019. The log has been opened nine times. Not one lesson has
> changed a document.**
>
> **Nobody has been lazy.** Every entry was written by somebody who had just finished something
> difficult and wanted the next person to be spared it. **The destination was wrong, not the
> intention.**
>
> **Four lessons moved on Tuesday.**
>
> | The lesson | In the log since | Now | Time taken |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | valve lead time runs from **approval**, not order | **2022**, three times, three wordings | a field in the **estimating template** | **4 min** |
> | the drawing register needs weekly reconciliation | **2021** | **line 3** of the project process record | 6 min |
> | self-scored progress runs ~12 points high | **2023** | a required column on the **progress report** | 9 min |
> | the matrix cannot count controls | **2020** | a quarterly step in the **risk procedure** | 7 min |
>
> **Each had been sitting in the log for between two and five years. None took more than ten
> minutes.**
>
> **The rule I am asking for.** **A lesson is closed when a named document changes**, not when it is
> written. If no document would change, the lesson is a story and belongs in a conversation.
>
> **The harder half.** **Karin's spreadsheet is thirty years of finished projects and she retires in
> 2027 — twenty-nine months.** The file is the easy part: I can copy it this afternoon. **The part I
> cannot copy is that she knows which of the nineteen stations was unusual and why, which suppliers'
> quotations have historically been optimistic, and which kind of client question means something
> has already gone wrong.** **Two hours a month, recorded, with the file open and one person from
> each team present. Fifty-eight hours. The first is booked for the ninth.**
>
> **What the standard gives us and what it does not.** It is a **requirements standard** and we are
> certified to it. It says plainly that **knowledge management is not information management or
> document management** — which is exactly what our log was. It calls the second half above
> **retention** and asks for it to be managed. **It prescribes no tool, no taxonomy and no method,
> and it does not say what knowledge we should keep.** **The certificate proves a system exists. It
> does not prove anybody knows anything.**
>
> *Source: ISO 30401:2018, Knowledge management systems &mdash; Requirements. It specifies
> requirements for a knowledge management system and may be certified against; it distinguishes
> knowledge management from information and document management; it prescribes no tools, taxonomies
> or methods and does not determine which knowledge an organization should retain; and certification
> concerns the existence and operation of the system rather than the organization's knowledge.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Start with the three numbers and the sentence that nobody has been lazy. The
> numbers are an indictment of a destination, not of the people who wrote 212 entries, and the
> second sentence is what allows anybody to read the first.

### 3 · Write

Write **110 to 130 words**: a lessons note.

**Open with the numbers and the sentence that nobody has been lazy. Use the present perfect with
*since* and *for*. Name at least three lessons and the document each should change. State the
destination rule. Say whose knowledge is leaving and when, and what you have booked. End with one
sentence on what the certificate does not prove.**

**Read your note aloud to your partner first.** Then check your own against the list, and swap.
