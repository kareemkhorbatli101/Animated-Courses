# Starting Point
%%SUB Four questions to ask any number, before you argue about whether it is too big%%
%%GOALS
Ask what a number is a number of
Ask how accurate it is, and who says so
Ask which year's money it is in
Ask who is obliged by it
%%

Books 1 to 4 taught you to describe a project, estimate it, measure it and name the method behind
it. This book is about the one part that ends up in a contract. Four questions, and then twelve
units.

### 1 · Six things you brought from Book 4

**1A  Read the six examples aloud with a partner. Which two did you use this month?**

%%FIG pe00-f1 | A single review sheet with six boxes. Each box has a heading — abstract nouns into people, must unless, over not instead of, hedging, any one of which, and by plus gerund — and one short example sentence from a project meeting.%%

| What you have | An example |
|---|---|
| Abstract nouns into people | **Who** telephones **whom**, how often? |
| *Must … unless* | It **must** be escalated **unless** it is within tolerance. |
| *Over*, not instead of | Working software **over** comprehensive documentation. |
| Hedging | It **is claimed that** most projects fail; the method **cannot be verified**. |
| *Any one of which* | Four criteria, **any one of which** stops the project. |
| *By* + gerund | Urgency was created **by showing** people real figures. |

Say each example to your partner, then say one sentence of your own with the same structure.

**1B  Listen and repeat.** %%AUD TL5-00-01%%

### 2 · Four questions to ask any number

**2A  Read the four. Then ask them about a number you have been given this month.**

%%FIG pe00-f2 | A card taped inside a notebook, printed with four questions in bold, each with one line of smaller type beneath it saying what the answer tells you.%%

> **1 · A number of what, exactly?**
> *Does it include design, land, connection charges, spares, the client's own staff, tax? A price
> without a boundary is not a price.*
>
> **2 · How accurate is it, and who says so?**
> *A single figure with no range is a guess wearing a suit. Ask what level of definition it was
> prepared at, and who prepared it.*
>
> **3 · Which year's money is it in, and over what period?**
> *Three million today and three million in year twelve are different amounts. Ask the year, the
> rate and the horizon.*
>
> **4 · Who is obliged by it, and from when?**
> *A number in a report obliges nobody. A number in a contract obliges somebody, usually within a
> stated number of days.*

Ask a partner all four about a figure from their own work. Do not accept an answer with no number
in it.

**2B  Listen.** %%AUD TL5-00-02%%

**What do you think happens to a number that travels without those four answers attached?**

### 3 · The number and the price are not the same thing

**3A  Read the two columns. Then say which column your own figures live in.**

%%FIG pe00-f3 | Two objects side by side. On the left, a single figure written large on a slide. On the right, the same figure with a range, a date, a level of definition, a currency-free unit, a scope list and a named preparer.%%

| What travels | What was prepared |
|---|---|
| 14 million | 14 million, **plus 30 per cent, minus 15** |
| in the board pack | at a **class 4** level of definition |
| quoted all year | prepared in **March**, in **this year's** money |
| attributed to the project | prepared by **O. Bondar**, cost engineer |
| understood as the price | **excludes** land, connection charges and client staff |

**Nobody removed the range deliberately.** A single figure fits on a slide, and a range invites a
question in a meeting that is already running late. Six months later the single figure is the
budget, and the person who prepared it is explaining a thirty per cent overrun on a number that was
never wrong.

**Which of the five right-hand rows would survive in your organization?**

### 4 · Two ways of answering *will it cost more than we said?*

**4A  Read the two answers. Which one could be checked by somebody else?**

%%FIG pe00-f4 | Two speech bubbles, one above the other. The first says we are confident the budget is adequate. The second gives a figure, a range, a level of definition, two excluded items, a date and a named preparer.%%

> **Answer 1.** *We are confident that the budget is adequate.*
>
> **Answer 2.** *The estimate is 14.2 million at a class 4 level of definition, which carries a
> range of plus thirty and minus fifteen per cent. It excludes land and connection charges, which
> are the client's. It was prepared in March in this year's money; at 4 per cent escalation the
> same scope is 15.4 million at the midpoint of construction. O. Bondar prepared it and will defend
> every line of it.*

**Answer 1 cannot be wrong, which is why it is worth nothing.** Answer 2 can be checked, challenged
and disproved by anybody with an afternoon, which is the only kind of financial sentence worth
putting your name on.

this month, and could you say what it is a number of?
