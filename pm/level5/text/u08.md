# Unit 8 · Lowest Price and Best Value
%%SUB A weighting nobody chose, twenty dead points, and four bidders who all scored full marks%%
%%GOALS
Describe a weighting
Say which criteria discriminate and which do not
Say who chose the weighting
%%

A standard on construction procurement sets out four ways to evaluate a tender: on price alone, on
price and preferences, on price and quality, or on all three. It does not choose your weighting. Ours
was ninety-ten, nobody could remember choosing it, and a fifth of our quality points could not tell
the four bidders apart.

> ## Lindhurst · procurement note 8
> *Procurement · the control system tender · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The standard.** **ISO 10845-1:2020**, *Construction procurement — Part 1: Processes, methods and
> procedures*, which sets out how an organization establishes a procurement system intended to be
> **fair, equitable, transparent, competitive and cost-effective**.
>
> **Four evaluation methods.**
>
> | Method | What is scored |
> |---|---|
> | **1** | the **financial offer** only |
> | **2** | the financial offer and **preferences** |
> | **3** | the financial offer and **quality** |
> | **4** | the financial offer, **quality** and **preferences** |
>
> **We used method 3.** **The standard gives the method and the arithmetic. The employer gives the
> weighting and the criteria.** **That division is the whole of this note.**
>
> **Our weighting: 90 per cent financial offer, 10 per cent quality.**
>
> | Bidder | Price (relative) | Quality /100 | Weighted total |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **W** | **100.0** (lowest) | **69** | **96.9** |
> | X | 97.8 | 73 | 95.3 |
> | Y | 95.6 | 84 | 94.4 |
> | Z | 94.1 | **91** | 94.0 |
>
> **The prices spanned about six per cent. The quality scores spanned twenty-two points.** **At a ten
> per cent weighting, twenty-two points of quality move a total by 2.2; six per cent of price moves it
> by 5.4.** **The quality submission was arithmetically incapable of changing the result**, and we
> spent **eleven weeks** writing, submitting and evaluating it.
>
> **Where the weighting came from.** **Nobody in this organization chose it for this tender.** It was
> **carried over from the invitation to tender for the previous contract, a supply of pipe**, where
> ninety-ten was a defensible choice. **This tender was for the design and build of a control
> system.**
>
> **Twenty dead points.**
>
> > One criterion, worth **20 of the 100 quality points**, asked whether the bidder has a health and
> > safety policy and procedures. **All four bidders scored full marks.** They all have one; they are
> > all required to.
> >
> > **A criterion on which every bidder scores identically does not discriminate.** It is a page
> > count. **With those 20 points removed, the effective quality weighting was 8 per cent, not 10.**
> >
> > **Two further criteria, worth 15 points between them, separated the four bidders by 3 points.**
>
> **The same tender at a different weighting.** Our own policy for design services is **70 per cent
> price, 30 per cent quality.** No one applied it, because this was procured as a works contract.
>
> | Bidder | at 90/10 | **at 70/30** |
> |---|---|---|
> | **W** | **96.9** | 79.3 |
> | X | 95.3 | 90.4 |
> | Y | 94.4 | **92.1** |
> | **Z** | 94.0 | **92.2** |
>
> **At seventy-thirty the ranking reverses almost completely.** **The weighting chose the winner, and
> the weighting was inherited from a pipe order.**
>
> **What I am not saying.** **I am not saying the wrong bidder won.** W has performed acceptably, and
> on this evidence I cannot show that Z would have performed better. **My objection is narrower and I
> think more serious: nobody in the evaluation room could state why W won.** **The answer is that W
> was six per cent cheaper, which is a perfectly good reason — and it is not the reason we told four
> bidders we would use.**
>
> **What the standard does not do.** It **specifies processes, methods and procedures**, not an
> organization's **policy**: it does not tell you what weighting to apply, which criteria to use,
> what preferences to grant, or what a quality point is worth. It is **about procurement, not about
> the contract** that follows. **And it cannot make a criterion discriminate.** Dead points are an
> employer's choice, and the standard has nothing to say about them.
>
> *Source: ISO 10845-1:2020, Construction procurement — Part 1: Processes, methods and procedures,
> which specifies processes, methods and procedures for establishing a procurement system that is
> fair, equitable, transparent, competitive and cost-effective, including methods of evaluating
> tender offers. The standard does not prescribe an organization's procurement policy, the weightings
> or evaluation criteria it applies, or the terms of any resulting contract.*

> **Grammar Note** — A weighting is only meaningful beside the spread of the thing it weights. *Ten
> per cent for quality* means nothing on its own; *ten per cent for quality, with prices spanning six
> per cent* says that quality cannot decide the award. Always state the two together.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Weightings are inherited, not chosen. Somebody copies the last invitation to tender because that is
the fast and sensible way to produce a document, and the weighting travels with it from a pipe order
to a design-and-build contract without anybody deciding anything. The result is an evaluation that is
procedurally perfect and substantively empty: four companies spend real money writing submissions
that the arithmetic cannot read. The second pattern is the criterion everybody passes. It survives
because it looks responsible — nobody wants to be the person who deletes safety from a scoring
sheet — and it quietly converts a tenth of your quality weighting into page count. Both failures are
invisible unless somebody re-runs the same scores at a different weighting, which takes about twenty
minutes in a spreadsheet, and almost nobody does it, because the result is awkward and the contract
is already signed.

**Discuss:** What at your organization travels from document to document without being chosen?
