> ## Lindhurst · money note 12
> *Commercial and cost engineering · one page of money decisions · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The standard.** **IAS 37**, *Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets*, issued by
> the international accounting standard-setter. It governs **what appears in financial statements**,
> and it is the reason the word *provision* cannot be used for a project contingency.
>
> **The three tests. A provision is recognized only if all three are met.**
>
> | | Test |
> |---|---|
> | **1** | there is a **present obligation** (legal or constructive) **arising from a past event** |
> | **2** | an **outflow of resources is probable** — more likely than not |
> | **3** | the amount can be **estimated reliably** |
>
> **Our 1.9 million fails test 1, which is the one nobody expects.** **There is no present
> obligation**: nothing has happened. **A project contingency is money for the future. A provision is
> a consequence of the past.**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | Project contingency in the cost report | **1.90** |
> | Provision recognized in the accounts | **0.34** |
> | **Gap** | **1.56** |
> | Years during which each side assumed the other held it | **3** |
>
> **What makes up the 0.34.** One claim arising from an event in **March**: the obligation exists, an
> outflow is assessed as more likely than not, and the amount is estimable. **All three tests met, so
> it is recognized.**
>
> **Two things that are real and are not provisions.**
>
> | | Treatment |
> |---|---|
> | the **aquifer finding** of Unit 11 | a **contingent liability** — a possible obligation of unknown amount: **disclosed in a note, not recognized** |
> | **operating losses** after the 2027 subsidy review | **nothing may be set aside at all**: the standard prohibits provisions for **future operating losses** |
>
> **The accounts are not a risk register.** Both items above are serious; neither is a present
> obligation arising from a past event. **A thing can be probable, expensive and entirely absent from
> a balance sheet, correctly.**
>
> **And the eleven money decisions of this programme, on one page.**
>

>
> **Every row has an owner and a date in the full register.** **The fourth column is the one worth
> keeping**: eleven documents, and the useful sentence about each of them is what it does not claim.
>
> **What the standard does not do.** It is a **financial reporting standard**: it governs **what is
> recognized, measured and disclosed in financial statements**, and it is **not a method for managing
> project risk**. Its word **probable** has a reporting meaning — **more likely than not** — which is
> not what a risk register means by *probable*. It does **not tell you how much contingency to carry
> on a project**, and it does **not permit a provision to be used for expenditure other than that for
> which it was recognized**. **It regulates a statement of position, not a plan.**
>
> *Source: IAS 37, Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets, issued by the
> international accounting standard-setter. It requires a provision to be recognized only where a
> present obligation arises from a past event, an outflow of resources is probable and the amount can
> be estimated reliably; requires contingent liabilities to be disclosed rather than recognized; and
> prohibits provisions for future operating losses. It is a financial reporting standard and is not a
> project risk management method. Nothing here is accounting, tax, legal or financial advice.*

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Five books of this series have ended on the same finding in different clothes, and this unit is the
plainest version of it: a document is only safe to use once you know what it does not claim. A class
5 estimate is not a budget. A learning curve says nothing about steel. A life-cycle cost is not a
whole-life cost. A notice is not an argument. An early warning is not a proposal. Three letters are
silent on ownership. A guidance document creates no obligation. A condition grade is not an
interruption. A criterion is not an indicator. And a balance sheet is not a risk register. None of
those documents is at fault in any of the eleven stories in this book; in nearly every case the
document said plainly what it was, on an early page, and somebody used it for the adjacent purpose.
The organizational version of the problem is the one in this unit: two honest documents, one shared
word, and a gap of one and a half million that both sides assumed the other was carrying. The cure
costs four columns.

**Discuss:** Which word do you and another department define differently?

> ### Money register · Calder programme · one page
> *From H. Sato and O. Bondar · 4 December · for E. Okonjo, the cost file and finance · one page*
>
> **Twelve money decisions. Four columns. The fourth is the one that was missing for three years.**
>
> | # | The number | The document | What it is not |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | 1 | **12** at class 5, range **6–24**; outcome **16.1** | estimate classification practice | **not a budget-quality estimate** |
> | 2 | **4,400** hours on an **85 %** curve | the 1936 learning-curve paper | **not applicable to material** |
> | 3 | pump B, **+0.50** over 30 years at **3.5 % real** | life-cycle costing standard, 2017 | **not a whole-life cost** |
> | 4 | **ENPV +14.8**; switching value **−23 %** | the 2014 appraisal guide | **not a decision** |
> | 5 | **1.34** time-barred at day 36; period **21** | the 2017 construction contract | **not the printed 28 days** |
> | 6 | **210** our share of a **420** delay | the 2017 engineering contract | **not self-administering** |
> | 7 | **186** uninsured on the road leg | the 2020 delivery-term rules | **silent on title and payment** |
> | 8 | award at **90/10**, reversing at **70/30** | the procurement standard, 2020 | **not a policy** |
> | 9 | **60 %** of plant hours, **90 %** at month 18 | sustainable procurement guidance | **not certifiable** |
> | 10 | **38 %** energy saving, about **84** a year | asset management standard, 2014 | **not a requirement** |
> | 11 | one criterion **unassessable** | the 2019 evaluation criteria | **not indicators** |
> | **12** | **contingency 1.90 · provision 0.34 · gap 1.56** | **IAS 37** | **not a risk register** |
>
> **Each row carries a named owner and a date in the full register.**
>
> **The reconciliation, which should have existed in year one.**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | Project contingency, cost report | **1.90** |
> | Provision recognized, accounts | **0.34** — one claim, event date **March**, all three tests met |
> | **Difference** | **1.56** |
> | Disclosed, not recognized | **the aquifer contingent liability**, amount unknown |
> | May not be provided for at all | **operating losses after the 2027 subsidy review** |
>
> **Why the 1.90 cannot be recognized.** **It fails the first test, not the third.** There is **no
> present obligation arising from a past event**: the money is for events that have not occurred. **It
> could be estimated to the nearest hundred and would still not be a provision.** **A contingency
> faces forward; a provision looks back.**
>
> **How 1.56 went unnoticed for three years.** **Both documents were correct.** **Our cost report used
> the word *provision* in its commentary beside a contingency figure; the accounts used it for the
> recognized liability only.** **I assumed finance had recognized our contingency; finance assumed our
> report carried their provision.** **Nobody was wrong and nobody checked.**
>
> **The recommendation, which is the whole of this book.** **Every money number in this organization
> gets a fourth column.** **The number, the document, the owner, and what it is not.** **Eleven
> documents in this programme, and in every case the useful sentence was what the document does not
> claim — including this one, which regulates a statement of position and not a plan.**
>
> *Source: IAS 37, Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets, issued by the
> international accounting standard-setter. A provision is recognized only where a present obligation
> arises from a past event, an outflow of resources is probable and the amount can be estimated
> reliably; contingent liabilities are disclosed rather than recognized; provisions for future
> operating losses are prohibited; and a provision may not be used for expenditure other than that
> for which it was recognized. It is a financial reporting standard and not a project risk management
> method. Nothing here is accounting, tax, legal or financial advice.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Write the fourth column first. If you cannot say what a number is not, you do
> not yet know what it is.
