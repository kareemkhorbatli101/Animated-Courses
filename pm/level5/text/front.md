## How to use this book

This is the fifth book of six. Books 1 to 4 were about the parts of a project, estimating it,
knowing where it is, and the methods organizations buy. This one is about **money** — what a number
means before anybody has designed anything, what a contract does when the number turns out to be
wrong, and who decides afterwards whether any of it was worth doing.

**Twelve units**, with the same six lessons in the same order every time.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Sixteen words, a second set of six, and a team research task |
| **2 · Reading** | One real standard, contract form or guide, with its edition and its limits |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures, with the mistake each one causes |
| **4 · At Work** | Clear English, a dialogue, a culture check and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A model, then your own |
| **Team Up & Review** | A picture, a team project, a survey, and one problem with no easy answer |

## What is different about this book

**Money is the only part of a project that cannot be described approximately without somebody
losing.** A schedule can be discussed in weeks. A risk can be discussed in words. A cost is a number
that goes into a contract, and the English around it carries obligations: a notice period, a
tolerance, a weighting, a rate, a definition of what the price includes.

So this book is strict about four things, and each of them is a structure you will practice.

1. **Every number carries its accuracy.** *Fourteen million* is not an estimate. *Fourteen million,
   plus thirty percent and minus fifteen, at a class 4 level of definition* is.
2. **Every obligation carries its period.** *We will notify you* is a sentiment. *Within twenty-eight
   days of the date on which the contractor became aware* is a clause.
3. **Every price carries its boundary.** The question is never only *how much* but *how much for
   what, delivered where, with whose risk from which point*.
4. **Every benefit carries a person and a year.** A benefit with no owner and no year is a reason
   for spending money, not a result of having spent it.

**Money has no currency symbol anywhere in this series.** Every figure is in whatever unit your own
project uses. This is deliberate: a symbol is a jurisdiction, and this book is written for people
whose contracts cross several.

## One warning before Unit 1

**Nothing in this book is legal, financial, contractual, tax or accounting advice, and none of it is
a substitute for reading the document you are actually working under.** Several of the documents
discussed here are copyright works sold by the bodies that publish them, and some are contract forms
whose precise wording is the whole of their effect. This book teaches the **English** used to
discuss them: what a notice period is, what a range means, why *should* and *shall* are different
words, and how to say that a price excludes something. Where it describes what a document contains,
it does so in order to teach that language, and it prints what the document itself says it does not
cover. **Your clause is the one in your contract. Read it.**
