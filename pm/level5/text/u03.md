# Unit 3 · Thirty Years, Not Three
%%SUB Two pumps, two numbers nobody argued about, and an answer that changes when either one moves%%
%%GOALS
Talk about long horizons
State a period of analysis and a discount rate
Say what a life-cycle cost does not include
%%

A standard published in 2017 sets out how to compare options over the life of an asset rather than
over the life of a capital budget. It does not contain the cost of anything, and it does not tell you
which discount rate to use — which matters, because in our case those two numbers decided the answer
and nobody discussed either of them.

> ## Lindhurst · cost engineering note 3
> *Cost engineering · two pumps, thirty years · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The standard.** **ISO 15686-5:2017**, *Buildings and constructed assets — Service life planning
> — Part 5: Life-cycle costing*, **second edition**, replacing the edition of **2008**. It sets out
> how to carry out and report a life-cycle cost analysis.
>
> **Its central distinction.**
>
> | | What it contains |
> |---|---|
> | **life-cycle cost (LCC)** | construction, **operation**, **maintenance** and **end of life** |
> | **whole-life cost (WLC)** | the LCC **plus** non-construction costs, **income**, and **externalities** |
>
> **What it requires you to state.** The **period of analysis**. The **discount rate** and whether
> figures are **real or nominal**. The **cost categories included and excluded**. The **assumed
> service lives**. **It is a reporting discipline as much as a calculation.**
>
> **Our two options.** Option **A**: capital **1.8**, annual operating and maintenance **0.210**.
> Option **B**: capital **2.6**, annual **0.128**.
>
> | Basis | Option A | Option B | Winner |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **3 years** (the capital budget) | **2.4** | **3.0** | **A by 0.6** |
> | **10 years at 3.5 %** | **3.55** | **3.66** | **A by 0.11** |
> | **15 years at 3.5 %** | **4.22** | **4.07** | **B by 0.15** |
> | **30 years at 3.5 %** | **5.66** | **4.95** | **B by 0.71** |
> | **30 years at 10 %** | **3.78** | **3.81** | **A by 0.03** |
>
> **Two numbers decide this, and neither was discussed in the meeting that chose option A.** The
> **period of analysis** and the **discount rate**. **The standard supplies neither**, and says so.
>
> **A correction to my own first issue.**
>
> > The pump's nameplate **design life is 20 years**, not thirty. A **mid-life refurbishment at year
> > 15, about 0.340**, was included in option A's maintenance line and **omitted from option B's**.
> > **Including it in both reduces B's thirty-year advantage from 0.71 to about 0.50.** **B still
> > wins, and my first issue was wrong in B's favor by about two hundred thousand.**
>
> **What is excluded here, and therefore what this is not.** **Energy emissions are not priced**, so
> this is a **life-cycle cost and not a whole-life cost**. **Land, the client's own staff time and
> tax are excluded.** **No residual value is taken** at year thirty for either option. **If
> emissions were priced at any positive figure, option B's advantage would widen**, because it uses
> about forty percent less energy — so the exclusion is conservative against my own recommendation,
> which is the only direction an exclusion should ever run.
>
> **What the standard does not do.** It **contains no cost data of any kind**. It **does not set a
> discount rate**, a period of analysis or a service life. It is written for **buildings and
> constructed assets**. And it is explicit about the purpose: **a life-cycle cost is for comparing
> options**, not for predicting what anything will actually cost in year twenty-eight. **Nobody knows
> the price of electricity in year twenty-eight, and the standard does not pretend to.**
>
> *Source: ISO 15686-5:2017, Buildings and constructed assets — Service life planning — Part 5:
> Life-cycle costing, second edition, replacing the 2008 edition. The standard distinguishes
> life-cycle cost from whole-life cost and requires the period of analysis, the discount rate, the
> basis of figures and the inclusions and exclusions to be stated. It contains no cost data, sets no
> discount rate, period or service life, is written for buildings and constructed assets, and
> presents life-cycle costing as a means of comparing options rather than of forecasting actual
> future expenditure.*

> **Grammar Note** — *Real* and *nominal* are not decoration. A real rate has inflation removed and
> must be applied to figures in today's money; a nominal rate must be applied to inflated figures.
> Mixing them is the commonest arithmetic error in long-horizon work, and it always flatters the
> option with the lower capital cost.

> **B:** Option A, by six hundred thousand. The lines cross ______________ year twelve.
> **A:** So which do I sign?
> **B:** That depends on the period this organization decides over, which is not a question about

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Long-horizon arithmetic fails for organizational reasons rather than mathematical ones. The capital
budget covers three years, the asset lasts fifty, and the two numbers live in different cost centers
with different managers, so the cheapest decision for the person signing is routinely the expensive
one for the organization. Nobody involved is behaving badly: the capital committee is doing its job,
which is to stay inside a three-year envelope. The second difficulty is that two numbers decide the
answer and neither feels like a decision. A period of analysis and a discount rate are set once, by
somebody who is not in the room, and they silently determine which option wins — which is why a
standard that tells you nothing about cost spends its pages insisting that you state them. The habit
worth carrying is to give both answers unprompted: *over three years A, over thirty B, crossing at
year twelve*. Give only the one that supports your recommendation and you will be believed once.

**Discuss:** Who pays the operating cost of your last capital decision?

a. Set the period of analysis and the discount rate in a policy, signed once
b. Always give both answers, and the crossover year
c. Build both options' profiles from the same kind of source
d. State plainly when a figure is a life-cycle and not a whole-life cost
e. Make the operating budget holder sign the capital decision

Report your order, and say which one your organization would resist.

> | Basis | A | B | Winner |

> | 3 years, undiscounted | **2.4** | 3.0 | **A by 0.6** |

> | 30 years at 3.5 % real | 5.66 | **4.95** | **B by 0.71** |

> the direction I was arguing.**
>
