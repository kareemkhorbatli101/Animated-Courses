A set of evaluation criteria, revised in 2019, asks six questions of a finished intervention. Five of
them we would have asked anyway. The sixth one — added in that revision — is the one that found two
schemes drawing from the same aquifer, approved fourteen months apart, by two organizations that had
each done everything correctly.

> *Benefits · Calder valley water scheme, two years after handover · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The criteria.** The evaluation criteria of the **Development Assistance Committee of the
> OECD**, **as revised in 2019**. The earlier set, in use since the early 1990s, had **five**
> criteria. The revision produced **six**, adding **coherence**, and set out definitions and
> principles for their use.
>
> | Criterion | The question it asks | Our judgment |
> |---|---|---|
> | **relevance** | was it the right intervention, and is it still? | **strong, and stronger than claimed** |
> | **coherence** | how does it fit with other interventions here? | **a serious finding — see below** |
> | **effectiveness** | were the objectives achieved? | **4 of 5 outcomes achieved, 1 partly** |
> | **efficiency** | were they achieved economically? | **no** |
> | **impact** | what wider and longer difference was made? | **measured and positive** |
> | **sustainability** | will the benefits continue? | **cannot be assessed** |
>
> **Relevance, and an appraisal that was wrong in our favor.** The appraisal rested on **two
> boreholes failing within eight to twelve years.** **One failed in year two.** The counterfactual
> was not merely real; **it was nearer than we claimed**, and that is worth recording precisely
> because it flatters us.
>
> **Effectiveness and impact.** **Four of the five stated outcomes achieved.** The fifth — a
> reduction in night-time pressure complaints — **partly achieved: 31 percent against a target of 50
> percent.** **Impact: 41 households that were tankered in August of year one were not tankered in
> August of year two.**
>
> **Efficiency: no.** **The scheme was delivered at 16.1 against a figure of 12 that had become the
> budget.** The cost engineering file explains how a concept estimate became an authorization, and
> **the explanation is sound and is not a defense.** **On this criterion the scheme performs badly
> and I decline to soften it.**
>
> **Sustainability: cannot be assessed, and here is why that is a finding rather than an evasion.**
>
> > **The tariff does not recover the operating cost.** The gap is met by a **subsidy**, and the
> > subsidy carries a **review in 2027**.
> >
> > If the review continues the subsidy, the benefits continue. **If it does not, the operating
> > company's own figures show the scheme running at a loss within two years.**
> >
> > **I have no evidence about a decision nobody has taken.** **“Cannot be assessed, because
> > the question turns on the 2027 review” is information.** A score of three out of five would
> > be a guess with a number in front of it, and somebody would quote the number.
>
> **Coherence — the criterion added in 2019, and the one that found something.**
>
> > **Our scheme abstracts from the Calder aquifer.**
> >
> > **A scheme serving a town 20 km away, approved 14 months after ours, abstracts from the same
> > aquifer.**
> >
> > **Neither application refers to the other.** I have read both. **Each organization followed its
> > own procedure correctly**, each consulted the bodies its procedure names, and **the two
> > procedures do not overlap anywhere**.
> >
> > **Combined licensed abstraction exceeds the figure used in our own recharge assessment by about
> > 18 percent in a dry year.** I am not a hydrologist and **this is a flag, not a conclusion**: it
> > needs one.
> >
> > **None of the five older criteria would have asked this question.** Relevance asks whether we
> > were right; effectiveness whether we delivered; **coherence asks who else is standing in the same
> > river.**
>
> **One thing I did badly.** **I applied all six criteria mechanically, in order, with a paragraph
> each**, which is precisely what the 2019 guidance warns against. The criteria are **not a
> checklist**, and using them as one produced four paragraphs of little value and two that mattered.
> **Had I asked which of the six could change a decision, I would have spent three weeks on coherence
> and sustainability and two days on the rest.**
>
> **What the criteria do not do.** They are **criteria, not indicators**: they say **what to ask**
> and **nothing about how to measure it**. They were developed for the evaluation of **development
> interventions**, not for judging project delivery, and much of their literature assumes that
> context. They **assign no weights**: nothing tells you whether a failure on efficiency outweighs a
> success on impact. **And they do not say who decides** — the criteria produce findings, and somebody
> else decides what the findings mean.

### 5 · Word study — evaluative judgment

**5A  Complete the chart.**

%%FIG pe11-f6 | A three-column chart headed the structure, what it does and an example, with rows for on the criterion of, judged against, to the extent that, partly achieved, cannot be assessed because and on balance, four filled in and two empty.%%

| Structure | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **on the criterion of** | names the test | **On the criterion of** efficiency, the scheme performs badly. |
| **judged against** | names the standard | **Judged against** its own five outcomes, four were achieved. |
| **to the extent that** | limits the claim | Effective **to the extent that** the stated outcomes were measured. |
| **partly achieved** | states a partial result honestly | **Partly achieved**: 31 percent against a target of 50. |
| **cannot be assessed because** | | |
| **on balance** | | |

> **Grammar Note** — *Cannot be assessed because …* is a finding, not a gap. It names what evidence
> would be needed and who holds it. *Not applicable* and a blank cell are gaps; a reader fills them
> in with whatever they already believe.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Evaluation is usually asked for as a verdict and delivered as a number, and both are wrong. A verdict
compresses six different questions into one, and a number survives long after the reasoning that
produced it: *three out of five* will be quoted for years by people who never knew it was a guess
about a decision nobody had taken. The honest alternative — *cannot be assessed, because the subsidy
is reviewed in 2027* — is harder to publish, because it leaves work for the reader and looks like
indecision. The second pattern is the one that matters more. The question that finds the real problem
is almost always the sideways question: who else is doing something in the same place, drawing on the
same resource, serving the same people. Every organization's procedure examines its own project
thoroughly and consults the bodies its own procedure names, and two entirely correct processes can
therefore produce a question that belongs to nobody. Nobody is at fault, and the water is still
finite.

**Discuss:** Who else is standing in your river?
