# Unit 5 · Within Twenty-Eight Days
%%SUB A claim of 1.34 million lost to a date, and a printed number that was not the contract's%%
%%GOALS
Write a time-barred obligation

Say why the printed form is not your contract

A standard form of contract published in 2017 requires a notice of claim within twenty-eight days of
becoming aware of the event. Our notice went in on day thirty-six, so the claim lapsed. Then the
commercial manager read our own copy and found that twenty-eight had been amended to twenty-one, and
that nobody on site had ever been told.

### 5 · Word study — time-bar obligations

**5A  Complete the chart.**

%%FIG pe05-f6 | A three-column chart headed the structure, what it does and an example, with rows for within days of, no later than, the period runs from, failing which, shall be deemed and unless within, four filled in and two empty.%%

| Structure | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **within … days of** | sets the length | **Within 21 days of** becoming aware. |
| **no later than** | sets the outer limit | **No later than** 25 March. |
| **the period runs from** | fixes the start | **The period runs from** the date of awareness. |
| **failing which** | states the consequence | **Failing which**, the entitlement is lost. |
| **shall be deemed** | | |
| **unless … within** | | |

> **Grammar Note** — A time bar needs three parts and loses its force without any one of them: a
> **length**, a **trigger** and a **consequence**. *Notice shall be given promptly* has a consequence
> and no length; *within 21 days* has a length and no trigger; and a trigger expressed as *when the
> commercial team is informed* moves the start date to somebody who was not there.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Time bars are lost by organizations, not by individuals. The event is seen by a foreman, the clock
starts in his handwriting, and the obligation belongs to a commercial manager who will hear about it
at the monthly meeting — by which time a third of the period has gone. Nobody is careless: the
information simply has to cross two or three people, and the contract does not wait for it. The
second failure is quieter and more expensive. Standard forms exist to be amended, so the number
everybody has learned is almost never the number in the contract, and site instruction packs are
copied from the previous job because that is the sensible way to produce a pack quickly. So two
numbers coexist in one company, one of them wrong, and the wrong one is the one on site. The fix is
not training. It is a mechanical trigger — a category of diary entry that generates a notice by
itself — and one line at the front of every pack saying that these periods come from this contract
and no other.

**Discuss:** Where in your organization do two different versions of one number coexist?

### 1 · Read a model

**1A  Look at the two notices. Answer with a partner.**

**1B  Listen. Why is a notice not an argument?** %%AUD TL5-05-14%%

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Notice of claim · Calder contract · ground conditions
> *From H. Sato, for the contractor · 7 March · delivered by hand and by email to the address at
> appendix page 2*
>
> **1. This is a notice of claim under the claims clause of the contract.**
>
> **2. The contractor became aware on 4 March of ground conditions at chainage 1,420**, recorded in
> the site diary of that date, **which the contractor considers to be unforeseeable** within the
> meaning of the contract.
>
> **3. A fully detailed claim, with substantiation and quantification, will be submitted within the
> period provided by the contract.**
>
> **4. All rights are reserved.** Nothing in this notice limits the contractor's entitlement, and the
> absence of any particular from this notice is not a waiver of it.
>
> ---
>
> *Internal note, not part of the notice.*
>
> **Why this is four lines.** **A notice is not an argument.** Its only job is to exist, in writing,
> inside the period, delivered as the contract requires. **Everything persuasive belongs in the
> fully detailed claim**, where it can be substantiated without a deadline running.
>
> **The period, from this contract and no other.** **Our particular conditions amend the printed 28
> days to 21.** **The period runs from 4 March**, the date of the diary entry, **not from any
> instruction and not from the date the commercial team was told.** **The twenty-first day is 25
> March. This notice is three days old.**
>
> **What went wrong last time.** On the previous event, notice was issued on **day 36** and the claim
> of **1.34** was time-barred. **Of fourteen claim events last year, eleven were notified late,
> median 41 days.** **Two had no substantive claim behind them**, so the exposure is **nine events,
> about 2.1** — a figure I can defend line by line.
>
> **The standing instruction, in force since 1 March.** **Six categories of diary entry generate a
> notice within seven days, drafted by me**: ground conditions, verbal instructions, late free issue,
> access denied, utility strikes, weather above the contractual threshold. **The diary entry is the
> trigger. Site staff sign nothing and remember nothing.**
>
> *Source: FIDIC Conditions of Contract for Construction, Second Edition, 2017, published by the
> International Federation of Consulting Engineers, replacing the 1999 first edition. The form is a
> standard form intended to be completed and amended by Particular Conditions, which prevail over the
> printed General Conditions; its printed periods are frequently amended; it is written for works
> designed by the employer; and nothing here is legal advice or a substitute for the contract signed.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Give the date of awareness and nothing else that can be argued with. Every extra
> assertion in a notice is a sentence somebody can attack while the real deadline is already met.
