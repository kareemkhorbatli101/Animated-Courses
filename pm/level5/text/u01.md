# Unit 1 · How Good Is This Number?
%%SUB Five classes, a figure that was never wrong, and four million of argument about it%%
%%GOALS
State a range with an estimate
Say what level of definition a number was prepared at
Say what a class 5 figure can and cannot be used for
%%

A number without a range is not an estimate. A recommended practice first issued in 1997 divides
estimates into five classes by how much of the project has actually been defined, and prints the
range each class typically carries. Our budget came from the widest class, lost its range in a
meeting, and arrived back eighteen months later as an overrun.

> **Reader's Tip** — Look for two things: what decides an estimate's class, and what the practice
> says about its own accuracy ranges. The second is the part that was missing from our budget.

%%FIG pe01-f5 | A one-page internal note: a heading, a line giving the recommended practice, its number, its issuing body and the year it was first issued, a five-row table of classes with their definition percentages and typical ranges, a boxed paragraph about one figure prepared in two days, a short table comparing the outcome with the issued range, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

> ## Lindhurst · cost engineering note 1
> *Cost engineering · five classes, one missing step · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The document.** **AACE International Recommended Practice No. 18R-97**, *Cost Estimate
> Classification System — As Applied in Engineering, Procurement, and Construction for the Process
> Industries*, **first issued 1997** and revised since. It is a **recommended practice**, not a
> standard, and it classifies estimates by **degree of project definition**.
>
> **The five classes.** The primary characteristic is **how much of the project is defined** when the
> estimate is made, expressed as a percentage of complete definition.
>
> | Class | Project definition | Typical use | Method |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **5** | **0 – 2 %** | screening, concept | stochastic: factors, curves, reference projects |
> | **4** | **1 – 15 %** | study, feasibility | mostly stochastic |
> | **3** | **10 – 40 %** | **budget authorization, funding** | mixed |
> | **2** | **30 – 75 %** | control, bid | mostly deterministic |
> | **1** | **65 – 100 %** | check estimate, tender | deterministic: counted quantities |
>
> **The typical accuracy ranges printed in the practice**, as variation at the low and high ends:
>
> | Class | Low | High |
> |---|---|---|
> | **5** | −20 % to −50 % | **+30 % to +100 %** |
> | **4** | −15 % to −30 % | +20 % to +50 % |
> | **3** | −10 % to −20 % | +10 % to +30 % |
> | **2** | −5 % to −15 % | +5 % to +20 % |
> | **1** | −3 % to −10 % | +3 % to +15 % |
>
> **What happened here.**
>
> > **March, year one.** A figure was requested in **two days** for a stakeholder meeting. **Nothing
> > was designed.** O. Bondar issued **12 million**, marked **class 5**, with the range above and a
> > basis of estimate of one page listing six exclusions.
> >
> > **The board pack carried the figure and not the range.** The template had one cell.
> >
> > **The 12 million became the budget** at the authorization gate, where the practice says a
> > **class 3** estimate belongs. **No class 3 estimate was ever prepared.**
> >
> > **Outcome: 16.1 million.**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | Issued figure, class 5 | **12.0** |
> | Range issued with it | **6.0 to 24.0** |
> | Outcome | **16.1** |
> | Reported as | **a 4.1 million overrun, 34 percent** |
> | Actually | **inside the issued range, in its upper half** |
>
> **The estimate was never wrong. The budget was wrong, and they were the same number.**
>
> **What the practice says about its own ranges — and this matters here.** The ranges are
> **typical**, not guarantees. They assume **contingency is included**, that the **reference data is
> of reasonable quality**, and that the project does not carry unusual **systemic risks** such as
> new technology, an unfamiliar location or an unproven team. **Our reference project was in another
> country with different ground conditions and a different labor market.** On the practice's own
> terms, **our real range was wider than the printed one**, and I did not say so in March. **That is
> my omission, not the document's.**
>
> **What it does not do.** It gives **no cost of anything**: there are no rates, factors or curves in
> it. It is written **for engineering, procurement and construction in the process industries** and
> says so on its cover; much of our work is civil and control systems. It **does not tell you which
> class to use for which decision** beyond typical usage, and it **cannot stop a class 5 figure being
> used as a budget**, which is the only failure that occurred here.
>
> *Source: AACE International Recommended Practice No. 18R-97, Cost Estimate Classification System —
> As Applied in Engineering, Procurement, and Construction for the Process Industries, first issued
> 1997 and revised subsequently. It is a recommended practice rather than a standard; it classifies
> estimates by degree of project definition; its accuracy ranges are described as typical and
> dependent on the quality of reference information, the inclusion of contingency and the presence of
> systemic risks; and it contains no cost data of any kind.*

### 1 · Ranges and accuracy

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD TL5-01-06%%

> **Ebele:** I have been told we are four million over. Are we?
> **Oksana:** **We are four million above a figure that was never a budget.** The estimate was **12
> million at class 5**, issued **in March of year one** with a range of **minus fifty to plus one
> hundred percent**. **Between six and twenty-four million.** The outcome is **16.1**.
> **Ebele:** Nobody told me the range.
> **Oksana:** **The range was in my spreadsheet and the basis of estimate, and it did not reach the
> board pack**, which has one cell for a cost. **I should have refused to fill in a single cell.**
> **Ebele:** Could you not have been more accurate?
> **Oksana:** **In two days, with nothing designed, no.** **Accuracy at that stage is no better than
> plus or minus a very large number**, and the practice prints exactly how large. **Precision was
> available: I could have written 11.84. Accuracy was not.**
> **Ebele:** Explain that distinction to me properly.
> **Oksana:** **Precision is how many digits I write. Accuracy is how close I am.** **A figure of
> 11.843 million at class 5 is a lie told in three decimal places.** I wrote 12 deliberately.
> **Ebele:** So what should have happened?
> **Oksana:** **A class 3 estimate before the budget was locked.** Ten to forty percent defined,
> range about **minus twenty to plus thirty**. **That step is in the practice, it takes about five
> weeks, and nobody asked for it.**
> **Ebele:** Why not?
> **Oksana:** **Because a number already existed**, and a number that exists is very hard to replace
> with a wider one. **Asking for a new estimate looks like asking for permission to be more
> expensive.**
> **Ebele:** And the honest version of this project's history?
> **Oksana:** **An estimate that was correct, a budget that should never have been set from it, and
> a missing step that would have cost five weeks.** **Also one thing of mine**: our reference
> project was in another country with different ground, so **my real range was wider than the
> printed table**, and I did not say so.

*What distinction does Oksana draw between precision and accuracy?*

> **Grammar Note** — *Precision* and *accuracy* are not synonyms, and the difference costs money.
> *11.843 million* is precise and may be wildly inaccurate; *of the order of 12 million* is vague and

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Every organization says it wants honest estimates and every organization has a template with one
cell for a cost. The cell is not a conspiracy: a board paper has to fit on a page, and a range
invites a question that makes a meeting longer. But a single figure is silent about its own accuracy,
and silence is read as confidence, so the widest number in the project's life becomes the one
everybody remembers. There is a second habit behind the first. Asking for a new estimate at a better
level of definition looks, to almost everyone in the room, like asking for permission to be more
expensive — which means the person best placed to widen a number is the person with the most to lose
by widening it. The two cheapest fixes in this unit are a second cell in a template and one question
at the authorization gate: *what class is this estimate?* Neither costs anything, and both are
resisted, because both make visible how little was known when the number that everybody has been
quoting was made.

**Discuss:** Who at your organization would have to approve a wider number?

### 🗣 Group ranking

*In fours.* Twelve million at class 5, sixteen point one at the end. Rank these, best to worst. You
must agree.

a. No figure issued without a class, a range and a list of exclusions
b. A second cell in every board template, for the range
c. One question at the authorization gate: what class is this?
d. A class 3 estimate before any budget is locked, costing five weeks
e. State when your reference data makes the printed range too narrow

Report your order, and say which one your organization would resist.

> ### Basis of estimate · pumping station, concept
> *From O. Bondar · 14 March · for E. Okonjo and the estimate file · one page*
>
> **Estimate: 12 million. Class 5. Range: 6 to 24 million.** **This figure may not be used to set a
> budget.**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | Level of definition | **under 2 percent** — a site plan and a flow figure |
> | Class, per 18R-97 | **5** (screening and concept) |
> | Typical range at class 5 | **−50 % to +100 %** |
> | Range in figures | **6.0 to 24.0** |
> | Money year | **this year**, unescalated |
> | Method | **stochastic**: one reference project, factored |
> | Prepared in | **two days** |
>
> **Excluded.** Land. Connection charges. Client staff time. Tax of any kind. Escalation beyond the
> current year. Decommissioning of the existing plant.
>
> **Two statements about my own range.** The printed range **assumes reference data of reasonable
> quality.** My single reference project is **in another country, with different ground conditions
> and a different labor market**. **On the practice's own terms my range should be read as wider than
> the printed one**, and I would not defend the low end at all.
>
> **What would narrow it.** **A class 3 estimate: ten to forty percent defined, about five weeks,
> range roughly −20 % to +30 %.** **The practice places budget authorization at class 3**, and I
> recommend that no budget be set before it exists.
>
> **On precision.** I have written **12**, not 11.84. **Three decimal places at two percent
> definition would be false precision**, and somebody would quote them.
>
> *Source: AACE International Recommended Practice No. 18R-97, Cost Estimate Classification System
> — As Applied in Engineering, Procurement, and Construction for the Process Industries, first
> issued 1997 and revised subsequently. A recommended practice rather than a standard; estimates are
> classified by degree of project definition; the accuracy ranges are typical and depend on reference
> data quality, the inclusion of contingency and systemic risks; the practice contains no cost data,
> and is written for the process industries.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Put the range in the same sentence as the figure, and state the prohibition in
> the first paragraph. A range in a later paragraph does not travel with the number.

### 3 · Write

Write **130 to 150 words**: a basis of estimate.

**Give the figure with its range in the same sentence. State the level of definition as a percentage
and the class. Give the range twice, once as percentages and once as absolute figures. List at least
four exclusions. State the money year. Say what the figure may not be used for, and what would
narrow the range and how long that would take. Use *between … and*, *no better than* and *of the
order of*.**

**Read your basis of estimate aloud to your partner first.** Then check your own against the list,
and swap.

| | ✓ |
|---|---|
| Is the range in the same sentence as the figure? | |
| Are at least four exclusions listed? | |
| Is the prohibition in the first paragraph? | |

**Peer feedback.** Read your partner's sheet as the sponsor. Would you set a budget from it?

**Then talk it over.** *In pairs, out loud.* What would your own widest honest range be?

> A figure of 12 million was prepared in two days, when less than two percent of a project had been
> defined. It was issued as a class 5 estimate with a range of minus fifty to plus one hundred per
> cent, which is six to twenty-four million, and with a basis of estimate listing six exclusions.
>
> The board pack carried the figure and not the range, because the template had one cell. The figure
> became the budget at the authorization gate, where the recommended practice places a class 3
> estimate: ten to forty percent defined, five weeks of work, and a range of about minus twenty to
> plus thirty percent. No class 3 estimate was prepared.
>
> The project cost 16.1 million. This was reported as an overrun of 4.1 million, or thirty-four per
> cent.
>
> Oksana's note on the file is three sentences. *The estimate was correct and the budget was wrong,
> and they were the same number, which is the only interesting fact in this file. A template with one
> cell for a cost deletes the range every time and nobody has to intend it, so the range must be in
> the same sentence as the figure or it does not travel. My own fault is smaller and real: the
> printed range assumes decent reference data and mine came from another country, so I should have
> written a wider range than the table gave me and I wrote the table's.*

2. What does a one-cell template do, and does anybody have to intend it?
3. Is it fair to report 16.1 million as a thirty-four percent overrun?
