# Unit 6 · The Warning Nobody Gave
%%SUB Thirty-one early warnings, four that changed anything, and eleven weeks with no accepted plan%%
%%GOALS
Use the contract present tense
Give an early warning before you have a solution
Say what a target contract shares and with whom
%%

A contract published in June 2017 is written in the present tense and expects to be administered
weekly: you notify, I reply within two weeks, we meet. Its most valuable clause costs nothing to use
and asks you to speak before you know the answer, which is the one thing nobody in our industry is
trained to do.

> **The contract.** The **NEC4 Engineering and Construction Contract**, published **June 2017** by
> the **Institution of Civil Engineers**, replacing the previous edition. Two things about it are
> unlike any other form we use.
>
> **First, the tense.** It is written in the **present tense**, in short clauses, with almost no legal
> vocabulary: *the contractor notifies the project manager*, *the project manager replies within two

> be. **It does not describe what the parties shall do in some future dispute; it describes what they

> solution*. Not *once you are certain*.**

### 5 · Word study — the contract present tense

**5A  Complete the chart.**

%%FIG pe06-f6 | A three-column chart headed the structure, what it does and an example, with rows for the present simple for obligations, replies within, if then present, does not, is treated as and gives an early warning of, four filled in and two empty.%%

| Structure | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| present simple for an obligation | states what happens, not what shall happen | The contractor **notifies** the project manager. |
| **replies within** | attaches a period to a party | The project manager **replies within** two weeks. |
| *if* + present, present | states the consequence | **If** the event **is** a compensation event, the project manager **instructs** a quotation. |
| **does not** | states the negative obligation | The contractor **does not** proceed without an instruction. |
| **is treated as** | | |
| **gives an early warning of** | | |

> **Grammar Note** — The present simple in a contract is not casual; it is an obligation expressed as
> a fact. *The contractor notifies* means *must*, and reads as a description of normal work rather
> than a threat — which is the whole design intent. The risk is the opposite one: people read it as
> advice.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

The most valuable clause in this contract is free, takes four lines to use, and is routinely
unused — because using it requires saying *I have a problem and no answer*, and every engineer in
every country has been trained that bringing a problem without a solution is unprofessional. That
training is good for most purposes and catastrophic here, because the value of a warning is exactly
the float that exists on the day it is given, and float only shrinks. There is a second habit to
watch, which is mutual and quieter: the documents the contract depends on go unmaintained because
chasing them is nobody's job. A programme submitted and not accepted leaves both parties working to
nothing, and assessments then have no baseline, which converts arithmetic into argument. The fix in
both cases is the same shape — make it mechanical and give it a name. One line in the weekly report
saying whether an accepted programme exists. And one senior person raising a warning, out loud, with
no solution attached, so that everybody else can see it is permitted.

**Discuss:** When did somebody senior at your organization last say *I do not have a solution*?

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Early warning · utility diversion, chainage 800
> *From H. Sato, for the contractor · week 3 · to the project manager · copied to the register*
>
> **1. This is an early warning under the contract.**
>
> **2. The utility diversion could delay completion and could increase the total of the prices.** The
> trench reaches chainage 800 in **week 14** on the accepted programme. **The diversion is not yet
> programmed by the diversion contractor**, and we have no date from them.
>
> **3. We do not have a proposed solution.** We have identified **three possible sequences** and
> cannot price or commit to any of them without a date from the diversion contractor.
>
> **4. We propose an early warning meeting this Thursday**, with the diversion contractor present,
> and we suggest the matter is entered in the register with an owner on both sides.
>
> ---
>
> *Internal note, not part of the warning.*
>
> **Why this goes out today.** **Today there are eleven weeks of float and three options. Float only
> shrinks.** **An early warning is worth exactly the float that exists on the day it is given**, and
> the same warning in week 14 is worth nothing because by then it is a compensation event with one
> option.
>
> **What this warning does not do.** **It does not say whose fault anything is**, and the clause does
> not ask. **It does not commit us to a solution, a sequence or a price.** **It does not waive or
> create any entitlement**: if this becomes a compensation event, it is notified separately within
> its own period.
>
> **The honest arithmetic of our register.** **Thirty-one entries in fourteen months; four changed
> the plan; twenty-seven were recorded and closed with no action.** **One of the four — the crane
> resequencing in month six — saved about 380.** **I am not going to defend a hit rate; I am going to
> defend one entry.**
>
> **And the gap I am closing this week.** **We had no accepted programme for eleven weeks**, because
> one was submitted, not accepted, and not chased by either side. **Three compensation events were
> assessed with no baseline, and the argument ran four months.** **From this week the weekly report
> carries one line: accepted programme — yes or no, and its date.**
>
> *Source: the NEC4 Engineering and Construction Contract, published June 2017 by the Institution of
> Civil Engineers. The contract is written in the present tense and designed to be actively
> administered within short stated periods; its option clauses and contract data determine what is
> agreed in any particular case; compensation event assessment depends on an accepted programme; and
> nothing here is legal advice or a substitute for the contract signed.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Say *we do not have a solution* in its own numbered line. Buried in a paragraph
> it reads as weakness; standing alone it reads as the contract being used correctly.
