# Unit 7 · What the Price Does Not Include
%%SUB Three words on a purchase order, a transformer damaged on a road, and 186,000 nobody had insured%%
%%GOALS
State the limits of what a term covers
Name a delivery term with its place and its version
Say where risk passes and where it does not
%%

Eleven three-letter rules, published by a chamber of commerce and revised in 2020, say who arranges
transport, who pays for what, and where risk passes from seller to buyer. They say nothing about when
you own the goods, when you pay, or what happens if somebody breaks the contract — and our purchase
order used one of them for a journey it does not cover.

> *Commercial · three transformers, one road · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The rules.** The **Incoterms 2020** rules, published by the **International Chamber of

> two families.**
>
> | For any mode of transport | For sea and inland waterway only |
> |---|---|
> | **EXW** ex works | **FAS** free alongside ship |
> | **FCA** free carrier | **FOB** free on board |
> | **CPT** carriage paid to | **CFR** cost and freight |
> | **CIP** carriage and insurance paid to | **CIF** cost, insurance and freight |
> | **DAP** delivered at place | |
> | **DPU** delivered at place unloaded | |
> | **DDP** delivered duty paid | |
>
> **Each rule settles three things**: who **arranges** carriage and insurance, who **pays** which
> costs, and **where risk passes** from seller to buyer. **The three letters must be followed by a
> named place, and by the edition year.**
>
> **What our purchase order said: “CIF site”.**
>
> > **CIF is a sea and inland waterway rule.** Under it the seller delivers **when the goods are on
> > board the vessel**, and **risk passes there** — at the **port of shipment**, not at a
> > destination. **The place named after CIF is a destination port.**
> >
> > **Our site is 40 km inland.**
> >
> > A transformer was **damaged on the road between the port and the site**. **Risk had passed on
> > board, some 1,900 km earlier.**
> >
> > **The I in CIF is insurance, and that is where the second trap sits.** Under **CIF** the seller
> > must insure at the **minimum level** of cover, for the **sea leg to the named port**. **The road
> > leg was not covered.** We had arranged nothing ourselves, because the word *site* in our own
> > order had persuaded everybody that delivery happened there.
> >
> > **Loss: 186. Uninsured. Entirely ours. The supplier complied with the contract exactly.**
>
> **Why the version year matters.** Between the **2010** and **2020** editions, the insurance
> obligation under **CIP** was **raised to a higher level of cover**, while **CIF remained at the
> minimum**. **DAT was renamed DPU.** **If a contract names a rule with no edition year, nobody can
> say which obligation was bought** — and our template offered three terms in a drop-down **with no
> year at all**.
>
> **What I found in fourteen purchase orders.**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | Orders reviewed | **14** |
> | Named a rule with **no version year** | **9** |
> | Used a **sea rule for an inland delivery** | **4** |
> | Said **“DDP, duties excluded”** | **1** |
> | Named no place at all, only three letters | **2** |
>
> **“DDP, duties excluded” is a contradiction in four words**: the last D stands for duty
> paid. Nobody noticed for eleven months because no shipment under that order was ever stopped.
>
> **What the rules do not deal with, in the publisher's own terms.** They do **not** transfer
> **ownership or title**. They say nothing about the **price** or **payment terms**. They do not
> determine the **law applicable** to the contract, nor the forum for disputes. They do not provide
> **remedies for breach**. They do not deal with **sanctions, prohibitions or tariffs**, and they are
> **not themselves a contract of sale** — they are incorporated into one. **Every one of those
> exclusions is a question somebody will eventually ask you**, and *CIF* is not the answer to any of
> them.
>
> **The fix, two lines in the template.** Every order for goods now states: **a rule, a named place,
> and the words “Incoterms 2020”**; and, where the destination is inland, **only a rule
> from the any-mode family.** **Twenty-two orders since: no sea rule for an inland site, no missing
> year, no missing place.**
>
> *Source: the Incoterms 2020 rules, published by the International Chamber of Commerce and in force
> from 1 January 2020, replacing the 2010 edition. The rules allocate the arrangement of carriage and

> with the transfer of ownership, the price or payment terms, the law applicable to the contract,
> dispute resolution, remedies for breach, sanctions, prohibitions or tariffs. Nothing here is legal

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Three letters look like a complete commercial arrangement and are nothing of the kind. They settle
carriage, cost and risk, and they are silent on ownership, payment, governing law and remedies — the
four questions people actually fight about. Organizations compound this by putting delivery terms in
a drop-down list: a list implies the options are interchangeable, offers no place to name a port or a
site, and carries no edition year, so the least informed person in the chain makes a choice whose
consequences surface years later on a wet road. There is a second pattern worth watching: a term can
be wrong for eleven months with no symptom at all. Nothing goes wrong while nothing goes wrong. Then
one shipment is damaged on a leg nobody insured, and the loss is not the cost of the goods but the
cost of a template that was never read by anybody who knew what the letters meant.

**Discuss:** What else in your organization is chosen from a drop-down list by whoever is nearest?

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Purchase order amendment · transformers 2 and 3
> *From H. Sato · 21 November · for L. Eriksen and the order file · one page*
>
> **Delivery term, replacing “CIF site”: Delivered at Place — Calder valley site,
> compound gate — Incoterms 2020.**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | **Risk** | passes when the goods are **at our disposal on the arriving vehicle** at the named place |
> | **Carriage** | arranged and paid by the **seller**, to the named place |
> | **Unloading** | **ours** |
> | **Export clearance** | **seller** |
> | **Import clearance and duties** | **ours** |
> | **Insurance** | seller's own arrangement to the named place; **we carry separate cover for unloading and on-site handling** |
>
> **Why the term changed.** **CIF is a sea and inland waterway rule.** Under it the seller delivers
> **on board the vessel** and **risk passes at the port of shipment**; the place named after CIF is a
> **destination port**, not an inland site. **Our site is 40 km inland.** On transformer 1, risk had
> passed **about 1,900 km before** the damage occurred, **on a road leg that nobody had insured**,
> and the loss of **186** fell entirely to us. **The supplier complied with the order exactly.**
>
> **Why the version year is now stated.** Between the **2010** and **2020** editions the insurance
> level required under **CIP was raised**, while **CIF remained at minimum cover**, and **DAT was
> renamed DPU**. **Subject to which edition is named, identical letters carry different
> obligations.** **Our template named none.**
>
> **What this term does not deal with, stated here so that nobody assumes it does.** It is **silent
> on the transfer of title**, **says nothing about the price or payment terms**, **does not determine
> the governing law** or the forum for disputes, and **provides no remedy for breach**. **Those sit
> in the conditions of contract.** The rules are **incorporated into a contract of sale and are not
> one.**
>
> **Two template changes requested.** **One: every order for goods states a rule, a named place and
> the words “Incoterms 2020”.** **Two: where the destination is inland, only the any-mode
> family may be used.** **Of fourteen orders reviewed, nine named no year, four used a sea rule for
> an inland site, two named no place, and one said “DDP, duties excluded”.** **Twenty-two
> orders since the change: all clean.**
>
> *Source: the Incoterms 2020 rules, published by the International Chamber of Commerce, in force
> from 1 January 2020 and replacing the 2010 edition. They allocate the arrangement of carriage and
> insurance, the division of costs and the point at which risk passes; they are incorporated into a
> contract of sale and are not themselves one; and they do not deal with the transfer of ownership,
> the price or payment terms, the applicable law, dispute resolution, remedies for breach, sanctions,
> prohibitions or tariffs. Nothing here is legal advice.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Put the list of what the term does not cover on the order itself. It costs four
> lines and it stops the next person treating three letters as the whole agreement.
