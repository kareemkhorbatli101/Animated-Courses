# Unit 4 · Who Does What
%%SUB Four roles, one signature, and a standard that gives you functions rather than job titles%%
%%GOALS
Say who is responsible for what
Use *report to* and *be accountable for*
Name the four roles on a project
%%

A project needs somebody to decide, somebody to pay, somebody to run the work and somebody to do it.
In a small project that can be two people. There is an International Standard with guidance on the
roles, and it is careful about one thing: it describes **functions**, not job titles.

> ## Lindhurst · project office note 4
> *Project office · roles and decisions · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The standard. ISO 21502**, *Project, programme and portfolio management — Guidance on project
> management*, dated **2020**. It is **guidance**. It describes **practices** and **roles**, and it
> uses the word *should*, not *must*.
>
> **The four roles, as we use them.**
>
> | Role | What the person does here |
> |---|---|
> | **sponsor** | pays for the project, signs, is **accountable** for the benefit |
> | **project board** | takes decisions above the manager's limit; meets monthly |
> | **project manager** | runs the work, **reports to** the board, is **responsible** for delivery |
> | **team member** | does the work, owns one or more work packages |
>
> **Responsible and accountable.**
>
> > These two words are used as if they were the same word, and they are not. **The person
> > *responsible* does the work and answers for the work. The person *accountable* answers for the
> > result, even though somebody else did the work.**
> >
> > On our pump station project: **Nadia is responsible for delivering it. Ebele is accountable for
> > whether the water company gets less water loss.** If the pumps are late, somebody asks Nadia what
> > happened. If the water loss does not fall, somebody asks Ebele why we built it.
>
> **The limits, written down.** A project manager here may decide alone:
>
> - up to **20,000** of unplanned cost;
> - up to **two weeks** of delay to the end date;
> - **any** change that does not affect cost, date or scope.
>
> **Above those limits the decision goes to the board.** **Before this was written down, the real
> limit was "whatever Nadia thought she could defend", which is not a limit at all.**
>
> **What the standard does not do.** It does not give **job titles** — the roles are **functions**,
> and **one person may hold two or three of them** on a small project. It does not say who in our
> organization should hold them. It gives **no limits**: the three numbers above are ours. And it is
> **not certifiable**: there is nothing here to pass or fail.
>
> *Source: ISO 21502:2020, Project, programme and portfolio management &mdash; Guidance on project
> management. It describes practices for managing a project and the roles involved. It is guidance,
> not requirements: it prescribes no job titles or organizational structure, sets no decision limits,
> and is not a standard against which anybody is certified.*

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Two things are missing from most project role descriptions, and both are missing for the same
reason: writing them down commits a named person. The first is the sponsor's name. A department is
easier to type than a person, it offends nobody, and it cannot sign, attend a board or answer a
telephone on a Thursday. The second is the decision limit — the amount of money and the number of
weeks a project manager may decide alone. Without it, the real limit is whatever the manager
believes they can defend afterwards, which means that two people in the same company are working to
different limits and neither knows it. Both repairs fit on one page, and both require somebody more
senior to agree to be named, which is why neither has been done.

**Discuss:** What is your decision limit, and who told you?
