# Unit 9 · Four Stages of a New Team
%%SUB Four words that rhyme, from a review of fifty studies in 1965%%
%%GOALS
Use sequencing words to describe stages
Name the four stages of a new group
Say what the 1965 paper was, and was not, about
%%

A new team is polite, then it argues, then it agrees how to work, and then it works. Four words were
given to those stages in a paper in 1965. They are the most quoted words in team management and they
come from a review of studies, most of which were not about project teams at all.

> ## Lindhurst · project office note 9
> *Project office · a new team · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The paper. Bruce W. Tuckman**, *Developmental sequence in small groups*, in the journal
> **Psychological Bulletin**, **1965**. It is a **review**: Tuckman read about **fifty studies** of
> small groups and found the same pattern in them.
>
> **The four stages.**
>
> | Stage | What it looks like |
> |---|---|
> | **forming** | polite, careful, nobody disagrees, everybody waits |
> | **storming** | open disagreement about the work, the roles or the leader |
> | **norming** | the group agrees how it will work |
> | **performing** | the group does the work, and disagreements are about the work |
>
> **A fifth stage was added later.** In **1977**, Tuckman and Mary Ann Jensen added **adjourning**:
> the group ends and the people go. **Most people who quote the four words have never heard of the
> fifth.**
>
> **What the studies were actually about.**
>
> > This is the part that is almost never mentioned. **Most of the fifty studies were of therapy
> > groups and laboratory groups, not project teams.** Tuckman says so himself.
> >
> > **The pattern still looks true on our projects, and that is not the same as evidence.** It is a
> > description of groups, from 1965, that we use because it names something everybody has seen.
>
> **What it does not tell us.**
>
> - **How long a stage takes.** Our pump station team took three weeks in the second stage. The
>   team before it took one afternoon.
> - **That the order is fixed.** Groups go back. **When one new person joined our team in March, we
>   had two days of the second stage again.**
> - **What to do.** It is **descriptive**, not a method. There are no actions in it.
>
> **What we do with it anyway.** One thing: **in week one of a new team we say out loud that the
> arguing will come, and that it is stage two and not a failure.** It does not stop the arguing. **It
> stops two or three people concluding that the team is broken.**
>
> *Source: Bruce W. Tuckman, Developmental sequence in small groups, Psychological Bulletin, 1965, a
> review of about fifty studies of small groups, proposing four stages; with a fifth stage,
> adjourning, added by Tuckman and Mary Ann Jensen in 1977. It is descriptive, not prescriptive: it
> gives no durations, no actions and no guarantee of order, and most of the groups reviewed were not
> project teams.*

### 1 · Sequencing

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD TL1-09-06%%

> **Chidi:** My new team had a terrible meeting yesterday. Two people shouted.
> **Nadia:** Tell me what happened, in order.
> **Chidi:** **First**, everybody was very polite for two weeks. **Then** Wei said the test plan was
> impossible. **After that** Mariama said the design was late, which it is. **Finally** they both
> looked at me.
> **Nadia:** And what did you do?
> **Chidi:** I said we would discuss it next week.
> **Nadia:** That is the only thing I would change. **You moved the argument to next week, and next
> week it will be bigger.**
> **Chidi:** It felt like the meeting was out of control.
> **Nadia:** It was the second stage and it arrived on time. **Two weeks of politeness, then an
> argument about the work: that is a team becoming a team.** The teams I worry about are the ones that
> are still polite in month three.
> **Chidi:** Why?
> **Nadia:** Because the disagreement has not gone. **It is being had in corridors instead of in the
> room, and I find out about it in month six, from somebody resigning.**
> **Chidi:** So what should I have said?

> rules while everybody is still in the room** — who decides a test is ready, how late is late, and
> what happens when a date moves.

> **Nadia:** **Write them down, because the third stage is the one everybody skips.** The group

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Four rhyming words from a 1965 review of about fifty studies — most of them not about project teams
— are the most quoted idea in team management, and they are useful for one specific reason: they
give a manager something to say in week three. A new team that argues has not broken; it has
arrived. What the four words do not do is tell you how long a stage lasts, or guarantee that a group
moves forward rather than back. The stage everybody skips is the third: the group agrees in the room
how it will work, nobody writes it down, and six weeks later two people remember the agreement
differently, which puts them back in the argument with no record of the first one. The cheapest
version of the third stage is one page, five single lines, and the initials of everybody present —
read aloud on the first day of the next person who joins.

**Discuss:** Has your team ever written down how it will work?

> ### Team agreement · depot booking screen
> *Agreed in the room, 4 April · six people present*
>
> **How we will work — five rules, one line each.**
>
> 1. **Wei Zhao decides** when a test is ready. Nobody else.
> 2. **Late means more than two working days.** Under that, nobody needs to say anything.
> 3. **When a date moves, the owner tells the Monday meeting the same day** — not at the next
>    meeting.
> 4. **One owner per package, and the owner is a person, not a team.**
> 5. **Disagreements are finished in the room.** If we cannot finish one, we write down what we
>    disagree about before we leave.
>
> **Signed by all six** — W.Z., M.B., L.T., A.S., H.V., C.N. — **4 April**.
>
> **Why this page exists.** The team was polite for two weeks. **Then, on 1 April, there was a
> two-hour argument about the test plan and the design dates. Both complaints were true.** We
> postponed the discussion, which made it worse, and finished it on the 3rd. **These five rules came
> out of that argument, and they would not exist without it.**
>
> **What happens when somebody joins.** **We read this page aloud on their first day.** In March a
> person joined another team here and it went back two days into open disagreement — **not because of
> anything he did, but because the rules were in five heads and not on a page**.
>
> **What this page is not.** **It is not a method.** The four stage names we use for this come from a
> **1965 review of about fifty studies of small groups, most of which were not project teams**. It
> describes what groups do. **It does not say how long anything takes, and it does not promise that a
> team moves forward.**
>
> *Source: Bruce W. Tuckman, Developmental sequence in small groups, Psychological Bulletin, 1965
> &mdash; a review proposing four stages, with a fifth added by Tuckman and Jensen in 1977. It is
> descriptive: no durations, no actions, and no guarantee of order.*

> **Writer's Tip** — One line per rule. A rule that needs two lines is two rules, and a page of
