# Unit 11 · Who Is Told, and When
%%SUB Four months amber, one month red, and a rule with a number and a day in it%%
%%GOALS

Somebody runs the project. Somebody else decides whether it continues. The second person can only
decide with what the first person sends them, and that is the whole of governance: who is told, what
they are told, and when.

### 1 · The passive for reporting

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD TL1-11-06%%

> **Hanna:** The pack says amber. It said amber in March.
> **Nadia:** **It was amber in March and it is amber now.** Both are true.
> **Hanna:** Then why **was** nothing **said** to the board?
> **Nadia:** **Something was said. The same thing, four times.** The square **was sent**, the
> commentary **was written**, and the board read it and moved on. **Nothing was hidden. Nothing was
> asked either.**
> **Hanna:** Because it looked the same every month.
> **Nadia:** A square that does not change looks like a project being managed. **The information was
> in the pack and it was invisible, which is almost the same as not being there.**
> **Hanna:** So we need a brighter color.
> **Nadia:** We need a rule. **Amber twice is reported as red.** The project is not worse in month
> two than month one. **But the board has to be told the thing the square cannot say, which is: this
> has not moved.**
> **Hanna:** And that is a report about the report.
> **Nadia:** **Yes. Most useful reporting is.** Not *how is the project*, but *what has changed since
> the last time you were told*.
> **Hanna:** Will the board like it?
> **Nadia:** No. **Three projects were turned red on the day the rule started and not one of them had
> got worse that week.** Two were recovered in six weeks because somebody senior finally asked a
> question.
> **Hanna:** And the third?
> **Nadia:** **The third was stopped**, eight months earlier than it would have been. **That is the
> saving, and nobody will ever put it in a report, because a project that was stopped early looks
> like a failure and not like 400,000 we did not spend.**

*Why does Nadia say the information was invisible?*

> **Grammar Note** — The passive is useful when the process matters more than the person: *decisions

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Everywhere in the world, status reporting fails in the same direction, and almost never by lying.
Each individual report is true; the color is defensible; the commentary is accurate. What the
reports cannot say is *this has not moved*, because a status is a photograph and the problem is a
film. So a project stays amber for four months, every report is correct, the board asks nothing, and
everyone is surprised in month five. The repair is not more honesty from the manager — they were
honest. It is a rule with a number and a day in it, applied by the calendar rather than by a person:
amber twice is red. Then nobody has to be brave to say red, which matters, because the person who
says red first is the one whose name is in the minutes, and the saving from stopping a project eight
months early never appears in any report as a saving.

**Discuss:** Would you be the first person to say red?
