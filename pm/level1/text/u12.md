# Unit 12 · One Page for the Whole Project
%%SUB Twelve units, one page, and the same word defined four different ways%%
%%GOALS
Write a one-page project brief
Review the twelve structures of this book
Say why a definition belongs to the document it is in
%%

A project brief is one page. It says what you are making, by when, for how much, who decides, what
might go wrong and how anybody will know. Everything in this book fits on it, which is the point of
the page and the point of the book.

> **Reader's Tip** — The standard defines words. Look for the sentence that says where its

%%FIG pa12-f5 | A one-page internal note: a heading, a line giving the standard and its year, a four-row table of the same word defined in four documents, a boxed paragraph about an empty box, a six-row table of the brief, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

> ## Lindhurst · project office note 12
> *Project office · the one-page brief · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The standard. ISO 9000**, *Quality management systems — Fundamentals and vocabulary*, dated
> **2015**. It is the document that **defines the words** used across the quality management family:
> *requirement*, *process*, *risk*, *objective*, and **project**.
>
> **One word, four documents.**
>
> | Where | Roughly what it says |
> |---|---|
> | **ISO 9000:2015** | a set of activities with a start and a finish date, undertaken to reach an objective, subject to constraints |
> | **ISO 21506:2018** | the vocabulary document for project, programme and portfolio management |
> | **ISO 21502:2020** | guidance on managing a project, with roles and practices |
> | **our brief** | the sentence **we** agree with the sponsor, in writing, for **this** project |
>
> **None of them is wrong and none of them travels.** **Each definition belongs to the document it is
> in**, and a definition written for quality management systems is not written to settle an argument
> on a building site. **The only definition that governs your project is the one on your page, signed
> by somebody.**
>
> **The six boxes.**
>
> | Box | What goes in it |
> |---|---|
> | **1 result** | what exists at the end that does not exist now |
> | **2 date** | one date, and what it is a date *for* |
> | **3 cost** | one number, and what is not in it |
> | **4 decider** | one name, with a **limit** |
> | **5 risks** | **five** rows, each with a name and a date |
> | **6 measure** | the number that will show whether it worked |
>
> **The empty box.**
>
> > A brief came back last year with *improve operational efficiency* in box 1. **It is writing, and
> > it is an empty box.** Three people read it and three of them described a different project.
> >
> > We now test every box the same way: **can a stranger act on it tomorrow?** *Improve efficiency*
> > fails. *Cut the time from order to dispatch from 11 days to 6 days* passes.
> >
> > **Of eighteen briefs rewritten on one page, eleven had an empty box 6.** Nobody had been asked
> > how anyone would know whether it had worked.
>
> **What the standard does not do.** It gives **no brief**, **no template**, and **no method**. It
> defines terms **for use in the quality management family**, and **ISO 9000 is not the certification
> standard** — that is a different document. **A definition tells you what a word means. It does not
> tell you what to do on Monday.**
>
> *Source: ISO 9000:2015, Quality management systems &mdash; Fundamentals and vocabulary. It sets out
> fundamental concepts and principles and defines the terms used across the quality management
> family. It is a vocabulary and concepts document: it contains no requirements, no templates and no
> project method, its definitions are written for use within that family of standards, and it is not
> itself a certification standard.*

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Everywhere, the project document grows and the project gets harder to describe. Sixty pages can all
be true and still have no point, because length is not the same as clarity and nobody has read page
forty. One page forces six decisions that a folder lets you postpone, and the box people postpone
longest is the last one: how will anybody know whether this worked. It is the box that decides
whether your organization learns anything, and it is empty in most briefs in most countries, because
filling it in means accepting in advance that the answer might be no. A measure written at the start
is a promise to find out. That is why it feels dangerous, and why a project without one can run for
nine months with everybody working hard and nobody able to say whether it is working.

**Discuss:** Would you write down a measure that might show your project did not work?

> true**. **The page is the project; the file is the evidence.** Where they disagree, **the page is
> changed, with a date, in front of everybody.**
>
> **On the words.** Four documents define *project* and none of them identically, **because each
> definition belongs to the document it is in**. They gave us the vocabulary. **They did not give us
> this page, and none of them claims to.**
>
> *Source: ISO 9000:2015, Quality management systems &mdash; Fundamentals and vocabulary. It sets out
> fundamental concepts and principles and defines the terms used across the quality management
> family. It contains no requirements, no templates and no project method; its definitions are
> written for use within that family of standards; and it is not itself a certification standard.
> The six boxes, the limits and the stranger test are ours.*
