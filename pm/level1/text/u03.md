# Unit 3 · Breaking the Work Down
%%SUB A tree of work with no dates in it, and the level where people stop%%
%%GOALS
Use part-and-whole language
Say what a work breakdown structure is
Say what it does not contain
%%

Take all the work and cut it into pieces. Cut the pieces into smaller pieces. Stop when a piece is
small enough for one person to own. The result is a tree with no dates in it, and almost every
problem later in a project can be found in the level where somebody stopped cutting.

> ## Lindhurst · project office note 3
> *Project office · the work breakdown structure · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The standard. ISO 21511**, *Work breakdown structures for project and programme management*,
> dated **2018**. It is **guidance**. It describes what a good structure looks like. It does not tell
> us how many levels to have.
>
> **What the tree is.** The whole scope of the project, broken down into parts, and the parts broken
> down again. The top box **is** the project. The lowest boxes are **work packages**.
>
> | Level | Our pump station project |
> |---|---|
> | **1** | the whole project — one box |
> | **2** | four parts: civil work, pumps, electrical, handover |
> | **3** | twelve work packages — three or four under each part |
>

>
> > Not when it reaches a certain number of levels. **A package is small enough when one person can
> > own it and can say today whether it is half finished.**
> >

> > to "how is it going?" was "fine", because nobody could measure it.** It is now four packages:
> > order, deliver, install, test. **Three of the four are finished and the fourth is not, and
> > everybody can see that.**
>

>
> - **No dates.** The tree says *what*. The bar chart says *when*.
> - **No order.** Nothing in the tree says which package comes first.
> - **No names of teams.** Who does the work is a different structure.
>

> include the same piece of pipework, and both teams thought the other one was doing it.**

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

*Fine* is the most common word in project reporting, and it is usually not a lie. It is what an
honest person says when the work they own has no visible end for four months. The fault is in the
structure, not the person: one box, one owner, no measurable point before April, and no amount of
better questioning will produce a number that does not exist. Cutting the box into four — order,
deliver, install, test — changes the conversation from a mood to a count, and it takes an afternoon.
The second habit is the line nobody writes: what a package does **not** include. Two packages that
each quietly include the same twelve meters of pipework will both be reported as fine, by two people
who each believe the other is doing it, until somebody asks who tested it.

**Discuss:** Which piece of work at your company is reported as *fine* every week?

**Read your card aloud to your partner first.** Then check your own against the list, and swap.
