A guide changed its own shape in 2021. The edition before it described forty-nine processes; the new
one describes twelve principles. Both editions are honest about what they are. The expensive part
was not the change. It was that nobody told the hundred and twelve pages of company procedure built
on the old one.

> *Project office · principles and processes · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The document.** *A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge*, **seventh edition**,

>
> **What changed.**
>
> | Sixth edition, 2017 | Seventh edition, 2021 |
> |---|---|
> | **5** process groups | **12** principles |
> | **10** knowledge areas | **8** performance domains |
> | **49** processes, with inputs, tools and outputs | **models, methods and artifacts**, with tailoring |
>
> **The reason given.** Projects are delivered in many different ways, and **a single process list
> fits some of them badly**. Principles travel between a construction project and a software
> release; a process list does not. **This is a real argument, made openly in the guide.**
>
> **The twelve principles, in short.** Stewardship. Team. Stakeholders. Value. Systems thinking.
> Leadership. Tailoring. Quality. Complexity. Risk. Adaptability and resilience. Change.
>
> **What it cost us.**
>
> > Our company procedure was written in **2018** against the sixth edition: **112 pages**, with the
> > forty-nine processes mapped to our own forms.
> >
> > **Of those 112 pages, 71 describe processes that no longer appear in the guide's structure.**
> >
> > **Not one of the 71 has become wrong.** A procedure for raising a change request still works.
> > **It simply no longer has a parent**, and anybody who goes looking for its authority will not
> > find it.
> >
> > **Our response in 2021 was to keep the 112 pages and tell nobody**, which is what most
> > organizations did, and it is defensible for about three years.
>
> **The Monday test.**
>
> > We took the twelve principles and asked one question about each: **what does a named person do
> > differently on Monday morning?**
> >
> > | | |
> > |---|---|
> > | principles that changed something | **3** |
> > | principles nobody could turn into an action | **9** |
> >
> > The three were **tailoring** (we wrote down what we had tailored, which we had never done),
> > **value** (the benefit owner in the client's plan, from Book 2) and **quality** (the eleven-line
> > process record, from Book 3).
> >
> > **The nine are not empty sentences.** They are **true, general and unfalsifiable at the level
> > of an individual Monday** — and a principle that told you what to do on Monday would be a
> > process, which is exactly what the edition had removed. **That is the trade, and it is

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Abstract nouns survive edits in every language, because nobody can disagree with them. *Engagement
has been improved*, *governance is embedded*, *assurance is in place*: each of those sentences has
passed a review in every organization on earth, and not one of them says who did what to whom. The
test that costs nothing is to put a person, a verb and a date back in, and the sentence either
becomes useful or disappears. The second habit in this unit is harder and fairer. When a method
changes shape, the pages built on the old shape do not become wrong — they lose their parent, and
the honest sentence is *nothing in this is incorrect and seventy-one pages have no authority behind
them any more*. That sentence keeps the people who wrote those pages in the room, which is the only
way the next edition gets adopted at all.

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Method record · Lindhurst project office
> *From A. Holm · 14 January · for E. Okonjo and the project office · one page*
>
> **What we use, and against which document.** *A Guide to the Project Management Body of
> Knowledge*, **seventh edition, 2021**, published by the **Project Management Institute**.
>
> | Element | Decision | Reason | Owner |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Tailoring | **adopted** | we now record what we do not use | A. Holm |
> | Value | **adopted** | the benefit has an owner in the client's plan | N. Rahimi |
> | Quality | **adopted** | eleven-line process record, each line named | W. Zhao |
> | Measurement | **tailored** | we report twelve lines with methods and collectors | H. Virtanen |
> | Uncertainty | **tailored** | matrix for screening, bow tie quarterly on the top five | I. Berg |
> | Team | **not used as written** | our teams are contractual, not assigned | A. Holm |
> | Stakeholders | **not used as written** | the client owns stakeholder work by contract | E. Caruso |
>
> **The Monday test.** We asked of each of the twelve principles: **what does a named person do
> differently on Monday morning?** **Three changed something. Nine could not be turned into an
> action by anybody in the room.** **The nine are not empty** — they are true, general and
> non-prescriptive, and **a principle that told you what to do on Monday would be a process**, which
> this edition removed on purpose.
>
> **The hundred and twelve pages.** Our company procedure was written in **2018** against the
> **2017** edition, with its **forty-nine processes** mapped to our forms. **Seventy-one of those
> pages describe processes that no longer appear in the guide's structure.** **Not one of them has
> become wrong**: the change request procedure on page 54 works and we use it weekly. **It has lost
> its parent, not its correctness.**
>
> **What we did in 2021, and what I am correcting.** **We kept the pages and told nobody.** I signed
> that, and it was reasonable for a year. **It has been four.** The pages stay — **they are our
> forms** — and this page now sits in front of them, so that nothing in the building claims to be a
> method without naming an edition.
>
> *Source: A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK Guide), Seventh Edition,
> Project Management Institute, 2021. It is a guide rather than a prescriptive method: it describes
> principles and performance domains instead of the process structure of the sixth edition (2017),
> expects tailoring to context, and prescribes no processes, documents or sequences. It is published
> by a membership and certification body, and the certification resting on it is examined
> separately.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Write *not used as written* rather than *not used*. The first invites a
> sentence about why; the second reads as a refusal, and an assessor will ask the same question
> either way.
