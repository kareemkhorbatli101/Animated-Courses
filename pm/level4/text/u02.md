There is a method with seven principles, seven practices and seven processes. The practices and
processes may be tailored almost without limit. The seven principles may not, and that is where the
honest conversation about a method's name begins.

**1B  Underline four conditional structures in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **must … unless** | Report weekly **unless** a tolerance is set. |
| **in which case** | A tolerance is set, **in which case** only exceptions are raised. |
| **only if** | The name may be used **only if** all seven apply. |
| **provided that** | Updated at each boundary, **provided that** there is one. |
| **failing that** | A tolerance by Friday; **failing that**, weekly reporting continues. |
| **where … applies** | **Where** delegation **applies**, the limit is written down. |

> **Grammar Note** — *Unless* means *except if* and does not take a negative: *unless a tolerance
> is set*, never *unless a tolerance is not set*. And *in which case* refers back to a whole
> situation, so it follows a comma, not *that*.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Reporting everything feels like diligence everywhere, and it is the most expensive habit in this
book. A sponsor who reads thirty-one pages a month has delegated nothing, and the project manager
working inside that arrangement is deciding things daily with no written authority to do so — which
is comfortable until the week something goes wrong and there is no document saying she was allowed.
A tolerance is one signature and two numbers, and it exists to protect the person doing the work
rather than to save the sponsor time. The second habit is quieter. Organizations claim methods in
tenders by name, in good faith, on the strength of certificates held by individuals, and a
certificate says that one person passed an examination. It says nothing about whether this year's
projects apply a method's non-negotiable parts.

**Discuss:** Who on your project is deciding things with no written authority?

> name**, because the method states that **all seven principles** must be applied, and **five
> are**.
>
> | Principle | Applied | Evidence | Gap |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | continued business justification | **no** | case written 2019, updated once in 2021 | **four stage boundaries have passed** |
> | learn from experience | yes | lessons closed by document change; 4 moved in November | — |
> | defined roles and responsibilities | yes | role sheet, one named person per box, with limits | — |
> | manage by stages | yes | four stages, three dates each | — |
> | **manage by exception** | **no** | **no written tolerance on any project** | **nothing can be an exception** |
> | focus on products | yes | twelve package cards, each with exclusions | — |
> | tailor to suit the project | yes | method record, four tailored, two not used as written | — |
>
> **Why the second gap is serious and the first is embarrassing.** With **no tolerance**, nobody
> below the sponsor has **written authority**. We report everything, weekly, to everybody. **That
> looks like diligence and is the absence of delegation**, and it leaves the project manager
> deciding things daily with no document saying she may.
>
> **What I am asking for, with dates.** A signed tolerance of **± 2 weeks and ± 5 per cent** by
> **28 February** — one signature, two numbers. The business case **updated at the next stage
> boundary (14 March)** and at every boundary after it, in **one page**.
>
> **The tender sentence.** Until both are done, our tender wording **may not name the method**. It
> may say: *we apply five of its seven principles, six of its seven practices, and have documented
> our tailoring.* **That is longer, duller and true.**
>
> **On the fourteen certificates.** They are real and they are **about the holders**. A certificate
> records that a person passed an examination. **It is not a statement about this company's
> projects**, and our bid library has been quietly treating it as one since 2019.
>
> *Source: the seventh edition (2023) of a project management method published by the commercial
> body that owns it, comprising seven principles, seven practices and seven processes. The method
> requires tailoring of its practices and processes and states that all seven principles must be
> applied for a project to be run under the method; it addresses the management of projects rather
> than the technical work; it does not set tolerances, which the organization must set; and the
> manual, accredited training and examinations are commercial products.*
