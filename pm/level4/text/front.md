## How to use this book

This is the fourth book of six. Books 1 to 3 were about the parts of a project, estimating it, and
knowing where it is. This one is about **the methods** — the named, branded, certified bodies of
practice that organizations buy, adopt and argue about. Every unit is built on one real published
document, named with its edition and its date, and every unit asks the same two questions: **who
publishes this, and what does it claim about itself?**

**Twelve units**, with the same six lessons in the same order every time.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Sixteen words, a second set of six, and a team research task |
| **2 · Reading** | One real method or standard, with its date, its owner and its limits |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures, with the mistake each one causes |
| **4 · At Work** | Clear English, a dialogue, a culture check and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A model, then your own |
| **Team Up & Review** | A picture, a team project, a survey, and one problem with no easy answer |

**The audio.** 🔊 marks a track. 195 tracks, numbered `TL4-unit-track`.

## What is different about this book

**Every method in it is owned by somebody.** A professional body, a certification company, a
foundation, a consultancy, a government department, a research group. That is not a criticism:
somebody has to maintain a body of knowledge, and maintenance costs money. But it changes how a
document should be read, and this book reads each one twice — once for what it says, and once for
what it is for.

**Most of these documents are more modest than their reputations.** The Scrum Guide is thirteen
pages long and says so. The Agile Manifesto has four values and says that its authors are
*uncovering* better ways. The seventh edition of one guide abandoned the process structure that two
decades of training courses had been built on. The methods are usually humbler than the people
quoting them.

**One unit is about a number nobody can check.** The most-quoted project failure statistic in the
world comes from a report whose sample and method have never been published, and which researchers
have been unable to reproduce. Unit 6 is about how to talk about evidence you cannot see.

**Money has no symbol in this series.** Every figure is written without a currency sign, because the
unit is whatever currency your project uses.
