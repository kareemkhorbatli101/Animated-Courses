### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Comparatives are heard as exclusions in every language, and the four most famous comparatives in
project work have been heard that way for twenty-five years. *A over B* means B still counts; the
document says so in the sentence immediately underneath, and that sentence is almost never on the
slide. What follows is predictable and expensive: somebody returns from a course, says a sentence
in a meeting, and four manuals come off a plan without anybody having read sixty-eight words that
take four minutes. The second habit worth carrying is to concede most of the ground. Saying *three
of the four statements apply completely, and thirty percent of this project by value can respond to
change* wins an argument that *that does not apply to us* always loses, because it shows you have
read the document and counted your own work.

> ### Tender response · question 14, delivery approach
> *From M. Bah, reviewed by A. Holm · 8 March · one page*
>
> **What we are agreeing to, statement by statement.** The document is the **Manifesto for Agile
> Software Development**, **February 2001**, **seventeen signatories**, **sixty-eight words**. Each
> statement is of the form *A **over** B*, and the sentence printed immediately underneath reads:
> *while there is value in the items on the right, we value the items on the left more.* **Over, not
> instead of.**
>
> | Statement | How far it transfers | What we will do |
> |---|---|---|
> | individuals and interactions **over** processes and tools | **completely** | a weekly joint working session instead of a letter exchange |
> | customer collaboration **over** contract negotiation | **completely** | one joint register; disputes raised in the room first |
> | responding to change **over** following a plan | **about 30 percent of this project by value** | the design and the control system in two-week passes |
> | working software **over** comprehensive documentation | **not as written** | **there is no software in this contract** |
>
> **Why thirty percent and not a hundred.** The design, the control system and the commissioning
> procedures **can be changed at weekly notice**. The civil works **cannot**: once a chamber is
> poured, **changing it is a demolition, not an iteration.** **No document from 2001 claims
> otherwise — it names software development in its title.**
>
> **What this will cost you.** *Collaboration over contract negotiation* is **not free for the
> client**: it means **fewer letters and more meetings**, and a disagreement raised in a room rather
> than in writing. **We are asking for that, not offering it.**
>
> **One thing we got wrong, which is why this answer is four rows long.** In **2021** a manager
> returned from a course and told a meeting that **documentation was not agile**. **Four handover
> manuals came off the plan.** Nobody in that room had read the sixty-eight words. **Your maintenance
> team discovered it in 2023**, in front of a pump, and the manuals were then written from memory
> with two of the original engineers gone: **about 40,000**, and they are worse than they would have
> been.
>
> *Source: the Manifesto for Agile Software Development, February 2001, signed by seventeen
> authors; sixty-eight words followed by twelve principles. It states expressly that there is value
> in the items it ranks lower; it names software development in its title; it defines no method,
> roles, events or artifacts; it is not a standard and cannot be certified against; and the
> frameworks and commercial training built on it are separate works by other authors.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Put your own mistake in the tender answer. It is the only way a bid reader
> believes the thirty percent, and it turns an argument about terminology into a sentence about a
> pump.
