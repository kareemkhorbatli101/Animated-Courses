# Grammar reference

Every structure in this book, in unit order. The second column is the mistake it prevents, which is
the column worth reading twice.

## Starting Point · Four questions to ask any method

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who publishes it?** | a standards body, a certification company, a consultancy, or seventeen people in a room |
| **Which edition is in front of you?** | an edition is not a detail; the 2021 guide replaced forty-nine processes with twelve principles |
| **What does it claim to be?** | a standard, a guide, a manifesto, a practitioner account, a report with an unpublished method |
| **What does it say it does not cover?** | printed in the document itself, and almost never read |
| **A document is not its training course** | and neither is it the deck somebody made from it |

## Unit 1 · Abstract nouns into people

| | |
|---|---|
| ***stakeholder engagement* → who talks to whom, about what, how often** | an abstract noun hides the person, the action and the object |
| **the Monday test** | if nothing can be done differently on Monday, it is a sentence, not a principle |
| **twelve principles · eight performance domains** | the 2021 edition; the 2017 edition had forty-nine processes |
| **71 pages of a 112-page procedure referred to nothing** | because the procedure was written against the previous edition |

## Unit 2 · *Must … unless*

| | |
|---|---|
| ***must* … *unless* · *only if* · *provided that* · *in which case* · *failing that*** | a rule with no exception clause is obeyed until it is inconvenient |
| **a tolerance is a number agreed in advance** | without one, nothing can be an exception, so everything is a judgment call |
| **five of seven practices applied** | and no written tolerance anywhere in the programme |
| **a business case written in 2019, updated once** | the method requires it to be maintained; nobody had read the clause |

## Unit 3 · *Over* and *rather than*

| | |
|---|---|
| ***over* · *rather than* · *in preference to* · *not instead of*** | *over* means valued more, not instead of — and the manifesto says so in its own last sentence |
| **four value statements · 68 words · 17 signatories · February 2001** | the shortest document in this book and the most often misquoted |
| **"while there is value in the items on the right"** | the sentence that is dropped when the manifesto is used to abolish documents |
| **four manuals dropped, 40,000 pages to rewrite** | and 30 percent of the work able to iterate at all |

## Unit 4 · Present simple for rules

| | |
|---|---|
| **the review *is held* · the owner *orders* the backlog** | present simple states a rule; *we usually try to* states a habit |
| **13 pages · 3 accountabilities, 5 events, 3 artifacts, 3 commitments** | eleven elements, and the document calls them indivisible |
| **the one document in this book that refuses tailoring** | you hold all of it or you call your process something else |
| **a subcontractor held 7 of 11** | which by the guide's own terms is not the thing it names |

## Unit 5 · *Be able to* and *has shown that*

| | |
|---|---|
| ***can* · *be able to* · *know how to* · *demonstrate* · *has shown that* · *be assessed as*** | competence is evidence of behavior, not a number in a box |
| **29 elements in 3 areas: perspective 5 · people 10 · practice 14** | the 2015 baseline; the areas are not equally easy to assess |
| **self-assessment averages 3.9 · 3.2 · 2.1** | and nine of eleven could not state the organization's strategy |
| **"fine" written in nine boxes** | and one person moved from tenth to second on colleague evidence |

## Unit 6 · Hedging a claim

| | |
|---|---|
| ***it is claimed that* · *according to* · *reportedly* · *cannot be verified* · *has been questioned* · *neither confirmed nor refuted*** | *cannot be verified* is not the same as *not true* |
| **a report first published in 1994** | whose sampling method, questionnaire and definitions have never been published |
| **two peer-reviewed critiques, 2006 and 2010** | both concerned with what the definitions do to the figures |
| **44 projects: 9 by the strict definition, 41 accepted, 3 canceled** | three true numbers about one company, in place of one famous number about everybody |

## Unit 7 · Iteration

| | |
|---|---|
| ***on each pass* · *each time* · *at the end of every cycle* · *until* · *successively*** | a description of iteration with no stopping rule acquires cycles five, six and seven |
| **four activities per turn, the fourth being a review and a commitment** | a turn with no decision at the end is a lap |
| **the radius is cumulative cost** | each turn is entered only because somebody funded it |
| **900 in week three against 60,000 in month nine** | the value is in the ordering, not in the repetition |

## Unit 8 · Strength of recommendation

| | |
|---|---|
| ***it is critical that* · *it is essential that … by* · *we recommend that* · *consideration should be given to*** | a date is what turns advice into an obligation |
| **six review points, 0 to 5, each held before a decision** | the placement is the instrument; the depth is four days |
| **4 days · 14 of 61 people · 41 documents, selected by us · 0 site visits** | a rating quoted without its evidence base becomes a fact in six months |
| **the report is confidential to the owner** | and the review recommends; it cannot decide |

## Unit 9 · Criteria and conditions

| | |
|---|---|
| ***all of the following* · *any one of which* · *unless* · *provided that* · *fails to meet* · *at least*** | without *any one of which*, three out of four becomes an argument |
| **must-meet refuses · should-meet ranks** | a low score is still a score |
| **31 projects, 31 passes, 0 stops, in four years** | with fourteen criteria, every one of them scored |
| **9 of the next 11 passed; 2 were stopped** | after four yes-or-no questions were added, one of which caught its own author |

## Unit 10 · *By* + gerund

| | |
|---|---|
| ***by* + gerund · *without* + gerund · *rather than by* · *the only way … is by*** | a claim of change with no *by* clause is almost always about a presentation |
| **eight steps, in order; six attempted** | skipping a step creates the illusion of speed |
| **41 days presenting · 0 days removing** | talking costs time; removing costs somebody standing |
| **a percentage traced through three decks to nothing** | the book says more than half of the companies observed failed in the first phase |

## Unit 11 · Attribution

| | |
|---|---|
| ***is set out in* · *is usually attributed to* · *himself credited* · *is widely known as* · *reaches us through secondary accounts*** | *is set out in* says you have the page; the others do not pretend to |
| **a 1939 book, edited by the man usually credited** | who called it by its author's name for the rest of his life |
| **the objection to *check*, in print, 1993** | *check* asks whether we did it; *study* asks whether it worked |
| **48 cycles · 48 ticks · 0 written conclusions · 11 problems back** | the citation error and the practice error were one error |

## Unit 12 · Declaring what you took and left

| | |
|---|---|
| ***we have adopted* · *the following have been omitted* · *this is a claim of tailored conformance* · *decided by … on …*** | a declaration needs four parts: what, what not, why, and who |
| **full conformance or tailored conformance** | you may leave things out; you may not leave them out quietly |
| **34 of 95 elements, across eleven documents** | a number your own files can disprove, which is why it is worth writing |
| **an omission with no author becomes a rule in eighteen months** | and is then defended by people who do not know why it exists |

# The British layer

This book is written in American English, and the series carries a switchable British layer: a fixed
list of words is respelled at build time. The list lives with the series rather than in this book.

What the layer does not touch:

1. **The date.** Write the month in letters where the sentence is prose, and the international format
   where it is data.
2. **A quoted document keeps its own spelling.** Every guide, standard, manifesto, article and book
   quoted here is quoted as published. Correcting a published document inside a quotation is an
   alteration, not a spelling choice.
3. **A document number keeps its published form.** ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207 is a citation, not a word.
4. **Money has no symbol anywhere in this series.** The unit is whatever currency your project uses.
5. **A name is never respelled.** Shewhart, Deming, Boehm, Cooper, Kotter, Schwaber, Sutherland,
   Jørgensen, Moløkken-Østvold, Eveleens and Verhoef are spelled as they are published, accents
   included.

**Programme** keeps that spelling throughout, as in Books 1, 2 and 3: it is the term ISO 21506:2018
defines, and this book's Units 8 and 10 discuss documents that use it. Where a quoted title uses it,
it is quoted; where this book uses it as the defined term, it is the defined term.

# Sources

Twelve source bodies carry the twelve reading texts, one per unit, and the Starting Point carries
none. Every one is a published guide, standard, manifesto, article, report or book, cited with its
edition or date, and every reading text prints what the publisher or the author says the document does
**not** cover. No source appears in any other book of this series or in its four sister series, and no
figure is reproduced from any of them: every chart, form and table in this book was drawn for it,
with invented numbers.

| Unit | Source | Edition cited |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | a body-of-knowledge guide published by a professional association | **7th edition, 2021** |
| 2 | a structured project management method published by the company that certifies it | **7th edition, 2023** |
| 3 | a manifesto for software development signed by seventeen people | **February 2001** |
| 4 | a guide to a development framework, by its two authors | **November 2020** |
| 5 | an individual competence baseline published by a project management association | **version 4, 2015** |
| 6 | an industry report on project outcomes, with two peer-reviewed critiques | **first published 1994; critiques 2006 and 2010** |

| 8 | a gateway review process published by a government commerce office | **2007**, now operated by an infrastructure and projects authority |
| 9 | an article proposing a stage-and-gate system | **Business Horizons, May–June 1990** |
| 10 | a book on leading change, developing an earlier article | **1996**, developing a **1995** article |
| 11 | a statistics text presenting a repeating cycle, and a later book objecting to one of its steps | **1939**, edited by the man usually credited; and **1993** |
| 12 | a standard for software life cycle processes, with full and tailored conformance | **2017 edition** |

**The honesty rule of this series, in this book's own words.** Every reading text names its source,
its edition and its date, and then prints the limits the source states about itself. In this book
those limits do most of the teaching:

- **Unit 1.** The guide changed shape between editions, and a 112-page company procedure written
  against the previous one left 71 pages pointing at nothing.
- **Unit 2.** A method with seven principles, seven practices and seven processes, in a programme with
  no written tolerance anywhere, so nothing could be an exception.
- **Unit 3.** Sixty-eight words, and the final sentence — *while there is value in the items on the
  right* — dropped by almost everybody who quotes the first four.
- **Unit 4.** Thirteen pages that refuse tailoring outright, held in part by a subcontractor and named
  in full by everybody.
- **Unit 5.** Twenty-nine competence elements, self-assessed; nine of eleven people could not state
  the strategy whose area they had scored 3.9.
- **Unit 6.** A famous percentage whose sampling method, questionnaire and definitions have never been
  published, and two peer-reviewed critiques saying so.
- **Unit 7.** A risk-driven model whose author published a second paper twelve years later because
  processes were using its name without its features.
- **Unit 8.** A four-day review of a project of sixty-one people, reading forty-one documents the
  project selected itself, with a report the project manager never saw.
- **Unit 9.** Thirty-one projects through one gate in four years with no stops, because every criterion
  was a score and a score cannot refuse.
- **Unit 10.** Six of eight steps attempted, forty-one days of presenting, nothing removed, and an
  opening slide whose percentage is not in the book it cites.
- **Unit 11.** Four words of citation with three errors in them, and forty-eight improvement cycles
  with a ticked box and no conclusion.
- **Unit 12.** A standard that permits tailoring and requires it to be declared — the one requirement
  most method statements fail.

# A note on everything else in this book

Every person, team, company, project, number and incident in this book is invented. Lindhurst does
not exist; neither do the control system programme, the plant with the 2004 protocol, the 2021
specification, the forty-eight improvement cycles, the eleven-row register or any of the people who
appear in the dialogues. The documents are real and cited; the stories around them are written to
teach the language.

Nothing in this book is legal, financial or contractual advice, and it is not a study guide for any
examination or certification. Several of the documents discussed here are owned by organizations that
sell training and credentials against them; this book is not affiliated with, endorsed by or derived
from any of them, and where it describes what a document contains it does so in order to teach the
English used to discuss it. Where a sentence sounds like a rule — *any one of which is sufficient to
stop the project*, *re-checked by 14 April*, *we hold seven of eleven* — that is one invented
company's internal practice, printed because a concrete number teaches better than an abstraction.
Your own numbers belong to you and your sponsor.

# Closing

You began this book able to name the method your organization claims. You end it able to say **which
edition**, **what the document says it does not cover**, **how many of its elements you actually
practice**, **who decided to leave the rest out and when**, **at what strength you are recommending
something**, **what your own evidence base is** — and which row of your own method page you would not
want published.

Eleven documents, and one habit: **say what you took, what you left, why, and who decided.**

Six sentences to take with you.

From Unit 1: **if nothing changes on Monday, it is a sentence, not a principle.**

From Unit 4: **one document in this book refuses to be tailored, and it is the shortest.**

From Unit 6: **cannot be verified is not the same as not true.**

From Unit 8: **a rating quoted without its evidence base becomes a fact.**

From Unit 10: **talking costs time; removing costs somebody standing.**

From Unit 12: **an omission with no author becomes a rule in eighteen months.**

Book 5 moves from methods to money: estimate classes, contracts, benefits and the arithmetic that
decides whether any of this was worth doing.
