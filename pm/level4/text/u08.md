# Unit 8 · Somebody From Outside
%%SUB Six review points, three strengths of recommendation, and a report the project manager never sees%%
%%GOALS

A review by people who are not on the project, at fixed points before money is committed, with every
recommendation marked *do now*, *do by* or *recommended*. It is a powerful instrument and a narrow
one: four days, the documents you give them, and the people who agree to be interviewed.

> **B:** **Eleven.** Two marked **critical — do now**, five **essential — do by** with dates, four
> **recommended**.
> **A:** And the confidence rating?
> **B:** **Amber.** Deliverable, with significant issues that need management attention.
> **A:** On what evidence?
> **B:** **Four days.** Fourteen interviews, and the **forty-one documents we chose to send them.**
> **A:** We chose which documents to send?
> **B:** **We did.** That is the honest limit of the instrument. **A review sees what it is given and
> hears from the people who agree to talk.** It is a snapshot, and it says so.
> **A:** Then what is it worth?
> **B:** **A great deal, because it happens before the money.** The review point sits **before the
> investment decision**, not after it. **Nobody on the inside can stop a project at month nine. An
> outsider can say** *do not proceed yet* **at the only moment when saying it is still cheap.**
> **A:** Can they stop it?
> **B:** **No. They recommend.** The decision stays with the owner. **The review has no authority at
> all, and that is deliberate.**

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

An external review is one of the few instruments that can change a decision from outside, and the
reason is timing rather than insight: it sits before the money is committed, at a moment when
stopping is still cheap. Organizations receive it in one of two ways. The first treats the four days
as an inspection to be survived: the document set is curated, the interviewees are chosen for
steadiness, and the report is read for its rating rather than its findings. The second treats it as
the cheapest week of outside attention the project will ever get, sends the uncomfortable file,
offers the people who will complain, and writes down what the review was not asked to look at. The
second costs no more than the first. The habit worth carrying is to state the evidence base out loud
every time the review is quoted — four days, fourteen of sixty-one, forty-one documents we chose —
because a rating repeated without its evidence base becomes a fact within about six months.

**Discuss:** Would your organization send the uncomfortable file?

> |---|---|---|
> | **critical — do now** | **2** | the project **must not proceed to contract until** the 2021 specification is reissued |
> | **essential — do by** | **5** | **it is essential that** the three missing interfaces are re-checked **by 14 April** |
> | **recommended** | **4** | **consideration should be given to** merging the two risk registers |
>
> **The evidence base, stated here so that it travels with the rating.** **Four days on site.
> Fourteen interviews out of sixty-one people. Forty-one documents — assembled by us, at the
> reviewers' request for a document set.** **No site visits.** **The supplier performance file was
> not sent, and nobody asked us to withhold it; we simply did not include it.**
>
> **Outside the terms of reference: the operating model.** **We wrote those terms.** The review is
> therefore silent on our largest risk, and **that silence is ours and not a failure of the review.**
>
> **What it was worth.** A reviewer **on her third day** identified that we were about to buy a
> system against a **2021 specification** with **three interfaces that no longer exist**. **Eleven
> of us have worked on that document for two years.** The sponsor has said she will not sign until it
> is reissued. **That is a decision changed four weeks before the money, by three people who do not
> work here, for four days of their time.**
>
> **What it cannot do.** The review team **recommends; it does not decide.** Its report is
> **confidential to the senior responsible owner** and I have not read it. **The published process
> describes the review as a point-in-time assessment based on the documents made available and the
> interviews held in the period** — which is exactly what it was.
>
> **One change for the next review.** **We send the document set we would not choose**, and we put
> the operating model inside the terms of reference. **Both are free.**
>
> *Source: the gateway review process published by a government commerce office in 2007 and now
> operated by that country's infrastructure and projects authority. The process describes itself as a
> short, independent, confidential review held at defined points before key decisions; reports are
> addressed to the senior responsible owner; the review team recommends and holds no decision-making
> authority; and each review is a point-in-time assessment based on the documents made available and
> the interviews conducted in the review period.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Put the evidence base in the same paragraph as the rating. Separated, the rating
> survives and the evidence base does not.
