Somebody will hand you a method this year. These four questions take about five minutes and will
change what you do with it.

### 1 · Six things you brought from Book 3

%%FIG pd00-f1 | A single review sheet with six boxes. Each box has a heading — for every, as of, whereas, should be reviewed, only if, and leads people to — and one short example sentence from a review meeting.%%

### 2 · Four questions to ask any method

%%FIG pd00-f2 | A card taped inside a notebook, printed with four questions in bold, each with one line of smaller type under it saying what the answer tells you.%%

**2A  Read the four. Then ask them about a method you have been given.**

> **1  Who publishes it, and what do they sell?**
> *A professional body, a certification company, a foundation, a government department. Somebody
> pays for the maintenance.*
>
> **2  Which edition, and what changed?**
> *The seventh edition of one guide abandoned the structure the sixth was built on.*
>
> **3  What does it claim about itself?**
> *A framework, a guide, a body of knowledge, a standard, a manifesto. These are different
> claims.*
>
> **4  What does it admit it does not do?**
> *This is printed, in the document, by its own authors, usually in the first pages.*

The fourth question is the one this whole series is built on. **Almost every document in these four
books states its own limits, and almost nobody reads that far.**

**2B  Listen.** %%AUD TL4-00-02%%

### 3 · A document is not its training course

%%FIG pd00-f3 | Two objects side by side. On the left, a thin document of thirteen pages. On the right, a thick folder of course notes, a certificate, an examination paper and an invoice.%%

**3A  Read the two columns. Then say which one you have actually read.**

| The document | What is sold around it |
|---|---|
| thirteen pages, free to download | a two-day course, an examination, a certificate |
| four values and twelve principles | a consultancy practice, a job title |
| a guide of principles and domains | an accredited training organization network |
| a government review process | a panel of reviewers at a daily rate |

**None of the right-hand column is dishonest.** But the claims made in a training room are not the
claims made in the document, and **only one of the two is quotable.**

### 4 · Two ways of answering *are we doing it properly?*

%%FIG pd00-f4 | Two speech bubbles, one above the other. The first says we follow the standard. The second names a document, an edition, four things adopted, two tailored and one rejected, with the reason.%%

**4A  Read the two answers. Which one could be checked?**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Answer 1** | "We follow the standard." |
| **Answer 2** | "We use the 2023 edition. We have adopted four of the seven practices, tailored two, and we do not use the third at all, because we have no programme board." |

**Answer 2 is longer and auditable.** Every method in this book expects to be **tailored**, says so
in writing, and is almost always quoted as though it did not.

**4B  Listen and read.** %%AUD TL4-00-03%%

**4C  One question to finish.** Which method does your company say it follows, and who has read the
document?
