# Unit 8 · Where the Safety Went
%%SUB Thirty-eight weeks cut to twenty-two, a nine-week buffer, and two welders who refused to play%%
%%GOALS

Every estimate has safety time hidden inside it, and almost none of that safety reaches the end of
the project. A novel from 1997 explains where it goes. The idea is excellent; the way it is usually
imposed is not.

> *Project office · safety and buffers · page 1 of 1*
>
> **Where it comes from.** **Eliyahu M. Goldratt** published *Critical Chain* with North River
> Press in **1997**. **It is a novel** — the argument is carried by characters in a business school
> — and it applies his theory of constraints to projects.
>
> **The argument.** Every task estimate contains **safety**, because each person is judged on their
> own date. That safety is then **wasted**, for three reasons:
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | **student syndrome** | with slack in the task, work starts late |
> | **multitasking** | three tasks at once means none finishes early |
> | **asymmetry** | an early finish is not passed on; lateness always travels |
>
> **The remedy.** **Cut each estimate** to roughly a **fifty-fifty** figure. **Aggregate** the
> removed safety into one **project buffer** at the end, with **feeding buffers** where other chains
> join the main one. Then **stop managing task dates** and manage **buffer consumption** instead.
> The **critical chain** is the longest chain of dependent tasks, counting **who is available** as
> well as **what follows what** — which is the part the 1959 critical path method left out.
>
> **Our trial, on one package.**
>
> | | Weeks |
> |---|---|
> | the eleven estimates as given | **38** |
> | the same eleven, cut | **22** |
> | project buffer | **9** |
> | **planned total** | **31** |
> | **what it actually took** | **29** |
>

> estimate is meant to be missed about half the time. **Buffer consumption at the end: 78 per
> cent.** The package finished **nine weeks earlier** than the thirty-eight-week plan would have
> predicted.
>
> **And student syndrome, measured.**
>
> > We looked at **19** tasks that had two weeks or more of slack. **In 14 of them, no hours were
> > booked in the first week.**
>
> **What went wrong, which matters more than the result.**
>
> > Telling a welder that his eight weeks is now four, and that this is **aggressive but
> > achievable**, sounds exactly like being squeezed.
> >
> > **Two of our welders refused to give a fifty-fifty number at all.** Their question was simple
> > and nobody had an answer: **what happens to me when I miss it?**

### 1 · The first conditional

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD TL2-08-06%%

> **Oskar:** I need a fifty-fifty number for the welding. Not your safe number.
> **Ahmad:** My number is eight weeks.
> **Oskar:** **And if everything goes normally?**
> **Ahmad:** Five. Six with the second shift.
> **Oskar:** So five is your fifty-fifty.
> **Ahmad:** **If I say five, what happens to me in week six?**
> **Oskar:** Nothing happens to you. The project has a nine-week buffer and week six comes out of
> it.
> **Ahmad:** **That is what you say now.** In October somebody will have a chart with my name on it
> and the word *late* next to it.
> **Oskar:** **That is a fair question and I am not going to pretend it is not.**
> **Ahmad:** Then write the answer down.
> **Oskar:** I will. **If a fifty-fifty estimate is missed, it is not reported as a late task** — it
> is reported as buffer used, with no name on it. **Unless the buffer goes past seventy percent**,
> and then we all sit in a room, which is the point of the whole thing.
> **Ahmad:** And if I give you eight and keep my safety?
> **Oskar:** Then the package is thirty-eight weeks and every week of safety gets spent anyway,
> because nobody ever hands an early finish back. **I have never once been told a task finished
> early.**
> **Ahmad:** Neither have I, and I have finished early.
> **Oskar:** **Everybody has.** That is the argument of the book in one sentence: the safety is real,
> and it is in the wrong place.
> **Ahmad:** Five, then. **As long as the sentence about names is in the plan, not in this
> conversation.**
> **Oskar:** It will be in the plan **before** you give me the number, not after.

*What does Ahmad want written down before he gives a number?*

**1B  Underline four conditional structures in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **if** + present, **will** | **If** we cut them, we **will** need a buffer. |
| **unless** | Not **unless** the buffer passes 70 percent. |
| **as long as** | **As long as** no name appears beside a miss. |
| **provided that** | **Provided that** the witness test is excluded. |
| **in case** | Keep a feeding buffer **in case** the chain merges late. |
| **even if** | **Even if** it is missed, no name is reported. |

> **Grammar Note** — The first conditional keeps the **present** after *if*, *unless*, *as long as*
> and *in case*, and *will* in the other half. *If we will cut* is wrong; and *in case* is not
> *if* — *in case* means *as a precaution*, so it never takes *will* either.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

The arithmetic of this method takes an afternoon and the trust it needs takes years. Asking somebody
for a number they will miss half the time is asking them to accept a visible failure for an
invisible collective gain, and in most workplaces the gain is shared and the failure has a name on
it. That is why the two welders were right to refuse, and why the only thing that ever makes this
work is a written rule produced *before* the numbers: a missed fifty-fifty estimate is reported as
buffer used, with nobody's name beside it. Where that rule does not exist, people hear *aggressive
but achievable* exactly as it will turn out to be used, and they pad the numbers again — and they
are not being obstructive. They are protecting themselves from a method that has offered them all of
the risk and none of the credit.

**Discuss:** What would have to be written down before you gave a fifty-fifty estimate?

>
> **The rule first, because nobody will give me a number until it exists.**
>
> > **A missed fifty-fifty estimate is reported as buffer used, with no name attached. No task on

> | | Weeks |
