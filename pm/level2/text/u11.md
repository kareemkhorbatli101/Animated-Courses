# Unit 11 · Choosing What Not to Do
%%SUB Eighteen projects, sixteen person-years, and three stopped in six years — none of them on purpose%%
%%GOALS

A portfolio is not a list of the work you are doing. It is a decision about the work you are not
going to do, and that is a different and much less popular document. Most organizations have the
first and almost none have the second.

| | Example |
|---|---|

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Starting things is rewarded everywhere and stopping them is not. A new project has a sponsor, a
launch and a line in somebody's objectives; a stopped project has a difficult meeting and somebody
who feels judged. So portfolios grow, capacity is never calculated because the answer would be
embarrassing, and projects end the only way that requires no decision at all — by losing their
sponsor and starving. Three in six years, none of them at a review, one of them running fourteen
months past the point where everybody privately agreed. The guidance cannot fix this; it says a
portfolio should be balanced against capacity and offers nothing on the meeting itself. What helps
is smaller than a method: two numbers on one slide, and the honest third option written down, so
that being short becomes a decision somebody made rather than a thing that happened.

> **On the scoring model.** The weights were set by **three people in an afternoon in 2021** and have
> not been reviewed. **Last year's top-ranked proposal was the one whose sponsor writes the best
> forms.** I am not asking the board to rebuild the model. **I am asking it to stop reading the
> output as a ranking.**
>
> **Three options, and it must be one of them.**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | **1** | add **six** person-years of capacity |
> | **2** | **stop or defer** components worth six person-years, by name, at this meeting |
> | **3** | **be six short on purpose**, and minute that decision |
>
> **I recommend 2, with 3 as the written fallback.** **Option 3 is a legitimate answer** and it is
> the one that has never once been written down here — which is why we keep arriving at it by
> accident in March.
>
> *Source: ISO 21504:2022, Project, programme and portfolio management &mdash; Guidance on
> portfolio management, second edition, replacing the 2015 edition. It is guidance, not
> requirements, and not intended for certification: it prescribes no selection or prioritization
> method, no scoring model or weights and no portfolio size, requires alignment with strategic

> **Writer's Tip** — Always write the third option. *Be short on purpose* is what will happen
