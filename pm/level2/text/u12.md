> ## Lindhurst · project office note 12
> *Project office · two pages, and one quotation to be careful of · page 1 of 1*
>
> **Where it comes from.** **Douglas R. Hofstadter** published *Gödel, Escher, Bach: An Eternal
> Golden Braid* with Basic Books in **1979**. It is a book about mathematics, music and mind, and in
> it he states, as a joke about his own work:

> > **It always takes longer than you expect, even when you take into account Hofstadter's Law.**
>
> **Why it is true and useless.** It is **self-referential** and therefore **cannot be disproved**.
> If a project overruns, the law is confirmed. If a project finishes early, the law is not refuted —
> that was simply not one of the cases it was about. **A statement nothing can contradict tells you
> nothing about what to do on Monday**, and this one is quoted in meetings as a reason not to try.
>
> **The eleven sources that are not like that.**
>
> | Source | What it lets you do |
> |---|---|
> | critical path, **1959** | find what cannot start until what |
> | PERT, **1958** | give three numbers and a spread |
> | Parkinson, **1955** | split a duration into work and named waiting |
> | planning fallacy, **1994** | ask how long the last four took |
> | reference class forecasting, **2006** | place your project on a distribution |
> | optimism bias guidance, **2003** | start at an upper bound and argue it down |
> | Brooks, **1975** | ask whether two pieces would need to agree |
> | critical chain, **1997** | hold the safety at the end, with a no-blame rule |
> | plan content, **2019** | be told fourteen questions you would not have asked |
> | programme guidance, **2022** | name a benefit, a date and an owner |
> | portfolio guidance, **2022** | put demand and capacity on one slide |
>

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Every workplace has a saying about estimates, and the best-written sayings do the most damage.
*It always takes longer than you think* is almost certainly true, it is funny, it was a joke in a
book about mathematics, and it cannot be contradicted by anything that happens. That is what makes
it so comfortable: it arrives at the end of a difficult conversation and converts a question — how
long did the last four take? — into a shrug everybody can agree with. The eleven other sources in
this book are all weaker than that. Every one of them can be shown to be wrong on your project, and
that is precisely their value. A plan that can be wrong can be checked; a plan built on a law that
cannot be wrong can only be regretted.

**Discuss:** What is the saying in your workplace, and what does it let people stop doing?

> *From C. Nkemelu · 28 July · for E. Okonjo and S. Marchetti*
>
> **PAGE ONE**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | **1 The chain** | Commissioning **cannot start until** the pressure test **passes** — **week 19**. Valves **cannot be ordered until** drawing 1.2-04 **is approved**, week 9; lead time **four weeks from approval**. |
> | **2 The range** | **5** weeks at best, **7** most likely, **11** at worst if the seals fail. **I would sign 7.** |
> | **3 The class** | **19** finished stations, median **22 per cent above** approval; **excluding two** with one borehole each, **12 per cent**. |
> | **4 The money** | Published upper bound **increases 2.40 by 44 per cent, to 3.46**. **Reduced to 12 per cent: 2.70.** **Not contingency. May not be spent.** |
> | **5 The buffer** | **11** tasks cut to **22** weeks, plus a **9-week** buffer. **A missed fifty-fifty estimate is reported as buffer used, with no name attached** — signed before estimates were collected. |
> | **6 The capacity** | **18** projects need **22** person-years; **16** available. **Two of eleven managers hold five each.** |
>
> **PAGE TWO — what we do not know**
>
> | | Assumption | Who confirms | By |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | 1 | Client engineer on site week 30 | **S. Marchetti** | **confirmed 9 July** |
> | 2 | No rock above the chamber base | W. Zhao | **confirmed, 4 boreholes** |
> | 3 | Valves four weeks **from approval** | O. Nasser | **confirmed 14 May** |
> | 4 | Drawing 1.2-04 approved week 9 | E. Okonjo | **not yet** |
> | 5 | Harbour inspector available in June | N. Haddad | **not yet** |
>
> **Eleven lines in all; six confirmed.** When this page was first written, **four lines had no name
> at all**, and **one of them was false**: the valve lead time, which runs from **approval** and not
> from **order**. **That was the five weeks we lost in the spring, and it was sitting on page two
> from the first draft with nobody's name beside it.**
>
> **One saying that is not in this plan.** *It always takes longer than you expect, even when you
> allow for that.* It is probably true and **nothing can disprove it**, so it cannot tell anybody
> what to do. **The other eleven sources behind this plan can all be shown to be wrong on this
> project, which is why they are in it.**
>
> *Source: D. R. Hofstadter, Gödel, Escher, Bach: An Eternal Golden Braid, Basic Books, 1979, where
> the law is a self-referential joke within a book about mathematics and mind. It is not a study,
> offers no method and no measurement, and is unfalsifiable by construction, so it provides no
> guidance for action.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Page two is the deliverable. Page one is arithmetic anybody could redo; page
> two is the only part of a plan that creates work for other people, which is why it is read.

### 3 · Write

Write **110 to 130 words**: a plan on two pages.

**Page one: the chain with *cannot start until*, the range with conditions, the class with a median,
the money with *by* and *to*, the buffer with its rule, and the capacity with quantifiers. Page two:
at least four assumptions, each with a name and a date. End with one sentence on a saying you have
left out, and why.**

**Read your plan aloud to your partner in ninety seconds.** Then check your own against the list,
and swap.

> A project plan of sixty-one pages was replaced for daily use by two sheets. Page one carries the
> chain, the range, the reference class, the money, the buffer and the capacity. Page two carries
> every assumption, with the name of whoever must confirm it and the date they will.
>
> Page two has never been longer than eleven lines. On the first version, four of the eleven had no
> name beside them. Two were confirmed within a week of somebody being asked. One was false: the
> valve lead time, which runs from approval and not from order, and which cost the project five
> weeks.
>
> Eleven of the book's twelve sources can be shown to be wrong on any particular project. The
> twelfth, from 1979, cannot be disproved by anything.
>
> Chidi's note on the file is three sentences. *The long plan is still true and still unread, and I
> have stopped treating that as a failure of the readers. Everything useful I learned this year is
> on two sheets, and the only page anybody argues with is the one that admits what we do not know.
> The best-written source in my whole list is the one I took out, because a sentence nothing can
> contradict is the most comfortable thing anybody can say at the end of a hard meeting.*

3. Do you agree that the best-written source was the right one to leave out?
