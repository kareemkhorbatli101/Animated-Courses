**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **to** + verb | Grouped **to** commission one system once. |
| **in order to** | **In order to** sequence the two together. |
| **so that** + clause | **So that** the board sees one paper. |
| **so as to** | **So as to** avoid a second mobilization. |
| **for** + noun | Grouped **for** efficiency. |
| **because of** *(not purpose)* | **Because of** the shared system. *(reason, not purpose)* |

> **Grammar Note** — *So that* takes a **clause** with its own subject and usually a modal: *so that
> the board sees one paper*. *To* and *in order to* take a **verb** and share the subject of the main
> clause. *For* takes a **noun**, never a verb: *for efficiency*, not *for to save time*.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

The word *programme* is useful in a way the guidance never mentions: in most organizations it
attracts a budget line, a senior sponsor and a seat at a table that individual projects do not get.
So groups of unrelated projects get the label, nobody is lying, and everybody involved can name the
real reason if asked privately. What the guidance quietly insists on is a different test, and it is
a hard one everywhere: name the benefit as a number with a date, and name the person who will
measure it. That person is usually in another organization, will be there years after your team has
dispersed, and has to agree. Getting one name into somebody else's operating plan took three weeks
here, and it is the only line that will still be true when the answer is known.

**Discuss:** Who outside your team would have to agree to measure your benefit?
