# Unit 7 · An Audit Is a Sample
%%SUB Twelve records of two hundred and thirty-one, three nonconformities, and no point estimate%%
%%GOALS
Write an audit finding
State the uncertainty in a sample
Say what an audit did not examine
%%

A standard revised in 2018 gives guidance on auditing, and the most important sentence in it is the
one nobody quotes: audit conclusions rest on samples of the information available, so there is an
element of uncertainty in auditing, and those acting on the conclusions should know that. Our 2022
audit of the same process found nothing. It examined five records from one month.

**5A  Complete the chart.**

%%FIG pf07-f6 | A three-column chart headed the structure, what it does and an example, with rows for on the evidence sampled, the sample does not support, a nonconformity was raised against, an opportunity for improvement, this audit did not examine and no conclusion is drawn as to, four filled in and two empty.%%

| Structure | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **on the evidence sampled** | limits the claim to what was seen | **On the evidence sampled**, three of twelve failed. |
| **the sample does not support** | refuses an inference | **The sample does not support** an estimate for the population. |
| **a nonconformity was raised against** | names the requirement, not the person | **A nonconformity was raised against** clause 4.3 of the procedure. |

| **this audit did not examine** | | |
| **no conclusion is drawn as to** | | |

> **Grammar Note** — A nonconformity is raised **against a requirement**, never against a person:
> *against clause 4.3*, not *against the project office*. The grammar matters because the response
> differs. A clause can be shown to have been met; a department can only defend itself.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

An audit is a sample and a report is read as a verdict, and the distance between those two things is
where most assurance goes wrong. A report that says *no nonconformities* is understood as *the
process is sound*, and once that sentence is in a slide it survives for years without the five
records and the one month that produced it. Writing the sample size, the period and the exclusions
costs four lines and is the single most useful thing an auditor can do. The second habit is about
whom you talk to. The interview list comes from the procedure, the procedure describes the official
process, and the official process is not where the interesting practice lives; one unscheduled
conversation with somebody not on the list is worth more than doubling the sample. And when that
conversation turns up a workaround, the response decides whether you ever hear another one: a drawer
of paper copies is evidence that a system is unusable in gloves, and an auditor who writes it up as
an individual failure has bought themselves an empty drawer and a quiet site for the next ten years.

**Discuss:** Whose workaround keeps your organization running?

### 🗣 Group ranking

*In fours.* Five records from one month against twelve across six. Rank these, best to worst. You
must agree.

a. State sample size, period, population and exclusions in every report
b. Ask one unscheduled question of somebody not on the list
c. Refuse to give a point estimate from a small sample
d. Write workarounds as design problems, not individual failures
e. Re-read any audit older than two years for its sample size before quoting it

Report your order, and say which one costs nothing.

### 3 · Write
