### 5 · Word study — distinguishing two measures

**5A  Complete the chart.**

%%FIG pf10-f6 | A three-column chart headed the structure, what it does and an example, with rows for measures but not, is not a proxy for, says nothing about, the two are not substitutes, covers only and tells you about, four filled in and two empty.%%

| Structure | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **measures … but not** | names the boundary of a figure | It **measures** individual harm **but not** process risk. |
| **is not a proxy for** | refuses a substitution | An injury rate **is not a proxy for** process safety. |
| **says nothing about** | states the silence | The figure **says nothing about** containment. |
| **the two are not substitutes** | blocks the trade | They are both needed; **the two are not substitutes**. |
| **covers only** | | |
| **tells you about** | | |

> **Grammar Note** — *Is not a proxy for* is the sentence to use when a number is good, true and
> being asked to carry a weight it cannot bear. It attacks the use and not the number, which is the
> only version of this argument that anybody listens to.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

One word covering two subjects is the most durable measurement error there is, because every
correction sounds like an attack on people who have earned their improvement. An injury rate that has
fallen by eighty percent is the achievement of hundreds of small decisions by people on a site, and
anybody who introduces process safety indicators by implying that the figure was a deception will
lose the argument and deserve to. Concede it in the first sentence, fully, and never return to it. The
second thing worth noticing is the shape of the evidence. The strongest argument for a new indicator
is always an event the old one could not have shown: not a hypothetical, but the worst thing that
actually happened last year, which injured nobody and therefore left no mark on the only number the
board sees. And then resist targets. A target on a count of reported near-misses improves the count
within a quarter by changing what gets reported, and the organization will have bought a better slide
and a worse plant — which is the lesson of a much earlier book in this series, learned again in a
different department.

**Discuss:** What is the worst thing that happened at your organization last year, and did it appear
in any number?
