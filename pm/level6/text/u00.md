# Starting Point
%%SUB Four questions to ask after a failure, and why *obvious in hindsight* is not a finding%%
%%GOALS
Ask what people knew at the time
Ask which decision would have had to be different
Ask which defenses were working
Ask who is accountable, as distinct from responsible
%%

Books 1 to 5 taught you to describe, estimate, measure, choose a method and price the work. This book
asks the question that comes after all of that has been done properly and the result is still bad.
Four questions, and then twelve units.

### 1 · Six things you brought from Book 5

**1A  Read the six examples aloud with a partner. Which two did you use this month?**

%%FIG pf00-f1 | A single review sheet with six boxes. Each box has a heading — a class and a range, falls to not falls by, over a period of, were it to fall by, within days of becoming aware, and what it is not — and one short example sentence from a project meeting.%%

| What you have | An example |
|---|---|
| A class and a range | 12 million at class 5: **between** 6 **and** 24. |
| *Falls to*, not *falls by* | Hours **fall to** 85 percent at every doubling. |
| A period and a rate | **Over a period of** 30 years **at a real rate of** 3.5 percent. |
| A switching value | **Were it to fall by** 23 percent, the result would be zero. |
| A time bar | **Within** 21 days **of** becoming aware, **failing which** the claim lapses. |
| The fourth column | A balance sheet **is not** a risk register. |

Say each example to your partner, then say one sentence of your own with the same structure.

### 2 · Four questions to ask after a failure

**2A  Read the four. Then ask them about something that went wrong at your own work.**

%%FIG pf00-f2 | A card taped inside a notebook, printed with four questions in bold, each with one line of smaller type beneath it saying what the answer tells you and what it does not.%%

> **1 · What did people know at the time?**
> *Not what you know now. The information available on the day, in the form it arrived, to the person
> who had to act on it.*
>
> **2 · Which decision, taken by whom, would have had to be different?**
> *If you cannot name a decision and a person, you have a story about bad luck rather than an
> account of a failure.*
>
> **3 · What defenses existed, and which of them were working?**
> *Most failures pass through several barriers that were all present and several that were present
> and not working.*
>
> **4 · Who is accountable, as distinct from who is responsible?**
> *Several people are responsible for doing things. One person answers for the whole. Those are
> different words and this book keeps them apart.*

Ask a partner all four about something real. Do not accept *it was obvious* as an answer to the
first.

**2B  Listen.** %%AUD TL6-00-02%%

**What do you think is wrong with the sentence *it was obvious this would happen*?**

### 3 · Reasonable and wrong

**3A  Read the two columns. Then say which column most review meetings live in.**

%%FIG pf00-f3 | Two columns side by side. The left column is headed how it looks afterwards and lists five judgments. The right column is headed how it looked on the day and lists the same five situations with the information that was actually available.%%

| How it looks afterwards | How it looked on the day |
|---|---|
| they ignored a clear warning | one of forty-one items on a weekly log |
| the trend was obvious | four readings, two of them disputed |
| nobody was willing to stop the job | stopping cost a known amount; carrying on cost an unknown one |
| the procedure was not followed | the procedure contradicted the method statement |
| a reckless decision | a decision that eleven similar decisions had survived |

**The right-hand column is not an excuse and it is not a softer version of the left.** It is what was
actually in front of somebody, and a review that works from the left-hand column produces findings
that cannot be acted on, because **nobody can be instructed to notice the obvious in advance.**

**Which of those five sentences have you heard said about real work?**

### 4 · Two ways of writing one finding

**4A  Read the two findings. Which one could somebody act on?**

%%FIG pf00-f4 | Two findings, one above the other. The first says the team failed to escalate a known risk. The second names the log, the item number, the number of competing items, the person, the absence of a threshold, and the change that would have made escalation automatic.%%

> **Finding A.** *The team failed to escalate a known risk.*
>
> **Finding B.** *The reading was recorded as item 29 of 41 on the weekly plant log on 3 and 10
> March. No threshold existed at which a plant log item required escalation, and the log's owner had
> no authority to stop work. A threshold of two consecutive readings above the stated limit would
> have made escalation automatic and is now in the procedure, owned by W. Zhao, from 1 May.*

**Finding A assigns blame and changes nothing.** Finding B names a document, a date, a missing
threshold, an authority that did not exist, and a change with an owner — **and it does not require
anybody to have been more alert than people are.**

decision, the person, and the defense that was present and not working?
