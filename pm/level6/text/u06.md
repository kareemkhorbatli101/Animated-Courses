# Unit 6 · Normal, By Then
%%SUB From two open waivers to nineteen, in seventeen reasonable steps, and two drifts that were right%%
%%GOALS
Dispute an account without calling anybody dishonest
Describe a drift in what counts as acceptable
Say when a drift was justified
%%

A study published in 1996 re-examined a decision that an official commission had investigated ten
years earlier, and argued that the commission's central explanation was mistaken. Not that the
commission lied: that it had reached for the wrong kind of explanation. The alternative it offers is
harder to act on and much harder to dismiss, and we have our own version of it in a list of nineteen
waivers.

### 5 · Word study — disputing an account

**5A  Complete the chart.**

%%FIG pf06-f6 | A three-column chart headed the structure, what it does and an example, with rows for the account offered was, on that reading, the difficulty with that account is, a better account is, she does not argue that and neither explanation requires, four filled in and two empty.%%

| Structure | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **the account offered was** | states the other explanation fairly | **The account offered was** that information did not reach the decision. |
| **on that reading** | works through its consequences | **On that reading**, the remedy is better reporting. |
| **the difficulty with that account is** | objects without accusing | **The difficulty with that account is** that each step was approved properly. |
| **a better account is** | proposes the alternative | **A better account is** that the standard moved while everybody complied. |
| **she does not argue that** | | |
| **neither explanation requires** | | |

> **Grammar Note** — *The difficulty with that account is* disputes an explanation; *that is not true*
> disputes a fact; and *they were covering it up* disputes a person's honesty. Most real disagreements
> in reviews are of the first kind and get expressed as the third, which ends the discussion and the
> learning with it.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

A number that moves by one, seventeen times, is invisible in every system an organization has,
because every system is built to examine decisions and a drift is not a decision. Each approval is
correct, recorded, authorized and defensible, and the only thing wrong is the sum, which nobody owns.
The usual remedy makes it worse: tightening the approval of each individual case adds work to the one
part of the process that was functioning, and leaves the cumulative question still unasked. Two
columns in a monthly report — the current number and the written limit beside it — cost nothing and
make the slope visible to anybody. The second habit is about the label. *Normalization of deviance*
has become a phrase used to blame people, which is precisely the opposite of what the study that
named it argued, and used loosely it also hides the opposite problem: a standard that was wrong when
written and has never once been met. Those are two different failures with two different remedies,
and printing them under one heading destroys the credibility of both.

**Discuss:** Which of your written limits has never once been met?
