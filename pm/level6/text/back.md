# Grammar reference

Every structure in this book, in unit order. The second column is the mistake it prevents, which is
the column worth reading twice.

## Starting Point · Four questions to ask after a failure

| | |
|---|---|
| **What did people know at the time?** | not what you know now, but what arrived, in what form, to the person who had to act |
| **Which decision, taken by whom, would have had to be different?** | without a decision and a person you have a story about bad luck |
| **What defenses existed, and which were working?** | most failures pass through barriers that were present and not working |
| **Who is accountable, as distinct from responsible?** | several people do things; one person answers for the whole |
| ***Obvious* is not a finding** | nobody can be instructed to notice the unobvious in advance |

## Unit 1 · Concession and counter-claim

| | |
|---|---|
| ***granted that* · *while it is true that* · *even so* · *that said* · *which does not mean that*** | a concession that gives nothing away signals that you are about to ignore the point |
| **concede the strongest version** | *granted that you have a point* concedes nothing; *granted that we have a clean instance in our own files* concedes everything |
| **description is not permission** | a thing that sometimes happens becomes a plan in one sentence of a board paper |
| **3 benevolent, 9 malevolent, 11 neither** | and all three of the first group are in the capability statement |

## Unit 2 · Hindsight and the narrative past

| | |
|---|---|
| ***by then* · *as it turned out* · *with hindsight* · *at the time* · the past perfect** | *with hindsight* and *at the time* must appear in the same paragraph or the paragraph is dishonest |
| **nine signals, seven holders, four systems, no one holding more than three** | the pieces failed to meet because one circuit had three names |
| **two signals led nowhere** | the incubation period is identified by its ending, so print the dead ends |
| **assemblable, not obvious** | one produces a mechanism; the other produces a recommendation about vigilance |

## Unit 3 · Inevitability and degree

| | |
|---|---|
| ***is liable to* · *is bound to* · *given* · *inherent in* · *not so much … as*** | *bound to* claims inevitability; use it only where a counter-example would refute you |
| **tight coupling needs central response; complex interaction needs local** | a system that is both needs both, and they conflict in the same minute |
| **the breach was correct and the procedure is not badly drafted** | two findings, neither canceling the other |
| **40 and an afternoon against a station** | and without a no-discipline clause the authority is decoration |

## Unit 4 · Layered causation

| | |
|---|---|
| ***the immediate cause* · *a contributing condition* · *lay dormant until* · *was itself a consequence of*** | *the cause* names the last event, which is always the person nearest the equipment |
| **active failures and latent conditions** | three of our four holes were opened by reasonable, recorded decisions |
| **an absence has no author** | and therefore no advocate and no opponent, so four reviews walked past it |
| **the only defense that worked had not been mentioned since 2021** | we review what fails and nothing asks what saved us |

## Unit 5 · Findings and recommendations

| | |
|---|---|
| ***it was found that* · *in our view* · *we recommend that* · *we were unable to determine*** | a finding printed beside a demand reads as an accusation |
| **four blocks: mandate, findings, recommendations, limits** | one list of twenty-two items makes every finding arguable and every recommendation personal |
| **every recommendation has a recipient and a date** | a recommendation with no recipient is a wish |
| **an investigation that concludes everything has concluded too much** | and a report with an unsupported name becomes a report about that person |

## Unit 6 · Disputing an account

| | |
|---|---|
| ***the account offered was* · *on that reading* · *the difficulty with that account is* · *a better account is*** | most disputes are about the kind of cause and get expressed as accusations of dishonesty |
| **17 approvals, each correct, and a number nobody chose** | tightening individual approval added work to the part that was functioning |
| **a drift is a deviation only if the standard was sound** | two of our five were standards that were never once met |
| **two columns in a monthly report** | the current figure beside the written limit, and no new decision at all |

## Unit 7 · The language of findings

| | |
|---|---|
| ***on the evidence sampled* · *the sample does not support* · *a nonconformity was raised against* · *this audit did not examine*** | a report that gives no sample size is read as a verdict and quoted for years |
| **raised against a clause, never against a person** | a clause can be shown to have been met; a department can only defend itself |
| **12 of 231, and no point estimate** | 2022's zero came from five records in one month |
| **one unscheduled question** | two of three findings came from somebody not on the interview list |

## Unit 8 · Accountability

| | |
|---|---|
| ***is accountable for* · *is responsible for* · *may delegate* · *remains accountable*** | *I delegated it* is an incomplete sentence about a delegation |
| **accountability cannot be delegated** | eleven blank rows were eleven activities the sponsor answered for without being told |
| **four accountable names is nobody** | the answer is *joint decision* plus an escalation route, not arithmetic |
| **one word, five senses** | owner, signatory, budget holder, reporter — and the fifth drove out the real one |

## Unit 9 · Obligations and their origins

| | |
|---|---|
| ***is required by* · *is bound by* · *has undertaken to* · *falls to*** | *we are required to* invites *by whom*; *we have undertaken to* invites nothing, because we wrote it |
| **147 obligations: 92 imposed, 55 chosen** | 23 of the 55 were sentences in tenders, written by people who did not know |
| **a certificate attests to the system** | not to compliance, and somebody will quote it as the second thing within a year |
| **61 owners had never seen the register** | which is how such a system passes every audit and changes nothing |

## Unit 10 · Distinguishing two measures

| | |
|---|---|
| ***measures … but not* · *is not a proxy for* · *says nothing about* · *the two are not substitutes*** | concede the good number in the first sentence and never return to it |
| **personal safety and process safety** | one word, two subjects, and they do not move together |
| **the worst event of the year could not appear in the only number** | which is the strongest argument for a new indicator and is never hypothetical |
| **no targets** | a target on reported near-misses buys a better slide and a worse plant in one quarter |

## Unit 11 · Blame and explanation

| | |
|---|---|
| ***was in a position to* · *had no means of* · *the question is not who but what* · *whose obligation is it*** | *should have known* has no factual answer; *was in a position to* has one |
| **retributive and restorative questions** | the second set is more expensive, because an obligation falls on the organization |
| **41, then 9, then 4** | one correct disciplinary decision and seventeen months of silence, in the same direction |
| **silence is worse than a sanction** | a sanction at least tells you somebody read it |

## Unit 12 · Saying what a protection cannot do

| | |
|---|---|
| ***depends on* · *cannot promise* · *creates no* · *assume that*** | a warning written against your own interest is the sentence that makes the rest believed |
| **anonymity depends on the size of the group concerned** | true of our software and false of a week on a site of six |
| **one promise entirely within your control** | a written reply in ten working days, even when the answer is no |
| **the fourth column** | thirty-six readings, six books, one column: what the document does not claim |

report being read aloud, because that is what they are.

# The British layer

This book is written in American English, and the series carries a switchable British layer: a fixed
list of words is respelled at build time. The list lives with the series rather than in this book.

What the layer does not touch:

1. **The date.** Write the month in letters where the sentence is prose, and the international format
   where it is data.
2. **A quoted document keeps its own spelling.** Every report, standard, book and paper quoted here is
   quoted as published.
3. **A document number keeps its published form.** ISO 19011:2018, ISO 37000:2021, ISO 37301:2021 and
   ISO 37002:2021 are citations, not words.
4. **Money has no symbol anywhere in this series.** The unit is whatever currency your project uses.
5. **A name is never respelled.** Hirschman, Turner, Pidgeon, Perrow, Reason, Vaughan and Dekker are
   spelled as published.

**Programme** keeps that spelling throughout, as in Books 1 to 5: it is the term ISO 21506:2018
defines, and the series uses it as that standard's defined term.

# Sources

Twelve source bodies carry the twelve reading texts, one per unit, and the Starting Point carries
none. Six are books or papers, one is an official commission report, one is the report of an
independent review panel, and four are international standards. Every one is cited with its edition
or date, and every reading text prints what the source says it does **not** establish. No source
appears in any other book of this series or in its four sister series, and no figure is reproduced
from any of them.

| Unit | Source | Edition cited |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | an essay on project ignorance and ingenuity, with a later critique | **The Public Interest, Winter 1967**; critique **2016** |
| 2 | a study of official accident inquiry reports | **first published 1978**; second edition **1997** |
| 3 | a book on accidents in complex, tightly coupled systems | **1984**; second edition **1999** |
| 4 | a book on organizational accidents, active failures and latent conditions | **1997**, developing **1990** |
| 5 | the report of a presidential commission on a space shuttle accident | **submitted June 1986** |
| 6 | a sociological study re-examining the same launch decision | **1996** |
| 7 | guidelines for auditing management systems | **ISO 19011:2018**, third edition |
| 8 | guidance on the governance of organizations | **ISO 37000:2021** |
| 9 | requirements for compliance management systems | **ISO 37301:2021**, replacing guidance of **2014** |
| 10 | the report of an independent safety review panel on five refineries | **January 2007** |
| 11 | an argument about accountability after failure | **first published 2007** |
| 12 | guidelines for whistleblowing management systems | **ISO 37002:2021** |

**A note about Units 5 and 6, which must be read together.** Unit 5 is about the structure of an
official commission's report on an accident in which seven people died. Unit 6 is about a study
published ten years later which argues that the commission reached for the wrong kind of explanation
of the launch decision. **Neither unit summarizes the accident, reconstructs any conversation, or
invents any detail**, and neither suggests that anybody involved was careless. They are in this book
because **holding two serious accounts of one event, and understanding what each is for, is the most
advanced thing this series asks of a reader** — and because the language of that disagreement is the
language of every difficult review.

**The honesty rule of this series, in this book's own words.** Every reading names its source, its
date and its publisher, and then prints what the source says it cannot establish. In this book those
limits carry more weight than anywhere else in the series, because six of the twelve sources are
arguments rather than instructions:

- **Unit 1.** An essay resting on eleven observed projects, presented as providential and
  unplannable, whose author states that a difficulty cannot be hidden from oneself deliberately.
- **Unit 2.** A sociological account derived from official inquiry reports, which offers no way of
  identifying an incubation period from inside one — and inherits the framing of the reports it draws
  on.
- **Unit 3.** A classification of systems, not a probability, whose central claim is contested by
  researchers of high-reliability organizations.
- **Unit 4.** An organizing metaphor with no number of layers, no method of counting holes and no
  order of priority among remedies.
- **Unit 5.** A commission charged with determining probable cause and recommending action, produced
  in months, which is not a determination of liability and states where it could not conclude.
- **Unit 6.** A reinterpretation of one decision in one organization, offering no threshold for drift
  and no way of detecting one, and itself contested.
- **Unit 7.** Guidance that prescribes no sample size and states that audit conclusions rest on
  samples and carry uncertainty.
- **Unit 8.** Guidance that prescribes no structure, no board composition and no legal duties, and
  cannot create a governing body that wishes to be told things.
- **Unit 9.** A requirements standard whose certification attests to a management system and not to
  compliance, and which identifies nobody's obligations for them.
- **Unit 10.** A panel reporting on five refineries of one company, which states that its findings
  may not be applicable elsewhere and was not the accident investigation.
- **Unit 11.** An argument, not a procedure: no test of culpability, no threshold for recklessness,
  and contested by people who hold that accountability requires consequences.
- **Unit 12.** Guidelines that create no legal rights, cannot be certified against, and cannot create
  the trust on which the system they describe entirely depends.

# A note on everything else in this book

Every person, team, company, project, number and incident **invented in this book is invented**.
Lindhurst does not exist; neither do the Calder station, the near-miss of 14 June, the nineteen
waivers, the thirty-one overdue items, the reporting figures of forty-one, nine and four, the 2019
dismissal, or any of the people who appear in the dialogues. The documents are real and cited; the
stories around them are written to teach the language.

**Two of this book's sources concern real accidents in which people died.** They are discussed because
they are among the clearest published accounts of how competent organizations fail, and because their
vocabulary — *latent condition*, *contributing cause*, *finding*, *normalized*, *accountable* — is the
vocabulary of every serious review. **They are discussed without reconstructed dialogue, without
invented detail, and without any suggestion that the people involved were careless.** Where this book
states what a report concluded, it names the report and the year and prints what the report itself
said it could not establish.

**Nothing in this book is legal, regulatory, safety, employment or professional advice**, and none of
it is a substitute for the investigation report, standard or law that applies to your own work. Where
a sentence sounds like a rule — *every report receives a written reply within ten working days*, *a
trip under these four conditions is never a disciplinary matter* — that is one invented company's
internal practice, printed because a concrete commitment teaches better than an abstraction. **Your
clause is the one in your contract, your procedure and your law. Read it.**

# Closing

You began this series able to say what a project is. You end it able to say **which edition of which
document**, **what the document says it does not cover**, **how much of it you actually practice**,
**what your number is a number of**, **what range it carries**, **who is obliged by it and from
when**, **which defense is currently working**, **which hole has no author**, **what your audit did
not examine**, **who answers in public**, and **what you cannot promise.**

Seventy-two units. Seventy-two documents. One habit.

**Write the fourth column.**

Six sentences to take with you, one from each book.

From Book 1: **a project is temporary and produces something; everything else is work.**

From Book 2: **an estimate with no range is silent about its own accuracy.**

From Book 3: **follow every number down until you reach a person collecting something.**

From Book 4: **an omission with no author becomes a rule in eighteen months.**

From Book 5: **if you cannot say what a number is not, you do not yet know what it is.**

From Book 6: **everybody in the story did something defensible, and the sum was nearly an
incident.**

And one sentence that belongs to no single book, because all six of them are about it:

**A document is only safe to use once you know what it does not claim.**
