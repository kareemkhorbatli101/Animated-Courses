# Unit 3 · Tightly Coupled
%%SUB An operator who broke the procedure and was right, and two requirements that genuinely conflict%%
%%GOALS
Talk about inevitability and degree
Place a system on two dimensions
Say when a procedure cannot be fixed by choosing a side
%%

A book published in 1984 argued that some systems produce accidents as a normal consequence of how
they are built: where parts interact in unexpected ways and where there is no slack between them,
failures combine faster than anybody can diagnose them. Its most uncomfortable claim is not that such
systems are dangerous. It is that the two things they need are in conflict.

> **B:** **Given what he was looking at, yes.** **Two readings disagreed, a valve position was
> unknown, and the time available was under a minute.** **A telephone call takes longer than that.**
> **A:** Then the procedure is wrong and we should change it.
> **B:** **Here is the difficulty, and it is the argument of a book from 1984.** **A system with no
> slack in it needs a centralized response: somebody who can see the whole thing and act for all of
> it.** **A system whose parts interact in unforeseen ways needs a decentralized one: somebody on the
> spot who can act before anybody understands why.**
> **A:** And our control system is both.
> **B:** **Interactively complex and tightly coupled, on both counts.** **So it needs centralized
> authority and local discretion at the same time, and those two requirements genuinely conflict.**
> **It is not a drafting error in our procedure. It is inherent in the thing we have built.**
> **A:** Then what do we do?
> **B:** **Not choose a side, because either choice is wrong in some condition.** **Bound the
> exception.** **Four named conditions in which an operator may trip without authority; notification
> within ten minutes; and a trip under those conditions is never a disciplinary matter, whatever it
> turns out to have been.**
> **A:** And if he trips unnecessarily?
> **B:** **Then we lose about forty thousand and an afternoon.** **If he waits for the call in the
> wrong minute, we lose a pumping station.** **That is not a close trade-off, and writing it down is
> the only way anybody will believe it at six in the morning.**

%%FIG pf03-f5 | A one-page internal note: a heading, a line giving the book, its year and its second edition, a two-by-two chart with four quadrants and two of our systems placed on it, a boxed paragraph about a trip at twenty to six, a four-row table of bounded exception conditions, a line citing researchers who dispute the framework, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

> ## Lindhurst · quality note 3
> *Quality · where our systems sit, and one procedural conflict · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The book.** **C. Perrow**, *Normal Accidents: Living with High-Risk Technologies*, **1984**, with
> a **second edition in 1999** carrying an afterword. Its argument is that in certain systems serious
> accidents are **normal** — not frequent, but **inherent in how the system is built** rather than
> caused by carelessness.
>
> **Two dimensions.**
>
> | | Loosely coupled | **Tightly coupled** |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Linear interactions** | a works yard: slack everywhere, order comprehensible | an assembly line: no slack, but you can see what feeds what |
> | **Complex interactions** | a design office: parts affect each other oddly, but there is time | **the dangerous quadrant: unforeseen interaction and no time** |
>
> **Where our two systems sit.**
>
> | System | Interactions | Coupling | Quadrant |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | the **civil works** | **linear** — excavate, pour, cure, backfill | **loose** — weeks of float, alternative sequences | the comfortable one |
> | the **control system** | **complex** — telemetry, interlocks, pumps, tariff logic and a standby set, all able to affect one another | **tight** — seconds, no buffer, no manual fallback since 2021 | **the dangerous one** |
>
> **The conflict, which is the part of the book that matters to us.**
>
> | A tightly coupled system needs | A complexly interacting system needs |
> |---|---|
> | **centralized** response: one view of the whole, acting for all of it | **decentralized** response: local judgment, acting before anybody understands why |
> | because a disturbance **crosses everything at once** | because **nobody at the top can diagnose an interaction nobody foresaw** |
>
> **A system that is both needs both, and they contradict.** **This is not a flaw in anybody's
> procedure. It is a property of what we have built.**
>
> **Our own instance, 14 June, 05:40.**
>
> > The emergency procedure reads: **notify the duty manager; trip if instructed.** **The operator
> > tripped and telephoned afterwards.**
> >
> > What he had: **two disagreeing readings**, a **valve position unknown**, and **under a minute**.
> > **A telephone call does not fit inside that minute.**
> >
> > **The breach was correct.** **The procedure, read literally, would have required him to wait.**
> > **Both of those sentences are true and neither cancels the other.**
>
> **What we are doing about it: not choosing a side.** Choosing central authority loses the minute.
> Choosing local discretion loses the whole-system view in the cases where that is what matters.
> **The answer is a bounded exception.**
>
> | | The bounded exception |
> |---|---|
> | **1** | the operator **may trip without authority** in **four named conditions** |
> | **2** | notification **within 10 minutes**, not before |
> | **3** | a trip under those conditions is **never a disciplinary matter**, whatever the cause turns out to have been |
> | **4** | every such trip is **reviewed within five working days**, for the conditions and not the person |
>
> **The arithmetic that makes it obvious and that nobody had written down.** **An unnecessary trip
> costs about 40 and an afternoon.** **A trip that comes one minute late costs a pumping station.**
> **That is not a close trade-off**, and at twenty to six in the morning a person needs it in writing
> rather than in their judgment.
>
> **What the book does not do, and who disagrees with it.** It is a **framework for classifying
> systems**, not a measurement: **the placement of any system in a quadrant is a judgment**, and I
> have made the two above. It **does not say how likely an accident is**, only that in one quadrant
> accidents are a normal product of the design. For some systems the author's conclusion is that the
> technology should not be used, which **is not a conclusion available to us about a pumping
> station**. And the framework is **disputed**: researchers of high-reliability organizations have
> argued that some complex, tightly coupled organizations achieve very low failure rates over long
> periods. **I am not in a position to adjudicate that argument and I am not going to pretend
> otherwise** — but the conflict between centralized and local response is visible in our own
> procedure, whoever is right about the larger claim.
>
> *Source: C. Perrow, Normal Accidents: Living with High-Risk Technologies, 1984, second edition 1999.
> The book classifies systems by interactive complexity and by coupling, and argues that in systems
> that are both interactively complex and tightly coupled, accidents arising from unanticipated
> interactions of multiple failures are a normal consequence of the design. The classification of any
> particular system is a matter of judgment; the framework offers no probability of accident; and its
> central claim has been contested by researchers of high-reliability organizations.*

### 5 · Word study — inevitability and degree

**5A  Complete the chart.**

%%FIG pf03-f6 | A three-column chart headed the structure, how strong it is and an example, with rows for is liable to, is bound to, given, inherent in, to the extent that and not so much as, four filled in and two empty.%%

| Structure | How strong | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **is liable to** | possible, and in the nature of the thing | Such a system **is liable to** fail in ways nobody modeled. |
| **is bound to** | strongest; avoid unless you mean it | With no fallback, a disturbance **is bound to** propagate. |
| **given** | states the condition that makes it so | **Given** two disagreeing readings, he had no diagnosis. |
| **inherent in** | locates the cause in the design | The conflict is **inherent in** the system, not the procedure. |
| **to the extent that** | | |
| **not so much … as** | | |

> **Grammar Note** — *Is bound to* claims inevitability and is almost always too strong; *is liable
> to* claims a tendency arising from the nature of the thing, which is what this book actually argues.
> Write *bound to* only where you would accept being shown a counter-example as a refutation.

### 1 · Inevitability and degree

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD TL6-03-06%%

> **Sofia:** Your operator breached a written procedure and you are defending him.
> **Wei:** **I am defending the action and criticizing the procedure, and the procedure is not badly
> drafted.** **It is as good as a procedure can be for a system like this.**
> **Sofia:** Explain that.
> **Wei:** **The system is tightly coupled: no slack, no buffer, and no manual fallback since 2021.**
> **Given that, a disturbance crosses the whole plant in seconds, and a response is liable to be
> wrong unless somebody can see all of it.** **That argues for central authority.**
> **Sofia:** Which is what the procedure gives.
> **Wei:** **And the same system is interactively complex: telemetry, interlocks, pumps, tariff logic
> and a standby set can all affect one another in combinations nobody designed.** **Given that, the
> diagnosis has to be made where the readings are, by whoever is there, often before anybody
> understands the mechanism.** **That argues for local discretion.**
> **Sofia:** So the procedure should say both.
> **Wei:** **It cannot say both in the same minute.** **This is not so much a drafting problem as a
> property of the design, and it is the central argument of a book from 1984.** **A system that is
> both complex and tightly coupled needs centralized and decentralized response simultaneously, and
> those are incompatible.**
> **Sofia:** Then what did you write?
> **Wei:** **A bounded exception. Four named conditions in which he may trip without authority;
> notification within ten minutes rather than before; and no disciplinary consequence, whatever the
> cause turns out to have been.**
> **Sofia:** The last part will be resisted.
> **Wei:** **It is the only part that matters.** **Without it the exception is theoretical, because at
> twenty to six in the morning a person weighs an unnecessary trip against a conversation with
> management, and the conversation wins.**
> **Sofia:** And the cost of being wrong?
> **Wei:** **Forty thousand and an afternoon for an unnecessary trip.** **A pumping station for a trip
> one minute late.** **To the extent that we are trading anything, we are trading forty thousand
> against the plant, and I would like that sentence in the procedure rather than in my head.**
> **Sofia:** Is this framework accepted?
> **Wei:** **It is contested.** **Researchers studying high-reliability organizations argue that some
> systems of this kind run for decades without serious failure.** **I am not able to settle that, and
> I do not need to: our own procedure contains the conflict, whoever is right.**

*Why does Wei say the no-discipline clause is the only part that matters?*

**1B  Underline five degree structures in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **is liable to** | A response **is liable to** be wrong without a whole-system view. |
| **given** | **Given** the coupling, a disturbance crosses the plant in seconds. |
| **inherent in** | The conflict is **inherent in** the design. |
| **not so much … as** | **Not so much** a drafting problem **as** a property of the system. |
| **to the extent that** | **To the extent that** we are trading anything, it is 40 against a station. |
| **is bound to** | With no fallback at all, the disturbance **is bound to** propagate. |

> **Grammar Note** — *Not so much X as Y* relocates a problem without denying X. It is the most
> useful structure in this book, because most governance disputes are about where a problem lives:
> *not so much a failure of attention as a failure of information design.*

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

The hardest finding to write is that two correct requirements conflict, because every organization is
built to believe that a conflict means somebody has drafted something badly. So a review that says
*the procedure required a call that cannot fit in the time available* gets read as *the procedure is
wrong*, and the next version swings to the other extreme and is wrong in a different minute. The
second difficulty is about courage, and it is where most bounded exceptions die. You can give an
operator authority to stop a plant, and unless you also remove the personal consequence of being
wrong, the authority is decorative: at twenty to six in the morning a person is weighing forty
thousand and an afternoon against a difficult conversation with people who will have all day to
think about it, and the conversation wins. Writing *this is never a disciplinary matter* costs
nothing and is resisted everywhere, because it looks like permission. It is the opposite: it is the only
way the written rule survives contact with the hour at which it is needed.

**Discuss:** What would it cost somebody at your organization to be wrong about stopping something?

### 🗣 Group ranking

*In fours.* Forty seconds, a telephone call, forty thousand against a station. Rank these, best to
worst. You must agree.

a. Name the conditions in which authority is not required
b. Write that such an action is never a disciplinary matter
c. Put the two costs side by side in the procedure itself
d. Review every such action within five working days, for conditions not people
e. Restore a manual fallback, so the system is less tightly coupled

Report your order, and say which one addresses the design rather than the response.
