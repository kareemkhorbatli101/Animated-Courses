# Unit 9 · The Obligation Register
%%SUB One hundred and forty-seven obligations, fifty-five of them volunteered, and three being breached now%%
%%GOALS
Distinguish a requirement from a commitment
Say where an obligation came from
Say what a certificate does not certify
%%

A standard published in 2021 requires an organization to write down everything it must comply with
and everything it has promised to comply with, and to treat both as obligations. We had never done
it. Fifty-five of our one hundred and forty-seven obligations were created by us, in tender
documents, by people who did not know they were creating anything.

%%FIG pf09-f5 | A one-page internal note: a heading, a line giving the standard, its year and the guidance standard it replaced, a two-column register summary with totals, a breakdown of the voluntary column by origin, a block naming three current breaches, a cover note about certification, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

> ## Lindhurst · compliance note 9
> *Commercial · the first obligation register · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The standard.** **ISO 37301:2021**, *Compliance management systems — Requirements with guidance
> for use*. Unlike most of the documents in this series it is a **requirements** standard: **an
> organization can be certified against it.** It replaced a **guidance** standard of **2014**, and
> that change — from *should* to *shall* — is the whole difference.
>
> **Its central vocabulary.** A **compliance obligation** is either:
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | a **requirement** | something the organization **must** comply with: statute, regulation, permit condition, binding contract clause |
> | a **commitment** | something the organization **has chosen** to comply with: a code of practice, a standard it says it works to, a promise it has made |
>
> **The standard requires both to be identified, recorded, assessed and owned.** **It does not
> distinguish them by seriousness**, and in our experience the volunteered ones are harder to
> explain.
>
> **Our first register: 147 obligations.**
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | **Requirements** (compulsory) | **92** |
> | **Commitments** (voluntary) | **55** |
> | **Obligations with a named owner** | **86** |
> | **Obligations owned by somebody who has never seen the register** | **61** |
>
> **Where the fifty-five came from.**
>
> | Origin | Count |
> |---|---|
> | codes of practice formally adopted by the board | 14 |
> | standards named in our quality policy | 11 |
> | agreements with neighbors and community bodies | 7 |
> | **promises made in tender documents** | **23** |
>
> **The twenty-three are the finding.**
>
> > They were written by **bid writers, over six years, in good faith**, and **none of them knew they
> > were creating an obligation.** *"We will exceed the requirement by maintaining a four-week rolling
> > look-ahead"* is a sentence written to win work. **It is also something we have undertaken to
> > do.**
> >
> > **There is no mechanism in this company by which a sentence in a tender becomes an entry in any
> > register.** The bid is written, the job is won, the bid goes into an archive, and **the promise
> > survives in a document the client keeps.**
>
> **Three are being breached now.**
>
> | | The commitment | The position |
> |---|---|---|
> | 1 | a **four-week rolling look-ahead** | **two weeks since March** |
> | 2 | a **monthly environmental return** to the client | **discontinued in year two** |
> | 3 | a **named site liaison officer** | **currently nobody** |
>
> **None of these is serious and nobody has noticed, which is why they are in this note.** **Each is
> in a document the client can produce**, and a promise we volunteered is **harder to argue about
> than a requirement imposed on us, because we chose it and we chose the wording.**
>
> **The ownership problem.** **Sixty-one of the hundred and forty-seven are owned by people who have
> never seen the register.** Until that changes, **this is a document about the compliance function
> and not about the company** — which is the commonest way for a system like this to fail while
> passing every audit.
>
> **One sentence I want printed on the certificate when we get it.**
>
> > **A certificate against this standard states that our compliance management system meets the
> > standard's requirements.** **It does not state that we are compliant with our obligations.**
> > **Somebody will quote it as if it did, within a year of our getting it, and I would rather the
> > sentence were already printed when they do.**
>
> **What the standard does not do.** It **does not list anybody's obligations** — identifying them is
> the organization's own job, and getting that list wrong is not something the standard can catch. It
> **does not grade obligations by importance.** It **requires a compliance culture and cannot create
> one**: the clauses about the governing body's commitment are, in the end, a description of
> behavior that a document can ask for and not produce. **And certification is of the system, not of
> the conduct**, which is the sentence above and the reason it needs printing.
>
> *Source: ISO 37301:2021, Compliance management systems — Requirements with guidance for use, a
> requirements standard against which an organization's compliance management system may be
> certified, replacing the guidance standard of 2014. It defines compliance obligations as comprising
> requirements that an organization must comply with and commitments it chooses to comply with, and
> requires them to be identified, assessed and managed. It does not identify any organization's
> obligations, does not grade them, and certification attests to the management system rather than to
> the organization's actual compliance.*

| Structure | Where it comes from | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **is required by** | law, regulation, permit | We **are required by** the permit to sample weekly. |
| **is bound by** | a contract | We **are bound by** clause 14 to report monthly. |
| **has undertaken to** | our own promise | We **have undertaken to** maintain a four-week look-ahead. |

| **falls to** | | |
| **is obliged to** | | |

> **Grammar Note** — *Is required to* and *has undertaken to* carry the same force and different
> histories, and the second is the harder one to explain away. *We are required to* invites the
> question *by whom*; *we have undertaken to* invites no question at all, because we wrote it
> ourselves.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Organizations track what is imposed on them and forget what they volunteered, and the asymmetry is
almost perfect: every legal requirement has a system behind it and every promise in a tender has
nobody. The reason is mechanical rather than cynical. A bid is written to win work, under time
pressure, by people whose job ends when the job is won; the document then goes into an archive named
by year, and the client keeps a copy in a folder they will open during a dispute. Nothing in between
turns a sentence into an entry in a register. The second habit worth examining is what a certificate
is read to mean. A certificate against a management system standard says the system meets the
standard; it says nothing about whether the organization is complying with its obligations today, and
those two statements will be treated as identical by somebody within a year. Printing the distinction
on the cover costs one sentence. Refusing to print it costs the moment, three years later, when
somebody waves the certificate at a breach.

**Discuss:** What has your organization promised that nobody is tracking?

> **Where the 55 commitments came from.** **14** codes adopted by the board; **11** standards named
> in our quality policy; **7** agreements with neighbors; and **23 promises made in tender
