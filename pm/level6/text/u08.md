# Unit 8 · Accountable, Not Responsible
%%SUB Sixty-one activities, eleven with nobody accountable, and one word meaning five things%%
%%GOALS
Distinguish accountability from responsibility
Say what can and cannot be delegated
Say who answers in public
%%

A standard published in 2021 is the first international guidance on the governance of organizations,
and it rests on a distinction our own documents do not make: responsibility can be delegated and
accountability cannot. Our programme chart has sixty-one activities. Eleven of them have nobody
accountable at all, and the word itself is used in five different senses across four documents.

> ## Lindhurst · governance note 8
> *Project office · sixty-one activities, one word · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The standard.** **ISO 37000:2021**, *Governance of organizations — Guidance*. It is **guidance**,
> not a requirements standard: **there is nothing in it to be certified against**, and it does not
> prescribe a board structure or anybody's legal duties.
>
> **Its definition.** Governance is the system by which the whole organization is **directed,
> controlled and held accountable** in order to achieve its **purpose** over the long term.
>
> **Its principles, in the order the standard puts them.** **Purpose** is primary. On it rest **value
> generation**, **strategy**, **oversight** and **accountability**; and around those sit
> **stakeholder engagement**, **leadership**, **data and decisions**, **risk governance**, **social
> responsibility**, and **viability and performance over time**.
>
> **The distinction this note is about.**
>
> | | Can it be delegated? |
> |---|---|
> | **responsibility** — the obligation to **carry out** a task | **yes** |
> | **accountability** — the obligation to **answer for an outcome** | **no** |
>
> **A governing body may delegate authority; it remains accountable.** **Two people cannot both
> answer for one outcome**, because when the question is asked in public only one answer can be
> given.
>
> **Our own chart: sixty-one activities.**
>
> | Names in the accountability column | Activities |
> |---|---|
> | **0** | **11** |
> | 1 | **45** |
> | 2 | 4 |
> | **4** | **1** |
>
> **The eleven blank rows are the finding and they are not what they look like.** **They are not
> eleven activities for which nobody answers.** **They are eleven activities for which the sponsor
> answers without having been told**, because accountability for the programme as a whole has not
> gone anywhere: **it cannot.** **The chart as drawn invites her to believe those rows are somebody
> else's or nobody's.**
>
> **The row with four names.** This one was an **honest attempt** and it should not simply be
> corrected.
>
> > It records the **interface decision with the client**, which genuinely is taken jointly. Writing
> > four names was somebody trying to be accurate.
> >
> > **The fix is not to choose one of the four.** It is to write **joint decision**, to name **the two
> > bodies** that take it, and to name the **escalation route if they disagree** — which at present
> > does not exist.
> >
> > **An activity with four accountable names has no escalation. An activity with two names and a
> > route has one.**
>
> **And the word itself: five senses in four documents.**
>
> | Where | *Accountable* means |
> |---|---|
> | the delivery procedure | the **owner of the task** |
> | the waiver form | the **signatory** |
> | the budget manual | the **budget holder** |
> | the incident procedure | the person who will be **criticized** |
> | the meeting terms of reference | the person who **reports** |
>
> **Those are five jobs and they are often five different people.** **Each of them has a perfectly
> good word of its own** — owner, signatory, budget holder, subject of a finding, reporter — **and the
> fifth sense is the one that has driven out the standard's meaning**, which is: **answering for the
> outcome, in public, without being able to pass it on.**
>
> **What the standard does not do.** It is **guidance**, and offers **no structure, no board
> composition, no committee list and no legal duties** — those belong to law and to the organization.
> It does not say **how many people should be accountable for anything**, and the distinction above is
> a consequence of the words rather than a rule it imposes. And **it cannot create a governing body
> that wants to be told things**, which is the subject of Unit 11 and the only part of governance that
> no document has ever solved.
>
> *Source: ISO 37000:2021, Governance of organizations — Guidance, the first international standard
> giving guidance on the governance of organizations. It defines governance as the system by which the
> whole organization is directed, controlled and held accountable in order to achieve its purpose, and
> sets out governance principles with purpose as the primary principle. It is guidance rather than
> requirements, is not intended for certification, and does not prescribe governance structures, board
> composition or legal duties.*

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

The word *accountable* has been destroyed in most organizations by one of its five uses: the person
who will be criticized. Once it means that, nobody volunteers for the column, charts acquire blanks,
and the blanks default silently upwards to whoever is accountable for the whole — who then discovers
in a meeting that they are answering for eleven things nobody told them about. The repair is
vocabulary, not courage. Owner, signatory, budget holder and reporter are four perfectly good words
for four different jobs; keep *accountable* for its real meaning, which is answering for an outcome
with no ability to pass it on. The second habit worth keeping is about delegation. *I delegated it* is
an incomplete sentence, and almost every delegation in every organization stops there. The complete
version has two halves — *I have delegated authority for this, and I remain accountable for the
outcome* — and the second half is the one that makes the first half safe, because it tells everybody
who will still be standing there when the question is asked.

**Discuss:** Which of the five senses does *accountable* have in your organization?
