Guidelines published in 2021 describe a system for receiving, assessing, addressing and concluding
reports of wrongdoing, resting on trust, impartiality and protection. We are building one. The first
honest sentence in our policy says that an anonymous report from a site of six people is not
anonymous, which is not what a policy is supposed to say and is the only reason anybody will believe
the rest of it.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Six books end on the sentence they have been circling from the first unit of the first one: a document
is only safe to use once you know what it does not claim. Every source in this series said so about
itself, usually on an early page, and in almost every story somebody used it for the adjacent purpose
in perfectly good faith. The last document is the hardest case, because what it cannot supply is the
thing it exists to produce. Guidelines can set out four stages, three principles and a reply period;
they cannot create the willingness of a person on a site of six to write something down about somebody
they will see on Tuesday. That willingness is made and destroyed by what happened to the last person
who tried, which is why Unit 11's three numbers — forty-one, nine, four — are the only measurement in
this book that tells you whether any of the rest of it is working. Write the limits down, answer every
report, and put a name in the last column. Nothing else in six books has been shown to work.

**Discuss:** Who at your organization is deciding today whether to write something down?
