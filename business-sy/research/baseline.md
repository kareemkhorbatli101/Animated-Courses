## A. Cambridge Business English Certificates — structural reference

* Cambridge Assessment English, *Business English Certificates Handbook for
  teachers*, https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/images/business-english-certificates-handbook-for-teachers.pdf
* Edition as printed in the document: **Copyright © UCLES 2019 |
  CER/3515/V1/APR19** (April 2019).
* Retrieved 2026-11-07, 3,935,892 bytes, 15,745 lines of extracted text.

### Status: these exams are WITHDRAWN

Cambridge retired the Business English Certificates. Reported final sessions
were **December 2023** (computer-based) and **June 2024** (paper-based), and
Cambridge's own exam-format pages for them now sit under `old-business-
preliminary` and `old-business-vantage`. The successor product, Linguaskill
Business, is adaptive and is **not** divided into B1/B2/C1 levels, so it
publishes band descriptors rather than a task budget.

**Consequence for this course, stated plainly.** The handbook is used here as
a *structural and pedagogical* reference for task types, item counts and text
lengths — facts about how business English has been assessed, which remain
true of the document whether or not the exam still runs. It is **not** cited
as a live standard, and no part of this course claims to prepare a learner
for an exam they can sit. Where the course needs a live standard it uses the
CEFR (baseline C). Where neither publishes a figure, the field is `null` and
the gate rule becomes relative-consistency rather than an invented absolute.

### B1 Business Preliminary, as published

**Reading and Writing: 1 hour 30 minutes** (combined paper).

Reading — 7 parts, 45 questions, 45 marks:

| Part | Qs | Task type | Focus | Format |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 | Multiple choice | Understanding short real-world notices, messages, etc. | Five short texts each followed by a 3-option multiple-choice question |
| 2 | 5 | Matching | Detailed comprehension of factual material; skimming and scanning | A text followed by questions that need matching to parts of the text |
| 3 | 5 | Matching | Interpreting visual information | A graphic or graphics followed by questions that need matching to parts of the graphic(s) |
| 4 | 7 | Right/Wrong/Doesn't say | Reading for detailed factual information | A single text followed by a choice of responses |
| 5 | 6 | Multiple choice | Reading for gist and specific information | A text followed by 3-option multiple-choice questions |
| 6 | 12 | Multiple-choice cloze | Grammatical accuracy and understanding of text structure | A gapped text with 3-option multiple-choice questions for each gap |
| 7 | 5 | Note completion | Reading and information transfer | Two short input texts, e.g. an email and a memo, followed by a form-filling/note-completion exercise |

Reading text word counts at this level: **not stated in the handbook.**

Writing — 2 parts. Part 1 carries one-third of the marks, Part 2 two-thirds:

| Part | Task | Words | Text type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One compulsory task | **30–40** | Internal communication (note, message, memo or email). (Re)arranging appointments, asking for permission, giving instructions |
| 2 | One compulsory task | **60–80** | Business correspondence (letter, fax, email). Apologising and offering compensation, making or altering reservations, dealing with requests, giving information about a product |

Listening — 4 parts, 30 questions, 30 marks. Two text types: monologues
(presentations, lectures, announcements, briefings) and interacting speakers
(telephone conversations, face-to-face conversations, interviews,
discussions). 5 minutes at the end to transfer answers.

| Part | Qs | Task type | Audio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8 | Multiple choice | Eight short conversations or monologues of approximately **15–30 seconds** each, not linked thematically. Each extract heard twice |
| 2 | 7 | Note completion | A conversation of approximately **1½ minutes**, heard twice |
| 3 | 7 | Note completion | A monologue of approximately **2½ minutes**, heard twice |
| 4 | 8 | Multiple choice | Conversation/interview/discussion between two or more speakers, approximately **3 minutes**, heard twice |

Speaking — 3 parts, two candidates and two examiners:

| Part | Timing | Task |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **2 minutes** | Conversation between the interlocutor and each candidate |
| 2 | **5 minutes** (including a 1-minute long turn for each candidate) | A mini-presentation by each candidate on a business theme |
| 3 | **5 minutes** | Collaborative task candidates do together |

Cambridge English Scale, as published for this exam: grade A **160–170**,
grade B or C **160–179** band text as printed, level B1 **140–159**, level A2
**120–139**.

### B2 Business Vantage, as published

Reading — **1 hour**, 5 parts, 45 questions, 45 marks. Parts 1–3 test reading
comprehension; Parts 4–5 test understanding of written English at word,
phrase, sentence and paragraph level.

| Part | Qs | Task type | Focus | Text length |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 7 | Matching | Scanning and reading for gist | One continuous text in four sections, or four short informational texts, approximately **250–350 words** in total |
| 2 | 5 | Matching | Understanding text structure | Single text (article, report) with sentence-length gaps, approximately **450–550 words** |
| 3 | 6 | Multiple choice | Reading for gist and specific information | Single text of approximately **450–550 words** |
| 4 | 15 | Multiple-choice cloze | Vocabulary and structure | A single informational text with lexical gaps, text including gapped words approximately **200–300 words** |
| 5 | 12 | Proofreading | Understanding sentence structure and error identification | Short text approximately **150–200 words**. Identification of additional unnecessary words |

Writing — **45 minutes**, 2 parts. Part 1 one-third of marks, Part 2
two-thirds:

| Part | Task | Words | Text type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 compulsory | **40–50** | A message, memo or email. Giving instructions, explaining a development, asking for comments, requesting information, agreeing to requests |
| 2 | 1 compulsory | **120–140** | Business correspondence, short report or proposal. Correspondence: explaining, apologising, reassuring, complaining. Report: describing, summarising. Proposal: describing, summarising, recommending, persuading |

Listening — 3 parts, 30 questions, 30 marks. Monologues (presentations,
lectures, briefings, telephone messages) and interacting speakers (meetings,
discussions, interviews, telephone conversations). 10 minutes to transfer
answers.

| Part | Qs | Task type | Audio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 12 | Note completion | Three short monologues or dialogues of approximately **1 minute** each, not linked thematically. Each extract heard twice |
| 2 | 10 | Matching | Short monologues; two sections of five short monologues, not linked thematically. Each section heard twice |
| 3 | 8 | Multiple choice | A monologue, interview or discussion lasting approximately **4 minutes**, heard twice |

Speaking — 3 parts:

| Part | Timing | Task |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **3 minutes** | Conversation between the interlocutor and each candidate |
| 2 | **6 minutes** (including a 1-minute long turn for each candidate) | A mini-presentation by each candidate on a business theme |
| 3 | **5 minutes** | Collaborative task, then the interlocutor extends the discussion with prompts on related topics |

Cambridge English Scale: grade A **180–190**, grade B or C **160–179**.

### Figures the handbook does NOT give

Recorded as `null` in `assets/spec.js`, not invented:

* Any readability measure or band (Flesch-Kincaid or other) for any text.
* Reading text word counts for **B1 Business Preliminary** (B2 Vantage gives
  them per part; B1 does not).
* Words-per-minute reading pace expectations.
* C1 Business Higher figures are present in the handbook but are **not used**:
  this course targets B1–B2 and records C1 only as the ceiling reference.

## C. CEFR — the live standard for level

* Council of Europe, *Common European Framework of Reference for Languages:
  Learning, teaching, assessment — Companion Volume* (April 2020).
* `rm.coe.int` returned HTTP 403 to this session (bot protection). **No figure
  in this project is quoted from the Companion Volume**, and no attempt was
  made to work around the block. CEFR is used here only for its band labels
  (A1–C2), which are universal and need no extraction, and the per-task band
  ranges in `spec.js` come from the Cambridge handbook above, not from the
  CEFR document.

This is the honest position: the course's levels are expressed in CEFR bands
because that is the common currency, and the evidence for which band a task
sits in comes from Cambridge.
