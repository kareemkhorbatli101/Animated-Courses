# Source policy · medical content, citation and the disclaimer

The rule that removes most of the risk before any sourcing is needed:

> **A language course almost never needs a real clinical specific. It needs the
> form of one.** Teaching "take one tablet twice a day, after food, for five
> days" requires no real drug at a real dose. Teaching a learner to read a
> prescription requires the anatomy of a prescription, not a prescribing
> decision. Clinical specifics appear only where the language point genuinely
> depends on them, and then only where they are textbook-stable.

---

## Three tiers

### Tier A — safety-critical

Infection control and decontamination, radiation protection, medical
emergencies, drug interactions, contraindications, dosing, anything a learner
might act on clinically.

- stated only at the level of established consensus
- cited to a named authority with a publication year
- listed in the unit's references and in the book's reference section
- recorded in the **verification register** with claim, source, year, status
- **gate:** the build fails if any Tier A claim has no register entry

Preferred sources: national and international professional bodies; standards
bodies; government health agencies; standard reference works. Recorded with the
date consulted.

### Tier B — professional practice

Roles and scope of practice, workflow, record-keeping, consent process,
referral routes, inspection expectations.

- never stated as universal fact
- always carried by the standing formula: what is commonly required, with what
  applies in any particular place set by local law and by the practice's own
  written procedures
- attributed generically in the data box's source line

### Tier C — illustrative

Cases, patients, practices, companies, numbers, percentages, prices, dates.

- invented, and marked as invented in the front matter
- never shaped to look like real data from a real body
- a figure built on Tier C numbers carries "illustrative" in its caption

---

## In the book

- **Source lines** under every data box, as now.
- **A references section** in the back matter, numbered, by unit.
- **An image credits register**, by figure number: source, licence,
  attribution text.
- **A verification register**, shipped with the sources but not printed:
  every Tier A claim and its check status.

## The disclaimer

Printed in the front matter of every volume, worded to be accurate rather than
defensive:

> This is a language course. It teaches the English used in dental practice and
> dental education; it is not a clinical reference and must not be used as one.
> Clinical decisions are governed by local law, by local protocol and by the
> professional judgement of a qualified clinician. Every patient, practice,
> company and case in this book is invented. All figures, counts and sums are
> illustrative. Where a professional requirement is described, what applies in
> any particular country or practice is set by that country's law and that
> practice's own written procedures.

## What this does not replace

A publishable clinical course should be read by a qualified dentist, and the
pharmacy titles by a qualified pharmacist, before sale. Teacher and student
pilots find pedagogical problems, which is what they are for. They are not a
domain review and are not treated as one. This policy is written to make the
domain review short, not to make it unnecessary.
