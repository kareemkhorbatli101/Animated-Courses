# Chapter 1 as extensive gapped summaries — the plan

A different shape from the cycle handouts. There is **one exercise type on the whole sheet**: a summary of a stretch of the chapter with words taken out. No multiple choice, no matching, no sorting, no rounds, no cycles.

## What "extensive" means here

Every sentence of the section appears in one of its summaries, in the order the section puts it. The set of summaries **is** the section rather than a selection from it, so a reader who fills in every gap has read the whole of it and written the load-bearing words themselves. Coverage holds by construction rather than by a gate.

The words are the chapter’s own. Nothing is paraphrased: a summary made of the chapter’s sentences is a summary by selection and sequencing, and that is the only kind that cannot introduce accounting the chapter does not state.

## The rules

| | |
|---|---|
| one gap per ~13 words | dense enough to be work, open enough to leave text to reason from |
| 2 to 12 gaps a block, ~135 words a block | built to a word target, always breaking on a sentence end |
| one spare word a list | a reader cannot finish by counting |
| never two gaps adjacent, never the first word | there is always readable text to work from |
| a repeated word is never gapped | the list holds it once; two slots would claim it |
| no singular against its own plural | "expense" and "expenses" in one list is one gap with two answers |
| no entry that sits inside another | so is "cash flows, balance sheet" against "income statement, cash flows, balance sheet" |
| a number is never gapped | recalling 300 from a list of numbers is a memory trick with nothing behind it |
| gaps are numbered inline | marking is reading down a column, not counting underscores |
| the section’s own vocabulary supplies the spares | a wrong answer has to be plausible |
| no instruction names a source | the sheet is used instead of the chapter, not beside it |

The chapter’s own sub-headings become dividers between summaries, never titles on them: a derived title would have to name the block’s content, and the content is what the gaps hide.

Its tables are summaries in tabular form, so they are gapped the same way — about 45 per cent of their cells, scattered, at most one a row, with the first column left to name the row. A table of names against amounts is the exception: there the amounts are the clue and the names are gapped instead, which is the only way a trial balance yields an exercise at all. A cell the chapter itself leaves blank is handed over as a space, never as a slot, so no gap is asked for that has no answer.
