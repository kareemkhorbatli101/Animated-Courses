Investments in Debt and Equity Securities

الاستثمار في أدوات الدين وأدوات حقوق الملكية

Companies invest spare cash in bonds and shares, and they buy shares in suppliers and partners. U.S. GAAP measures these investments in different ways, depending on what the company intends to do with them and how much influence it has. In this chapter you learn the categories, how each one is measured, and where the gains and losses go.

What you already know

If you studied IFRS 9, you classify financial assets by business model and cash flows. U.S. GAAP classifies debt by management's intent, and it has one big surprise for IFRS readers: there is no option to put gains on shares in OCI. If you studied the older IAS 39, the words available-for-sale and held-to-maturity will feel familiar.
