How to use this book

What the book covers, what each box does, who the case companies are, and how this site works with no internet connection.

This book covers every Learning Outcome Statement in CMA Part 1, Section A (External Financial Reporting Decisions), effective September 1, 2024. It is written in graded English (B1+ to B2) with the exact exam terms, and it bridges each term to Arabic.

From the September/October 2026 testing window, English-language CMA exams have 100 multiple-choice questions followed by two case-based questions (CBQs). The Learning Outcome Statements did not change. Every chapter therefore ends with multiple-choice practice and a case-style set in the CBQ format.

Four parts. Part I, Chapters 1 to 5, sets up the language of accounting and the four financial statements. Part II, Chapters 6 to 10, measures assets. Part III, Chapters 11 to 15, covers revenue, liabilities, income taxes, leases and income measurement. Part IV, Chapters 16 to 18, covers consolidation, the six tested differences from IFRS, and integrated reporting.

Each chapter follows the same pattern: an opener figure, learning objectives with LOS codes, key terms, numbered sections, a section check after each section, a chapter summary figure, practice questions, a case question in the official exam format and full answers with explanations.

The case companies

■ U.S. GAAP

Orontes Foods, Inc.

A food and beverage producer based in Amman, Jordan, listed in the United States, so it reports under U.S. GAAP. It makes olive oil and tahini in Amman and frozen pastries in Dubai. Its 2025 statements are built in Chapters 2 to 5 and reused later. Its January and February transactions are the worked example and the Your Turn in section 1.3.

■ U.S. GAAP

Barada Wholesale, Inc.

A small U.S. GAAP distributor in Amman and one of Orontes's largest customers, used for the case-style sets. Its trial balance is Figure F01-10, and Extended Example 1A builds its complete set of statements from it.

● IFRS

Cedar Retail S.A.L.

The IFRS company, introduced in section 1.5. Many countries in the Middle East and North Africa — Jordan and the United Arab Emirates among them — require IFRS for listed companies.

Numbers

Amounts are in whole U.S. dollars unless a table says USD 000. Chapter 1 works entirely in USD 000. Examples that begin with “Suppose”, and all dated examples after 2025, are illustrative: they are not part of Orontes's 2025 statements. Figure and question numbers keep their chapter prefix, for example F11-03 or P11-07.

Where the exam asks you to type an amount, enter it without the $ sign and without commas — for example 1250. The numeric boxes in this site accept answers the same way.

The boxes in each chapter

WHAT YOU ALREADY KNOW

Links the topic to IFRS or to earlier chapters. You have studied accounting before — this box says what is genuinely new.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A calculation or analysis, step by step.

YOUR TURN

A faded exercise: the first lines are done; you complete the rest. In this site the fading is literal — see the February effect grid in section 1.3, where rows 1 to 3 are filled in and rows 4 to 6 are yours.

EXAM TRAP

A common wrong answer and why it is wrong. Every trap in this site is also a scored exercise, and any trap you get wrong is logged by name on the mixed review page so you can come back to that specific confusion.

FALSE-FRIEND ALERT

An Arabic or French term that looks right but means something else in U.S. GAAP. Stock is the classic: shares in U.S. English, inventory in French and British English.

IFRS CONTRAST

How IFRS differs, for readers who trained in IFRS. Most ideas are identical; some names and some rules are not. Chapter 17 tests six differences, so these boxes make that chapter revision.

LANGUAGE FOCUS

The English that the exam's wording turns on: the five accounting verbs, how to describe a transaction's effect, and the cue words — MOST, BEST, NOT, EXCEPT, immediately after, net effect — that change what a correct answer looks like.

TERM BRIDGE

Key words in this section

Each English exam term with its Arabic equivalent, and a 🔊 icon on both. Translation work in this site is English ↔ Arabic only. The French warnings in the False-Friend boxes are kept, because they are concepts the book teaches, but there are no French exercises and no French column.

SECTION CHECK

Test yourself

The section's own exam-style questions. Answers and the reason for every option are inside the exercise and gathered in the answer section.

How this site works

  • Every exercise has Check Answers and Try Again. Try Again re-shuffles the item and clears your marks — it never reveals the answer as a shortcut.
  • Answers are shuffled every time, and in a matching or sorting exercise there are always more tiles than places to put them, so the last one is never free.
  • Feedback leads with your own choice. When you are wrong, the first thing you read is the reason for the option you picked, taken from the book's own explanations.
  • Drag-and-drop always has a click path. Tap a tile, then tap where it goes. Everything is operable from the keyboard too: Tab to a tile, Enter to pick it up, Tab to a target, Enter to drop.
  • Pronunciation uses your computer's voices. On Windows, Chrome and Microsoft Edge expose the installed voices directly. If an Arabic icon is dimmed, no Arabic voice is installed — Windows: Settings → Time & language → Speech → Add voices → Arabic.
  • Progress is saved in this browser on this computer. Nothing is sent anywhere. The foot of any chapter page has a button to clear it.
  • No web server, no internet, no install. Open index.html straight from the folder. Press ← and → to move between pages, and use your browser's print command for a clean worksheet of any practice page.
