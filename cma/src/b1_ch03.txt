WHAT YOU ALREADY KNOW   What you already know
IFRS calls it the statement of profit or loss, and many IFRS companies show expenses by nature: materials, staff costs, depreciation. U.S. companies almost always show expenses by function: cost of goods sold, selling, and administrative. The totals are the same; the grouping is different.
3.1  Purpose and structure of the income statement
The income statement reports a company's revenues, expenses, gains and losses for a period, usually a year. The result is net income, which increases retained earnings. Where the balance sheet is a photograph, the income statement is a film of the whole year.
Investors use it to judge how profitable the company is and to predict its future earnings and cash flows. Lenders use it to see whether the company earns enough to pay interest. Managers use it to compare results with budgets and with competitors.
U.S. GAAP does not require one layout. A multi-step income statement shows subtotals: gross profit, operating income and income before income taxes. A single-step income statement adds all revenues and gains, deducts all expenses and losses, and reaches net income in one step. SEC registrants such as Orontes follow the SEC's list of captions, which gives a multi-step layout.

3.2  Building the multi-step income statement
Gross profit is net sales minus cost of goods sold. It shows how much the company earns on what it sells before its other costs. Orontes's gross profit is $14,700,000, which is 35% of net sales.
Operating income is gross profit minus operating expenses: selling, general and administrative expenses, research and development, credit losses and impairments. Interest and income tax are never operating expenses. Orontes's operating income is $4,176,000.
Other income and expense follows operating income. It contains interest, investment income and gains and losses. A gain or loss comes from a peripheral or incidental transaction, and it is shown net. For example, Orontes sold an old oven and reported a gain of $3,000: the proceeds minus the carrying amount, not the full proceeds.
Income tax expense is then deducted from income before income taxes. Public companies also show earnings per share (EPS) on the face of the statement. Orontes's basic EPS is $0.37: net income divided by the weighted-average number of shares outstanding. Part 2 of the CMA exam analyzes EPS in depth.

WORKED EXAMPLE   Worked example: Orontes's subtotals
Step 1. Gross profit: $42,000,000 − $27,300,000 = $14,700,000.
Step 2. Operating income: $14,700,000 − $10,524,000 of operating expenses = $4,176,000.
Step 3. Income before income taxes: $4,176,000 − net other expense of $217,200 = $3,958,800.
Step 4. Net income: $3,958,800 − income tax of $989,700 = $2,969,100.

3.3  Unusual items and discontinued operations
Some items need special attention because they may not happen again. U.S. GAAP no longer has extraordinary items; the FASB removed them in 2015. An unusual or infrequent item, such as a large fire loss, is shown as a separate line within continuing operations, before tax. It is never shown net of tax.
A discontinued operation is a component of an entity that the company has sold, has classified as held for sale, or has abandoned. The disposal must also be a strategic shift with a major effect on the company. Examples are leaving a major line of business or a major geographic area.
Closing one store or stopping one small product does not qualify. The results of a discontinued operation, including any gain or loss on disposal, appear below income from continuing operations, net of their own tax. This is called intraperiod tax allocation.

EXAM TRAP   Extraordinary nostalgia
Older textbooks show earthquakes and expropriations as extraordinary items, net of tax, below continuing operations. On today's exam, such an item is unusual or infrequent: a separate pre-tax line in continuing operations. Only discontinued operations are shown net of tax below continuing operations.
● IFRS CONTRAST   Discontinued operations under IFRS 5
IFRS 5 uses a similar idea: a separate major line of business or geographic area. IFRS also prohibits extraordinary items, so the two frameworks now give very similar results in this area.

3.4  Comprehensive income
Some changes in value do not go through net income. U.S. GAAP sends them to other comprehensive income (OCI) until they are realized. Comprehensive income is net income plus OCI for the period.
Orontes's available-for-sale debt securities lost $1,500 in fair value in 2025. After tax, the loss in OCI is $1,125, so comprehensive income is $2,969,100 − $1,125 = $2,967,975. Companies show this in one continuous statement, as Orontes does, or in two consecutive statements. Showing OCI only in the statement of changes in equity is not allowed.
OCI builds a balance in equity called accumulated other comprehensive income (AOCI). When an item is realized, for example when Orontes sells an AFS security, the gain or loss moves from AOCI into net income. This reclassification adjustment stops the same gain being counted twice. Under IFRS, a revaluation surplus on PP&E, fair value changes on equity investments elected to OCI, and pension remeasurements stay in OCI forever. U.S. GAAP does not allow PP&E revaluation, and it reclassifies its OCI items to net income later.
EXAM TRAP   Comprehensive income traps
Comprehensive income adds this year's OCI, not the AOCI balance.
Unrealized gains on equity securities go to net income, not OCI.
Use OCI after tax, unless the question shows the tax on a separate line.

3.5  Limitations and links to the other statements
Net income depends on many estimates: useful lives, credit losses, impairment tests and warranty costs. Different companies also choose different methods, such as FIFO or LIFO for inventory. Two similar companies can therefore report different profits from the same activity.
The income statement also leaves things out. An increase in the value of Orontes's land, or of a brand it built itself, is not reported. Managers can influence results by timing sales or discretionary costs, such as research or advertising, near the year-end.
Most importantly, net income is not cash. Accrual accounting records revenue when it is earned and expenses when they are incurred. Figure F03-09 shows that Orontes's cash from operating activities was $7,531,100, far more than its net income of $2,969,100. Depreciation and impairments of $4,824,000 reduced income but used no cash in 2025.

Net income then moves to the statement of changes in equity, where it increases retained earnings. OCI moves to AOCI. In the statement of cash flows, the indirect method starts from net income.
WATCH: NOT YET TESTABLE   Coming changes: DISE and IFRS 18
ASU 2024-03 (DISE) requires public companies to break down some expense captions in the notes, such as employee pay and depreciation. The face of the statement does not change. IFRS 18 replaces IAS 1 and adds required subtotals, including operating profit. Both apply from 2027 and are not testable before about January 2028.
FALSE-FRIEND ALERT   Résultat and إيرادات
French résultat means profit, not result in general, and résultat exceptionnel is not a U.S. category. Arabic إيرادات can mean revenue or income; in U.S. GAAP, revenue comes from main activities, and income is what remains after expenses.

Chapter summary
Figure F03-10 brings the chapter together.
Figure F03-10. Chapter 3 summary map: the key rule for each section.
The income statement shows revenues, expenses, gains and losses for a period. A multi-step statement shows gross profit, operating income and income before income taxes, while a single-step statement reaches the same net income without subtotals. Gains and losses from peripheral transactions are shown net in other income and expense. Unusual or infrequent items are a separate pre-tax line in continuing operations; extraordinary items no longer exist. Discontinued operations appear net of tax below continuing operations. Comprehensive income is net income plus OCI, and OCI builds AOCI. Net income depends on estimates and methods, omits some value changes, and is not the same as cash.

Written task: model answer
The fire destroyed inventory, which is unusual for Barada, but it happened in the business that Barada keeps. U.S. GAAP no longer has extraordinary items, so the fire loss of 70 is shown before tax, as a separate line within continuing operations. The catering-supply division was one of Barada's two lines of business, and selling it is a strategic shift with a major effect. Its results are therefore discontinued operations. The operating loss and the loss on the sale are shown net of tax, 75 in total, below income from continuing operations of 240.
A good answer: identifies the fire as unusual but part of continuing operations; states that extraordinary items no longer exist; shows the fire loss before tax on a separate line; explains the strategic shift for the division; presents the division's results net of tax below continuing operations.
