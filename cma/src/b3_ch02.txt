2.1  Analysing the external environment
External factors are outside the company's control. They create opportunities to use and threats to defend against. A PEST analysis reviews four groups of them: political, economic, social and technological factors. Figure F302-02 applies it to tahini exports.
Figure F302-02. PEST analysis for Orontes tahini exports.
Porter's five forces describe the industry itself: new entrants, suppliers, buyers, substitutes and rivalry. The stronger the forces, the harder it is to earn a good profit. Figure F302-03 rates each force for Jordan's juice market; the average intensity is 3.6 out of 5.
Figure F302-03. Porter's five forces in Jordan's juice market.
In the juice market, rivalry is the strongest force and supplier power the weakest, partly because Jordan Glass, which supplies the bottles, is a company Orontes partly owns. The external analysis therefore points to a threat from rivals and substitutes, and an opportunity in stable supply.
EXAM TRAP   Strong forces, weak industry
A strong force is bad news for profit. An answer that calls a market with powerful buyers and many substitutes 'attractive' has read the forces backwards.

2.2  Analysing the internal environment

2.3  SWOT and situational analysis
A SWOT analysis brings the two views together: strengths and weaknesses from inside, opportunities and threats from outside. Figure F302-05 is the Orontes SWOT for 2028.
Figure F302-05. SWOT matrix for Orontes.
A situational analysis is the wider study of the company's current position that feeds the SWOT. The useful step is the next one: match strengths to opportunities, for example the tahini brand to Gulf demand, and plan how to fix the weaknesses that expose the company to threats.

2.4  Porter's generic strategies
Porter described three ways to win. Cost leadership sells at or near the market price with the lowest costs. Differentiation offers something customers value and will pay more for. A focus strategy serves a narrow segment, either at low cost or with a difference. Figure F302-06 shows the three strategies.
Figure F302-06. Porter's generic strategies and the Orontes divisions.
Each Orontes division follows one strategy, as Figure F302-07 shows. Beverages competes on cost in the mass juice market; Oils differentiates with traced, cold-pressed oil; Tahini & Spreads focuses on premium Gulf buyers and private label for Barada.

2.5  Portfolio and competitive analysis
The BCG growth-share matrix places each business unit by market growth and relative market share: the unit's share divided by the share of its largest competitor. Growth above 10.0% and relative share above 1.0 mark the dividing lines.
WORKED EXAMPLE   Worked example: placing Oils
Oils holds 22.0% of its market; the largest rival holds 15.0%. Relative share = 22.0 ÷ 15.0 = 1.47. The market grows 3.0% a year, below the line, so Oils is in the Cash cow quadrant. Figure F302-08 does the same for every unit.

2.6  Scenario planning and contingency planning
Scenario planning prepares for several possible futures instead of one forecast. For tahini exports in 2032, the board considers three scenarios, from open Gulf and EU markets to a new EU import tariff. Figure F302-12 shows the operating income in each.
Figure F302-12. Tahini export scenarios for 2030 and the contingency trigger
Scenario for tahini exports, 2030
Operating income (USD)
Response
Open Gulf and EU markets
2,400,000
no action
Gulf growth as planned
1,800,000
no action
New EU import tariff
900,000
start the contingency plan
Contingency trigger
below 1,200,000
shift export sales to Gulf buyers; delay the EU warehouse
Contingency planning prepares a response in advance and names the trigger that starts it. If the export forecast falls below $1,200,000, as it would in the tariff scenario, Orontes will shift sales to Gulf buyers and delay its EU warehouse.
The two tools work together: scenarios show what could happen; the contingency plan says what the company will do, and when.
FALSE-FRIEND ALERT   Contingence, العوارض
The IMA Arabic glossary calls contingency planning التخطيط للعوارض; most Arab textbooks say التخطيط للطوارئ. French 'plan de contingence' is common in business, but 'plan d'urgence' is also used.

Chapter summary
Figure F302-14 brings the chapter together.
Figure F302-14. Chapter 2 summary map: the key rule for each section.
External analysis, using PEST and the five forces, shows opportunities and threats; internal analysis of resources and capabilities shows strengths, weaknesses and possible competitive advantages. SWOT combines them. Each business then chooses cost leadership, differentiation or focus. The BCG matrix places units by market growth and relative share, competitive analysis studies the main rivals, and scenario and contingency planning prepare the company for more than one future.

Case question: tahini exports to the Gulf
Orontes began selling tahini in the Gulf three years ago. Sales have grown fast, but the business is still small next to the market leader, a large regional food group. The board must decide whether to invest more in the Gulf or to keep the business small.
The strategy team, led by Hala, has gathered data. The Gulf tahini market is growing quickly, and healthy traditional foods are popular. Orontes's brand is well known in Jordan but not yet in the Gulf, and its sales team there has only three people. The market leader has started to cut prices, and the EU may add a tariff that would push more exporters towards the Gulf.
Hala has also rated the five forces in the Gulf market from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong), and she has prepared three scenarios for the operating income of the Gulf business in 2030.
The board wants to know where the business sits in the Orontes portfolio, how attractive the market is, which generic strategy suits a premium product sold to a narrow group of buyers, and how far the results could vary.
