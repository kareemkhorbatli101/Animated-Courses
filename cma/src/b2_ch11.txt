11.1  Joint products, by-products and scrap
Joint products are two or more products from the same process, each with significant sales value. Extra-virgin oil (EVOO) and virgin oil (VOO) are joint products: the press runs to make both.
A by-product comes from the same process but has a small sales value compared with the joint products. Pomace, the olive cake left after pressing, is a by-product. Scrap has even less value. Figure F211-02 compares the three.

11.2  The split-off point and separable costs
The split-off point is where the joint products become separately identifiable. All costs before it are joint costs: olives, crushing labor and crushing overhead, $1,400,000 for the batch.
Costs after split-off that belong to one product are separable costs. Bottling adds $468,000 to EVOO and filtering adds $32,000 to VOO. Figure F211-03 marks the line between the two kinds of cost.
Figure F211-03. Batch H-2711: the split-off point, joint and separable costs.
Joint costs cannot be traced to one product, however hard managers try. Any split is an allocation. Separable costs can be traced, and they matter for every later decision.

11.3  Accounting for by-products
Under the production method, the by-product's net realizable value is recorded as inventory at split-off and deducted from joint cost. Pomace sells for $120,000 with $20,000 of disposal costs, so its NRV is $100,000 and the joint products share $1,300,000.
Under the sales method, nothing is recorded at split-off. The joint products share the full $1,400,000, and the by-product's net revenue is other income when it is sold. Figure F211-04 compares the two.

FALSE-FRIEND ALERT   Sous-produit, منتج عرضي
French 'sous-produit' and Arabic منتج عرضي mean by-product. 'Coproduit' and منتج مشترك mean joint product. Do not translate 'by-product' as 'secondary product' in an exam answer: the accounting is different.

11.4  Physical measure and sales value at split-off
The physical measure method shares joint cost in proportion to a physical quantity, such as liters. It is simple and needs no prices. But it ignores value: a liter of VOO takes the same share as a liter of EVOO, which sells for much more.

11.5  Net realizable value and constant gross margin
The net realizable value method uses each product's final sales value minus its separable costs. It works when there is no market price at split-off. It is Orontes's policy method.
The constant gross-margin percentage method first finds the gross margin of the whole batch, 28%. It then gives each product the joint cost that makes its own margin the same 28%. Figure F211-07 shows both methods step by step.

EXAM TRAP   Final sales value, not NRV
In the constant gross-margin method, apply the margin to final sales value, then deduct separable costs. Applying it to NRV gives the wrong joint cost.

Chapter summary
Figure F211-14 brings the chapter together.
Figure F211-14. Chapter 11 summary map: the key rule for each section.
Joint products have significant value; by-products have small value. Costs before the split-off point are joint and must be allocated; costs after it are separable. By-products reduce joint cost at NRV under the production method or become income when sold under the sales method. The physical measure, sales value at split-off, NRV and constant gross-margin methods all share the same joint cost in different ways. Joint costs are irrelevant to the decision to process further.

Tahini & Spreads roasts and presses sesame in one joint process. The process yields two main products, tahini base and sesame oil, and a small amount of hulls. The hulls have a low value and are sold to a feed mill.
Sesame oil is sold as it comes out of the press. Tahini base can also be sold at the split-off point, but the division usually processes it further into jarred tahini, which sells at a higher price. Further processing adds its own costs.
Orontes values inventory under U.S. GAAP, so the joint cost of the process must be shared between the two main products. The division treats the hulls as a by-product and deducts their value from the joint cost before it shares the rest.
Mona, the cost accountant, must allocate the joint cost with two methods for the controller's review. The sales team has also asked whether jarring the tahini base is still worth it now that jar prices have risen. The exhibit gives the volumes, prices and costs. The feed mill collects the hulls itself, so selling them costs the division nothing. Both main products are sold to the same Jordanian buyers throughout the year.
