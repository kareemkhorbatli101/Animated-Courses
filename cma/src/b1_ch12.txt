WHAT YOU ALREADY KNOW   What you already know
IFRS readers know warranty provisions under IAS 37 and the right-to-defer test of IAS 1. U.S. GAAP reaches different answers in two places: refinancing after year-end, and the meaning of probable. Watch for both.
12.1  What makes a liability current
Current liabilities are obligations that a company expects to settle by using current assets or by creating other current liabilities, normally within one year. They are measured at the amount to be paid. Because the time is short, they are not discounted to present value.

12.2  Payroll, taxes collected and compensated absences
Payroll creates several liabilities. The employer deducts withholding, such as income tax and the employees' share of social security, from gross wages. These amounts belong to the government, so they are liabilities, not expenses. The employer's own payroll taxes, such as its share of social security, are an additional expense.
Suppose Barada's gross wages for a month are $40,000. It withholds $4,000 of income tax and $2,800 of social security, so employees receive $33,200. Barada's total payroll cost is the gross wages plus its own social security.
Sales tax and VAT collected from customers are also owed to the government. They are recorded as sales tax payable, not as revenue. Compensated absences, such as earned vacation, are accrued when employees have already worked for them, the rights vest or accumulate, and payment is probable.

12.3  Short-term debt expected to be refinanced
A short-term obligation is normally current. It may be shown as noncurrent only if the company intends to refinance it on a long-term basis and shows that it can. Chapter 2 introduced this rule; here we apply it in detail.
The company shows its ability in one of two ways, before the statements are issued. It can actually refinance: issue long-term debt or shares after the balance sheet date and use the cash for the short-term debt. Or it can sign a qualifying financing agreement.
A qualifying agreement does not expire within one year, cannot be cancelled except for an objective violation, has no violation, and the lender can pay. An agreement that the lender may cancel for a 'material adverse change' does not qualify, because that clause is subjective.
Three limits apply. The noncurrent amount cannot exceed the amount refinanced or available. Debt repaid with current assets before the new financing is obtained stays current. Refinanced debt moves to noncurrent liabilities, never to equity, even when shares are issued. Figure F12-04 puts these rules in order.
Figure F12-04. Decision chart: can short-term debt be shown as noncurrent?.

12.4  IFRS and covenants
IAS 1 asks only one question: at the reporting date, does the company have a right to defer settlement for at least twelve months? Refinancing agreed after the reporting date does not change the classification, even if it happens before the statements are issued. In scenarios A, C and D above, an IFRS company would show the whole commercial paper as current.
Covenants follow the same logic. Under IFRS, only covenants that must be met on or before the reporting date affect classification; future covenants are disclosed. Figure F12-06 compares the rules.

12.5  Warranties
An assurance-type warranty only promises that a product will work as agreed. It is not a separate performance obligation. The company estimates the repair cost at the time of sale, records warranty expense and a warranty liability, and reduces the liability as repairs are made.
A service-type warranty gives the customer an extra service, such as extended cover. It is a separate performance obligation: part of the price is recorded as a contract liability and recognized as revenue over the coverage period. A warranty that customers can buy separately is always service-type. Figure F12-07 shows the test.
Figure F12-07. Decision chart: assurance-type or service-type warranty?.

EXAM TRAP   Warranty traps
Standard warranty: accrue the cost at the sale; do not defer revenue.
Extended plan sold separately: defer revenue; do not accrue a cost.
Expense warranty costs in the year of the sale, not when repairs are paid.
FALSE-FRIEND ALERT   مخصص, محتمل, provision
Arabic مخصص can mean a U.S. allowance, a liability or an IFRS provision; use warranty liability here. محتمل can translate both probable (accrue) and contingent or possible (disclose). French provision pour garantie is the IFRS term; never call a U.S. liability a reserve.

Chapter summary
Figure F12-10 brings the chapter together.
Figure F12-10. Chapter 12 summary map: the key rule for each section.
Current liabilities are settled with current assets or new current liabilities and are shown at the amount to be paid. Withholdings and taxes collected are owed to the government; employer payroll taxes are expenses; vested absences are accrued. Short-term debt may be shown as noncurrent only if the company intends to refinance it and, before the statements are issued, refinances it or signs a qualifying agreement, up to the amount raised or available. IFRS looks only at rights at the reporting date. Assurance-type warranties are accrued as an expense at the sale; service-type warranties create a contract liability that is earned over the coverage period.
