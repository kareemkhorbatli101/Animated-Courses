WHAT YOU ALREADY KNOW   What you already know
Under IFRS you call it the statement of financial position, and many IFRS balance sheets put noncurrent assets first. U.S. companies usually start with current assets and list them in order of liquidity: cash first. The rules for classifying debt are also different, as Section 2.3 shows.
2.1  Purpose and structure of the balance sheet
The balance sheet reports a company's assets, liabilities and equity at one point in time. It answers three questions for investors and lenders. Can the company pay its bills in the coming months? This is liquidity. Can it pay all its debts over time? This is solvency. Can it raise cash or change its plans if something unexpected happens? This is financial flexibility.
U.S. companies that file with the SEC present a classified balance sheet. It separates current from noncurrent assets and current from noncurrent liabilities, so users can see the short-term position at once. Some companies, such as banks, list items in order of liquidity instead, without a current subtotal.

2.2  Current and noncurrent items
Current assets are cash and other assets that the company expects to turn into cash, sell or use up within one year or within its operating cycle, whichever is longer. The operating cycle is the average time from buying materials to collecting cash from customers. Current liabilities are obligations the company expects to settle by using current assets or by creating other current liabilities.
Figure F02-03 shows Orontes's cycle. It takes about 32 days to sell its inventory and 34 days to collect from customers: 66 days in total. Because the cycle is shorter than a year, Orontes uses one year to classify its items.
Figure F02-03. Orontes's operating cycle: cash to inventory to receivables to cash.
Some assets that look current are not. Restricted cash set aside to repay long-term debt or to build a plant is noncurrent. So are long-term investments, receivables that will not be collected within the period, the cash surrender value of life insurance, and long-term prepayments. On the liability side, current portion of long-term debt is current, because that part of the principal is due within a year.
Two measures come directly from these totals. Working capital is current assets minus current liabilities. The current ratio is current assets divided by current liabilities. For Orontes at the end of 2025, working capital is $14,188,100 − $4,280,000 = $9,908,100, and the current ratio is 3.31. A year earlier the ratio was 2.35. Part 2 of the CMA exam analyzes these ratios in depth.
EXAM TRAP   Working capital or the ratio?
Paying a supplier in cash reduces current assets and current liabilities by the same amount: working capital does not change, but the current ratio changes.
Reclassifying debt from current to noncurrent increases working capital and the current ratio.
Use the operating cycle only when it is longer than one year.
WORKED EXAMPLE   Worked example: classifying three Orontes items
Step 1. Next year's installment on long-term debt, $400,000. It is due within one year, so it is a current liability; the rest of the debt stays noncurrent.
Step 2. The delivery van held for sale, $46,000. Orontes expects to sell it within the year, so it is a current asset, outside PP&E.
Step 3. The equity-method investments, $1,807,000. Orontes plans to hold them for the long term, so they are noncurrent assets.

2.3  Classifying debt
Debt is where classification gets difficult, and where the exam likes to test. Long-term debt that is due in installments is split: the current portion of long-term debt is current and the rest is noncurrent. Orontes shows $400,000 as current and $6,000,000 as long-term.
Loan agreements contain conditions called covenants, for example a maximum debt-to-equity ratio. If a company breaks a covenant, the lender may be able to demand repayment at once. Such callable debt is current, even if the company does not expect the lender to act. It stays noncurrent only if, before the statements are issued, the lender gives a waiver for more than one year, or a grace period exists and the company will probably cure the violation within it.
A subjective acceleration clause, such as a right to demand payment after a 'material adverse change', makes debt current only if acceleration is probable. Debt that is due on demand is always current.
Short-term debt can be shown as noncurrent through refinancing, but only if the company intends to refinance it on a long-term basis and shows that it can. It shows this by issuing long-term debt or shares after the balance sheet date but before the statements are issued, or by signing a qualifying financing agreement. 2.4  Other presentation matters
A few more rules decide what appears on the balance sheet and how. All deferred tax assets and liabilities are noncurrent. Orontes shows its deferred tax liability of $999,625 below long-term debt. Assets and liabilities may be offset only when a right of setoff exists: the two parties owe each other determinable amounts, the company has the legal right and the intent to set them off, and the right is enforceable.
A loss contingency is an existing situation that may cause a loss, such as a lawsuit. Figure F02-07 shows the treatment. If the loss is probable and can be reasonably estimated, the company records a liability. If it is only reasonably possible, the company discloses it in a note. If it is remote, the company does nothing. Gain contingencies are never recorded before they are realized.

Subsequent events happen after the balance sheet date but before the statements are issued. If an event gives more information about a condition that already existed at year-end, the company adjusts the statements. If the condition arose after year-end, the company only discloses it. ● IFRS CONTRAST   Provisions under IAS 37
IFRS records a provision when a loss is more likely than not, a lower threshold than the U.S. 'probable'. When the estimate is a range, IFRS usually uses the midpoint; U.S. GAAP uses the best estimate or, if none, the minimum.
FALSE-FRIEND ALERT   Reserve, provision, allowance
Arabic مخصص can mean an allowance (a contra-asset), a provision (a liability) or even a reserve in equity (احتياطي). In U.S. GAAP answers, use allowance for contra-assets, accrued liability or loss contingency for liabilities, and never call either a reserve.

2.5  Limitations and links to the other statements
The balance sheet is useful, but it is not a valuation of the company. Most assets are at historical cost, not current value: Orontes's land and buildings are shown at what they cost years ago. Many amounts depend on estimates, such as the allowance for credit losses and useful lives.
Important resources are missing. Internally developed brands, customer loyalty and a skilled workforce are not recorded, so the balance sheet usually shows much less than the market value of the company. Some obligations are only in the notes.
Finally, the balance sheet shows one day. A company can improve its year-end picture, for example by delaying payments to suppliers for a few days. Readers therefore compare several dates and read the other statements too.
The four statements are connected. Net income from the income statement increases retained earnings in the statement of changes in equity, and the closing retained earnings appear on the balance sheet. The net change in cash in the statement of cash flows explains the change in the balance-sheet cash. Figure F02-09 shows these links for Orontes.
Figure F02-09. How the Orontes 2025 statements link to the balance sheet.
Other comprehensive income works the same way. Orontes's OCI of $(1,125) does not go into retained earnings. It goes into accumulated other comprehensive income, which is $(1,125) on the balance sheet.
EXAM TRAP   Book value is not market value
Total stockholders' equity is a book amount. It is not what the company is worth, and it is not the market value of the shares.

Chapter summary
Figure F02-10 brings the chapter together.
Figure F02-10. Chapter 2 summary map: the key rule for each section.
The balance sheet shows assets, liabilities and equity at one date. A classified balance sheet separates current from noncurrent items using one year or the operating cycle, whichever is longer, so users can judge liquidity and solvency. Debt that is due within a year, callable or due on demand is current, unless a waiver or long-term refinancing is in place before the statements are issued; IFRS looks only at rights at the reporting date. Probable, estimable losses are accrued and reasonably possible ones are disclosed, and events after year-end are adjusted only if the condition existed at year-end. The balance sheet relies on historical cost and estimates, omits many intangibles and shows one day, and it links to the other statements through retained earnings, AOCI and cash.
