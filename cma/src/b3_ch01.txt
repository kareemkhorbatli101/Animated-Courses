WHAT YOU ALREADY KNOW   What you already know
Book 2 built standard costs and budgets for single products. Book 3 shows how those budgets come from a company-wide plan, and how managers are judged against them.
1.1  What strategic planning does
Strategic planning decides where an organization wants to be in the long term and how it will get there. It chooses the markets, products and ways of competing that will lead to the long-term goals.
The result is a strategic plan. Every other plan sits below it, as Figure F301-02 shows: the mission and vision at the top, then goals and strategies, then objectives, tactics and operational plans, and finally the budget.
Figure F301-02. The planning hierarchy, from mission to budget.
Because it comes first, strategic planning sets the path for everything else. A company that plans to grow exports will budget for export staff and certification; a company that plans to cut costs will budget for new equipment and fewer products.
EXAM TRAP   The budget is not the strategy
An exam option that says the budget sets the strategy has the order backwards. The strategy comes first; the budget puts the first year of it into money.

1.4  Congruence: objectives, tactics, plans and budgets
Short-term objectives are measurable results for the coming year that move the company toward its goals. Tactics are the specific actions used to reach them, and the operational plan sets out the people, activities and resources. The budget puts all of this into money.
Each level must fit the level above it. This fit is called congruence. If the budget cuts export marketing while the plan calls for export growth, the company will miss its goal even if every manager meets the budget.
A simple way to set objectives is to divide the change needed over the plan into equal yearly steps. Exports must rise by 12.0 points over 5 years, or 2.40 points a year, so the 2028 objective is 20.4%. Figure F301-07 shows the path.

1.5  Characteristics of successful strategic plans
A successful strategic plan gives a clear direction that follows from the mission. Its goals are measurable and realistic, based on a careful study of the company and its environment, which Chapter 2 covers.
It is also shared: managers help set their own objectives and understand how they contribute. It is linked to resources, so that people and money are available, and it is reviewed regularly, with contingency plans for the main risks. Figure F301-10 lists the characteristics.
