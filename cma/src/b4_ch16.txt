16.1  The four components of a time series
Any series measured over time can be thought of as four things added together. Figure F416-02 names them.
Figure F416-02. The four components of a time series
Component (F.4.r)
What it is

trend is the long-run direction and seasonal variation is the pattern that repeats within the year. Those two are usually the visible ones. cyclical variation is slower and tied to the wider economy. It needs many years of data before it can be told apart from trend at all.
irregular variation is what remains: a port strike, a heatwave, a single large order. It is unrepeatable by definition, which is why no model should try to explain it. A model that fits the irregular component is the overfitting of Chapter 15.

16.2  Separating the components
The standard method is a moving average. Averaging four consecutive quarters removes the seasonal pattern, because every average contains one of each quarter. Most of the irregular variation cancels as well. What is left is close to the trend.
Figure F416-03. The Beverages series decomposed into trend and seasonal factors.
Figure F416-03 does this to the Beverages data. The first panel is the series as recorded, with its clear peak in the third quarter of each year. The second is the four-quarter centred moving average, which rises from 219,750 to 223,662 cases and is far smoother. The third shows what is left once the trend is taken out.
Those remainders are the seasonal factors: 20,003 cases in the third quarter and (13,022) in the first, a swing of 33,025 cases between the best quarter and the worst. They are adjusted so that the four sum to zero, because a season redistributes the year's demand rather than adding to it.

The limitation that matters most applies to both, and it is simply stated: each technique assumes that the pattern in the past will continue. 16.4  Judging how good a model is
Three measures are named by the syllabus, and Figure F416-05 distinguishes them.
Figure F416-05. Standard error, goodness of fit and confidence interval
Measure (F.4.t)
What it is
In this model
Note
Standard error of the estimate
The typical distance of an observation from the fitted line
In the units of the result, so 555 cases here
Smaller is better
Goodness of fit
How closely the model matches the observed data
R squared is the usual measure, 0.9989 here
High fit with few observations invites caution
Confidence interval
A range around the estimate, with a stated level of confidence
The estimate plus or minus a multiple of the standard error

The standard error of the estimate is the typical distance of an observation from the fitted line, expressed in the units of the thing being predicted. For the Beverages model it is 555 cases, which is readable directly: a prediction is typically wrong by about that much.
goodness of fit asks how closely the model matches what was observed, and R squared is the usual measure of it. A confidence interval turns a single estimate into a range, by adding and subtracting a multiple of the standard error. It also states how confident one is that the true value lies inside it.
EXAM TRAP   A narrow interval is not a better one
A confidence interval is honest about uncertainty, not a measure of skill. An interval that is narrow because the model overfits a small sample is worse than a wide one that reflects what the data can actually support.

16.6  Extended Example 16A: decomposing the Beverages series
This example performs the decomposition and then says what it is and is not good for. It is exam-core.
1. Compute the trend
A four-quarter moving average is taken, then centred so that it lines up with an actual quarter. With 8 observations this gives 4 trend values, from 2027 Q3 to 2028 Q2. The two quarters at each end have no trend value, which is a real cost of the method.
2. Take the trend out
Subtracting the trend from the actual leaves the seasonal and irregular components together. In 2027 Q3, actual demand was 240,400 cases against a trend of 219,750, leaving 20,650.
3. Average to get the seasonal factor
Where a quarter appears more than once, averaging its remainders cancels most of the irregular part and leaves the seasonal factor. Adjusting the four so they sum to zero gives (13,022), 691, 20,003 and (7,672) for the first to the fourth quarter.
4. What it is good for
The factors say how much of the third-quarter peak is simply the season. A manager who congratulates the sales team on a 20,003-case rise in the third quarter is congratulating them on the weather. The same manager should not treat the first-quarter fall as a failure.
5. What it is not good for
Two years is very little. Each seasonal factor here rests on two observations, so a single unusual quarter moves it substantially. The cyclical component cannot be seen at all. The honest statement is a direction and a rough size, not a precise forecast.

Chapter summary
Figure F416-08 brings the chapter together.
Figure F416-08. Chapter 16 summary map: the key rule for each section.
A time series combines trend, cyclical, seasonal and irregular components. A four-quarter centred moving average removes the seasonal pattern and most of the noise, leaving the trend; subtracting the trend and averaging by quarter gives seasonal factors, which are adjusted to sum to zero. Both regression and time series analysis use every observation and quantify what they find, and both assume the past pattern will continue. The standard error of the estimate gives the typical distance from the line in the units of the result, goodness of fit measures how closely the model matches the data, and a confidence interval expresses the estimate as a range. Exploratory data analysis comes before all of it, because the eye finds what no statistic reports.

Case question: congratulating the weather
The Beverages division has eight quarters of sales history. Demand peaks in the third quarter of each year and falls back in the first. A four-quarter centred moving average has been computed, giving four trend values that rise steadily across the period.
Subtracting the trend and averaging by quarter gives seasonal factors of -13,022 for the first quarter, +691 for the second, +20,003 for the third and -7,672 for the fourth. The four have been adjusted so that they sum to zero.
At the quarterly review the divisional director congratulated the sales team on a large rise in the third quarter and asked for the same again next quarter. He has also asked the analyst to identify the economic cycle in the data, and whether the model can give a forecast accurate to within a few hundred cases.
The analyst has not yet plotted the series, having gone straight to the computation. The standard error of the estimate for the related regression is 555 cases. No one has yet asked whether the pattern of the past two years can be expected to hold into the next.
