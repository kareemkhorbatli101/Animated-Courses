18.1  Cost standards in budgeting
A standard is the expected cost of one unit of output under efficient conditions. The standard cost card lists, for one unit, the standard quantity and the standard price of every input.
Budgets are built from the card: standard cost per unit times planned units. Figure F218-02 shows the idea for June 2028, when Beverages plans 75,000 cases.
Figure F218-02. Standards in budgeting: card × planned volume = budget.

18.2  Ideal and currently attainable standards
An ideal standard assumes perfect conditions: no waste, no rest, no breakdowns. A currently attainable standard allows for normal loss, rest, setups and normal downtime. It is hard but possible for a trained team working efficiently.
For juice labor, the ideal is 0.040 hours a case; the standard set is 0.050 hours. Figure F218-04 compares four views of the same job, including the engineers' first proposal and the average of the last twelve months.

18.3  Authoritative and participative standards
An authoritative standard is set by management or engineers alone. A participative standard is set with the people who must meet it. The juice labor standard started as an engineers' proposal of 0.045 hours a case.
The line supervisors pointed out that the proposal had no time for cleaning between runs. After the discussion, the division set 0.050 hours. Figure F218-06 compares the two approaches.
Figure F218-06. Authoritative and participative standards

Participation builds acceptance, because people help set the target. The risk is padding: people may ask for easy standards. Management keeps the final decision and checks the result against activity studies and history.

18.5  Techniques for setting standards
Activity analysis sets a standard by studying how the work should be done: time and motion studies, recipes and engineering tests. The juice time study found a basic time of 0.040 hours a case.
Historical data uses past results. Over the last twelve months, labor averaged 0.056 hours a case, and the best month reached 0.052. Figure F218-11 compares the two techniques.

Chapter summary
Figure F218-14 brings the chapter together.
Figure F218-14. Chapter 18 summary map: the key rule for each section.
Standards turn into budgets by multiplying the cost per unit by planned output, and later measure actual performance. Currently attainable standards allow for normal loss and breaks; ideal standards show the direction of improvement. Participative standards build acceptance, while management keeps the final decision. A price standard includes discounts, freight and receiving; a quantity standard includes normal loss; labor standards add allowances and benefits. Activity analysis and historical data are used together to set standards that are demanding but fair.

Tahini & Spreads is setting new standards for its 500 g tahini jar before the 2028 budget.
