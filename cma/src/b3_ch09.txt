Chapter 9
The Capital Expenditure and Cash Budgets
موازنة النفقات الرأسمالية والموازنة النقدية
Beverages plans an operating income of $3,182,000 for 2028. Yet in the first quarter it will pay for the new filling line, buy concentrate for the season and wait sixty days for its customers to pay. Profit and cash are not the same thing, and a profitable division can still run out of money. This chapter plans the cash.

9.1  The capital expenditure budget
The capital expenditure budget plans spending on long-term assets, such as plant, equipment and systems, by project and by period. Each project is approved separately, usually with capital budgeting methods (a CMA Part 2 topic), and then placed in the quarter when cash will be paid.

The project budget of Chapter 4 is placed on its timeline: equipment in the first quarter, installation and half the training in the second, the rest in the third. Figure F309-02 gives $1,800,000, $350,000 and $350,000, a total of $2,500,000.

9.2  Why a cash budget?
The cash budget plans the cash coming in and going out each period and the cash left at the end. Its purposes are to show shortages early, so that borrowing can be arranged in time, and to show surpluses that can be invested or used to repay loans.
It is linked to every other budget. Figure F309-04 shows how sales, materials, labor, overhead, selling and administrative costs, capital spending and the financing policy all send their cash effects to it.
Figure F309-04. Every budget feeds the cash budget.
Two things change on the way to the cash budget. Timing changes, because customers and suppliers pay later; and non-cash items, such as depreciation, drop out.

9.4  Preparing the cash budget
The cash budget has four parts: beginning cash plus collections gives cash available; minus disbursements gives cash before financing; borrowing, interest and repayments give ending cash. Management keeps a minimum cash balance of $400,000.
Non-cash costs are left out. Fixed overhead is paid at $300,000 a quarter, not $450,000, because $150,000 of it is depreciation; fixed selling and administrative costs are paid at $550,000.
WORKED EXAMPLE   Worked example: the first quarter
Cash available $4,640,000 − disbursements $5,163,750 = $(523,750) before financing. To stay above the minimum after interest, Beverages borrows $1,000,000 in steps of $100,000; after interest of $20,000, ending cash is $456,250. Figure F309-08 shows the year.

9.5  Credit and payables policies
Credit and payables policies change the timing of cash, and therefore the borrowing. Collecting faster from customers brings cash in earlier; paying suppliers faster sends it out earlier.
Figure F309-10 tests two changes in the first quarter. Collecting 50% in the quarter of sale would add $420,000 of cash; paying for concentrate in full at once would take more cash out and raise the loan.

Tighter credit has a cost: customers such as Levant and the supermarkets may buy less or ask for discounts. Slower payment to suppliers may lose discounts or goodwill. The cash budget shows the cash effect; management weighs it against these costs.
EXAM TRAP   Policy changes move cash, not profit
A change in credit or payment terms changes when cash arrives or leaves, not the sales or expenses themselves. Unless the question adds a discount or lost sales, operating income does not change.

Case question: the tahini cash budget for the second quarter
Tahini & Spreads sells most of its jars on credit to Barada, the Gulf importer and the supermarkets. It collects most of each quarter's sales in the same quarter and the rest in the next. It buys sesame paste by sea and pays the supplier half in the quarter of purchase and half in the next. Wages, jars and the variable costs of running the line are paid in the quarter they arise, and the fixed costs of the plant and the sales team are paid evenly through the year.
In the second quarter, the division will also pay for a new sesame mixer. The controller, Rana, must prepare the cash budget for the quarter and tell the division manager, Samir, whether the division will need to borrow to keep its minimum cash balance. Head office wants to know at once if the division will need a loan, because its own cash is planned around the summer season.
The new assistant has prepared a first draft. It includes the depreciation of the plant in the cash payments and uses the sales for the quarter as the cash collected. Rana must correct both errors before the meeting.
Samir also asks what would happen to cash if the supermarkets were given longer to pay, as they have requested.
