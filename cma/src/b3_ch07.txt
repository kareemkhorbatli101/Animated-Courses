WHAT YOU ALREADY KNOW   What you already know
Book 2, Chapter 18 set the standard of 6.0 liters of concentrate, 12 bottles, 0.05 labor hours and 0.02 machine hours a case. Book 2, Chapter 17 moved bottle deliveries to just-in-time.
7.1  From production to inputs
Every input budget starts from the production budget, not the sales budget, because materials and labor are used when cases are filled, not when they are sold. Figure F307-02 shows the three budgets that follow from production.
Figure F307-02. From the production budget to the input budgets.
For each input, the budget multiplies the cases to produce by the quantity per case: liters of concentrate, labor hours and machine hours. Machine hours then drive the variable overhead, while fixed overhead is budgeted item by item.
The link runs both ways. If an input budget shows a shortage of materials, labor or machine time, the production budget, and perhaps the sales budget, must be changed.

7.2  The direct materials budget
The direct materials budget has two parts: the materials to use and the materials to buy. Purchases equal usage plus the desired ending stock minus the beginning stock, just like the production budget.

7.4  The overhead budget
The overhead budget has a variable part and a fixed part. Variable overhead moves with its driver, machine hours, at $50.00 an hour; fixed overhead is budgeted item by item and does not change with production.

Chapter summary
Figure F307-13 brings the chapter together.
Figure F307-13. Chapter 7 summary map: the key rule for each section.
The direct materials, direct labor and overhead budgets all start from the production budget. Materials purchases equal usage plus desired ending stock minus beginning stock, and the procurement policy decides the stock; just-in-time items are bought as used. Labor hours equal production times standard hours, and must be checked against the hours available. Variable overhead follows its driver, fixed overhead is budgeted by item, and non-cash items such as depreciation are marked for the cash budget.

Case question: sesame and labor for the tahini jars
The Tahini & Spreads division has finished its production budget for 2028. Each 500 g jar needs sesame paste and a few minutes on the filling line, at the standard quantities set in Book 2. The sesame comes by sea from Sudan and Ethiopia, so deliveries can be late, and the division keeps a stock of sesame equal to a share of the next quarter's needs. Last year one ship arrived three weeks late, and the division came close to stopping the filling line.
The filling team works a fixed number of hours each quarter. In the second quarter several operators will be on annual leave, so fewer hours are available than usual. The plant manager, Samir, wants to know whether the second-quarter plan can be met with the team he has.
The controller, Rana, must prepare the sesame purchases budget and the labor budget for the second quarter, test the labor plan against the hours available, and explain the purchasing policy to the new buyer, who suggested buying sesame just-in-time like the bottles in the Beverages division. Rana plans to show her figures to Samir and the buyer together, so that both understand the trade-off between stock and risk.
