8.1  Plantwide, departmental and cost-driver rates
A plantwide overhead rate, also called a corporate-wide rate, uses one rate and one base for all overhead. A departmental overhead rate gives each department its own rate and base. Individual cost-driver rates go further, with a rate for each activity; that is activity-based costing (Chapter 12).
Tahini & Spreads has two departments. Grinding is machine-heavy and packing is labor-heavy. Its plantwide rate of $30 per machine hour ignores that difference. Figure F208-02 compares the three methods.

8.2  Fixed overhead

8.3  The allocation base and the denominator
The base for fixed overhead should measure how products use capacity. In an automated grinding plant that is machine hours. The base must also be measurable for every job.
The fixed overhead rate is budgeted fixed overhead divided by a denominator level of the base. The choice matters: the same $2,400,000 gives $16 per hour at theoretical capacity, $20 at practical capacity, $24 at normal capacity and $25 at master-budget capacity. Figure F208-05 shows all four.
Figure F208-05. Fixed overhead rate at four capacity levels for Tahini & Spreads.
Orontes uses normal capacity: 100,000 machine hours, inside a normal range of 90,000 to 110,000 hours a year, which gives $24 per hour and keeps inventory in line with U.S. GAAP. Chapter 9 shows why managers also look at practical capacity.
● IFRS CONTRAST   ASC 330 and normal capacity
U.S. GAAP and IAS 2 allocate fixed production overhead on normal capacity. Fixed overhead per unit is not increased when production is abnormally low; the unallocated amount is expensed in that period.

8.4  Why overhead is over- or under-applied
Actual overhead collects in Manufacturing Overhead Control; applied overhead collects in Manufacturing Overhead Applied. When actual is more than applied, the difference is underapplied overhead and product costs are too low. When applied is more, the difference is overapplied overhead.
There are three causes. Spending can differ from the budget. Actual activity can differ from the denominator, so fixed overhead is applied to more or fewer hours than planned. And the base can be used more or less efficiently. Figure F208-06 summarizes the logic.
Figure F208-06. Why overhead is under- or overapplied, and what each balance means.
In 2027 Tahini & Spreads worked 92,000 machine hours, below the normal-capacity denominator of 100,000. So applied fixed overhead fell short even before spending is considered.

8.5  Closing the difference at year-end
During the year the difference stays open, because monthly differences often cancel out. At year-end it must be closed so that both overhead accounts return to zero.
If the difference is immaterial, it is closed to cost of goods sold. If it is material, it is spread by proration over ending work in process, ending finished goods and cost of goods sold. The shares come from the overhead applied in each balance this year, not from the total balances.
Orontes treats a difference above 2% of applied overhead as material. Figure F208-07 compares the two methods.

8.6  Extended Example 8A: Tahini & Spreads closes 2027
This example analyzes the year's underapplied overhead, prorates it, records the closing entry and compares the result with a write-off. The proration and its effect on income are exam-core; the variance split previews Book 3.
1. Scenario
Rate: $30 per machine hour, set at normal capacity of 100,000 hours.
Actual: 92,000 machine hours; fixed overhead $30,000 above budget; variable overhead $8,000 above the flexible budget.
Balances before closing: work in process $690,000, finished goods $1,380,000, cost of goods sold $6,090,000.
2. Plan
Find the underapplied amount and its causes, test materiality, prorate on applied overhead, record the entry and compare the income effect of the two methods.
3. Calculations
Fixed rate: $2,400,000 ÷ 100,000 hours = $24; plus $6 variable = $30 per machine hour.
Applied: 92,000 × $30 = $2,760,000. Actual: $2,990,000. Underapplied: $230,000.
Materiality: 230,000 ÷ 2,760,000 = 8.3%, above the 2% policy, so the difference is prorated.
4. Why it was underapplied
Figure F208-09 splits the $230,000. Most of it, $192,000, is the production-volume effect: fixed overhead was budgeted for normal capacity but applied to only 92,000 hours. Spending adds $30,000 and variable overhead $8,000. Book 3 calls these variances.

Chapter summary
Figure F208-14 brings the chapter together.
Figure F208-14. Chapter 8 summary map: the key rule for each section.
A plantwide rate is simple, while departmental and cost-driver rates follow the real drivers and give more accurate costs at a higher record-keeping cost. Fixed overhead is the cost of capacity; its rate is budgeted fixed overhead divided by a chosen capacity level, and Orontes uses normal capacity. Overhead becomes under- or overapplied through spending, volume and efficiency. At year-end an immaterial difference is closed to COGS and a material one is prorated on the overhead applied in WIP, finished goods and COGS.
