WHAT YOU ALREADY KNOW   What you already know
Under IFRS you may have learned different names for the parts of equity, such as share capital and share premium. U.S. GAAP says common stock, additional paid-in capital, retained earnings and accumulated other comprehensive income. The False Friend box in Section 4.4 compares the words.
4.1  Components of equity and the statement of changes in equity
The statement of changes in equity explains why each part of equity changed during the period. It separates two kinds of change. Transactions with owners, such as issuing shares and paying dividends, are one kind. Comprehensive income, which the company earns, is the other.
Equity has two main parts. Contributed capital is what owners have paid in: common stock and preferred stock at par or stated value, plus additional paid-in capital. Earned capital is retained earnings and AOCI. Treasury stock is deducted from the total. A noncontrolling interest, if any, is shown separately within equity (Chapter 16).

4.2  Issuing and buying back shares
When a company issues shares for cash, it credits common stock with the par value and APIC with the rest. If the shares have no par and no stated value, the whole amount goes to common stock. Direct costs of issuing shares, such as underwriting fees, reduce APIC; they are not expenses.
Shares issued for a noncash asset, such as land, are recorded at the fair value of the shares or of the asset, whichever is more reliable. Preferred stock is recorded in the same way, in its own account.
A company may buy back its own shares and hold them as treasury stock. Under the cost method, treasury stock is recorded at the price paid and deducted from equity. Shares held as treasury stock are issued shares but not outstanding shares: they receive no dividends and have no votes.
A company never records a gain or loss on its own shares in net income. Figure F04-04 shows what happens when treasury stock is reissued above or below cost, or retired.
Figure F04-04. Treasury stock under the cost method: buy back, reissue or retire.
WORKED EXAMPLE   Worked example: treasury stock under the cost method
Step 1. Suppose Orontes buys back 50,000 shares at $16 in 2026. Treasury stock is debited at cost: $800,000.
Step 2. It reissues 20,000 shares at $18. Cash is $360,000 and the cost removed is $320,000, so APIC–treasury stock is credited with $40,000.
Step 3. It reissues 20,000 more at $13. The shortfall below cost is $60,000. APIC–treasury stock absorbs $40,000, and retained earnings absorb the remaining $20,000.

WATCH: NOT YET TESTABLE   ASU 2025-12: a new retirement option
ASU 2025-12 allows companies to charge the whole excess on a retirement to APIC, as long as APIC does not become negative. It applies to annual periods beginning after December 15, 2026, so it is not testable before about January 2028. Use the current rules on the exam.
● IFRS CONTRAST   Treasury shares under IFRS
IFRS also deducts treasury shares from equity and never shows a gain or loss in profit. It does not prescribe a cost or par-value method, so IFRS companies show the effect in reserves in different ways.

4.3  Dividends, stock dividends and stock splits
A cash dividend has three dates. On the declaration date the company debits retained earnings and credits dividends payable, a current liability. On the date of record no entry is made; it only decides who is paid. On the payment date the liability is paid. Orontes declared $400,000 in December 2025, so that amount is still payable at year-end.
A property dividend is paid with a noncash asset. The company first remeasures the asset to fair value and records any gain or loss, then debits retained earnings at fair value. A scrip dividend creates a note payable. A liquidating dividend returns contributed capital, so it reduces APIC rather than retained earnings. Figure F04-06 compares the types.

4.4  Retained earnings
Retained earnings are the profits a company has kept since it began, less the dividends it has declared. A board may set part of retained earnings aside, for example for a new plant. These appropriated retained earnings stay within equity; no costs are ever charged to them.
If a company finds an error in earlier financial statements, it does not correct it through this year's net income. It makes a prior-period adjustment: it restates the earlier years and adjusts the opening balance of retained earnings, net of tax.
Retained earnings may also be restricted by law or by loan covenants. Restrictions are disclosed in the notes. They do not change the balance.
FALSE-FRIEND ALERT   Reserves, احتياطيات, réserves
In IFRS and in MENA company law, reserves (احتياطيات, réserves) can mean a legal reserve, a revaluation surplus, a translation reserve or share premium. None of these is simply retained earnings. On a U.S. exam, separate APIC, retained earnings (appropriated or not) and AOCI. French report à nouveau is only the carried-forward part of retained earnings.

4.5  Limitations and links to the other statements
Total equity is a book value: assets minus liabilities, as measured by the accounting rules. It is not what the company is worth. Most assets are at historical cost, and internally built brands and know-how are not recorded.
Treasury stock is shown at the price paid, not at today's share price. Legal capital rules also differ from state to state, so equity does not show exactly how much a company may legally distribute.

The statement of changes in equity takes net income and OCI from the statement of comprehensive income, and dividends and share issues from the company's owner transactions. Its closing balances are the equity section of the balance sheet. Chapter 5 shows how the cash parts of these transactions appear in the financing section.

Chapter summary
Figure F04-10 brings the chapter together.
Figure F04-10. Chapter 4 summary map: the key rule for each section.
Equity is contributed capital plus earned capital, less treasury stock, and the statement of changes in equity explains every change. Issued shares credit par to common stock and the excess to APIC; issue costs reduce APIC. Treasury stock is recorded at cost and deducted from equity, and reissues never create gains or losses in net income. Cash, property and scrip dividends reduce retained earnings and total equity; small stock dividends move fair value, large ones move par, and splits need no entry. Prior-year errors adjust opening retained earnings, net of tax. Equity is a book value, not a market value.

Written task: model answer
The 55,000-share stock dividend moved 385 from retained earnings to common stock and APIC at fair value, because it was a small stock dividend; no assets left Barada, so total equity did not change. The cash dividend of $0.20 per share on 605,000 outstanding shares created a liability of 121, so total equity fell. Treasury shares received no dividends because they are not outstanding. Buying and reissuing treasury stock are transactions with owners, so the excess and the shortfall went to APIC–treasury stock instead of net income. The 50,000 shares still held are deducted from equity at their cost of 300.
A good answer: explains that the stock dividend moved retained earnings at fair value; states that total equity did not change for the stock dividend; links the cash dividend to a liability and lower equity; uses outstanding shares, excluding treasury stock; explains why treasury trades bypass net income.
