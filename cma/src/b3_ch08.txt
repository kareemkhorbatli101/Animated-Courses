The cost of goods sold budget has three components: beginning finished goods, plus the cost of goods manufactured in the period, minus ending finished goods. Figure F308-02 shows the Beverages figures for the year.

EXAM TRAP   Manufactured is not sold
Cost of goods manufactured is the cost of cases finished; cost of goods sold is the cost of cases sold. An answer that uses one for the other ignores the change in finished goods.

8.3  Selling costs and the contribution margin
Selling and administrative costs affect the contribution margin only if they are variable. Freight and commissions are deducted before the contribution margin; marketing, salaries and administration are deducted after it. Figure F308-07 compares the functional format with the contribution format.
Figure F308-07. Functional and contribution formats of the operating budget.

EXAM TRAP   Which costs go above the line?
Variable selling costs reduce the contribution margin; fixed selling and administrative costs do not. Deducting marketing or salaries before the contribution margin, or leaving out freight and commissions, gives the wrong margin.

8.4  The operating budget
The operating budget, or budgeted income statement, brings the schedules together: sales, minus cost of goods sold, minus selling and administrative expenses, gives budgeted operating income. Interest and income taxes are added later, in Chapter 10.
WORKED EXAMPLE   Worked example: the Beverages operating budget
Sales $18,000,000 − cost of goods sold $11,338,000 = gross margin $6,662,000; − selling and administrative $3,480,000 = operating income $3,182,000. Figure F308-09 sets it out.

Chapter summary
Figure F308-13 brings the chapter together.
Figure F308-13. Chapter 8 summary map: the key rule for each section.
The cost of goods sold budget is beginning finished goods plus cost of goods manufactured minus ending finished goods, at standard cost. The selling and administrative budget has variable selling costs per unit and fixed costs by item; only the variable costs reduce the contribution margin. The operating budget combines sales, cost of goods sold and selling and administrative expenses into budgeted operating income, which differs from the contribution-format figure when inventory changes.

Case question: the tahini operating budget for the second quarter
The Tahini & Spreads division has finished its sales and production budgets for 2028, and the controller, Rana, is now preparing the budgeted income statement for the second quarter for the division's review meeting. The division values its finished jars at a standard full cost: the variable manufacturing cost per jar plus fixed manufacturing overhead spread over its normal monthly volume. Sales in the second quarter are lower than in the summer, when Gulf buyers order most.
The division's selling costs are of two kinds. Freight to the Gulf buyers is paid per jar, while the sales team, marketing and the division office cost a fixed amount each month. A new assistant has placed the depreciation of the tahini plant in the selling and administrative budget, and Rana must correct the classification before the meeting.
The division manager, Samir, wants three figures from the budget: the cost of the jars sold, the gross margin and the operating income. He also asks how the contribution margin would change if the freight rate to the Gulf rose next year. Rana will also explain why the budget uses jars sold, not jars produced, for the cost of goods sold.
