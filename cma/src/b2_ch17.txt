WHAT YOU ALREADY KNOW   What you already know
Chapter 14 looked at the value chain inside one company, and Chapters 15 and 16 removed waste from processes. This chapter follows materials across companies and uses relevant costs, as in make-or-buy decisions.
17.1  Supply chain management
A supply chain links every company that helps a product reach the final consumer: suppliers, manufacturers, distributors, retailers and customers. Supply chain management coordinates the flows of goods, information and money along the whole chain.
Figure F217-02 shows the Tahini & Spreads chain. Goods move toward consumers; orders, forecasts and payments move back. The aim is the right product, in the right place, at the right time and the lowest total cost.
Figure F217-02. The Tahini & Spreads supply chain.
Supply chain management is more than purchasing. When partners share sales data, small changes in consumer demand no longer grow into large swings in orders further up the chain, and every partner needs less stock.

At Orontes, lean started with a value stream map of the Beverages line. It showed that a bottle spent weeks in storage and minutes in production. Kanban signals from the filling line to the store, and later to Jordan Glass, cut that waiting time and the space it needed.

17.3  Material requirements planning
Material requirements planning (MRP) calculates which materials are needed, how many, and when to order them. It uses three inputs: the master production schedule, the bill of materials, and the stock records. Figure F217-05 shows the logic.

17.4  Just-in-time systems
Just-in-time (JIT) is a pull system. Materials arrive and products are made only when the next step, or the customer, asks for them. Figure F217-07 compares push and pull.
Figure F217-07. Push and pull systems compared.
JIT needs reliable suppliers, short setups and good quality, because there is almost no stock to hide problems. Figure F217-08 lists the operational benefits for the Beverages division.

WHAT YOU ALREADY KNOW   Backflush costing
With JIT, work in process is small, so some companies use backflush costing: they record costs only at a few trigger points, such as completion or sale. Under U.S. GAAP this is acceptable only when the result is close to what full costing would give (ASC 330).

17.5  Enterprise resource planning
Enterprise resource planning (ERP) is an integrated system in which sales, purchasing, production, inventory, accounting and human resources all work from one shared database. Figure F217-09 shows the idea.
Figure F217-09. ERP: every function works from one shared database.
Because data is entered once, the copying and re-typing removed in Chapter 15 disappear across the company. Managers see stock, orders and costs as they change, and MRP runs on live data instead of last month's reports.
Figure F217-10 lists the main operational benefits. ERP is not only an accounting system: its value comes from linking every function through the same processes and data.
Figure F217-10. Operational benefits of ERP

FALSE-FRIEND ALERT   PGI, تخطيط موارد المؤسسة
French often says 'progiciel de gestion intégré (PGI)' for ERP. The Arabic تخطيط موارد المؤسسة translates the English name; both mean the integrated system, not only planning.

17.6  Outsourcing
Outsourcing means buying from an outside supplier something the company used to do itself. Figure F217-11 lists its benefits and limitations.
Figure F217-11. Benefits and limitations of outsourcing

The financial test compares the supplier's price with the costs that would really disappear, not with the full cost. Allocated plant overhead and depreciation on equipment that cannot be sold stay whether or not the work is outsourced.

Chapter summary
Figure F217-14 brings the chapter together.
Figure F217-14. Chapter 17 summary map: the key rule for each section.
Supply chain management coordinates goods, information and money across companies. Lean removes the eight wastes and brings benefits in time, space and quality. MRP plans materials from the production schedule by netting gross needs against stock and receipts. JIT pulls materials only when needed, and ERP links every function through one database. Outsourcing is judged on avoidable cost plus its risks, and every change is valued by its yearly effect and any one-time release of cash.
