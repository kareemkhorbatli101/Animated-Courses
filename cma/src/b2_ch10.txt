WHAT YOU ALREADY KNOW   What you already know
Chapter 1 introduced cost pools and allocation bases. Chapter 8 put the divisions' service charges into their fixed overhead. This chapter shows where those charges come from.
10.1  Why allocate service department costs
A service department supports other departments but does not make products: at Orontes these are Maintenance, IT, the Quality Lab and HR. A production department works on the products: the Oils, Tahini & Spreads and Beverages divisions.
Service costs are allocated for three reasons. Products must carry their full cost for inventory and pricing. Users become careful when a service has a price. And managers can compare an internal service with an outside supplier.
Service departments also serve each other. Figure F210-02 shows the Orontes links: Maintenance gives 20% of its work to IT, and IT gives 20% of its work to Maintenance.

10.2  Choosing allocation bases

10.3  The direct method
The direct method ignores the services that service departments give each other. Each service department's cost is shared among the production departments only, in proportion to their use.
It is simple and common, but it is least accurate when service departments serve each other a lot.

10.4  The step-down method
The step-down method recognizes some service-to-service links. Service departments are allocated one at a time. A department that has been allocated receives nothing back.

10.5  The reciprocal method
The reciprocal method recognizes all service-to-service links. Write one equation for each service department: its total cost is its own cost plus its share of the other's total.
Solving the two equations gives Maintenance a total of $1,000,000 and IT $600,000. These totals are larger than the original costs because they include the services exchanged. Each division then takes its share of both totals. Figure F210-06 shows the steps.

10.6  Single, dual and budgeted rates
A single-rate method charges one rate per hour for fixed and variable service costs together. When users need fewer hours than planned, the fixed cost is spread over fewer hours, and one user's change moves cost to the others.
A dual-rate method charges variable cost on actual use and fixed cost on the capacity each user planned for. Orontes charges Maintenance at a budgeted rate of $40 per hour plus fixed shares of $560,000, so users do not pay for Maintenance's overspending.
Figure F210-08 compares the two for 2027. The dual rate charged $860,000 and left only the $12,000 spending difference in Maintenance. The single rate left $47,000, which mixes that spending difference with $35,000 of idle capacity.

Chapter summary
Figure F210-13 brings the chapter together.
Figure F210-13. Chapter 10 summary map: the key rule for each section.
Service department costs are allocated so that products carry full cost and users value the services. Bases should follow cause and effect. The direct method ignores service-to-service links, the step-down method recognizes them in one direction, and the reciprocal method recognizes them all. Budgeted dual rates charge variable cost on use and fixed cost on planned capacity, so users do not pay for the service department's waste or idle capacity.
