13.1  What makes data Big
Big Data is data whose volume, speed or variety puts it beyond what traditional tools were built to handle. Figure F413-02 sets out the four characteristics the exam names.
Figure F413-02. The four Vs of Big Data.
COMMON ERROR IN OTHER BOOKS   'The five Vs of Big Data'
Many guides add a fifth V, value, and some add others. The official outcome names four: volume, velocity, variety and veracity. Answer with four unless a question gives you a different list to work from.
Veracity is the one that matters most to an accountant, and the one most often skipped. It asks whether the data can be trusted, and Chapter 11 showed what that means in practice: one distributor recorded under three spellings, fourteen orders with no delivery date. Volume without veracity produces confident answers that are wrong.

Figure F413-03. Structured, semi-structured and unstructured data

Structured data is what Chapter 10 described: rows and columns with a fixed meaning, which a query can read directly. It is the minority of what a company holds and the overwhelming majority of what it analyses, for the simple reason that it is the easiest to use.
Unstructured data is the opposite and the larger share: emails, photographs, recordings, free text. A photograph of a damaged pallet contains information no table holds, and extracting it has until recently needed a person. That is what has changed, and it is why variety now appears beside volume.

13.3  From data to action
Data by itself decides nothing. Figure F413-04 shows the five steps by which it becomes a decision, using the Beverages figures.
Figure F413-04. From data to action, in five steps.
Read it upward and the dependencies are clear. Action needs insight into why a pattern holds; insight needs the knowledge that a pattern exists; knowledge needs enough information to see across cases; and information is a fact placed in context. Each step is worth more than the one below it, and none can be skipped.
The step companies stop at is knowledge. Knowing that the third quarter is always the peak is useful, but it does not say whether to schedule the extra shift to the calendar or to the weather. Chapter 15 will show that the Beverages peak follows temperature far more than it follows the promotion calendar, and that is an insight rather than a pattern.

The last row deserves attention because it worsens as data grows. With enough variables, patterns appear by chance alone: two series that happen to move together, with no connection between them. 13.6  Business intelligence
business intelligence (BI) is the collection of applications, tools and best practices that turn data into information people can act on. Figure F413-07 unpacks that definition.
Figure F413-07. What business intelligence covers
Business intelligence (F.4.f)
What it covers
At Orontes
Applications
The software people actually use to ask questions
Dashboards and reporting tools
Tools
What moves and prepares the data behind them
The warehouse and loading of Chapter 10
Best practices
How the work is organised so answers can be trusted
Agreed definitions, one version of each measure
Purpose
Turning data into information that can be acted on
Not a report, but a decision
What it is not
A synonym for analytics or for a dashboard
Analytics is a technique; BI is the whole arrangement
The third element is the one that is usually missing. Applications and tools can be bought; agreed definitions cannot. If two dashboards show different figures for the same measure because nobody settled what it means, the arrangement has failed however good the software is.
EXAM TRAP   Business intelligence is not a dashboard
A dashboard is one application within business intelligence. The term covers the whole arrangement: the applications, the tools that feed them, and the practices that make the numbers trustworthy. A question equating the two has narrowed it too far.
Business intelligence looks backward and sideways: what happened, where, and compared with what. Part IV goes further, into what will happen and what should be done, which is the distinction between the four types of analytics that Chapter 15 sets out.

13.7  Extended Example 13A: one number becomes a decision
This example takes a single Beverages figure and walks it up the progression to an action. It is exam-core, because the exam gives a statement and asks which step it represents.
1. Data
The number is 240,000. On its own it means nothing at all: it could be cases, dinars, kilometres or an account code.
2. Information
Place it in context and it becomes information: 240,000 cases sold in 2028 Q3. Now it can be compared with something, which is the whole purpose of context.
3. Knowledge
Set it beside the other quarters and a pattern appears. The third quarter is the peak in both years, at 240,400 cases in 2027 and 240,000 in 2028. That is knowledge: a regularity across cases rather than a single fact.
4. Insight
Knowledge says what happens; insight says why. The third quarter is also the hottest, at 34 degrees against 15 in the first. Chapter 15 will show that temperature explains the pattern and that promotion weeks explain much less of it.
5. Action
The decision follows from the insight, not from the pattern. If the peak follows the weather rather than the promotion calendar, the extra shift should be scheduled to forecast temperature, and promotion spending in the hottest weeks may be buying demand that would have arrived anyway.
6. Why the last step is the hard one
Everything up to insight is analysis and can be delegated. The action requires somebody with the authority to change the shift pattern, and willing to accept an analyst's finding over a long-standing assumption. That is the capability the fourth row of Figure F413-06 describes, and it is not bought with software.

Chapter summary
Figure F413-09 brings the chapter together.
Figure F413-09. Chapter 13 summary map: the key rule for each section.
Big Data is data beyond what traditional tools were built to handle, described by four characteristics: volume, velocity, variety and veracity. Data comes structured, semi-structured and unstructured, and the unstructured share is the largest and the hardest to use. It becomes useful through a progression from data to information to knowledge to insight to action, and only insight supports a decision because only insight says why. Every opportunity carries a matching challenge, of which the most common is that nobody acts on the answer. Data is a strategic asset because it is specific and compounds, but the capability to use it matters more than the data itself. Business intelligence is the applications, tools and practices that together turn data into something actionable.

Case question: the dashboard that nobody trusted
Orontes has built a sales dashboard on the new ERP. It refreshes hourly and shows revenue, cases sold and margin by division, by product family and by market. The board was pleased with it for two months.
Then the Beverages director and the Foods director presented different margin figures for the same month at the same meeting. Both had taken their number from the dashboard. It emerged that the two divisions had never agreed whether margin is calculated before or after the allocation of distribution cost, and each had configured the view accordingly. No one was given responsibility for defining the measures when the dashboard was built, and the project was treated as a technical exercise.
Separately, an analyst has reported that cases sold correlate strongly with the number of staff birthdays recorded in the payroll system over the past three years. He has proposed investigating further.
The sales director has also asked why the dashboard shows what happened but never says what to do about it, and whether more data would fix that. Rania has been asked to answer all three points.
