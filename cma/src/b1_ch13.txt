WHAT YOU ALREADY KNOW   What you already know
IAS 12 uses the same balance sheet approach as U.S. GAAP. The differences are in the details: how deferred tax assets are recognized, which tax rates are used, and some exemptions. Section 13.5 lists them.
13.1  Why book income and taxable income differ
Financial statements follow U.S. GAAP and are prepared for investors. Tax returns follow tax law and are prepared for the government. The two sets of rules have different goals, so taxable income is usually not the same as income before income taxes.
If a company simply reported the tax it paid, its tax expense would jump around when the rules timed items differently. Interperiod tax allocation solves this. The company recognizes the tax effects of transactions in the same periods as the transactions themselves, using deferred taxes.
Figure F13-02 shows Orontes's tax figures for 2025. Income tax expense of $989,700 has two parts: current tax expense of $889,700, the tax on 2025 taxable income, and deferred tax expense of $100,000. Net taxable temporary differences grew by $400,000, mainly because tax depreciation was faster than book depreciation.

13.2  Temporary and permanent differences
A temporary difference arises when the tax basis of an asset or liability differs from its carrying amount on the balance sheet. It reverses in later years. A taxable temporary difference will increase future taxable income; a deductible temporary difference will reduce it.
A permanent difference never reverses. The item is in book income but never in taxable income, or the reverse. Tax-exempt municipal bond interest and fines are common examples. Permanent differences create no deferred tax; they only change the effective tax rate.
Figure F13-03 lists common examples, and Figure F13-04 gives the test. A taxable temporary difference creates a deferred tax liability: tax that will be paid later. A deductible temporary difference creates a deferred tax asset: tax that will be saved later.

13.3  Measuring current and deferred taxes
U.S. GAAP uses the balance sheet approach. Current tax is taxable income times the tax rate. Deferred tax liabilities and assets are the temporary differences times the enacted tax rate that will apply when they reverse. Deferred tax expense is the change in the net deferred tax liability during the year.
WORKED EXAMPLE   Worked example: Orontes's 2026 income tax
Step 1. Suppose Orontes earns $2,000,000 before tax in 2026. It removes the permanent differences: municipal interest of $20,000 and a fine of $10,000.
Step 2. New equipment costs $900,000. Tax law allows 100% bonus depreciation, but book depreciation is $300,000. The difference of $600,000 reduces taxable income now: a taxable temporary difference.
Step 3. The dispenser warranty from Chapter 12 was expensed at $60,000, but only $22,000 of repairs were paid. The $38,000 not yet deductible increases taxable income now.
Step 4. Taxable income is $1,428,000, so current tax is $357,000. Figure F13-05 shows the calculation.

13.4  Rate changes, valuation allowances, losses and presentation
When a new tax law changes the rate, the company remeasures its deferred taxes at once. The whole effect goes to income from continuing operations in the period that includes the enactment date, even if the original item was in OCI. For example, a U.S. tax law signed on July 4, 2025 changed several deduction rules; companies recorded the effects in the quarter that included that date.
Figure F13-08 follows the deferred tax liability on Orontes's equipment. Suppose a law enacted in 2027 cuts the rate to 21% from 2028. At the end of 2027 the liability falls from $75,000 to $63,000. The $12,000 decrease is a benefit in 2027.
Figure F13-08. The DTL on Orontes's 2026 equipment over three years, with a rate cut enacted in 2027.
A deferred tax asset is useful only if the company will have enough taxable income to use it. If it is more likely than not that some of the asset will not be realized, the company records a valuation allowance. Figure F13-09 shows the test.
Figure F13-09. Decision chart: does a deferred tax asset need a valuation allowance?.
A tax loss creates a net operating loss carryforward, which is a deferred tax asset. Under current U.S. federal law, most losses cannot be carried back. They can be carried forward without a time limit, but they can reduce only 80% of taxable income in any year.
All deferred tax assets and liabilities are noncurrent. Within one tax jurisdiction they are offset and shown as one net amount. Tax is also allocated within the period: Orontes's OCI loss was shown net of its $375 tax effect, and discontinued operations carry their own tax (Chapter 3). This is intraperiod tax allocation.
EXAM TRAP   Rate and allowance traps
Use enacted rates only; an announced but unsigned rate is ignored.
Record the full effect of a rate change in continuing operations when the law is enacted.
Record the full DTA, then a valuation allowance: do not record only the probable part.
An uncertain tax position is recognized only if it is more likely than not to be accepted by the tax authority. The global minimum tax (Pillar Two) is treated as a current cost in the year it arises, with no deferred taxes.

ASU 2023-09 is testable now for public companies. They show a rate reconciliation in dollars and percentages with eight standard categories, and they list separately any item of at least 5% of pretax income times the statutory rate. They also disclose income taxes paid by federal, state and foreign jurisdictions.
WATCH: NOT YET TESTABLE   ASU 2023-09 for private companies
For companies that are not public business entities, ASU 2023-09 applies to annual periods beginning after December 15, 2025. That version is not testable before about January 2027.
FALSE-FRIEND ALERT   'Provision for income taxes'
U.S. companies often call income tax expense the provision for income taxes. It is an expense, not an IAS 37 provision (مخصص) and not a reserve. Arabic مخصص التقييم, the valuation allowance, is a contra-asset, not a liability.

Chapter summary
Figure F13-11 brings the chapter together.
Figure F13-11. Chapter 13 summary map: the key rule for each section.
Book income follows U.S. GAAP and taxable income follows tax law. Temporary differences reverse and create deferred tax liabilities (taxable) or deferred tax assets (deductible); permanent differences never reverse and create no deferred tax. Current tax is taxable income times the rate; deferred taxes are temporary differences times the enacted rate; tax expense is the sum of current tax and the change in the net deferred tax liability. Rate changes are recognized in continuing operations at enactment. A valuation allowance reduces a deferred tax asset when realization is not more likely than not. All deferred taxes are noncurrent and netted within a jurisdiction.
