18.1  Critical success factors and KPIs
A critical success factor (CSF) is something an organization must do well if its strategy is to succeed. A key performance indicator (KPI) is the measure that shows how well it is being done. The CSF is the thing; the KPI is the number.

18.2  The balanced scorecard and its four perspectives
A balanced scorecard is a set of measures drawn from four perspectives, each with its objectives, measures, targets and the actions planned to reach them. It is balanced because financial results alone describe only what has already happened.
Each perspective carries the same four components: what the company wants to achieve, the measure that tracks it, the target for the period and the action planned to reach it.

EXAM TRAP   Balanced means four perspectives, not four financial ratios
A scorecard with profit, margin, return and growth on it is not balanced; it measures the same thing four ways. Each perspective must bring information the others do not.
The scorecard is not a report produced after the year. It is agreed with the plan, reviewed during the year, and used to decide what to do next, which is why Chapter 1 and this chapter belong together.

18.3  What makes a scorecard effective
A scorecard fails more often from its design than from its arithmetic. Figure F318-06 sets out the characteristics of one that works.
Figure F318-06. Characteristics of an effective balanced scorecard

The commonest fault is too many measures. A board that reviews forty numbers reviews none of them, and managers whose bonus depends on a long list will work on whichever is easiest to move.
The second commonest is a measure nobody owns. Every measure on the Orontes scorecard has a manager who can influence it, which is the controllability principle of Chapter 15 applied to non-financial numbers.

18.4  The strategy map
A strategy map draws the cause-and-effect links between the objectives in the four perspectives. It is read upward: investment in people improves processes, better processes satisfy customers, and satisfied customers produce the financial result.
Figure F318-07. A strategy map from training to return on investment.
Figure F318-07 traces one chain at Orontes, from training hours through changeover time and on-time delivery to export share and the return. This is the chain the variances of Chapters 12 and 14 revealed: the long changeover that raised costs also delayed deliveries.
The map explains why leading indicators matter. Training hours and changeover time move before the financial result does, so they give warning while there is still time to act. A lagging indicator such as return on investment reports a year that is already over.

Chapter summary
Figure F318-12 brings the chapter, and Book 3, together.
Figure F318-12. Chapter 18 summary map: the key rule for each section.
A critical success factor is what an organization must do well; a key performance indicator measures it. A balanced scorecard sets objectives, measures, targets and actions across four perspectives: financial, customer, internal process, and learning and growth. An effective scorecard has few measures, each owned and each traceable to the strategy, and mixes leading with lagging indicators. A strategy map shows the cause-and-effect chain between them. Reading the scorecard means reading the pattern, and each measure should be reported as often as it can be acted on.

Case question: the tahini scorecard
The Tahini & Spreads division has been asked to grow, and head office wants its progress tracked on a balanced scorecard rather than on profit alone. The division manager, Samir, has agreed five measures for the quarter with the controller, Rania.
The quarter has now closed. The division's segment margin came in below target, but the Gulf supermarket chain received a higher share of its orders in full than the division had promised, and every operator booked for training on the new line completed it.
Two results worry Rania. The sesame yield from each kilogram of seed fell short of the standard, which raises the cost of every jar, and complaints from the Jordanian supermarkets were almost double the target.
Rania must sort the five measures into the four perspectives of the scorecard, count how many were met, read the pattern for the board, and recommend one further measure for the next quarter with the interval at which it should be reported. Head office has made clear that it will judge the division on the whole scorecard, not on the margin alone, and that the measures will be reviewed again at the end of the year.
