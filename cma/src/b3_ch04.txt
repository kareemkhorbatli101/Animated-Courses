4.1  The master budget
The master budget is the complete annual budget of the organization. Its purpose is to plan and coordinate every unit for one year, usually split into quarters and months. Every organization prepares one.
It has two groups of components. The operating budget plans sales, production and costs, and ends in the budgeted income statement. The financial budget plans capital spending and cash, and ends in the budgeted balance sheet. Figure F304-02 shows how they link.
Figure F304-02. The master budget: operating and financial budgets.
Every link matters. The sales budget sets production; production sets materials, labor and overhead; these set the cost of goods sold. Sales also drive cash collections, so an error in the sales budget spreads to every other budget.

4.3  Project budgets
A project budget plans the costs of one project over its whole life, which may be shorter or longer than a year. It is used for building a plant, installing a new line, launching a product or putting in a new system.

4.4  Activity-based budgeting
Activity-based budgeting plans overhead from the activities that planned output will require. It is most useful when products use overhead very differently, as lentils and za'atar do in the Pulses & Spices line.
Figure F304-07 shows the logic. Planned output sets the demand for each activity; each demand is multiplied by the activity rate from Book 2, Chapter 12. Facility costs do not change with output and are budgeted as a fixed amount.
Figure F304-07. How an activity-based budget is built.

4.5  Benefits and limitations
No system suits every purpose. Figure F304-11 compares the benefits and limitations of the three systems.
Figure F304-11. Benefits and limitations of the three budget systems
System
Benefits (B.4.d)
Limitations
Master budget
Coordinates every unit; links to strategy
Takes time; can become fixed and out of date
Project budget
Controls a project over its whole life
Hard to estimate new work; shared costs may be missed
Activity-based budget
Shows why overhead changes; finds waste
Needs activity data; costly to set up and maintain
The master budget coordinates everything but can become a fixed ritual. The project budget controls a project well but may miss costs shared with the rest of the business. The activity-based budget explains overhead but needs activity data that many companies do not have.
Companies usually combine the systems: a master budget for the whole company, project budgets for major investments, and activity-based budgets where overhead is large and varied.

Chapter summary
Figure F304-14 brings the chapter together.
Figure F304-14. Chapter 4 summary map: the key rule for each section.
The master budget links operating budgets, ending in the budgeted income statement, and financial budgets, ending in the budgeted balance sheet; it starts with sales. A project budget covers every cost of one project over its life, with a contingency. An activity-based budget multiplies planned activity demand by activity rates and budgets facility costs separately. Each system has benefits and limitations, and companies usually combine them.

Case question: budgeting Levant's service costs
Levant Distribution, the Orontes subsidiary that delivers to shops, hotels and supermarkets, is preparing its 2028 budget. In past years its manager, Karim, budgeted warehouse and delivery costs by adding a percentage to last year's total. The costs kept rising, and no one could say why. Last year the total rose again, mainly because more customers asked for rush orders and returned damaged goods.
This year the controller wants an activity-based budget. Book 2 of this series measured the cost of Levant's main activities: taking an order, making a delivery, handling a rush order and processing a return. The sales team has now planned the number of each activity for 2028.
Karim is also planning a new cold-storage warehouse that will take about eighteen months to build. He asks whether it should simply be included in next year's normal budget. The warehouse will cost several million dollars, and the board wants its spending tracked separately from Levant's normal costs.
Finally, the controller wants Karim's staff to understand how Levant's budgets fit into the Orontes master budget, and which parts are operating budgets and which are financial budgets.
