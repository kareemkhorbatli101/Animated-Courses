10.1  What enterprise resource planning is
enterprise resource planning (ERP) is a single suite of software modules running on one shared database, covering production, purchasing, sales, payroll, finance and more. Figure F410-02 shows the change it makes.
Figure F410-02. Separate systems before, and one ERP on one database after.
The defining feature is the shared database, not the breadth of the modules. Several separate systems that exchange files are still several systems. One system means one stored value for each fact, which is where every advantage below comes from.
Those advantages come at a price, and the exam expects both sides. Figure F410-03 pairs them.
Figure F410-03. The advantages and disadvantages of ERP

The second row is the one that sinks implementations. An ERP embodies a way of working, so a company either changes its processes to match the software or pays to change the software. Companies that assume a third option exists are the ones whose projects run years late.

10.2  How ERP answers the six challenges
Figure F410-04 takes the six challenges of Chapter 9 in order and shows what integration does to each.
Figure F410-04. How ERP answers the six challenges of Chapter 9

One mechanism produces all six answers: the fact is stored once. Because production output is a row in the same database the ledger reads, it cannot be keyed twice, cannot disagree with itself, needs no reconciliation, arrives without delay and can be joined to a customer or a product.
The sixth answer is the one candidates overlook. The unreconciled monthly upload of Chapter 9 does not become a better-controlled upload; it ceases to exist, because there is nothing to transfer between. Removing a control weakness by removing the thing that needed controlling is the strongest form of the remedy.
EXAM TRAP   Integration is not the same as interfacing
Two systems that send each other files are interfaced. One system holding one copy of the data is integrated. A question describing nightly file transfers between modules is describing interfaced systems, whatever the vendor calls the product.

10.3  Relational databases
A relational database stores data in tables of rows and columns, with tables linked by shared values rather than by position. Figure F410-05 shows two Orontes tables and the link between them.
Figure F410-05. A relational database: two tables joined by a key.
A primary key uniquely identifies each row: no two suppliers may share a supplier number, and none may be left blank. A foreign key is a column in one table holding the primary key of another, which is how the invoice knows which supplier it belongs to.
Two control consequences follow directly. The supplier name is stored once, so correcting it corrects every invoice at the same moment. And an invoice cannot reference a supplier that does not exist, because the database refuses a foreign key with no matching primary key. That is referential integrity, and it is the validity check of Chapter 7 enforced by the database itself.
A database management system (DBMS) is the software that holds the tables, enforces the keys, decides who may read or change what, and answers queries. The distinction the exam tests is simple: the tables are the data, and the management system is what guards and serves them.

10.4  Data warehouses and data marts
An operational system is built to process one transaction quickly. Analysis asks the opposite question, reading millions of rows at once, and running that against the live system would slow down the people working in it. That is why analysis happens somewhere else.
A data warehouse is a separate store holding history across every subject, loaded from the operational systems, read but never updated by transactions. A data mart is a smaller store serving one department or subject, usually drawn from the warehouse. Figure F410-06 compares them.

The relationship is one of scope, not of quality. A mart is not a lesser warehouse; it is a warehouse's worth of structure applied to one subject, so that the sales team can answer its own questions without waiting for, or disturbing, anyone else.

10.5  Enterprise performance management
enterprise performance management (EPM), also called corporate or business performance management, is the set of processes and software that links strategy to plans, budgets, forecasts, reporting and review. Figure F410-07 sets out what it covers.
Figure F410-07. What enterprise performance management covers
EPM activity
What it does
At Orontes
Strategy
Turn objectives into measurable targets
The Gulf expansion becomes a volume and margin target
Planning and budgeting
Build the plan from drivers, not last year plus a percentage
Cases times price, with named assumptions
Forecasting
Re-forecast as conditions change, not once a year
A rolling forecast updated monthly
Consolidation and reporting
Combine divisions and report one result
Three divisions and Levant into one group figure
Monitoring
Compare actual with plan and show the gap
The variance analysis of Book 2
Review and act
Decide what to change, and feed it back into the plan
The balanced scorecard of Book 3, Chapter 18
It is not a synonym for ERP, and the difference is worth stating plainly. An ERP records what happened; enterprise performance management decides what should happen, measures whether it did, and feeds the answer back into the next plan. One is the system of record, the other the system of management.
Four things follow for planning, and they are what a Level C question asks about. Planning becomes driver-based rather than incremental: the budget is built from cases and prices and rates, so a change in any assumption flows through without rebuilding the model. Forecasting becomes continuous rather than annual.
Reporting becomes comparison rather than description, because plan and actual sit in the same place. And review becomes connected to the next plan, which is the loop Book 3 described in the balanced scorecard: a measure that nobody acts on is only a number.
The software matters less than the discipline. A company can manage performance well with a spreadsheet and badly with an expensive suite, because what the term describes is a set of linked processes rather than a product. The suite makes the processes cheaper to run, not more likely to exist.

10.6  Extended Example 10A: what Orontes gains, and what it gives up
This example judges the Orontes ERP proposal rather than describing it. F.1.i is a Level C outcome, so the exam will ask what performance management makes possible and whether it is worth having.
1. The question from Chapter 9
The sales director asked what one case of premium oil cost and how much was labour. Under the present arrangement the answer took two days and arrived unverifiable. On an integrated system it is a query: the batch, its hours, its materials and its output all sit in one database.
2. What that changes about planning
Once cost per case is available on demand, the budget can be built from drivers rather than from last year. Volume times price gives revenue; volume times cost per case gives cost; and a change in the olive price flows through to margin without anyone rebuilding the model.
3. What it changes about review
The variance analysis of Book 2 could already explain a difference after the month closed. With plan and actual in one place, the same analysis becomes available while the month is still running, which is the difference between explaining a loss and preventing one.
4. What Orontes gives up
Three things, and none of them trivial. A long implementation during which attention goes to the project rather than the business. Processes changed to match the software, including some that the plant believes are better than the standard. And dependence on one vendor, with the cost of leaving rising each year.
5. The judgement
For a company expanding into the Gulf the case is strong, because the questions it will need to answer are exactly the ones separate systems cannot answer: which products earn most in which market, and what a change in volume does to cost. A company not growing might rationally defer.
6. What would make it fail
Not the software. Implementations fail when a company tries to keep every existing process, customises heavily to do so, and then cannot upgrade. The discipline that makes integration work is accepting a standard process unless there is a real reason not to.

Chapter summary
Figure F410-09 brings the chapter together.
Figure F410-09. Chapter 10 summary map: the key rule for each section.
Enterprise resource planning is one suite of modules on one shared database, and the shared database is what defines it. Its advantages are integration, one version of the data, real-time information, standard processes, stronger control and scalability; its costs are a long implementation, processes forced to match the software, training, lost flexibility, a single point of failure and vendor dependence. It answers all six challenges of separate systems by one mechanism: each fact is stored once. A relational database holds rows and columns joined by primary and foreign keys, with referential integrity enforced by the database management system. A data warehouse holds history across every subject; a data mart serves one. Enterprise performance management links strategy to plans, forecasts, reporting and review, making planning driver-based and review timely enough to act on.

Case question: the implementation that wanted everything
The Orontes board has approved an ERP to replace the plant, payroll and accounting systems of Chapter 9. The vendor's standard purchasing process requires a goods received note to be matched against the order before an invoice can be posted, which is close to, but not identical with, the process the Amman plant has used for years.

Separately, the sales director wants the new system queried directly for a report listing every order, line by line, for the past three years, refreshed hourly. The IT manager has objected but has not explained why.
The board has asked Rania to advise on the modifications, on the sales director's request, and on what the company should expect the new system to make possible that the old arrangement could not.
