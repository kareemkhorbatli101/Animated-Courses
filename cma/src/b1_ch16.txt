WHAT YOU ALREADY KNOW   What you already know
IFRS readers know IFRS 10: one control model, and a choice between full and partial goodwill. U.S. GAAP uses two control models and allows only full goodwill. Many exam traps come from these two differences.
16.1  What consolidated statements are
Consolidated financial statements present a parent and its subsidiaries as a single economic entity. They are prepared mainly for the parent's shareholders and creditors, who need to see all the resources the group controls and all its obligations.
A company consolidates another entity when it has a controlling financial interest in it. Consolidation adds all of the subsidiary's assets, liabilities, revenues and expenses to the parent's, line by line, even when the parent owns less than all of its shares.
The part of a subsidiary's equity that the parent does not own is the noncontrolling interest (NCI). It is shown within consolidated equity, separately from the parent's equity. Net income and comprehensive income are split between the parent and NCI on the face of the statements.
FALSE-FRIEND ALERT   Minority interest, حقوق الأقلية, intérêts minoritaires
These older terms still appear in many MENA and French texts. The exam uses noncontrolling interest (الحصص غير المسيطرة, participations ne donnant pas le contrôle). It is equity, never a liability.

16.2  Two control models: VIE first, then votes
U.S. GAAP has two ways to identify control, and they are applied in order. First, the company asks whether the other entity is a variable interest entity (VIE). An entity is a VIE if its equity at risk is too small to finance its activities, or if its equity holders do not have the power to direct it or do not bear its losses and returns.
A VIE is consolidated by its primary beneficiary: the party that has both the power to direct the activities that most affect the VIE's performance and the obligation to absorb losses, or the right to receive benefits, that could be significant. Both conditions are required.
If the entity is not a VIE, the voting interest model applies. A company that holds more than 50% of the votes generally controls the entity, unless other owners have substantive participating rights, such as the right to approve budgets. Figure F16-02 shows the order.
Figure F16-02. Decision chart: test the VIE model first, then the voting interest model.

16.3  Full consolidation, proportionate consolidation and the equity method
Full consolidation is used for every entity the company controls. The subsidiary's items are added in full, and NCI shows the part the parent does not own.
The equity method, which the LOS call 'equity consolidation', is used when the investor has significant influence, usually 20% to 50% of the votes, and for most joint ventures. The investment is one line on the balance sheet, and the investor's share of income is one line on the income statement, as for Jordan Glass in Chapter 9.
Proportionate consolidation adds only the investor's share of each asset, liability, revenue and expense. U.S. GAAP generally does not allow it; it survives only for some construction and oil and gas interests. Figure F16-04 compares the three methods.

16.4  The acquisition: goodwill and noncontrolling interest
When a company gains control of a business, it uses the acquisition method. It measures the subsidiary's identifiable assets and liabilities at fair value, and it measures NCI at fair value too. Goodwill is the price paid plus the fair value of NCI, minus the fair value of the net assets.
Orontes pays $8,000,000 for 80% of Levant, and the other 20% is worth $1,900,000. Levant's net assets are $7,300,000 on its books, but its equipment is worth $1,200,000 more, so the net assets at fair value are $8,500,000. Figure F16-05 shows that goodwill is $1,400,000.
Figure F16-05. Goodwill at acquisition: price plus NCI fair value minus net assets at fair value.

16.5  Eliminating intercompany balances and transactions
A group cannot sell to itself, owe money to itself or earn income from itself. Every intercompany transaction is therefore eliminated in full, whatever the percentage owned: sales and cost of sales, receivables and payables, loans and interest, and dividends.
Unrealized profit on goods still held inside the group is also removed from inventory and income until the goods are sold to outsiders. In a downstream sale the parent earned the profit, so the whole elimination reduces the parent's share. In an upstream sale the subsidiary earned it, so NCI bears its share. 16.6  IFRS differences and what is changing
IFRS 10 uses one control model: an investor controls an entity if it has power over it, exposure to variable returns, and the ability to use its power to affect those returns. There is no separate VIE model, and an investor can have de facto control with less than half of the votes.
IFRS 3 lets a company measure NCI either at fair value or at its share of net assets, which gives partial goodwill: for Levant, $1,200,000 instead of $1,400,000. Under IFRS 11, joint ventures use the equity method and joint operations recognize their own share of assets and liabilities. Figure F16-10 lists the differences.

Chapter summary
Figure F16-11 brings the chapter together.
Figure F16-11. Chapter 16 summary map: the key rule for each section.
Consolidated statements present a parent and its subsidiaries as one economic entity. A company first tests whether an entity is a VIE and consolidates it if it is the primary beneficiary; otherwise it consolidates when it holds more than 50% of the votes. Controlled entities are fully consolidated; significant influence and joint ventures use the equity method; proportionate consolidation is rare. At acquisition, NCI is measured at fair value, and goodwill equals the price plus NCI minus net assets at fair value. All intercompany balances and transactions are eliminated in full, and upstream unrealized profit is shared with NCI.

Written task: model answer
Barada owns 75% of Tadmor, so it has control and adds all of Tadmor's assets, liabilities, revenues and expenses to its own. The other 25% is a noncontrolling interest, shown in equity at its fair value of 190 at the start. Goodwill of 90 is the price plus NCI minus Tadmor's net assets at fair value. Barada must eliminate the intercompany sales, the 40 Tadmor owes it, and unrealized profit of 35 on goods still held in the group. Because part of that profit came from upstream sales by Tadmor, NCI shares it, so NCI's 2028 income is 25 and NCI ends the year at 205.
A good answer: explains control and full consolidation; presents NCI in equity at fair value; computes goodwill with NCI; eliminates intercompany balances and unrealized profit in full; shares upstream profit with NCI.
