15.1  The four responsibility centers
A responsibility center is a unit whose manager answers for a defined set of results. There are four kinds, and they differ only in how much the manager controls. Figure F315-02 defines them with an example from Orontes.
Figure F315-02. The four types of responsibility center.
A cost center answers for costs, and a revenue center for revenue and the selling costs of earning it. A profit center answers for both, and an investment center also for the assets used.
The four form a ladder. Each center carries the responsibilities of the one before it and adds another, so a manager promoted from a cost center to a profit center is answering for revenue for the first time.

EXAM TRAP   Authority first, label second
Calling a unit a profit center does not give its manager control over prices. If the question says that another unit sets the price or the volume, the center is a cost center however the company labels it.
The choice also shapes behavior. A unit made a profit center will look for ways to raise revenue, which is useful when it can really influence customers, and misleading when it cannot.

15.3  The contribution margin report
A segment report in contribution format has three margins, each answering a different question. Figure F315-04 shows them for Beverages.
Figure F315-04. The three margins in a contribution report.
The contribution margin is revenue less all variable costs: $18,000,000 − $10,620,000 = $7,380,000. It shows what each extra case adds, and it is the figure used throughout Chapters 5 and 11.
The controllable margin then deducts the fixed costs the manager can change, such as marketing and supervision. The segment margin deducts the other fixed costs traceable to the division, such as depreciation of its plant. Judge the manager on the first; judge the segment on the second.

15.5  Common costs and how to allocate them
A common cost serves more than one segment and cannot be traced to any of them. Head office costs $1,100,000 a year, the zero-based budget of Chapter 5, and every division is charged a share.
Figure F315-08 shows two methods, using a cold store shared by Beverages and Tahini & Spreads. Stand-alone cost allocation shares the cost in proportion to what each would have paid alone. Incremental cost allocation charges a primary user its stand-alone cost and the other user only the extra cost it adds.
Figure F315-08. Two ways of sharing a common cost.

EXAM TRAP   Never close a segment on the allocated figure
A segment with a positive segment margin is covering its own costs and contributing to common costs. Closing it does not remove those common costs; they are simply shared among fewer segments.

Chapter summary
Figure F315-12 brings the chapter together.
Figure F315-12. Chapter 15 summary map: the key rule for each section.
A responsibility center is a cost, revenue, profit or investment center, according to what its manager controls, and the center should be chosen to match that authority. A contribution report shows the contribution margin, then the controllable margin, then the segment margin, and managers are judged on the controllable margin while segments are judged on the segment margin. Segments may be divisions, product lines, areas or channels. Common costs cannot be traced, and may be allocated by the stand-alone or incremental method; the allocated figure should never decide whether a segment stays open.

Case question: the Oils division quarter
The Oils division has closed its quarter. Its manager, Nadia, sets prices for the division's bottled olive oil, approves its marketing and decides how many staff the division employs, but head office owns the bottling plant and decides when it is replaced.
The quarterly report shows revenue, variable manufacturing costs and variable selling costs, the fixed costs Nadia controls, other fixed costs traceable to the division such as the depreciation of the plant, and a share of head-office costs allocated on revenue. The division sells through supermarkets in Jordan and exports to the Gulf, and both channels use the same bottling plant.
Nadia is unhappy with the last line. The allocation rose this quarter even though her own revenue was unchanged, because the Beverages division grew. She argues that she cannot be judged on a figure that moves with another division's results.
The controller, Rania, must calculate the contribution margin, the controllable margin and the segment margin for the quarter, say which of them should be used to judge Nadia and which to judge the division, and advise the board on whether the allocated charge belongs in either measure.
