# IELTS 2026 — Volume 1 (B1, band 5.0)

**File:** `IELTS_2026_B1_Volume_1.docx` — 468 pages, 436 figures, 4.3 MB
**Units:** 1–10 (the first ten of the sixty-unit main sequence)
**Level:** CEFR B1 · cover band 5.0 · reading speed 100–120 words a minute

This is the entry volume of the eight-book series, and the first one that
counts *downwards*: Volumes 2 to 6 were built before it, so every band,
every density window and every head word in it had to be set against
books that already existed. Nothing here repeats a head word taught
later, and the register sits a measured step below Volume 2.

lighter, because a B1 candidate meets those texts first.

## 2. Technique taught, unit by unit

The series rule holds here as everywhere: **skills and technique, not
hacks, and receptive before productive.** Volume 1 introduces the core
loop and nothing else:

1. **Orient, then load the questions, then read one paragraph at a time.**
   Taught in unit 1 and used in all fifty cycles.
2. **Headings before detail.** Unit 2 teaches matching headings and the
   unequal pair; unit 3 teaches matching information.
3. **Out-of-order questions as a memory aid** — unit 4's keyword trap and
   overlap test.
4. **Underline and keep reading.** Taught from unit 1, drilled throughout.
5. **Recovering from a miss in Listening** — unit 5, the skill that keeps
   one lost answer from costing three.
6. **Maps** — unit 7, with two site plans of the same twelve hectares.
7. **Phase boundaries in a process** — unit 8, with a seven-stage,
   three-phase dairy flow and the intruder exercise.
8. **Matching features** — unit 9, on a mobile-network schematic.
9. **A full timed Reading section under the clock** — unit 10.

Writing is built the same way: unit 1 outlines the six tests, unit 3
fills an outline from a passage, unit 5 turns an outline into an essay,
unit 6 runs passage → outline → essay end to end, unit 7 brainstorms
nine ideas, unit 9 judges a published plan, and unit 10 puts the whole
toolkit together. The eleven essay structures and the ten Task 1
principles are introduced at the pace a B1 writer can carry; 1D, 2D and
hybrid Task 1 organisation all appear. The 2-to-5 speaking rule runs
through every speaking block. No templates anywhere.

## 3. Register: how the level was set

The level is a measurement, not a claim. Two numbers are gated: the
share of running words from the first 1,000 most frequent English words
(`k1`), and the share beyond the first 2,000 plus the academic list
(`beyond`).

| | k1 | beyond | words | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unit academic passages | 70.0% | 13.5% | 8,152 | in window |
| Timed Reading paper | 69.4% | 12.9% | 1,437 | in window |
| **Both together (gated)** | **69.9%** | **13.4%** | **9,589** | **in window** |

Window: k1 69.0–71.5%, beyond 11.5–14.5%.

Getting there took three passes. The first draft of unit 1 measured
76.1% / 7.1% — far too easy. A denser rewrite overshot to 64.5% / 18.6%.
The landing point came from a measured back-substitution pass: 75
heavy→plain rules, applied in order, with the k1 and beyond figures read
off at each step; 45 rules put the volume in the window. The rules were
applied to the academic cycles only, with answer tokens protected, so no
question lost its key.

The two productive bands do **not** scale down with the cover band: a
model answer has to satisfy the Cambridge rubric at any level, so the
writing models in Volume 1 are held to the same standard as those in
Volume 6.

## 5. Three figure defects found by eye, not by a gate

Reading the rendered pages turned up three faults that every gate passed
over. All three were in shared machinery, so all three were latent in
other volumes too; all three are now fixed at source.

1. **`process_flow` — a phase that wraps onto a second row.** The phase
   band was drawn as one rectangle round the whole bounding box, which
   reaches back under the row above and paints over the previous phase's
   label. Unit 8 printed **"On the far"**. Now one band per row, and
   every band is drawn before any label. *Also latent in Volume 2 unit
   18 and Volume 4 units 32, 36 and 38.*
2. **`site_map` — road labels.** They were set 11px clear of the road
   band, which put them under the buildings whenever a road ran close to
   one. Unit 7 clipped "Brackwell Road" in both panels and lost "New
   footpath" entirely. Labels now go on last, on the road itself, each
   on its own paper patch.
3. **`device_schematic` — flow captions.** The caption patch hung above
   its anchor, so a label on a short vertical arrow rode up into the box
   it came from; the offset ignored the patch's own extent, so a label
   could cover the arrow it names; and nothing stopped two labels
   landing on each other. Unit 9 showed all three at once. The patch is
   centred now, pushed clear by its own extent along the normal, and the
   side is chosen by scoring both candidates against the parts, the
   plate edge and the labels already placed.

A fourth fault was caught before the build: units 1 and 2 passed
single-series bar charts as `[(label, value), …]` with no `cats` key,
which crashed the build with `KeyError: 'cats'` *after every content
gate had passed*. Both are rewritten, and the build now renders every
figure in the volume directly before it starts, so that whole class of
error surfaces in seconds rather than six minutes in.
