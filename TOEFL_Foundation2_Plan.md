## 1. The principle

Foundation 1's principle was *nothing is removed, everything is made reachable*.
Foundation 2's is different, because the problem is different:

> **Meet every field before it matters.**

Volume 1 Unit 1 should be the student's **second** encounter with American
history, not the first. So Foundation 2 takes **Volume 1's own ten fields**, in
Volume 1's own order, at two-thirds of the load and on different sub-topics.
When the student opens Volume 1 they already own the field, the word field and
the shape of the argument. All that is new is the density.

## 5. Grammar: the threshold, and one deliberate overlap

**Taught productively** — the A2→B1 structures Volume 1 assumes and never stops to teach:

| # | Grammar |
|---|---|
| 1 | must, have to, mustn't, don't have to |
| 2 | superlatives · the + -est · one of the + plural |
| 3 | infinitive of purpose · verb + to / verb + -ing |
| 4 | too, enough, so, such |
| 5 | time clauses: when, while, before, after, until |
| 6 | quantifiers with of · all/most/some/none of them |
| 7 | adverbs of degree · quite, rather, almost, nearly |
| 8 | the language of cause: because of, due to, so that |
| 9 | the language of contrast: but, however, although |
| 10 | review, and the shape of a complex sentence |

**Introduced receptively, not produced** — the three structures Volume 1 teaches
in its first six units: **present perfect** (V1 U1), **the passive** (V1 U3),
**defining relative clauses** (V1 U6). Foundation 2 lets the student *meet and
understand* them in reading and listening, with a recognition exercise and no
production task, so that Volume 1 consolidates rather than introduces.

This is a **deliberate overlap with Volume 1 and the opposite of Foundation 1's
rule**, which taught nothing Volume 1 teaches. It is the standard
recognition-before-production sequence, and it exists because Volume 1 Unit 1
currently asks a new B1 reader to meet the present perfect and a 265-word
passage in the same hour. A gate will enforce the distinction: those three may
appear in texts, and may not appear in any production exercise.

## 6. Four new strands, all academic

Foundation 1's four strands have done their job by A2. These replace them:

1. **How an Academic Paragraph Works** — the topic sentence, the support, and
   the reference words (*this, these, such, it, the latter*) that hold a text
   together. The highest-leverage academic reading skill there is.
2. **Where Words Come From** — prefixes, suffixes, word families and the Greek
   and Latin roots of academic English (*geo-, bio-, -ology, -tion, -ism*). This
   is what turns *historians* and *geography* from unknown words into guessable
   ones, and it attacks the measured 22 directly.
3. **Taking Notes from a Lecture** — symbols, abbreviations, the two-column
   split, and what not to write down. The real test's talks are 190 words at
   speed and nobody passes them from memory.
4. **The Shape of an Argument** — claim, evidence, concession, conclusion; and
   how to see which sentence is which. Feeds the Reading, the Academic
   Discussion and the Interview at once.

## 10. What it still will not do

Two books of ten units do not make a B1 reader out of an A1 one in a term.
Foundation 1 and Foundation 2 together are **two full CEFR levels**, which is
normally two years of serious study, and no book changes that arithmetic. What
they do is make every step the right size and put nothing in the student's way
that is not the language itself.

And Foundation 2 still does not prepare anyone to sit the test. It prepares
them to open Volume 1 and recognise what they are looking at — which, after the
measurement above, is more than Foundation 1 could honestly claim on its own.
