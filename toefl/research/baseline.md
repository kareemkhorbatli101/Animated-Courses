# Research baseline

Two independent, citable baselines. Structural facts only (unit titles, lesson
counts, standards codes, blueprint figures). No publisher text or artwork is
reproduced here or anywhere in this project.

## A. TOEFL iBT 2026 — authoritative

* ETS, "TOEFL iBT Test Content", https://www.ets.org/toefl/test-takers/ibt/about/content.html
* ETS, "TOEFL iBT Test Blueprint and Specifications Document" (PDF, 2026),
  linked from the page above. Extracted 2026-11-05, 4,062 words.

### Blueprint as published

| Section | Task types (ETS's own names) | Items | Est. time | Scoring |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reading | Complete the Words (B1–C1+, 30) · Read in Daily Life (A1–C1, 5–15) · Read an Academic Passage (B1–C2, 5–15) | 50 | 18–21 min | 1 pt/item |
| Listening | Listen and Choose a Response (A1–B2, 15–19) · Listen to a Conversation (A2–C1, 10) · Listen to an Announcement (A2–C1, 6–10) · Listen to an Academic Talk (A2–C2, 8–16) | 47 | 7 min lower / 11 min upper | 1 pt/item |
| Writing | Build a Sentence (A1–C2, 10) · Write an Email (B1–C2, 1) · Write for an Academic Discussion (B1–C2, 1) | 12 | 23 min | 1 pt; 5 pts; 5 pts |
| Speaking | Listen and Repeat (A1–C2, 7) · Take an Interview (A1–C2, 4) | 11 | 8 min | 5 pts each |

Language-use contexts: Academic, Academic Navigational, Social Interpersonal.

Scoring, verbatim: "Receive four section scores, as well as an overall score, on
the 1 – 6 scale." / "The overall score will be the average of all four sections,
rounded to the nearest half band" / "For a two-year transition period after
January 2026, students will also receive a comparable overall score on the
0 – 120 scale."

Timing, verbatim: "Test time does not include directions. Please allow
approximately two hours to complete the test. As the test adapts, test time and
items may vary."

### Constraints the spec states, that bound content authoring

* Reading text length: easier 15–50 words; complex up to 200 words.
* Reading difficulty: TextEvaluator 800–1100 (medium, ~Gr 10–11), 1100–1360+
  (high, ~Gr 11–12+); Flesch-Kincaid 7.4–10.5 (medium), 10.5–20.0 (high).
* "All texts remain visible throughout the task."
* Reading stimulus inventory, verbatim: "sign, menu, email, social media post"
  or extended academic passages.
* Audio length: brief utterances "no longer than six stressed syllables";
  intermediate 35–100 words; extended monologic up to 250 words.
* Audio: "delivered using AI-generated audio and sometimes human voice actors",
  with "U.S./Canadian, Australian, and British English varieties".
* Listening visual: "Each spoken input is accompanied by a visual
  representation of the speaker(s) to provide contextual cues."
* Speaking visual: "schematic images, maps, forms, or photos of speakers".
* Reading/Listening are machine-scored; Write an Email and Write for an
  Academic Discussion are AI-scored; Speaking items are AI-scored.

### Lesson function — double-corroborated

The correlations do not name the lessons, but the standards assigned to each
slot are stable across all twelve units and identify the function
unambiguously. CCRS and CASAS agree.

| Slot | CCRS signature | CASAS signature | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | R.5.A text features; L.4.B (e.) glossaries and dictionaries | 7.7.2, 7.7.3 learning strategies; 7.4.4 alphabetize; 7.2.6 generate ideas | **Vocabulary** |
| L2 | W.3.A narratives; W.5.B plan, revise, edit; L.2.B spelling/punctuation | 0.2.3 complete a form, 0.2.4; or 7.4.2 take notes, 7.3.1/7.3.2 | **Real-life writing** |
| L3 | L.1.B (b.) function of word classes, (h.) verb tenses, (l.) simple, compound and complex sentences; L.2.B (c.) commas in greetings and closings of letters | 2.3.1/2.3.2/2.3.4 time and calendar; 1.2.1 | **Grammar** |
| L4 | SL.2.B main ideas of a text read aloud; SL.6.B complete sentences; **RF.2.A (g.) isolate and pronounce phonemes** | 0.1.8 clarification; 0.2.1 respond to questions; 2.8.6 | **Everyday conversation** (listening, speaking, pronunciation) |
| L5 | R.1.B key details; R.2.A main topic and retell; **R.7.C interpret information in charts, graphs, diagrams, timelines** | **6.8.1 interpret graphs/charts (every unit)**; 7.5.6 reading strategies | **Reading**, always with a data visual |
| L6 | SL.1.B (d.) explain own ideas in light of the discussion; W.3.B/W.4.B extended writing | 7.3.1, 7.3.2 problem solving; 7.2.7; 4.8.1 teamwork | **Teamwork & Language Review** |

Two details that drive the build:

1. **L5 carries "interpret graphs/charts" in all twelve units.** Every reading
   lesson has a data visual. This is a structural requirement, not decoration.
2. **L3 already teaches "commas in greetings and closings of letters."** The
   correspondence genre is present in the source architecture at Level 3, which
   is what makes the Write an Email augmentation a widening of an existing slot
   rather than a bolt-on.
