## 3. Email — a spine genre, not a gap

**This is the correction that matters most.** The book teaches email writing
across the course, in Lesson 2 of at least seven units:

| Unit | Lesson 2 writing task | Named writing strategy |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | journal entry (narrative) | focusing on the main idea in different paragraphs |
| 2 | **email asking for information to plan a party** | writing a business email |
| 3 | **email describing the rules** / about neighbourhood problems | writing a title for an email |
| 4 | **thank-you email after a job interview** | using colons in a formal business letter |
| 5 | **email to your school board** | the purpose of each paragraph in a multi-paragraph email |
| 10 | letter asking for assistance | using quotation marks |
| 11 | **email to reschedule a meeting** | what to avoid when writing formal messages |
| 12 | **email asking for assistance** / about volunteering | providing examples and details |

Unit 1's Teamwork page also closes with **PROBLEM SOLVING → write a letter**
to a named person, on a scaffolded template.

Unit 2 Lesson 2 (p. 22–23) is a complete email lesson: a realistic mail-client
chrome (window dots, Subject / From / Sent / To), a full model business email
with salutation, purpose sentence, bulleted request, a deadline, closing and a
four-line signature block, a **WRITER'S NOTE** on brevity and bullets, *underline
the answers in the model*, then the same chrome again as a **fill-in template**,
a **NEED HELP?** sidebar of sentence frames, a four-point editing checklist
(reason · specific request · time frame · signature), peer exchange, revision,
and TEST YOURSELF.

The book's email pedagogy is more scaffolded than the TOEFL task it feeds.

## 5. The visual system

This is the quality bar, and it is denser and warmer than a modern editorial
page.

**Colour.** Maroon `#8B2E1F`-ish for lesson banners and table headers; orange
`#E8772E` for section numbers, exercise letters and accents; teal `#00A4B4` for
Vocabulary/Language column headers and SPEAKING NOTE; blue `#0072BC` for IN
OTHER WORDS; mint-green tint for model texts; cream/tan for realia and
*Language connection*; pale blue for charts and readings; peach for alternating
table rows and the footer objective bar.

**Lesson banner.** A maroon chevron with a pointed right end, preceded by a
faceted orange-and-white geometric motif that bleeds off the top-left corner.

**Two voices in the body type.** Model dialogue and sample answers are set in
**blue italic serif**; learner-produced sample answers appear in a **teal
handwriting face**. A learner can always tell the book's voice from a student's.

**Named sidebars**, each with its own colour and a pointer tail:
WRITER'S NOTE (maroon) · READER'S NOTE (orange) · IN OTHER WORDS… (blue) ·
SPEAKING NOTE (teal) · NEED HELP? (teal) · GRAMMAR NOTES (maroon) ·
*Language connection* (tan panel).

**Illustration.** Full-colour cartoon scenes, not photographs, for vocabulary
and teamwork pages — numbered callouts (1–9) for the picture-dictionary set, and
**name tags** on figures for the conversation and review scenes. Photographs
appear only as thumbnails inside realia such as the schools brochure.

**Realia pastiche.** The advice column has a masthead, a columnist's photo, two
columns, line numbers every five lines and a numbered footnote. The email has a
mail-client chrome. The schedule, the notebook page and the brochure each look
like the thing they are.

**Furniture.** Answer boxes are thin rounded blue outlines sized to the answer.
Checkboxes are rounded blue squares. Audio icons carry a track number. A pencil
icon means *underline in the text*; a notepad icon means *write in your
notebook*. Vertical AT WORK / ACADEMIC tabs sit on the page edge. TEST YOURSELF
closes every lesson under an orange arrow; BRING IT TO LIFE closes Lesson 5
under a power icon.
