# Unit 2 · Everything Else Is Allowed
%%SUB A schema says nothing about the fields it does not mention%%
%%GOALS
Use *a*, *an*, *the* and no article
Say what a schema asserts and what it only notes
Say what validation does not prove
%%

A schema is a list of rules about the shape of a document. Most teams read it as a list of the
fields that are allowed. It is not that, and the difference has let unexpected fields through
thousands of interfaces without anybody noticing. This unit is about one default, one draft that
never became a standard, and the difference between a rule that fails and a rule that merely makes
a note.

### 2 · Read

> **Reader's Tip** — Find the defaults. A specification's defaults are what your system does on the
> days nobody is thinking about it, which is most days.

%%FIG b02-f5 | A one-page internal note: a heading, a line giving the specification and its date, a four-row table of keyword groups with one line each, a boxed paragraph on the default for unmentioned fields, a short list of what validation does not prove, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

> ## Meridian · architecture note 14
> *Platform team · schemas and validation · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The specification.** The schema specification we use is the one dated **2020-12**, published in
> **December 2020** and titled *JSON Schema: A Media Type for Describing JSON Documents*. Our
> interface description format aligns with exactly this version.
>
> **It is a draft.** This matters and is often left out. The document is an expired **Internet
> draft**. It is **not an RFC** and not an International Standard. It is nevertheless the version
> that tools implement and that our description format points at, so it functions as a standard
> without being one. **Write the date, not the word *standard*.**
>
> **The keywords, in groups.**
>
> | Group | Examples | What it does |
> |---|---|---|
> | **identity** | the dialect line, an identifier, a reference | says which rules apply and where blocks live |
> | **applicators** | properties, items, the subschema keywords | says **where** other rules apply |
> | **assertions** | type, required, minimum, pattern | **can fail** a record |
> | **annotations** | title, description, default, examples | attach information; **fail nothing** |
>
> **The default that surprises everybody.**
>
> > A schema is a set of **constraints**. A field the schema does not mention is **not
> > constrained**, so it is **allowed**. Listing four properties does not forbid a fifth. If you
> > want the fifth forbidden, there is a keyword for that and **you have to write it**.
> >
> > The same logic applies to **required**: listing a property does **not** make it required. The
> > required keyword is separate, it takes a list of names, and a property can be required without
> > appearing in the properties list at all.
> >
> > **Nothing in a schema is implied.** That is the single most useful sentence in this unit.
>
> **Format is a note, not a rule — by default.** There is a keyword for saying that a string is a
> date, or an electronic mail address, or a duration. In this version, **it is an annotation by
> default**: an implementation may check it, and is not obliged to. There is a separate vocabulary
> that makes it an assertion. **So a string marked as a date may be anything at all**, unless you
> know which vocabulary your validator is using.
>
> **What validation does not prove.**
>
> - **It does not prove the data is correct.** A record can be perfectly shaped and completely
>   wrong: a currency code that is three letters and not a currency, a date that is a date and in
>   the wrong century.
> - **It does not prove the sender meant what you think.** Shape is not meaning.
> - **It does not prove anything about fields you did not mention.** By default, they passed.
> - **It does not version anything.** Two schemas, a year apart, are two documents; the
>   specification has nothing to say about which is newer or whether the change was safe.
>
> **What we do, in one line.** Extras are forbidden at the edge of a payment and allowed on an event
> we only read, the choice is written down with its reason, and no schema in this company relies on
> something being implied.
>
> *Source: JSON Schema: A Media Type for Describing JSON Documents, the 2020-12 draft published in
> December 2020, and its companion validation document: the separation of assertion and annotation
> keywords; the rule that properties not described by a schema are unconstrained and therefore
> permitted; the independence of the required keyword from the properties keyword; and the treatment
> of format as an annotation by default, with assertion behavior available through a separate
> vocabulary. The documents are expired Internet drafts and are not standards-track specifications.*

attaches information and fails nothing · **a vocabulary** — a named group of keywords a dialect
includes</sub>

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

*We validate* is one of the most reassuring sentences in software, and it is almost always heard as
a bigger claim than it is. It means the shape was checked against the rules somebody wrote. It does
not mean the data is right, that nothing unexpected arrived, or that anybody looked at the rules
recently. The sentence survives because it is true, which makes it very hard to argue with — and
because nobody is lying, nobody feels responsible for the gap between what was said and what was
heard. This pattern is worth recognizing beyond schemas. *It is encrypted.* *It is backed up.* *It
is tested.* All true, all much narrower than they sound. The useful habit is to answer the implied
question rather than the literal one: not *do we validate* but *what gets through*.

**Discuss:** Which true sentence does your team say that a customer hears as a bigger promise?

> ### Field note · the shipment schema
> *Platform team · interface document, section 2.3 · 17 February*
>
> **Dialect.** The schema declares the **2020-12** dialect. That document is an **expired draft**,
> not a standard; we name the date rather than calling it one.
>
> **Properties.** Six are described. **Three are required**, and the required list is a **separate
> keyword** — describing a property does not make it required.
>
> **Extra fields.**
>
> | Interface | Unmentioned fields | Why |
> |---|---|---|
> | payment requests | **forbidden**, since 14 March | a field we neither read nor describe must not reach storage |
> | shipment events we receive | **accepted and dropped** | a sender may add their own fields; we will not break them, and we will not store them |
> | internal queue messages | accepted and kept | one team owns both ends |
>
> **Keywords that are only notes.** The **format** keyword on three string fields is an
> **annotation** in this dialect. **Our validator does not assert it.** A field annotated as a date
> may hold any string, and the service checks the value.
>
> **What the service checks, not the schema.** That a currency code is a current code. That a date is
> inside the contract period. That an identifier belongs to the customer who sent it. **Shape is
> here; meaning is there.**
>
> **Not in this note.** **What we do with a record that fails.** The error format is section 11.
