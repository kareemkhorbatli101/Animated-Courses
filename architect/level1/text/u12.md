> ## Meridian · architecture note 12
> *Platform team · architecture descriptions · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The standard. ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010:2022**, *Software, systems and enterprise — Architecture
> description*, is the **second edition** of this standard. It replaced the **2011** edition, which
> itself grew out of a document from **2000**. It is about **the description** of an architecture,
> not about what makes an architecture good.
>
> **Four ideas.**
>
> | Idea | In one line |
> |---|---|
> | **stakeholder** | a person or group with an interest in the system |
> | **concern** | something a stakeholder needs the description to address |
> | **architecture viewpoint** | a way of looking at a system, chosen to address concerns |
> | **architecture view** | what you get when a viewpoint is applied to **your** system |
>
> **The rule that changes documents.**
>
> > An architecture description **identifies the stakeholders and their concerns**, and **each
> > concern is framed by at least one architecture viewpoint**. Nothing in the description exists
> > without a reader and a question.
> >
> > Read that backwards and it becomes a test you can apply to any document: for every section, who
> > asked for it? For every reader, which section answers them? **A section with no reader and a
> > reader with no section are the two commonest faults in architecture documents, and this rule
> > finds both.**
>
> **Decisions and reasons.** The standard provides for recording **architecture decisions** together
> with their **rationale** — the reasons, the alternatives considered, and what was accepted as a
> consequence. **A recorded reason is the only part of a decision that survives the team.**
>
> **Correspondences.** Two views of one system will describe the same things in different ways. The
> standard provides for recording **correspondences** between them, and **rules** about those links,
> so that a reader can tell whether two pictures agree.
>
> **What the standard does not do.**
>
> - **It does not give you a set of viewpoints.** You choose or define your own.
> - **It does not prescribe a notation, a tool or a template.** Boxes, text and tables are all
>   acceptable.
> - **It does not define a process.** When you write the description, and how often, is yours.
> - **It does not say whether your architecture is good.** It describes how to describe. A clear
>   description of a poor design is a clear description.
>
> **What we do, in one line.** Name the readers, write each concern as a question, and put the
> one-page summary at the front.
>
> *Source: ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010:2022, Software, systems and enterprise — Architecture description,
> second edition, replacing the 2011 edition: the concepts of stakeholder, concern, architecture
> viewpoint and architecture view; the requirement that an architecture description identify
> stakeholders and their concerns and that each concern be framed by at least one architecture
> viewpoint; and the provisions for recording architecture decisions with their rationale and for
> correspondences and correspondence rules between views. The standard does not prescribe a set of
> viewpoints, a notation, a tool, a template or a development process, and it makes no judgment about
> the quality of an architecture.*

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD KS1-12-06%%

> **Adeel:** *This document is for four readers. Each of them arrives with one question, and the

> **Mei:** Three tenses and a possessive in two sentences, and nothing wasted. A year ago you would

> **Adeel:** That sentence says nothing.
> **Mei:** It says nothing and it is grammatically perfect, which is why it survives in so many

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

The reader who cannot ask a question is the one every document forgets: the person who joins in two
years, when everybody who made the decisions has moved on. Teams write for the colleague across the
desk, who can simply ask, and so the hardest-won knowledge — why the obvious approach was rejected,
what was tried and failed, which customer made the rule necessary — is the part least likely to be
written down. Across a whole industry this is enormously expensive, and it is paid in rediscovery:
the same argument held for the third time, the same mistake made because the record of the first one
is gone. A reason written in one sentence beside a decision is the cheapest thing in this book and
the only one that compounds.

**Discuss:** Which decision in your own system has no recorded reason, and who would need it most?
