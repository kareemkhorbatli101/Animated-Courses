# Starting Point · What a Format Is For
%%SUB Four questions to ask before you store, send or print anything%%
%%GOALS
Review the English you already have
Say what a format protects
Ask the four questions before you agree a field
%%

A format is an agreement about what a string of characters means. It exists so that two systems
built in different places, in different years, by people who have never spoken, can read the same
field and understand the same thing. Twelve units of this book are about twelve such agreements, and
about the trap in each one.

### 1 · What you already have

%%FIG a00-f1 | A single review sheet with six boxes. Each box has a heading — the present simple, there is and there are, countable and uncountable, comparatives, modals, and the passive — and one short example sentence from a software team's working day.%%

**1A  Say one sentence from each box to your partner.** Then say which box your own English uses
least.

### 2 · What a format protects

%%FIG a00-f2 | Three numbered boxes in a column. Each one has a format named in bold, a line reading what it produces, and a line reading what it prevents, with an arrow at the foot pointing to a box reading two systems that never met, agreeing.%%

| | What it produces | What it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| **An agreed date format** | one way to write a day | a report for the wrong month |
| **An agreed character encoding** | one way to store a name | a customer's name printed as boxes |
| **An agreed code list** | one way to name a country | two systems counting the same place twice |

**2A  Listen.** %%AUD KS1-00-01%%

**2B  Which of the three would cause the most trouble in your work?** *Talk with a partner, out
loud.*

### 3 · Four sentences an architect says every week

%%FIG a00-f3 | A card taped inside a laptop lid, printed with four questions in bold, each with one line of smaller type under it explaining when to ask it.%%

> **1  Which document, and which edition?**
> *When somebody says "the standard says".*
>
> **2  Is that a time zone or an offset?**
> *Before any field that stores a time.*
>
> **3  How many bytes, and in which encoding?**
> *Before any field with a length limit.*
>
> **4  Is that a MUST or a SHOULD?**
> *Before you agree to anything in a specification.*

**3A  Listen and repeat.** %%AUD KS1-00-02%%

**3B  Cover the explanations.** Your partner reads a situation; you say which question fits. Take
turns.

### 4 · The three things a field needs

%%FIG a00-f4 | Three numbered boxes in a column, each with a heading in bold and two short lines under it, and an arrow at the foot pointing to a fourth box reading only now may you store it.%%

> **1  A name and a type**
> What the field is called, and what kind of value it holds.
>
> **2  A format, with a document**
> Not "a date" but "a date in the format of the standard named here".
>
> **3  A limit, with a unit**
> Not "short" but "at most 64 characters" — and say characters or bytes.

**4A  Listen.** %%AUD KS1-00-03%%

**4B  Think about it.** *Talk with a partner.*

1. Which of the three is missing most often in your work?
2. What happens when the limit has no unit?
3. What would you add to the three?
