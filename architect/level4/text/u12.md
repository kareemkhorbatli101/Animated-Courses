There is a standard devoted to the architecture processes themselves — not the architecture, the
processes. It names six. This unit reads them, reviews the language of the whole book, and ends where
the book began: with a record somebody will read when everyone who wrote it has left.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Of the six architecture processes, the one most often missing is the least rewarding. Conceptualizing
and elaborating an architecture is the work architects trained for and enjoy; governance and
management are imposed from outside and therefore happen; evaluation at least produces a meeting. But
equipping the teams who must use the architecture — a working example, a template, a checklist, a
person to ask — is unglamorous, has no natural owner and produces nothing anybody presents. Its
absence does not look like a failure, either. It looks like several teams being individually slow,
which is read as a problem with the teams. The same shape explains the decision record, which is why
this book spent eleven units on language for one page: a record has no deadline, no reviewer and no
reward, and its only beneficiary is a stranger who arrives after you have gone. Both of them are
work you do for somebody you will never meet, which is a hard thing to ask of anybody and the
difference between an architecture and a picture of one.

**Discuss:** What have you written that was only ever useful to somebody who arrived later?

> **Decision.** We will build one **working example** in a running repository, one **template**, one
> **review checklist**, and we will name a **contact** in the platform team. One week of one person's
> time. **Enablement will be added to our declared process set with that person as its owner**,
> because a process without an owner is the situation we are in now.
>
> **Alternatives considered.**
>
> | Alternative | Why not |
> |---|---|
> | a longer architecture document | We wrote one in 2023. It is accurate and nobody has opened it. |
> | a training session for the four teams | **It does not survive the people in the room**, and two of the four teams have turned over since. |
> | leaving it | **If we had spent this week last year, three of the four adapters would not exist.** That sentence is the whole argument. |
>
> **Consequences.**
>
> - Somebody must maintain the example. **It will be wrong within a year unless they do**, and a wrong
>   example is **worse than none**, because people trust an example more than a document.
> - The contact will be interrupted. **That is the point**, and it should be in their objectives rather
>   than tolerated.
> - **This commits us to a recurring cost for something that produces nothing anybody presents.** We
>   are stating that plainly so the next person to propose cutting it has to argue with it.
>
> **Who will read this, and when.** The next engineer who finds four adapters and assumes they are
> missing something — **which is exactly what happened in January, twice**. Probably within two years,
> probably in their first month, probably without anybody to ask.
>
> **Not in this record.** Which of the four adapters we keep. **That is a separate decision and a
> separate record, and conflating the two is how this one would fail to get approved.**
>
> *A decision not written down is a decision that will be taken again, differently, by somebody who
> does not know it was taken.*

> **Writer's Tip** — The strongest record contains at least one sentence against its own proposal.
> A record with only advantages reads like a sales document and is treated as one.
