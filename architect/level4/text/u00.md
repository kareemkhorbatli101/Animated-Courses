# Starting Point · What a Decision Record Is For
%%SUB Four questions a record answers, and the reader it is written for%%

The person who will need your decision record has not joined the company yet. That single fact
decides everything about how it should be written.

### 1 · Six structures you already have

%%FIG d00-f1 | A single review sheet with six boxes. Each box has a heading — the first conditional, concession, correlation and cause, probability, exception, and parallel lists — and one short example sentence from an architecture review.%%

**1A  Read the six examples aloud with a partner. Which two do you use most?**

| | |
|---|---|
| **The first conditional** | **If** we split the service, the deploy **will** get simpler. |
| **Concession** | It is faster, **although** the value may be stale. |
| **Correlation and cause** | Small changes **are associated with** faster recovery. |
| **Probability** | Exploitation is **likely** within a month. |
| **Exception** | All controls apply **unless** excluded in writing. |
| **Parallel lists** | accurate, complete, consistent and current |

### 2 · Four questions a record answers

%%FIG d00-f2 | Four numbered boxes in a column. Each one has a question in bold, a line reading who asks it, and a line reading what happens without the answer, with an arrow at the foot pointing to a box reading a reader who was not in the room.%%

**2A  Read. Then say the four questions to your partner in your own words.**

| | Who asks it | Without the answer |
|---|---|---|
| **1 What had we already committed to?** | the engineer who arrives later | the decision looks arbitrary |
| **2 What else did we consider?** | the person proposing to change it | the same options are re-explored |
| **3 What becomes harder because of this?** | whoever hits that difficulty | the cost looks like somebody's mistake |
| **4 Who will have to live with it?** | you, when you write it | the record is written for the wrong reader |

The fourth question is the one that changes the writing. A record written for your colleague across
the desk is a note. A record written for a stranger in four years is a decision record.

**2B  Listen.** %%AUD KS4-00-02%%

### 3 · The four questions of this book

%%FIG d00-f3 | A card taped inside a laptop lid, printed with four questions in bold, each with one line of smaller type under it explaining when to ask it.%%

**3A  Read the four questions. Which one would have helped you last month?**

> **1  What had we already committed to?**
> *Before a decision is described as free.*
>
> **2  What else did we consider, and why not?**
> *Before somebody spends a month rediscovering it.*
>
> **3  What becomes harder because of this?**
> *Before the consequences are somebody else's surprise.*
>
> **4  Who will read this, and when?**
> *Before the first sentence is written.*

**3B  Ask your partner all four. Answer about a decision you were part of.**

### 4 · Two kinds of record

%%FIG d00-f4 | Two columns side by side. The left column is headed written for now and lists three lines; the right column is headed written for later and lists three different lines; an arrow at the foot points to a box reading the same decision, two documents.%%

**4A  Read the two columns. Then discuss: which kind does your team write?**

| Written for now | Written for later |
|---|---|
| names people by first name only | names roles as well as people |
| assumes today's constraints are known | states the constraints that applied |
| records what was chosen | records what was rejected, and why |

Nothing in the left column is wrong. It is simply addressed to somebody who already knows what you
know — and that person will not be reading it.

**4B  Listen and read.** %%AUD KS4-00-03%%

**4C  One question to finish.** What decision in your system can nobody explain?
