# Unit 11 · Four Things the License Asks
%%SUB A permissive license, four conditions, and a short label that is not the license%%
%%GOALS
Use conditions and provisos accurately
Name the four conditions on redistribution
Explain what a short license identifier is and is not

One of the most widely used open source licenses is permissive: it lets you use, modify and
redistribute the work, including in a product you sell. In exchange it asks four things, and only
when you distribute. This unit reads those four conditions and the short labels used to record
them. **Nothing in this unit is legal advice.**

> ## Meridian · architecture note 47
> *Platform team · license conditions · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The license. The Apache License, Version 2.0**, dated **January 2004**. It is **permissive**: it
> grants use, reproduction and distribution, in source or object form, with or without modification,
> including in something you sell.
>
> **The four conditions on redistribution.** Section 4 permits distribution **provided that** you meet
> four conditions. In plain terms:
>
> 1. **Give recipients a copy of the license.**
> 2. **Mark any file you changed** with a prominent notice saying you changed it.
> 3. **Retain the notices that were there** — copyright, patent, trademark and attribution — in the
>    source form of what you distribute, **except** those that do not pertain to any part of the
>    derivative work.
> 4. **If the work includes a NOTICE file**, carry a readable copy of the attribution notices in it
>    into what you distribute.
>
> **When they apply.**
>
> > The four conditions are conditions **on distribution**. They are what Section 4 asks of somebody
> > who reproduces and distributes the work or a derivative work. **A project you run only inside your
> > own company may not trigger them at all** — and whether what you do counts as distribution is a
> > legal question, not an engineering one. **This note does not answer it.**
>
> **What the license does not ask.** It does **not** require you to publish your own source. It does
> **not** require a payment. It does **not** prohibit commercial use. **You may add your own copyright
> statement and your own license terms for your modifications, so long as your use and distribution of
> the work itself still complies with the four conditions.**
>
> **Short identifiers.** A short label such as **Apache-2.0** is a standard identifier from a published
> license list, written in a file header so a tool can read it. Three things to know:
>
> - **A label is not a license.** It points at a license text; it does not contain one, and shipping
>   the label is not shipping the license.
> - **Labels change.** Some identifiers are **deprecated** — an older label for one family was
>   replaced by two clearer ones distinguishing *this version only* from *this version or later*,
>   because the original was ambiguous.
> - **A label is not a determination.** It records what somebody believed when they wrote it. **An
>   identifier in a header is evidence of an intention, not proof of a legal position.**
>
> **What we actually do.** We ship a license file, we carry the notice file forward, we mark changed
> files, and we keep a list of the identifiers we depend on. **We have never had that list reviewed by
> a lawyer, which is the gap in this note.**
>
> **Not legal advice.** **Nothing in this note is legal advice.** It is a reading of a license text by
> engineers, for the purpose of telling our legal colleagues precisely what we ship.
>
> *Source: the Apache License, Version 2.0, January 2004, Section 4 (Redistribution), which permits
> reproduction and distribution provided that four conditions are met: a copy of the license to
> recipients; prominent notices on modified files; retention of copyright, patent, trademark and
> attribution notices from the source form, excluding those not pertaining to the derivative work; and
> a readable copy of the attribution notices in any NOTICE file. Short license identifiers come from a
> published license list and are labels, not license texts, not determinations, and in some cases
> deprecated in favor of clearer ones. The license text does not define what counts as distribution in
> any given arrangement, and nothing here is legal advice.*

### 1 · Conditions and provisos

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD KS4-11-06%%

> **Chiamaka:** Your note says *you may distribute provided that you include the license*. Why not
> just *you must include the license*?
> **Grace:** Because the two sentences do different work. **A proviso attaches the condition to a
> specific permission.** *Provided that* says: here is something you are allowed to do, and here is
> what it costs. A bare *must* floats free of the permission, and then somebody applies it to a
> situation where no permission was needed.
> **Chiamaka:** Which is what happened to us.
> **Grace:** Exactly. Somebody read *you must include a copy of the license* and asked whether we had
> to put a license file on an internal tool that we never ship. **The condition is on distributing. If
> we are not distributing, the question does not arise.** The proviso carries that with it; the bare
> *must* loses it.
> **Chiamaka:** And *so long as*?
> **Grace:** For a condition that has to keep holding, not one you satisfy once. You may add your own
> terms for your own changes **so long as** the work itself still complies. That is a continuing
> state, not a checkbox.
> **Chiamaka:** *Unless* and *in which case*?
> **Grace:** *Unless* names the exception inside the condition: retain the notices **unless** they do
> not pertain to the derivative work. *In which case* gives the consequence of an exception you have
> just raised: we may not be distributing, **in which case** none of this is triggered.
> **Chiamaka:** You use that one a lot.
> **Grace:** Because I am an engineer reading a legal text, and **the honest shape of most of my
> sentences is a condition with an exception I am not qualified to resolve**. *In which case* is how
> you hand that over cleanly instead of guessing.

*Why does Grace prefer a proviso to a bare* must*?*

**1B  Underline six conditional structures in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **provided that** | You may redistribute **provided that** the four conditions are met. |
| **on condition that** | They agreed **on condition that** the notice file is carried forward. |
| **so long as** | Add your own terms **so long as** the work still complies. |
| **unless** | Retain all notices **unless** they do not pertain to the work. |
| **in which case** | This may not be distribution, **in which case** nothing is triggered. |
| **subject to** | The grant is **subject to** the terms of this license. |
| **except where** | All files, **except where** we did not change them. |

> **Grammar Note** — *Provided that* and *on condition that* are formal and attach to a permission.
> *So long as* suits a condition that must keep holding. *Unless* names an exception; *in which case*
> gives its consequence.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

A permissive license is widely believed to be stricter than it is. Engineers routinely think it
requires them to publish their own source, forbids commercial use, or applies to software they never
ship — three beliefs the text contradicts in a page. The cause is not carelessness: almost nobody
reads a license, the summaries circulate second-hand, and the one person who could settle it is a
lawyer who is expensive to ask badly. So the question arrives in the form *are we allowed to use
this?*, which cannot be answered without a list that only the engineer can produce, and three weeks
later the reply is a request for that list. The whole delay is a formatting problem. An engineer who
brings the list, names which conditions are met, and asks one question converts weeks into an hour —
and still has not given legal advice, which is the part worth being careful about.

**Discuss:** How are license questions asked in your company, and how long do they take?

> license identifier recorded for each. **Thirty-four of the forty-one carry the same permissive
> license**, the Apache License, Version 2.0 of January 2004.
>
> **The four conditions it places on redistribution.** Section 4 permits reproduction and distribution
> **provided that**: a copy of the license is given to recipients; modified files carry prominent
> notices stating that we changed them; copyright, patent, trademark and attribution notices from the
> source form are retained, **unless** they do not pertain to the derivative work; and, where the work
> includes a notice file, its attribution notices are carried into what we distribute.
>
> | Condition | State today |
> |---|---|
> | license copy to recipients | **11 of 41** |
> | changed files marked | **none** |
> | notices retained | met, as far as we can tell |
> | notice file carried | **4 of 41** |
>
> **What we are doing regardless.** Three of the four can be met by a build step, and we are building
> it this month, **so long as** nothing in your answer makes it pointless. **We are doing this whether
> or not the conditions are triggered, because it costs us a day.**
>
> **The one question.** Section 4 applies to redistribution. We host the software and customers reach
> it over a network; we do not hand them a copy. **Does that arrangement count as distribution, in
> which case all four conditions apply to all forty-one components?** We are not able to answer that,
> and we have not tried to.
>
> **Not in this brief.** **The seven components not carrying the permissive license.** These have four
> different licenses between them, one of which we do not understand, and they need a separate page.
>
> *Nothing in this brief is legal advice. It is a reading of a license text by engineers, written so
> that a lawyer can answer one question instead of forty-one.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Supply the facts, name the conditions, state what you meet, and ask one question.
> The sentence *we are not able to answer that, and we have not tried to* is what keeps the brief
> honest.

### 3 · Write

Write **140 to 170 words**: a brief for legal.

**Say why the page exists. Say what you ship, with a number. Name the conditions, using *provided
