## How to use this book

Book 1 was about fields. Book 2 was about interfaces. Book 3 was about measurement. This book is
about **decisions** — how they are recorded, why the record outlives everybody who was in the room,
and what happens to a company that has made a thousand of them and written down none.

**Twelve units**, twenty pages each, with the same six lessons in the same order every time.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Sixteen words, a second set of six, and a team research task |
| **2 · Reading** | A real standard, paper, license or published method, with its date and its limits |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures, with the mistake each one causes |
| **4 · At Work** | Clear English, a long dialogue, a culture check and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A model, then your own |
| **Team Up & Review** | A picture, a team project, a survey, and one problem with no easy answer |

**The audio.** 🔊 marks a track. 195 tracks, numbered `KS4-unit-track`.

## What is different about this book

**The sources are older and stranger.** A blog post from 2011 that changed how a generation records
decisions. A four-page article in a trade magazine from 1968 whose one sentence explains more
reorganizations than any management book. A list of eight assumptions with a messy attribution. Two
papers from the 1980s, one of which proves that something you rely on every day cannot be
guaranteed. A metaphor from 1992 that is now used to mean almost the opposite of what its author
said. And four International Standards that describe processes without telling you to follow any of
them.

**Every unit is about a record.** Not about being right — about being legible in four years, to
somebody who was not there, when the person who decided has left and the reason has become
invisible. That is the central skill of this level and it is almost entirely a writing skill.

**The grammar is the grammar of the record.** The past perfect, for what had already been true when
you decided. Cause and result. Imperatives, for a method. Levels of detail, for a diagram.
Nominalization — and how to undo it. The second conditional, for assumptions. The third conditional,
for what cannot be undone. The language of impossibility. Metaphor against literal statement.
Reference and cohesion, which is what makes a long document readable. Conditions and provisos. And
finally, the decision record itself.

**Four sentences you will write until they are automatic.** *What had we already committed to?* *What
else did we consider, and why not?* *What becomes harder because of this?* And *who will have to live
with it, and when will they read this?*

## Nothing here is official

This is an independent teaching draft. It is **not published or endorsed by any standards body**,
and it carries no official status. **Nothing in it is legal advice**: Unit 11 discusses the text of a
published license and is not a substitute for a lawyer. Every person, team, company, system,
incident and decision about Meridian in it was written for this book. The standards, papers,
licenses and published methods quoted in the reading texts are real, and each text names the
document, its date or edition, and the limits its own authors state.
