# Unit 10 · Quality, Extended
%%SUB A quality model with new words in it, and still not one number%%
%%GOALS

The product quality model you met in an earlier book has an extension for systems that learn. It
adds words the old model had no place for, and like its parent it contains no measures, no targets
and no thresholds. The new words are useful for one specific reason: they let two people compare two
systems without arguing about what *better* means.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Long technical arguments are rarely about facts; they are usually two correct statements colliding
inside one adjective. Two engineers each measure something real, each conclude that their option is
*better*, and each hear the other making a false claim — and because the adjective hides which
characteristic is being discussed, more measurement does not help. What helps is somebody else's
list of names. Using a published vocabulary feels bureaucratic and has two specific advantages: both
parties accept names they did not write, and the list contains rows nobody in the room would have
drawn. A home-made comparison sheet at most companies has accuracy, latency and cost, because those
are the things the company already measures; a published quality model has rows for whether a user
can stop the system and whether a person can correct a stored decision, and those rows come back
empty, which is the most useful result the exercise produces.

**Discuss:** What row would be empty on your company's comparison sheet if somebody added it?

> rows, taken from the AI extension of the SQuaRE quality model, **ISO/IEC 25059:2023** — which
> supplies the names and **no measures, no targets and no thresholds**.
>
> | Characteristic | Model A | Model B |
> |---|---|---|
> | accuracy, main case | **8.4 min** mean error | **9.1 min — less accurate by 0.7 min** |
> | robustness, malformed input | returns a value, **silently wrong** | **rejects, recovers, no restart** |
> | adaptability, new region | retrained in **6 weeks** | retrained in **2 weeks** |
> | user controllability | **no user-facing stop** | stop reaches the queue in **under 1 s** |
> | intervenability | **no route** to correct a stored outcome | override, **logged** |
> | transparency | one internal page, undated | **published page, dated** |
>
> **One honest floor.** On adaptability we have **one retraining data point each**. **B is at least as
> adaptable as A**; we are not claiming more than that, and a second data point would change the
> sentence.
>
> **The decision, and what we are accepting.** **We choose B and accept being less accurate by 0.7
> minutes of mean error.** For a planning horizon of two hours that is immaterial; **if the horizon
> changes to twenty minutes, this decision should be revisited and this sentence is why.**
>
> **Who chose the priority.** The standard names the characteristics and **says nothing about which
> matter**. **The priority order — robustness, then intervenability, then accuracy — was chosen by
> the machine-learning group in April**, and it is a judgment, not a finding.
>
> **What we found by accident.** **Nobody had ever assessed intervenability for either model**,
> because until this list gave us the word there was no row to leave empty. **For A, the answer is
> that there is no route at all** — the same finding as governance note 20, reached from a completely
> different direction.
>
> **Not on this page.** **The retraining cost of B in engineer-weeks.** **That** belongs with the
> budget and is not a quality characteristic.
>
> *The word "better" does not appear on this page.*

> **Writer's Tip** — A comparison needs the characteristic, the direction and the size in one
> sentence. If you cannot give the size, say *no less than* and say what evidence you have.
