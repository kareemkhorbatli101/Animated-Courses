A companion document to the voluntary framework in Unit 1 names twelve risks that are either new
with generative systems or made worse by them. Like its parent it requires nothing. This unit reads
it, reviews the language of the whole book, and ends with the page that all twelve units have been
preparing: one sheet that somebody who is not you will be asked to defend in public.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Every document in this book had the same shape: a date, a status, a list of things it names, and a
sentence somewhere saying what it cannot do. None of the twelve can require anything of a company on
its own — the voluntary ones by design, the regulation only on dates and only for some systems, the
certifiable one only within a scope you declare, the research proposals not at all. That sounds like
a book about weak documents, and the opposite is true: what each one supplies is rows you would not
have drawn, names for things your own vocabulary had merged, and questions that cannot be answered,
which is where everything useful in twelve units turned out to live. The language follows from the
same place. A page written by somebody who will be in the room can assert; a page written for
somebody who will not be there has to carry the attribution, the conditions, the limits and the
unknowns, because the reader cannot ask. That is the whole reason the honest version is longer, and
the only part that was ever difficult is the name at the foot.

**Discuss:** Whose name is at the foot of your company's most important page?
