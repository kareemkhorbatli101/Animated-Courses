### 1 · Future time and commencement

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD KS5-02-06%%

> **Hana:** I have found the sentence that caused all of it. In an email from last year somebody
> wrote *the regulation is in force, so we are now non-compliant*.
> **Nour:** Both halves are wrong in different ways.
> **Hana:** The first half is right and useless. It entered into force in August 2024. The second
> half assumes that force and application are the same thing, and they are not. **A provision in
> force but not yet applicable requires nothing of you.**
> **Nour:** How do you keep them apart in writing?
> **Hana:** By never using a bare date. **Every date gets a verb: *entered into force on*, *applies
> from*, *will apply from*, *applicable as of*.** A date on its own in a compliance email is an
> invitation for the reader to supply their own verb, and they will supply the frightening one.
> **Nour:** And when the date is in the future?
> **Hana:** *Will apply from* if you are talking about the obligation, which is fine. But I prefer
> *applies from*, in the present, because the text already says so. **The obligation's start date is
> a present fact about a future event, and *applies from August 2026* is both shorter and more
> accurate than *will become applicable*.**
> **Nour:** What about our own deadlines?
> **Hana:** Different verbs entirely, and this is where people blur the two. The law says *applies
> from*. We say *no later than*, *by*, or *with effect from*, because those are our commitments and
> we chose them. **If a document uses the same verb for a legal date and an internal plan, the
> reader cannot tell which dates somebody can be fined over.**
> **Nour:** That is a real distinction.
> **Hana:** It is the whole of this unit. **Their dates are facts about a text. Our dates are
> promises. Both are in the same table, and they need different verbs or that table is dangerous.**

*Why does Hana insist that every date carries a verb?*

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Non-lawyers reading a legal text almost always make the same mistake, and it is a grammatical one.
They find a date, they cannot see a verb they recognize, and they attach the present tense: the law
exists, therefore it applies to me, therefore I am late. Nobody does this out of panic; they do it
because *in force* sounds like *in effect* and in ordinary English those are the same thing. In a
legal text they are not, and the distance between them can be two years. The expensive version of
this mistake is not missing a deadline. It is spending four months remediating obligations that have
not arrived while leaving undone the one-week task that would tell you which obligations will ever
apply to you — because remediation feels like progress and classification feels like exposure. The
cure is mechanical and it costs nothing: never write a date without the verb that governs it, and
never put your own deadlines in the same column as somebody else's.

>

> sentence.**
>
> **The distinction that caused it.** The regulation **entered into force on 1 August 2024**. Its
> provisions become **applicable** on four later dates. **A provision in force but not yet
> applicable requires nothing of us.**
>
> | Applicable from | What applies | Does it reach us? |
> |---|---|---|
> | 2 Feb 2025 | prohibited practices | **yes — checked, we do none of them** |
> | 2 Aug 2025 | general-purpose models, governance, most penalties | **unknown until classification** |
> | 2 Aug 2026 | the general body, including most high-risk duties | **unknown until classification** |
> | 2 Aug 2027 | high-risk safety components of regulated products | probably not; **not confirmed** |
>
> **What we have not done.** **We operate eleven systems and have classified none of them.** Until
> that exists, three of the four rows above cannot be answered, and no date in them binds us in any
> way we can describe. **This has been true for eighteen months.**
>
> **Our own dates, which are promises and not law.**
>
> - Eleven system descriptions with legal **no later than 30 April**.
> - Classification returned by legal, on our facts, **by mid-May**.
> - Register restructured against whichever rows apply, **with effect from 1 July**.
>
> **Why this is a week of work and not a program.** Our lawyer needs four facts per system: what it
> does, who it affects, whether we built it or bought it, and whether we place it on the market.
> **A week of ours and an hour of hers.** That is the entire blocking task, and it has been blocked

>
