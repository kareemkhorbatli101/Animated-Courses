# Unit 11 · Measured on What, Exactly
%%SUB One number, six conditions, and a figure that was true and useless%%
%%GOALS
Report a measurement with all its conditions
Say what a classification assessment document covers
Explain why one metric cannot be chosen for you
%%

A published specification sets out how to assess the performance of a classifier. It covers what to
compute, how to split the data and what to report alongside the result. It does not tell you which
metric to use or what number is good enough, and the most important thing it asks for is the list of
conditions that must travel with every figure.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

The most dangerous figures in a company are the true ones. A number that is wrong gets found and
corrected; a number that is correctly computed, properly sourced and deeply misleading travels for
years, because every challenge to it is answered by recomputing it. The usual mechanism is a missing
comparison rather than a missing fact: nobody asked what a trivial method would score, so nobody
knows whether the model is doing anything at all. That comparison is almost never computed, and the
reason is structural rather than personal — it is a voluntary act of making your own result look
worse, carried out by the person who built the thing, before anyone has asked for it, with no process
step requiring it and no reviewer expecting it. The habit worth building is small: compute the
baseline first, put it beside the result, and date every figure. An undated figure never becomes
wrong. It simply stops being true, and nobody notices because nothing changed on the slide.
