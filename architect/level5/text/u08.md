# Unit 8 · A Report That Calls Itself a Report
%%SUB A survey of what might help, published in 2020, honest about being neither%%
%%GOALS
Make a cautious claim without sounding evasive
Say what a technical report is
Describe a measure that helps without guaranteeing
%%

Standards bodies publish several kinds of document, and one of them is a **technical report**: a
survey of what is known, carrying no requirements at all. One such report surveys the approaches
people use to make artificial intelligence trustworthy. It recommends almost nothing, which makes it
one of the most quotable and most misquoted documents in the field.

> **B:** Not by that document. **It is a technical report. On its own cover it is marked informative,
> which in standards language means it contains no requirements at all.**

> **B:** Because it is genuinely useful and almost nobody reads the cover. **It surveys threats,
> pitfalls and mitigations. It tells you that adversarial inputs, data poisoning and model stealing
> exist, and what people do about them. It does not tell you to do any of it.**
> **A:** So it is weaker than a standard.
> **B:** It is a different thing, and in one way more honest. **A requirements document has to be
> testable, so it can only require what can be checked. A report can say *this may help* — which for

> | **data poisoning** | corrupting what the model learns from | provenance controls **can help**; detection is **hard** |

> **What *informative* buys you.**
>
> > A requirements document can only require what is **testable**. That is a real constraint, and it
> > means a standard often cannot speak about the measures that matter most, because their
> > effectiveness is not established.
> >
> > **A report can say *this may help*, which for most of the measures in this field is the true
> > statement.** The report describes approaches to transparency, explainability and

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD KS5-08-06%%

> **Oksana:** Your draft says adversarial testing *may reduce* susceptibility. I want *reduces*. We
> do the testing; it works.
> **Hana:** It does work, in the sense that it finds things. **What we cannot say is by how much it
> reduces the risk, because nobody can measure the risk it is reducing.** *Reduces* implies a
> quantity that does not exist.
> **Oksana:** *May reduce* sounds as though we are not sure it does anything.
> **Hana:** Which is why it never travels alone. **One cautious sentence about the world, one flat
> sentence about us.** *Adversarial testing may reduce susceptibility and guarantees nothing; we run
> it quarterly and two of the last four runs changed a decision threshold.*
> **Oksana:** The second half is stronger than *reduces*.
> **Hana:** The second half is much stronger, because it is checkable. **Somebody can ask me which
> two runs and I can tell them.** Nobody can check *reduces*, which is exactly why it is comfortable
> and exactly why it is worthless under pressure.
> **Oksana:** Under what pressure?
> **Hana:** An incident. **If something gets through and our document said *reduces*, we are
> explaining why our claim failed. If it said *may reduce* and named what we actually do, we are
> describing a known limitation and the work we did anyway.** Those are different meetings.
> **Oksana:** You are describing the cover-your-back version.
> **Hana:** I would describe it as the accurate version, and I accept that those overlap. **But look

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Over-claiming is usually understood as something organizations do to outsiders, and in careful
companies the opposite is true: the customer-facing text is reviewed by three people and hedged
properly, while the internal page says *eliminates*. That is worth noticing, because it reveals what
the over-claim is for. It is not persuading a customer; it is closing a question for the people who
would otherwise have to keep it open. The consequence is not reputational, it is budgetary: nobody
funds work on a solved problem, so a control described as eliminating a risk stops improving on the
day that sentence is written. The repair is not to weaken the page but to move its strength — one
cautious sentence about what a measure may do, one flat checkable sentence about what you actually
do, and an honest evidence line that is sometimes empty. An empty evidence line is uncomfortable in a
meeting and is the most useful thing in the document, because it is a request for work with no
adjectives in it.

**Discuss:** Which problem does your company consider solved because a document says so?

> | provenance controls | **can help to** detect poisoned records | detection at all; the report says detection is hard |
> | rate limiting | **tends to** raise the cost of model extraction | that a determined attacker fails |
> | output coarsening | **there is some evidence** it reduces leakage | that no training record can be inferred |
>
> **What we actually do, and what it has earned.**
>
> - **Adversarial testing: quarterly, two customer-facing models.** **Two of the last four runs
>   changed a decision threshold**, in June and December.
> - **Provenance controls: on all eleven datasets since 2024.** **Evidence earned: none. We have never
>   detected a poisoned record** — which is consistent with there being none **and equally consistent
>   with detection not working.** Those two states produce identical reports.
> - **Rate limiting: on both public endpoints.** Evidence: one extraction attempt slowed and
>   abandoned, in February.
> - **Output coarsening: not implemented.** Assessed in March and deferred on utility grounds.
>
> **What this revision changed, and the uncomfortable part.** Three of the four measures were
> previously described as **eliminating** or **preventing** a risk. **All three over-claims were in
> internal documents; our customer-facing pages were already careful.** The over-claim was not
> deceiving anybody outside. **It was closing a question: an internal page saying our provenance
> controls eliminate poisoning is why nobody has proposed improving them in two years.**
>
> **Not in this note.** **Whether detection works.** **That** is a test somebody has to design, and it
> is the one thing on this page I would fund first.
>
> *An empty evidence line is a budget request with no adjectives in it.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Put the evidence line in even when it is empty. The empty ones are the reason
> the document exists.
