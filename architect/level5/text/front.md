## How to use this book

Book 3 was about decisions. This book is about **the documents that other people will hold you to**:
a voluntary framework, a regulation with four different commencement dates, a certifiable management
system, two one-page documentation forms invented by researchers, and a technical report that is
careful to call itself a report.

**Twelve units**, twenty-two pages each, with the same six lessons in the same order every time.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Sixteen words, a second set of six, and a team research task |
| **2 · Reading** | A real framework, regulation, standard, paper or published form, with its date and its limits |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures, with the mistake each one causes |
| **4 · At Work** | Clear English, a long dialogue, a culture check and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A model, then your own |
| **Team Up & Review** | A picture, a team project, a survey, and one problem with no easy answer |

**The audio.** 🔊 marks a track. 195 tracks, numbered `KS5-unit-track`.

## What is different about this book

**The documents are about systems that learn.** Every source here was written in the last eight
years, by committees and researchers who were describing something that was changing while they
wrote. Several of them say so in their own first pages, which is the most useful sentence in each
one.

**Four kinds of authority, and they are not the same.** A **voluntary framework** that nobody can
require. A **regulation** that applies on dates already fixed in law. A **management system
standard** that an auditor can certify. **A paper proposing a form**, which has no authority at all
and is used everywhere. An architect who treats these four as interchangeable will promise the wrong
things to the wrong people, and this book is largely about not doing that.

**The grammar is the grammar of careful claims.** Hedged attribution, because almost nothing here is
yours to assert. The language of dates that have not arrived yet. The passive of obligation, and how
to put the responsible person back into the sentence. Guidance verbs and their real strength.
Intended use, and the sentence that says what something is *not* for. Indirect questions, which is
what a documentation form actually is. Stipulative definitions. Cautious claims about trust. Rights,
exceptions and safeguards. Precise comparison. Reporting a measurement with its uncertainty. And
finally, a model documentation page you can hand to somebody who will be asked about it in public.

**One sentence you will write in every unit.** *This document is a __________ , dated __________ ,
and it does not __________ .*

## Nothing here is official

This is an independent teaching draft. It is **not published or endorsed by any standards body,
regulator or research group**, and it carries no official status. **Nothing in it is legal advice.**
Units 2 and 9 discuss published legal texts, including dates on which obligations apply; they are
teaching material and are not a substitute for a lawyer, and the law may have changed since this
book was written. Every person, team, company, model, dataset, system and incident about Meridian in
it was written for this book. The frameworks, regulations, standards, papers and published forms
quoted in the reading texts are real, and each text names the document, its date or edition, and the
limits its own authors state.
