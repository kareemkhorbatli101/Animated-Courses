decisions taken by a machine alone. It is short. It has three exceptions and three minimum
safeguards, and two of its words decide whether it applies to anything your company does. **Nothing

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Automated decisions about people rarely arrive by a decision to automate. They arrive one service at
a time, each change individually sensible, and the route for a person who disagrees stays where it
was built — usually an inbox designed for a world in which a human made the decision and could
therefore revisit it. The result is a company that answers every complaint courteously and cannot
change a single outcome, which it experiences as good service. The language matters here because it
is where the self-deception lives: *a human in the loop* can mean a named person with authority and
time, or somebody approving ninety-four of a hundred cases in under four seconds with four fields on
a screen and no way to evaluate what she is seeing. Both are described with the same five words. The
second is not a failure by that reviewer — four seconds is the right amount of time to spend on a
decision you have no basis to question — but it is a different system, and the difference is
invisible until somebody writes down the numbers.

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Brief for legal · the credit-limit decision
> *Machine-learning group · section 9.1 · 24 April · not legal advice*
>
> **What we need.** One classification, on our facts. **We are not asking whether we comply; we are
> asking whether Article 22 engages at all**, which turns on two words in it.
>
> **The process, with numbers.**
>
> | Step | Who acts | How long | What they can change |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | score produced | the model | — | — |
> | review | one named reviewer, four fields on screen | **94 of last 100 in under 4 seconds** | the limit; **done twice this year** |
> | decision sent | automated letter | same day | — |
> | complaint | **a general inbox** | answered within a day | **nothing** |
>
> **The two words.** The right engages only where a decision is **based solely on automated
> processing** **and** produces **legal effects or similarly significantly affects** the person.
> **We cannot answer either.** A refused credit limit plausibly affects somebody significantly; a
> reviewer who approves 94 of 100 in four seconds may or may not be meaningful human involvement.
> **Both are determinations on the facts above, which is why the facts are above.**
>
> **The three safeguards, and what we have.** Where an exception applies, the article requires, **at a
> minimum**, a route to **human intervention**, a way to **express a point of view**, and a way to
> **contest the decision**.
>
> > **We have none of the three.** We have a courteous inbox. **Three customers have written in two
> > years; all three received a reply within a day, from three different people, none of whom could
> > change the outcome.** A reply is not an intervention.
>
> **What we are doing regardless of your answer.** A **form**, a **queue**, and a **named reviewer
> with authority to change an outcome**. **About a week.** If the article does not engage we have
> gained a complaints route; if it does, we are most of the way there. **We are not waiting.**
>
> **What is not our judgment.** Whether the reviewer's involvement is meaningful. Whether the effect
> is significant. **Whether any exception applies, and which.** We have written down everything we
> know and classified nothing.
>
> **Not in this brief.** **The other three automated decisions.** **These** need the same page, and
> one of them has no route at all.
>
> *Nothing in this brief is legal advice. It is a description of our own process, written so that one
> question can be answered in an hour.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Replace every adjective about your process with a number. *A human reviews each
> case* becomes *94 of 100 in under four seconds*, and the second version is the one a lawyer can
> use.
