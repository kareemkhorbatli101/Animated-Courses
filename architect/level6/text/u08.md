# Unit 8 · Somebody Has Found Something
%%SUB A guideline on receiving reports, and a file at a known address%%
%%GOALS
Hold a long argument together with discourse markers
Say what a disclosure policy has to contain
Explain why the first ninety minutes decide everything

Somebody outside your company has found a weakness in your product and is trying to tell you. There
is a published guideline on how to receive that, and a short specification for a file at a known
address so that they can find out where to send it. Most organizations have neither, and the first
ninety minutes of a report arriving at a general inbox decide how the following six months go.

### 1 · Discourse markers

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD KS6-08-06%%

> **Nour:** I read your note as somebody who had not been in the discussion, and the thing I noticed
> is that it is one argument rather than six paragraphs.
> **Hana:** That is what I was trying for, and the markers are doing it. **A note of this length has
> about four places where the reader can lose the thread, and a marker at each one costs three words
> and holds it.**
> **Nour:** Walk me through yours.
> **Hana:** *To that end* after the claim about acknowledgment, because the next sentence is the
> action that follows from it. **Without the marker, the handbook sentence reads as a new topic.** *In
> which connection* for the second instruction, which is related but not consequent. **Then
> *conversely* for the legal sentence, because it is the mirror image.**
> **Nour:** And *that said*?
> **Hana:** The one I argued with myself about. **It concedes something against my own argument: all
> three finders waited for us despite an eleven-day silence.** I could have left it out and the note
> would be stronger and less true.
> **Nour:** Why include it?
> **Hana:** **Because somebody in the meeting will say it, and a note that has already said it keeps
> the argument.** A note that has not said it loses ten minutes and some credibility. *That said* is
> how you put a concession where you want it rather than where somebody else does.
> **Nour:** Is there a marker you avoid?
> **Hana:** Several, and for one reason: **the markers that sound like structure and signal nothing.**
> *Furthermore* usually means *I have another sentence*. *It is worth noting that* means *the next
> clause is weak and I am propping it up*. **If I cannot say what relation the marker is marking, I
> delete it and the paragraph is better.**
> **Nour:** And *more importantly*?
> **Hana:** Earns its place once per document, because it ranks two things. **Mine is at the end:
> more importantly, the guideline assumes an organization that wants reports.** That is the real
> finding and it is not about the standard at all.

*Why does Hana include a concession against her own argument?*

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

When a stranger finds a weakness in your product, almost everything that follows is decided in the
first ninety minutes, and the deciding factor is not technical. A finder who has heard nothing
concludes that nobody is reading and publishes; a finder who receives four lines from a human waits,
sometimes for months. Yet the commonest first reply contains a sentence about unauthorized access,
written by somebody trying to be careful, which reliably converts a person who was helping into a
person with a grievance and an audience. Underneath both failures is usually an absence rather than a
decision: no published contact, no stated interval, no sentence about good faith — and an absence of
that kind has already answered the question of whether the company wants reports, without anybody
having decided it. The repair is a page that fits on one screen and a four-line template that any
support agent may send without approval. The approval clause is the part that gets removed in review
and the part that makes the whole thing work.

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Security report policy · one page
> *Platform team · to be published at the known address · 26 May*
>
> **Why this page exists.** **Three reports have reached us in two years. All three arrived in a sales
> inbox, and the fastest acknowledgment took eleven days.** **That said**, all three finders waited
> for us — **which was luck, and three patient people in a row is not a process.**
>
> **1 Contact.** Reports to **security@** *(named address)*. A **public key** is published beside it,
> and **a plain-text file at the known web address** carries both, following **RFC 9116 of April
> 2022**, **so that somebody who finds something at two in the morning does not have to guess which
> inbox to try**.
>
> **2 What happens, and when.** **We acknowledge within one hour in working hours and within twelve
> hours otherwise.** **To that end**, a four-line acknowledgment sits in the support handbook and
> **any member of the support team may send it without approval**. **In which connection**, that
> template says **nothing about legality**, and the handbook forbids adding anything of the kind to a
> first reply.
>
> **3 Our interval.** A substantive reply within **72 hours**; a remediation plan within **10 working
> days**; publication **coordinated with you**. **The guideline we follow sets no timeline** — the
> interval above is ours, and we are published to it.
>
> **4 Scope.** The products listed below, at the domains listed below. **Conversely**, two systems are
> **out of scope** and named, and we would rather say so than receive a report we cannot act on.
>
> **5 Credit.** We will **name you in the advisory, in the form you choose**, unless you ask us not
> to. **Most finders want their name on it, and we have never asked the three who reached us.**
>
> **6 Good faith.** **Good-faith testing within the scope above will not be met with legal action from
> us.** **This sentence has been reviewed and weakened once at legal's request. A weakened sentence
> published next week is worth more than a perfect one in six months.**
>
> **What this page does not do.** It does not describe our **internal handling** of a report, which is
> a separate standard and a separate document. **More importantly, it does not make us good at this
> yet**: it makes us findable, which is the part that has been missing.
>
> *A company with no published route has already answered the question of whether it wants reports,
> and nobody here ever decided that.*
