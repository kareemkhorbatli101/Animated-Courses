# Unit 6 · An Awareness Document
%%SUB Two ranked lists of ten, built partly from a survey, and quoted as if they were standards%%
%%GOALS

Two of the most widely quoted documents in software security are ranked lists of ten. Both say, on
their own pages, that they are awareness documents rather than standards. Both appear in contracts.
Rarely does anybody ask where the ranking came from, and in both cases the answer changes what the
list can be used for.

### 1 · Inversion

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD KS6-06-06%%

> **Oksana:** You have written *Not until we compared our own incidents did anybody notice*. That is
> a nineteenth-century sentence.
> **Hana:** It is, and I want exactly one of them on that page. **The point it carries is the single
> most useful thing in the note, and in its ordinary form — *nobody noticed until we compared our own
> incidents* — I have watched three people read past it.**
> **Oksana:** Read past it how? It is the same information.
> **Hana:** The same information in a shape the eye is used to. **A sentence beginning with *nobody*
> looks like a complaint, and a reader who is skimming files it as one and moves on.** Inverting it
> breaks the pattern. The reader's eye stops because the grammar is unfamiliar, and by the time they
> have worked out why, they have read the clause.
> **Oksana:** That is manipulative.
> **Hana:** It is rhetoric, which is what you use when a fact is important and boring. **And it stops
> working immediately if I do it twice.** Three inversions on a page and the reader hears a style
> rather than a point; by the third one they are skimming again, and now the whole page is
> theatrical.
> **Oksana:** So one per page.
> **Hana:** One per page, on the sentence I would most hate to be told nobody had read. **And it has
> to be a sentence that deserves it.** *Rarely does a company's incident history match the published
> order* earns it, because everybody assumes the opposite. **_Nowhere does it claim to be a
> standard_** earns it, because the contract we have been sent assumes it does.
> **Oksana:** And where it does not deserve it?
> **Hana:** Then it reads as pomposity, which is worse than being skimmed. **_At no point was this
> team consulted_ is an inversion in the service of a grievance, and a reader hears the grievance and
> discounts the fact.** Same trap as the clefts in the first unit: the form foregrounds whatever you
> put in it, including your own feelings.
> **Oksana:** You have a rule for this.
> **Hana:** **Invert a fact the reader assumes the opposite of. Never invert a fact about how badly
> you have been treated.**

*What is Hana's rule for when to invert?*

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

A ranked list of ten is the most quotable document in any technical field, and quotability is
independent of applicability. The order in a well-built list reflects the data its authors could
collect — in one widely cited case, eight positions derived from contributed data about applications
other organizations chose to test, and two from a survey of practitioners. That is an honest method
and it describes a population of which your company is not a member in any particular way. What
happens next is not carelessness but something more interesting: reading a ranked list feels like
learning something, while comparing it with your own incident history feels like homework, so the
first happens twenty times and the second never. The comparison usually takes under an hour and
usually moves something from the bottom of the published list to the top of yours. The second habit
worth having concerns version: any list that is revised, renamed and reordered every two years is
telling you that the understanding beneath it has not settled, and writing it into a contract as a
fixed set of ten guarantees an argument about which version somebody meant.

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Contract response · clause 7.4, application security
> *Platform team · for Verano Retail · 19 May*
>
> **The clause as drafted.** *"The supplier shall be compliant with the OWASP Top 10."*
>
> **Why we are proposing different wording.** **Nowhere does that document describe itself as a
> standard**; it describes itself as an **awareness document**, and it states that it should not be
> used as a checklist. **Compliance with it is not available to anybody**, and a clause that cannot be
> satisfied as written is of no use to either of us after an incident.
>
> **Where the published ranking comes from.** In the **2021** list, **eight of the ten positions were
> derived from data contributed by organizations about the applications they tested; two came from an
> industry survey.** That is an honest method, openly described. **It reflects a population of
> applications rather than ours.**
>
> **Our own order, and why.** **Rarely does a company's incident history match the published order,
> and ours does not.** Both of our **customer-visible incidents in three years** fall in **security
> logging and monitoring failures — published position nine**. Accordingly:
>
> | Our order | Category | Published position | Reasoning |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | 1 | logging and monitoring failures | **9** | **both of our incidents; 9 days to detection in one** |
> | 2 | broken access control | 1 | highest prevalence in the data, and our surface is wide |
> | 3 | vulnerable and outdated components | 6 | we now have a component list (note 51) and 14 unknowns |
>
> **What we propose to commit to.** **All ten categories of each list, addressed, in the documented
> order above, with one sentence of reasoning per item, reviewed annually.** **That is more than the
> clause asks for**, and unlike the clause it is something we can be held to.
>
> **One question for your legal team.** The companion list for **applications built on large language
> models** was first published in **2023** and **has already been revised with renamed and reordered
> entries**. If the clause is to reference it, **which version, as at which date?** **A list that
> moves that quickly is telling us the understanding beneath it has not settled.**
>
> **Not in this response.** **Evidence per category.** **That** is eleven documents, two of which are
> older than we would like, and we will say which two.
>
> *Reading a ranked list feels like learning something; comparing it with your own incidents feels
> like homework, which is why the first happens twenty times and the second never.*

> **Writer's Tip** — One inversion per page, on a fact your reader assumes the opposite of. Two and
> the reader hears a style; an inverted grievance and they hear only the grievance.

### 3 · Write

Write **190 to 220 words**: a response to a clause quoting a ranked list.

**Quote the clause. Say what kind of document the list is, using one inverted sentence. Say where the
ranking came from, with the split between data and survey if you know it. Give your own order for at
least three items, with the published position and one sentence of reasoning beside each. Say what
you propose to commit to, and that it exceeds the clause. Ask one question back about version and
date. End with one thing not in the response.**

**Read your response aloud to your partner first.** Count your inversions: more than one, and cut.
Then check your own against the list, and swap.
