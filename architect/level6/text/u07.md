# Unit 7 · Bought Against, Not Followed
%%SUB A standard nobody complies with voluntarily, because somebody else is buying%%
%%GOALS
Write and read conditional procurement obligations
Say how a standard is used in public buying
Explain what a self-declared conformance report is
%%

Most of the documents in this series are quoted because somebody chose to quote them. This one is
different: it is written into public purchasing, so a supplier meets it because a customer is buying
against it. That changes the English completely. The sentences are conditional obligations, the
answers are claims about yourself, and the three permitted answers include one that most companies
never use.

> ## Meridian · governance note 23
> *Platform team · accessibility in procurement · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The standard. EN 301 549**, *Accessibility requirements for ICT products and services*, produced
> jointly by the three European standards organizations; the version most tenders cite is **3.2.1,
> of March 2021**, and **later versions exist**. It was developed in response to a **European
> Commission mandate** and is **written into public procurement**.
>
> **Why it reaches us.** We sell to no public body. **Our customers do, so they are asked for it, and
> they ask us.** **Not one of our contracts requires this standard directly; four require us to
> answer against it.**
>
> **What it covers.**
>
> | Area | What it addresses |
> |---|---|
> | functional performance | what a user with a given limitation must be able to do |
> | generic requirements | closed functionality, biometrics, preservation of information |
> | **web content** | **incorporates the content guidelines at the first two levels** |
> | non-web documents and software | the same ideas outside a browser |
> | **documentation and support services** | manuals, help, **and the people who answer the telephone** |
>
> **The shape of every requirement.**
>
> > **Where ICT provides a particular feature, it shall also provide something else.** The obligation
> > is **conditional**. That is why the response form permits **not applicable** as an answer — and
> > why a *not applicable* without a **justification** is worthless.
> >
> > **At no point does the standard test anything.** A conformance report is **self-declared**: it is
> > the supplier's statement about its own product, in a standard shape, verified by nobody unless the
> > buyer chooses to check.
>
> **The three answers, and the one nobody uses.**
>
> | Answer | What it means | How often we see it |
> |---|---|---|
> | **supported** | the requirement is met | **in competitors' reports: almost always** |
> | **supported with exceptions** | met, with named gaps | **almost never** |
> | **not supported** | not met | almost never |
>
> **A page of unbroken *supported* is read by an experienced procurement officer as a page nobody
> checked.** **Supported with exceptions**, with the exception stated in six words, is the answer that
> earns a point.
>
> **Where we are, honestly.** The **web clauses** are mostly answerable from the accessibility work
> described in an earlier note, because they incorporate the same content criteria. **The clauses we
> fail are documentation and support services.** Our manuals are not navigable by a screen reader,
> and **nobody has ever tested our help desk** for the ability to take a request in an accessible
> form. **Our honest answer there is *not supported*, and this is the first year we will write it.**
>
> *Source: EN 301 549, Accessibility requirements for ICT products and services, produced by CEN,
> CENELEC and ETSI following a European Commission mandate; the widely cited version is 3.2.1 of
> March 2021, and later versions exist. Its requirements are conditional in form, it incorporates web
> content criteria at the first two levels from a separate set of guidelines, and conformance is
> self-declared by the supplier: the standard itself tests nothing and verifies nothing.*

### 1 · Obligation language

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD KS6-07-06%%

> **Lucia:** I have to answer two hundred clauses, and I want to understand the sentence shape before
> I start, because it is not how we write.
> **Samira:** Read one out.
> **Lucia:** *Where ICT has closed functionality, it shall be operable without requiring the user to
> attach assistive technology.*
> **Samira:** That is the shape of the entire document. **A condition, then a *shall*. The obligation
> does not exist unless the condition holds.**
> **Lucia:** Which is why *not applicable* is an answer.
> **Samira:** Which is exactly why, and it is the answer most often misused. **_Not applicable_ with
> no reason beside it is read as *we did not look*, and it is scored that way.** *Not applicable: this
> product has no closed functionality; all functions are reached through a browser* takes nine words
> and is unarguable.
> **Lucia:** And the *shall* — is that us or the product?
> **Samira:** Read the subject, and it moves. **_It shall be operable_ binds the product. _The
> supplier shall provide a conformance report_ binds us.** The second kind is the one to count
> before signing, because every one of them is a thing somebody here has to do.
> **Lucia:** Our own answers should use the same shape?
> **Samira:** No, and this is the part people get wrong in the other direction. **Their sentences are
> conditional obligations because they are addressing every product in the market. Our answers are
> statements about one product, so they should be flat and specific.**
> **Lucia:** Give me the pair.
> **Samira:** Theirs: *Where ICT provides visual output, it shall provide a text alternative.* Ours:
> **_Supported with exceptions: all images carry alternative text except the four chart types in the
> reporting module, which are described in the caption instead._** **Their conditional, our
> declarative.** Mirroring their grammar back at them produces an answer that looks compliant and
> says nothing.
> **Lucia:** And *it shall be demonstrated that*?
> **Samira:** The phrase to notice, because it is an evidence obligation rather than a product one.
> **It does not ask whether something is true; it asks what you will show.** We have four of those in
> this tender, and for two of them the honest answer is that we have nothing to show yet.

*What is the difference between their sentence shape and yours?*

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

A response form with three permitted answers is, in practice, used with one. Reports arrive with two
hundred consecutive *supported* entries, and the people who read forty such reports a year have long
since stopped reading them as claims about products: an unbroken page is a claim that nobody opened
the software. What earns a point is evidence that somebody inside the company tried something — one
named exception of about six words, specific enough that the reader could go and check. The same
asymmetry applies to failures. A scattered set of them looks like a product built without thought;
a cluster of them in one clause area usually tells the truth about a company, which is that the
software work was done and the manuals and the telephone were never once considered. And there is a
sentence almost every report omits: which version, as at which date. A self-declared report with no
version line will be quoted in two years against a version that did not exist when it was written,
and the supplier will have no way to say so.

**Discuss:** Which of your company's standing answers has no version or date on it?
