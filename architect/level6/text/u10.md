A reference model for digital preservation says that keeping the bits is the easy part. What makes a
preserved file meaningful is the information that travels with it, and whether it is understandable
depends on **whom you have declared you are preserving it for**. The company in this unit can prove
its 2019 files are unaltered and cannot open two of them, which is where the previous unit stopped.

### 1 · Modal perfects

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD KS6-10-06%%

> **Paula:** I want to argue with one sentence in your draft, and it is the sentence everybody will
> agree with.
> **Mei:** Which is why you are arguing with it. Go on.
> **Paula:** *Nobody could have known the format would become unreadable.* **That is false, and it is
> the most comfortable sentence in the note.**
> **Mei:** Why false?
> **Paula:** **The format was proprietary in 2019 and the license was annual. Somebody was renewing
> it every year, so somebody must have known it could stop.** The accurate sentence is harder: **we
> could have known, and nobody was asked to find out.**
> **Mei:** Those are very close in sound and very different in meaning.
> **Paula:** They are, and the modal is doing all of it. ***Could not have known* claims an
> impossibility. *Was not asked to find out* describes an absence of instruction**, which is a
> management fact rather than a technical one, and it has somebody's name attached to it — probably
> mine.
> **Mei:** And *would have had to*?
> **Paula:** For the conditions, and it is the form I use most in this note. **_The specification
> would have had to be stored beside the file._ It states a necessary condition that was not met,
> without claiming anybody failed.** Then I list three such conditions and add that **any one of the
> three would have been sufficient**, which is the real finding: this was not a hard problem with one
> expensive solution. It was an easy problem with three cheap ones.
> **Mei:** Is there a modal you avoid in these notes?
> **Paula:** ***Should have***, mostly. **It is an obligation in the past and it reads as blame, and
> blame makes everybody argue about the person instead of the window.** I use it once, deliberately,
> where the obligation genuinely existed and was written down.
> **Mei:** And *must have been*?
> **Paula:** For a deduction, and it is the most useful one in an investigation. **_Somebody must have
> been renewing that license annually_ — I do not know who, and the invoice exists, so the person
> exists.** That sentence is how you find out who to ask.

*Why does Paula reject* could not have known*?*

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

*Nobody could have known* is the most comfortable sentence in any incident note, and it is usually
false. It asserts an impossibility, and impossibilities are rare; what is common is an absence of
instruction. The honest replacement — *we could have known, and nobody was asked to find out* — is
one clause longer and much worse to write, because it has a name attached to it, usually the name of
whoever is writing the note. That is the whole reason the first sentence gets written. The second
habit this unit asks for is similar in shape: a preservation standard requires an archive to declare
who it is preserving for, and almost nobody does, because the declaration immediately settles
arguments that are more comfortable left open. Write down that your readers are a competent stranger
in 2040 with nobody here to ask, and a great many questions about formats, provenance and context
answer themselves — mostly against you, mostly cheaply, and mostly while there is still time.

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Preservation note · what we can prove and what we can read
> *Records · section 10.1 · 2 June · continues note 25*
>
> **The model.** **ISO 14721**, the **Open Archival Information System reference model**, **second
> edition 2012**, **since revised — this citation should be checked against the current edition before
> it is quoted externally**. It is a **reference model**: **no technology, no formats, no
> certification** — audit and certification are a separate standard.
>
> **What we can prove.** **Every object in the archive is checksummed.** Of the five preservation
> descriptions the model names, **fixity** is complete, **reference** and **access rights** are mostly
> complete, and **provenance** and **context** are thin. **Fixity is the easiest of the five and it is
> the one we did.**
>
> **What we cannot read.** **Two of the four formats we were writing in 2019 require a component we no
> longer license.** **We can prove those files are unaltered and we cannot open them.**
>
> **What would have had to be true.**
>
> > **The format specification would have had to be stored beside the files.** Or **a working reader
> > would have had to be preserved**. Or **the files would have had to be converted while the license
> > still ran**.
> >
> > **Any one of the three would have been sufficient. None was done.** **This was not a hard problem
> > with one expensive remedy; it was an easy problem with three cheap ones and no owner** — about a
> > week of somebody's time at any point in five years.
>
> **The sentence this note does not contain.** **It does not say that nobody could have known.** The
> license was **annual** and **somebody renewed it four times**, so **somebody must have known the
> format depended on a payment that could stop**. **We could have known, and nobody was asked to find
> out.** **After 2021 that is my name**, and the accurate sentence is worth more than the comfortable
> one.
>
> **The declaration we have never made, now made.** The model requires an archive to name a
> **designated community**. Ours, from today:
>
> > **A competent auditor or engineer in 2040, with no access to anybody who has worked here.**
>
> **Once that sentence exists there is a criterion**, and most of our open format arguments resolve
> immediately — **mostly against us, mostly cheaply, and mostly while there is still time.**
>
> **Not in this note.** **The conversion itself.** **That** has a closing window: the license runs
> until **31 March**, and after that none of the three remedies is available at all.
>
> *"Nobody could have known" has no name in it, which is exactly why it is the sentence everybody
> reaches for.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Replace every *could not have known* with *was not asked to find out*, and check
> who the second sentence names. If it names you, it is probably the true one.
