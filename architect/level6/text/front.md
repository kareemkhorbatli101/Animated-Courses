%%SUB English for AI-Enabled Software Architects · International Edition · CEFR B2+ → C1%%

## How to use this book

This is the last book of the series, and it is about **the documents other people hold the evidence
in**: a machine-readable list of everything in your product, an advisory that says a vulnerability
does not affect you, a procurement standard a customer buys against, a records standard that decides
how long a thing must exist, and a set of stage codes that tell you whether the standard you are
quoting is still alive.

**Twelve units**, twenty-four pages each, with the same six lessons in the same order every time.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Sixteen words, a second set of six, and a team research task |
| **2 · Reading** | A real standard, specification, framework or awareness document, with its edition and its limits |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures at C1, with the mistake each one causes |
| **4 · At Work** | Clear English, a long dialogue, a culture check and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A model, then your own |
| **Team Up & Review** | A picture, a team project, a survey, and one problem with no easy answer |

**The audio.** 🔊 marks a track. 195 tracks, numbered `KS6-unit-track`.

## What is different about this book

**The documents are machine-readable, or bought against, or both.** Several sources here are formats
rather than prose: a list a tool produces, an advisory a tool consumes, a set of requirements a
procurement officer scores you against. The English matters more rather than less, because the
sentence a human writes **beside** the machine-readable part is the only place the limits can go.

**Every unit has somebody outside holding the other end.** A customer's procurement team. A security
researcher who has found something. An auditor. A regulator. A colleague in nine years who needs a
record to still exist. This book is written for the moment when the reader is not a friendly
colleague.

**The grammar is the grammar of the long, careful document.** Cleft sentences, for putting the real
point where a tired reader will find it. Layered attribution, for a claim that passes through three
suppliers. Heavy noun phrases, and how to unpack somebody else's. Participle clauses, which
condense. Scope quantifiers, which are where over-claiming hides. Inversion, for the one sentence in
a page that must not be skimmed. The obligation language of procurement. Discourse markers that hold
twenty paragraphs together. Modal perfects, for what would have had to be true. Quantified ranges.
Concessive subordination at length. And finally the register of record: the document that outlives
the project, the team and the company.

**Two questions you will ask in every unit.** *Which edition, and is it still current?* And *who
outside this building is going to read this sentence?*

## The people

The same team, five books later, and most of them now appear only when the work is theirs. **Mei
Chen** leads the architecture group. **Chiamaka Obi** leads the machine-learning group and signs the
pages. **Hana Sato** owns model documentation. **Yusuf Demir** owns the data. **Adeel Khan**,
**Tomas Novak**, **Priya Raman**, **Lars Eriksen**, **Oksana Melnyk**, **Ines Duarte**, **Grace
Okafor**, **Marek Nowak**, **Ravi Menon**, **Junseo Park**, **Karin Holm**, **Samira Aziz** and
**Nour Rahal** are all still here.

Two voices arrive for this book, both of them from outside the engineering group, because the
documents in it are read by people who are not engineers.

**Lucia Ferrara** answers the procurement questionnaires and reads the contracts. She has discovered
that four of Meridian's standing answers describe documents that have been withdrawn, superseded or
confirmed under a different number, and that nobody had ever checked whether the things the company
cites still exist.

**Paula Reis** owns records and retention. She asks the question that makes everybody uncomfortable
in Units 9 and 10: *how long must this exist, and who will be able to open it?* She has a list of
things that must be kept for eleven years and no system that can be relied on to keep them for four.

The company is called **Meridian**, and its customers — **Northwind Rail**, **Alba Health**, **Cobalt
Freight**, **Verano Retail** and **Tarn Energy** — are invented, on purpose.

## Nothing here is official

This is an independent teaching draft. It is **not published or endorsed by any standards body,
regulator, research group or project**, and it carries no official status. **Nothing in it is legal
advice.** Units 5, 7 and 9 discuss documents with legal and contractual consequences; they are
teaching material, not a substitute for a lawyer or a records professional. **Every document number,
edition, date and status in this book was correct to the best of the author's knowledge at the time
of writing, and Unit 12 exists because that sentence has a short shelf life**: standards are
amended, confirmed, superseded and withdrawn, and a citation must be checked rather than
remembered. Every person, team, company, system, dataset, incident and advisory about Meridian in it
was written for this book.
