# Unit 9 · How Long Must This Exist
%%SUB Four characteristics, one definition, and a period that has to start somewhere%%
%%GOALS
State a retention period with precision
Say what makes something a record
Explain why a standard cannot give you a number
%%

A records management standard defines what a record is and what makes one trustworthy. It does not
tell you how long to keep anything, and it cannot: a period comes from legal and business
requirements in your own jurisdiction. What it does give you is the vocabulary to notice that *seven
years* is not a retention period until somebody says seven years **from when**. **Nothing in this
unit is legal advice.**

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

A retention schedule is the document most likely to be approved without being executable. Every row
has a number of years, every row looks complete, and the column that would make the number mean
something — the event the years are counted from — is either absent or empty. The reason is not
negligence: the trigger is usually a legal question, the person building the schedule is not a
lawyer, and a duration is available while an answer is not. So the duration is written, the schedule
is approved, and for years afterwards the company describes itself as compliant with a document that
cannot be carried out by anybody. The repair has two halves, and the second is the interesting one.
First, write *no fewer than*, *not later than*, *from the end of*, *inclusive* — and accept looking
pedantic. Second, never leave the unanswerable cell empty: write the question and the date you asked
it. The information is identical, and only one version gets raised in a review.

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Records note · the retention schedule cannot be executed
> *Records · section 9.1 · 30 May · not legal advice*
>
> **What the standard does and does not do.** **ISO 15489-1:2016** defines a **record** as
> information kept as **evidence** of a transaction, and gives four characteristics of an
> authoritative record: **authenticity, reliability, integrity, usability**. **It sets no retention
> periods** and says so: they derive from **legal and business requirements in each jurisdiction**.
> **That column is therefore not mine, and nothing here is legal advice.**
>
> **The finding.** Our schedule has **14 record types**. **Every row carries a duration. Eleven have
> no trigger event**, and of the **three** that do, **two specify different triggers for the same
> underlying contract**.
>
> **Why a duration is not a period.** For one customer of **eleven years' standing**, *seven years*
> can mean:
>
> | Reading | End date |
> |---|---|
> | seven years **from creation** of the record | earliest |
> | seven years **from the end of the contract** | +4 years |
> | seven years **from the end of the financial year in which** the contract ended | +4 years, +months |
> | seven years **from the last transaction** | latest |
>
> **The earliest and the latest are eleven years apart**, and every one of the four is a defensible
> reading of the cell as written.
>
> **What I have changed today.** **I have not left the eleven cells empty.** Each now reads **"trigger
> unknown — asked legal 12 May"**. **An empty cell looks finished; a cell with a question and a date
> in it is an open item somebody raises in a review.** The information is identical.
>
> **The precision we will use from now on.** **No fewer than** six years and **not later than** the
> eighth anniversary; **from the end of** the financial year in which the trigger falls; **up to and
> including** the boundary year; **between** two years **inclusive**. **We will look pedantic. The
> alternative is a schedule nobody can carry out.**
>
> **The second problem, which is worse.** **Integrity is met: every archive object is checksummed and
> I can prove nothing has altered. Usability is not: two of the four formats we wrote in 2019 require
> a component we no longer license.** **We can prove a file is unaltered and we cannot open it, and a
> record that cannot be read cannot be evidence** — which is the only reason it was kept.
>
> **Not in this note.** **The conversion of those two formats.** **That** has a closing window and
> belongs in note 26.
>
> *For four years we have described ourselves as compliant with a document that cannot be executed by
> anybody.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Never leave the unanswerable cell empty. Write the question and the date you
> asked it: the information is the same, and only one version gets raised in a review.

### 3 · Write

Write **190 to 220 words**: a records note.

**Say what the standard does and does not do, and that the period column is not yours. Count your
rows and your gaps. Show one duration with at least three possible end dates and the distance between
them. Say what you changed today, including what you wrote in an unanswerable cell. List the
precision expressions you will use from now on. Name a second, worse problem if you have one. End
with one thing not in the note and the line that nothing in it is legal advice.**

**Read your note aloud to your partner first.** Check that every period has a trigger. Then check
your own against the list, and swap.
