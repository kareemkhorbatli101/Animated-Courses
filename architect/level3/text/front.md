## How to use this book

Book 1 was about the agreements underneath a field. Book 2 was about the promise between two
systems. This book is about **the words and the numbers people use to argue about whether a system
is any good** — and about how much of that argument is conducted with figures that do not mean what
everybody assumes.

**Twelve units**, twenty pages each, with the same six lessons in the same order every time.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Sixteen words, a second set of six, and a team research task |
| **2 · Reading** | A real standard, paper or published measure, with its edition and its limits |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures, with the mistake each one causes |
| **4 · At Work** | Clear English, a long dialogue, a culture check and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A model, then your own |
| **Team Up & Review** | A picture, a team project, a survey, and one problem with no easy answer |

**The audio.** 🔊 marks a track. 195 tracks, numbered `KS3-unit-track`.

## What is different about this book

**Half the sources are not standards.** They are papers: a queueing identity from 1961, a conference
paper from 1967 and its answer from 1988, a theorem from 2002 and the trade-off somebody added in
2012. An architect who can quote the limits of Amdahl's assumption is worth more in a planning
meeting than one who has read every vendor's performance page, and the English of a paper —
*assumes*, *holds only if*, *is associated with* — is the English of an honest estimate.

**Every number in this book has a denominator, and most arguments are about it.** Three nines of
what, over what period, measured where? A defect count per what? A severity score that assumes which
exposure? A survey's top quartile defined by whose distribution, in which year? **The unit of a
measurement is a political question and it is nearly always left unstated.**

**The grammar is the grammar of measurement.** The language of classification, of percentages and
approximation, of rates and proportions, of multiples and factors, of trade-off and concession, of
correlation against cause, of agents and instruments, of probability, of exception, of *-able*
adjectives, of parallel lists — and finally the six-part scenario that turns *it should be fast*
into something you can test.

**Four sentences you will write until they are automatic.** *Over what period, and measured where?*
*Compared with what?* *Is that severity or risk?* And *what response measure would settle it?*

The same team, two books later. **Mei Chen** leads the architecture group. **Adeel Khan** has been
there two years and now runs reviews of his own. **Tomas Novak** runs the platform. **Priya Raman**
owns the data model and this book's eleventh unit. **Lars Eriksen** is on call and has strong views
about what an availability figure means. **Oksana Melnyk** is the security architect. **Ines

upwards. **Marek Nowak** tests. **Chiamaka Obi** leads the machine-learning group and arrives
properly in Book 4. **Ravi Menon** builds integrations. **Junseo Park** builds the mobile client.

Two more voices, because measurement needs somebody who does it for a living. **Karin Holm** runs
Meridian's reliability review and asks *over what period* before she asks anything else. **Samira
Aziz** is an accessibility specialist who works with three of Meridian's customers and is the only
person in this book who tests with the people the product is for.

The company is called **Meridian**, and its customers — **Northwind Rail**, **Alba Health**,
**Cobalt Freight**, **Verano Retail** and **Tarn Energy** — are invented, on purpose.

## Nothing here is official

This is an independent teaching draft. It is **not published or endorsed by any standards body**,
and it carries no official status. Every person, team, company, system, incident and figure about
Meridian in it was written for this book. The standards, papers and published measures quoted in the
reading texts are real, and each text names the document or paper, its edition or year, and the
limits its own authors state.
