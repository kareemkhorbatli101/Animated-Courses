# Grammar reference

Every structure in this book, in unit order. The second column is the mistake it prevents.

## Starting Point · What a quality argument is made of

| | |
|---|---|
| **Four things a figure needs** | a quantity · a unit · a period · a condition |
| **The fourth is usually missing and the third is usually wrong** | a figure with two of the four is a mood |
| **The four questions** | over what period and measured where · compared with what · severity or risk · what response measure would settle it |
| **Standards and papers, read the same way** | find the sentence about the limits, and read it twice |

## Unit 1 · Classifying and defining

| | |
|---|---|
| **the model *consists of* nine characteristics** | claims there is no tenth — stronger than *contains* |
| **each *is divided into* sub-characteristics** | the parts, not the whole |
| **availability *falls under* reliability** | and is one sub-characteristic of one of nine |
| **the model contains no numbers** | so *the standard requires 99.95* means *somebody here chose it* |

## Unit 2 · Percentages and approximation

| | |
|---|---|
| **99.94% is *point nine four*** | after a decimal point, digits one at a time |
| **three nines: ~8¾ h a year, ~43 min a month, ~10 min a week** | one percentage, three promises |
| ***just under* / *a little over*** | when the direction is the point, never *about* |
| **the worst customer and the longest outage** | the two figures that cannot be met by being large |

## Unit 3 · Rates and proportions

| | |
|---|---|
| **two hundred requests *per second*** | a rate has two units; a count has one |
| **L = λW** | know two, know the third — and 30 ÷ 200 is a latency nobody timed |
| ***one in thirty-three* beats *three percent*** | for a small proportion, and especially a bad one |
| ***On average*,** at the front | an average with no warning label is read as a promise |

## Unit 4 · Multiples and factors

| | |
|---|---|
| ***nine times as fast as* one worker** | clearer than *nine times faster* |
| **ceiling = 1 ÷ the sequential fraction** | one twentieth sequential → twenty times, whatever you spend |
| **nine times as fast, so a ninth of the time** | say the speed and the duration in one breath |
| ***sooner* or *more*** | one word decides which of two famous papers applies |

## Unit 5 · Concession and trade-off

| | |
|---|---|
| **fast, *although* the value may be stale** | *although* marks what you concede — so concede the cost, not the benefit |
| ***whereas*** for two subjects, not two facts | and never with *but* after *although* |
| ***at the expense of*** / ***in exchange for*** | what you lost · what you paid |
| **two sentences per store** | what it does in a partition · what a normal read does |

## Unit 6 · Correlation and cause

| | |
|---|---|
| ***is associated with*** for survey data | *causes* needs an experiment or a mechanism |
| ***tends to*** admits the exceptions | and keeps the point |
| ***suggests*** for their result, *I think* for yours | mixing them puts your opinion in a paper with a citation |
| **all four, or none** | three of the four improve when you cut corners |

## Unit 7 · Agents and instruments

| | |
|---|---|
| **detected *by* the scanner** | a passive with no agent overstates every result |
| **measured *using* the list** | the instrument, not the agent |
| **zero = that list, that tool, that scope** | three conditions, all real |
| **a count needs a size** | the count rose and the density fell, in the same two quarters |

## Unit 8 · Probability and certainty

| | |
|---|---|
| **one hedge, precisely placed** | three hedges read as *I have not looked* |
| ***likely* · *possible if* · *cannot be ruled out*** | and *possible* is almost free without a condition |
| **severity is the weakness; risk is your system** | reachable · exploit in use · what is behind it |
| **a judgment with two reasons** | can be engaged with; a number can only be accepted or rejected |

## Unit 9 · Exception and applicability

| | |
|---|---|
| **all apply *unless* excluded in writing** | the mechanism |
| ***with the exception of* two** | the items themselves |
| ***not applicable* ≠ *not yet implemented*** | the most common audit finding, and a language mistake first |
| **the scope line is the whole certificate** | 93 controls, four themes, and a scope the organization chooses |

## Unit 10 · *-able* adjectives

| | |
|---|---|
| **perceivable · operable · understandable · robust** | three of the four are *-able*, and all mean *somebody can* |
| ***-ible* is a closed set** | accessible, visible, legible; anything new takes *-able* |
| **name the person, not the noun** | *a keyboard user can complete it* beats *operability* |
| **a full page, and every page in a process** | three of four pages conforming is a process that does not |

## Unit 11 · Parallel lists

| | |
|---|---|
| **accurate, complete, consistent and current** | every item the same part of speech |
| ***neither … nor*** takes no second negative | and the verb agrees with the nearer item |
| **inherent or system-dependent** | the two words that decide who can fix it |
| **precise and inaccurate** | three decimals, wrong in the first digit |

## Unit 12 · Review

| | |
|---|---|
| **source · stimulus · environment · artifact · response · measure** | six parts; most requirements have two |
| **a requirement that cannot be failed has no content** | which is why it was easy to approve |
| **four lists, no score** | risks · non-risks · sensitivity points · tradeoff points |
| **the ranking belongs to the stakeholders** | an evaluation without them produces your own opinions, formatted |

# The British layer

This book is written in American English, and the series carries a switchable British layer: a fixed
list of words is respelled at build time. The list lives with the series rather than in this book.

What the layer does not touch:

1. **A quoted standard or paper keeps its own spelling.** Every document quoted here is quoted as
   published, including the titles of papers from 1961, 1967 and 1988.
2. **A characteristic name keeps the spelling of the standard that defines it.** *Interaction
   capability*, *currentness*, *recoverability*, *perceivable*, *operable*: these are defined terms,
   and respelling a defined term misquotes its source.
3. **A document number keeps its published form.** ISO/IEC 25010:2023, ISO/IEC 5055:2021, ISO/IEC
   27001:2022, ISO/IEC 25012:2008, WCAG 2.2 and CVSS v4.0 are citations, not words.
4. **A figure keeps its form.** 99.94 percent, 8.76 hours, L = λW, 93 controls: the numbers and the
   symbols are the same in both variants, and the decimal point is never a comma in this series.
5. **The six parts of a scenario keep their order.** Source, stimulus, environment, artifact,
   response, response measure — because the order is what makes the sentence readable aloud.

# Sources

Twelve source bodies carry the twelve reading texts, one per unit, and the Starting Point carries
none. **Six are standards and six are papers or published research**, which is the balance this book
is built on. Each reading text names the document or paper, its edition, date or year, and the limits
its own authors state. No source appears in any other book of this series or in its three sister
series, and no figure is used twice. The series note is in `SOURCES.md`.

| Unit | Source | What the text prints |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **ISO/IEC 25010:2023**, *SQuaRE — Product quality model*, **second edition**, November 2023 | **Nine characteristics** and their sub-characteristics; the renaming of **usability** as **interaction capability** and of **portability** as **flexibility**; the addition of **safety**; and the positions of **availability** (under reliability) and **scalability** (under flexibility). *Not covered: measures, targets, thresholds, priorities — and quality in use, which is a separate model.* |
| 2 | **ITU-T Recommendation E.800**, *Definitions of terms related to quality of service*, **September 2008** version | The definitions of **availability** (at a given instant, or within a given time interval) and **reliability** (for a given time interval, under given conditions), and the clause that availability **assumes external resources are provided**. **Twelve honesty notes, printed in the units as well as here.**

- **Unit 1.** Nine words instead of one make the argument possible; printing *owner unknown* beside a
  target is what makes it happen. One figure had kept an engineer on call for four years for a
  contract that no longer existed.
- **Unit 2.** The average was honest and told one customer nothing about their own year. An annual
  commitment permits nearly nine hours in one go, and almost nobody in the building knew that.
- **Unit 3.** Nobody was short of data; they were short of one multiplication and the confidence to
  do it in a meeting.
- **Unit 4.** A month was spent optimizing for *sooner* when the business needed *more*. The
  arithmetic was never the hard part; the question was.
- **Unit 5.** Both settings were right and unchosen, which is not the same as right. Nine minutes of
  partition got a review; eleven million reads a day got none.
- **Unit 6.** One number out of a set of four was requested, and exactly that was delivered. The
  failure rate was the line nobody asked for and the only one worth a meeting.
- **Unit 7.** Every published figure was correct and every sentence built on it was too broad. The
  scope was in the standard and not in the report, which is where it was needed.
- **Unit 8.** Sorting by score needs no evidence and no courage, and it put a nine in dead code ahead
  of a six on the front door.
- **Unit 9.** A yes-or-no question was asked and a yes was filed. The document answered a more useful
  question, in a line nobody read.
- **Unit 10.** The audit found it and the summary lost it, which is what summaries do to exceptions.
  Forty minutes of somebody completing the task with a keyboard would have found it before a customer
  did.
- **Unit 11.** Fifteen names fixed nothing by themselves. They separated four problems that had been
  one word, and three of them turned out to be small.
- **Unit 12.** The conflict was two years old and invisible because two requirements were never on one
  page. The method produced the argument; two people in a room produced the answer.

# Closing

You began this book able to say that a system was fast, reliable and good. You end it able to ask
what that would mean: over what period, measured where, compared with what, in which units, under
what load, for which customer, and what number would settle it.

Twelve sources — six standards and six papers — and one habit: find the denominator, find the
assumption, and find the sentence in which the authors say what their work does not cover.

Four sentences to take with you.

From Unit 2: **over what period, and measured where?**

From Unit 4: **compared with what, and which question are we answering?**

From Unit 8: **is that severity or risk?**

From Unit 12: **what response measure would settle it?**

And from the whole book, which is really one idea: **the useful version of any statement is the one
that can be wrong — a figure with a period, a judgment with two reasons, a requirement that can be
failed — so stop treating *unfalsifiable* as a safe place to stand.**

Decisions, their reasons, and the records that outlive the people who made them, in Book 4.
