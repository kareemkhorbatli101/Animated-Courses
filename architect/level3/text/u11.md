# Unit 11 · Fifteen Things Data Can Be
%%SUB Some data problems belong to the data, and some belong to the system holding it%%
%%GOALS
Write parallel lists
Name data quality characteristics
Say who can fix which kind of problem
%%

*The data is bad* is a sentence that starts a long meeting. There is a published model that divides
the idea into fifteen named characteristics, and it adds a distinction that settles most of the
argument before it begins: some of them are properties of the data itself, and some are properties
of the system holding it. Those two kinds of problem have different owners.

### 2 · Read

> **Reader's Tip** — A model with fifteen names is not asking you to use all fifteen. It is giving
> you the one word that ends an argument.

%%FIG c11-f5 | A one-page internal note: a heading, a line giving the standard and its year, a three-group table of the fifteen characteristics, a boxed paragraph on the inherent and system-dependent division, a short list of what the model does not provide, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

> ## Meridian · architecture note 35
> *Data team · the data quality model · page 1 of 1*
>
> **The standard. ISO/IEC 25012:2008** is the **data quality model** in the same series as the
> product quality model of Unit 1. It defines **fifteen characteristics**, each with a definition,
> and groups them in a way that is the most useful part of the document.
>
> **Fifteen characteristics, three groups.**
>
> | Group | Characteristics |
> |---|---|
> | **inherent** — of the data itself | **accuracy**, **completeness**, **consistency**, **credibility**, **currentness** |
> | **both**, depending on viewpoint | accessibility, compliance, confidentiality, efficiency, **precision**, **traceability**, understandability |
> | **system-dependent** — of the system holding it | **availability**, **portability**, **recoverability** |
>
> **The division that settles arguments.**
>
> > An **inherent** characteristic belongs to the data **wherever it is held**. If a customer's date
> > of birth is wrong, it is wrong in the database, in the export, in the backup and on paper. No
> > change of technology affects it.
> >
> > A **system-dependent** characteristic is a property of the **thing holding** the data.
> > Recoverability, availability and portability are about the system, not the values.
> >
> > **So *the data is bad* can mean two completely different kinds of problem, with two completely
> > different owners.** You cannot make a value accurate by buying faster storage, and you cannot make
> > a slow query accurate.
>
> **Two characteristics people confuse.** **Accuracy** is whether the value is right. **Precision**
> is how finely it is recorded. A weight recorded to three decimal places that is wrong in the first
> digit is **precise and inaccurate**, and that combination has caused more trouble in this company
> than any single wrong value.
>
> **Two more that get overlooked.** **Currentness** — the data was right and is now old. And
> **traceability** — you cannot tell how a value came to be what it is, which is the characteristic
> that makes every other investigation slow.
>
> **What the model does not provide.**
>
> - **Thresholds.** No numbers, no acceptable levels, nothing to compare against.
> - **Measures.** How to turn *completeness* into a figure is a **separate standard** in the same
>   series.
> - **Priorities.** Which of the fifteen matter for your data is yours to decide, and for most
>   datasets the honest answer is three or four of them.
> - **A method.** Nothing here tells you how to improve anything.
>
> **What we do, in one line.** Every data complaint we receive gets one of the fifteen names and a
> mark saying inherent or system-dependent, because those two words have halved the length of our
> data meetings.
>
> *Source: ISO/IEC 25012:2008, Software engineering — SQuaRE — Data quality model: fifteen data
> quality characteristics with their definitions, grouped as inherent, system-dependent, or both,
> with accuracy, completeness, consistency, credibility and currentness as inherent and availability,
> portability and recoverability as system-dependent. The standard defines a vocabulary: it provides
> no thresholds, no measures — these are the subject of a separate standard in the series — and no
> priorities or improvement method.*

<sub>**inherent** — belonging to the data wherever it is held · **system-dependent** — depending on
the system holding the data · **currentness** — the degree to which the data is up to date</sub>

### 1 · Parallel lists

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD KS3-11-06%%

> **Priya:** Read me the list from your report.
> **Adeel:** *The data is accurate, complete, and we trust the source.*
> **Priya:** Two adjectives and a clause. The reader's ear expects a third adjective and gets a
> sentence, and the effect is that the third item sounds less certain than the other two — which may
> not be what you meant.
> **Adeel:** *The data is accurate, complete and credible.*
> **Priya:** Three adjectives, one list, and now all three carry the same weight. **A list in English
> promises that its items have the same shape, and a reader notices a broken promise without being
> able to say why.**
> **Adeel:** What about the pair structures?
> **Priya:** *Both … and*, *either … or*, *neither … nor*, *not only … but also*. Each one takes the
> same form on both sides. *Both accurate and complete* — two adjectives. *Either inherent or
> system-dependent* — two adjectives. If you find yourself writing *both accurate and it is
> complete*, move the *both*.
> **Adeel:** *Neither … nor* is the one I get wrong.
> **Priya:** Because it is already negative, so it never takes another negative. *The value is
> neither accurate nor current.* Not *is not neither*. And the verb agrees with the nearer item,
> which almost nobody knows and nobody will notice if you keep the items the same number.
> **Adeel:** And in a long list?
> **Priya:** Keep every item the same part of speech, and if one item refuses to fit, take it out of
> the list and give it its own sentence. **A list with one awkward item in it is harder to read than
> a list and a sentence, and much harder than it looks when you are writing it.**

*Why does Priya say the third item in Adeel's first list sounds less certain?*

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **both … and** | **both** inherent **and** measurable |

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

*Bad data* is one of those phrases that holds a company still. It describes five different problems
with five different owners, so any meeting about it contains people who cannot act on each other's
concerns, and the result is usually a large project with a vague name and no end. The fifteen names
in this unit are not valuable because they are precise; they are valuable because they
**disaggregate**. Once a complaint is called *completeness* and marked *inherent*, it has an owner
and a size. The same move works far beyond data: *the platform is slow*, *the process is broken*,
*the documentation is bad*. Each of those is a phrase waiting to be split into four things, three of
which somebody could start on Monday.

**Discuss:** Which single phrase in your company holds the most unsplit problems?

>
> **Why the groups matter.** An **inherent** problem is in **every copy** of the data — database,
> export, backup and paper. A **system-dependent** problem lives in the **thing holding** the data.
> **Cleaning values will not make an export faster, and an index will not make a value right.**

> recorded, so the honest answer is to **mark them** rather than to investigate them.
>
> **Accuracy and precision are not the same.** Two of our financial figures are recorded to **three
> decimal places** and are **wrong in the first digit**. **Precise and inaccurate**, and the
> precision is why nobody questioned them for two years.
>
> **What we are not claiming.** That these four are the only problems. The model has **fifteen
> characteristics** and we have named **four**; we have not assessed the other eleven, and **saying
> so is cheaper than implying we have**.
>
> **Not in this note.** **Measures.** Turning any of these into a tracked figure is a separate
> standard and a separate piece of work, in section 11.4.
>
> *There is no project called *fix the data*. There are four projects with four owners, and three of
> them can start this week.*

> **Writer's Tip** — The *estimate* column is what turns a complaint into work. *An afternoon* and
> *not recoverable* are both estimates, and the second is the more valuable one.
