Unit 3 · Everyday Banking Transactions

In this unit you learn the language of everyday transactions at the counter, how to talk about amounts, how to follow a conversation with a customer, and how to write a short internal note. Grammar focus: quantifiers (much, many, a few, a little).

What you can do at the end of this unit

Operator track

  • I can list what a customer can do at a counter in one day, and say which checks a teller makes before paying out.
  • I can use this unit's transaction words correctly, sort them into what money comes in and what goes out, complete a counter sentence with the right one, and pair the verbs and nouns that go together.
  • I can tell a countable noun from an uncountable one at the counter, choose much, many, a few or a little for each, correct the commonest mistake, and ask a customer three clear questions about amounts.
  • I can predict how a teller opens, follow a withdrawal, a deposit and a longer multi-step visit, and name the type of transaction from what the customer says.
  • I can take a customer through a transaction at the counter from greeting to receipt, serve three requests in a sensible order while saying what I am doing, and close the transaction properly.
  • I can read a bank's counter guidelines, find the quantifier that carries each rule, and compare the limits with those where I work.
  • I can recognise the parts of a short internal note, complete one from a word list, and write my own note about a counter problem in 50 to 70 words.
  • I can use this unit's transaction words and quantifiers again without prompting, and tick honestly what I can now do.

Practitioner track

  • I can list what a customer can do at a counter in one day, and say which checks a teller makes before paying out.
  • I can run a counter as a supervisor does: decide what to do when the queue grows, say what I need from each teller, and keep the checks that cannot be skipped.
  • I can read how a bank cut its queue, take the vocabulary of throughput and waiting from it, and argue my own view.
  • I can read what a branch does when the exchange rate moves during the day, weigh the risk to the customer and to the bank, and decide.
  • I can use this unit's transaction words and quantifiers again without prompting, and tick honestly what I can now do.
