What you can do at the end of this unit

Operator track

  • I can describe a scam I have heard of, and say what makes someone fall for it.
  • I can use this unit's security and fraud words correctly, tell one kind of scam from another, and write a short warning using at least five of the words.
  • I can state a standing rule with the zero conditional and a real possibility with the first, choose between them correctly, correct the commonest mistake, and warn a customer in both.
  • I can predict how a scam call opens, follow three conversations about fraud, and identify what the caller wanted and what gave them away.
  • I can warn a customer without frightening them, advise what to do next in a clear order, and refuse a request that would breach security while keeping the customer's trust.
  • I can read a security notice for the rule and the reason behind it, find the conditionals that carry it, and say what a customer should do first.
  • I can recognise the parts of a safety-advice message, complete one from a word list, and write my own in 50 to 70 words.
  • I can use this unit's fraud words and conditionals again without prompting, and tick honestly what I can now do.

Practitioner track

  • I can describe a scam I have heard of, and say what makes someone fall for it.
  • I can keep customers safe as a manager does: decide what to tell staff after an incident, what to tell customers, and what must be reported further.
  • I can read how a bank ran a scam-awareness campaign, take its vocabulary, and judge what would work with my own customers.
  • I can read what a branch does about a suspicious phone call, weigh the duty to the customer against the duty to the bank, and decide.
  • I can use this unit's fraud words and conditionals again without prompting, and tick honestly what I can now do.
