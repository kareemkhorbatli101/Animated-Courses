Unit 2 · Customer Service and Communication

What you can do at the end of this unit

Operator track

  • I can describe service I have received, good and bad, and act the same question twice - once badly and once well.
  • I can use this unit's polite service phrases, sort them by what they do, rewrite a blunt sentence as a professional one, and write about a good service moment using at least five of the words.
  • I can choose between can, could and may for a request and for an offer, say which is more polite and why, correct the commonest modal mistake, and build a short service dialogue around three of them.
  • I can predict the problems customers bring to a bank, match each conversation to its purpose, judge True or False on what I hear, and take the useful phrase out of each exchange.
  • I can offer and request in the register the counter needs, run a service dialogue from both sides, calm a customer who has waited too long, and close the conversation properly.
  • I can read a branch's service guidelines for what staff must and may do, find the polite modals that carry it, and compare the guidelines with my own bank's.
  • I can recognise the parts of a short service email, complete one from a word list, and write my own reply to a customer in 50 to 70 words.
  • I can use this unit's service phrases and polite modals again without prompting, and tick honestly what I can now do.

Practitioner track

  • I can describe service I have received, good and bad, and act the same question twice - once badly and once well.
  • I can coach a team member on service as a manager does: give feedback that names the behaviour rather than the person, model the phrase I want to hear, and agree what changes next week.
  • I can read how a bank builds a service culture, take its vocabulary, and give my own view on what makes service consistent.
  • I can read what a crowded branch does about waiting, weigh the options, and decide which to try first and why.
  • I can use this unit's service phrases and polite modals again without prompting, and tick honestly what I can now do.
