# From material to coursebook — the Volume 3 plan, version 2

**Volume 3 is the pilot.** Everything in this document is built once, in
Volume 3, proved by gates, and only then rolled across the other seven
books and the new bridge level. The series plan is
`IELTS_Series_Rebuild_Plan.md`; the visual system is
`IELTS_Visual_System.md`; the evidence for the diagnosis is
`IELTS_Volume_3_Pedagogy_Audit.md` and `IELTS_Series_Benchmark.md`.

## Why there is a version 2

Version 1 of this plan answered your reading of the book: technical
guide, not textbook. It added a preview reading and a preview listening,
rebuilt the grammar slot, added lead-ins and debriefs, added teacher
notes, added a contents and preview section, and added two units for
subject diversity.

Then we scored the plan against Complete IELTS and Bridge to IELTS on 72
factors. Version 1 would have taken us from **82/222 to about 150/222**,
and the benchmark found the gap version 1 did not close:

> Complete IELTS teaches a sub-skill, practises it on a short text, and
> *only then* sets the exam task. Our cycles go straight to the exam
> task. Factor 15: ours 0, theirs 3.

Previews fix the cold start. They do not fix that. A learner who
previews a passage and then answers eight matching-headings questions has
still never been taught how to match a heading. **Version 2's central
change is the sub-skill lesson**, and six smaller strands the benchmark
exposed. Nothing from version 1 is withdrawn.

## Part 1 · The new cycle: skill, then task

Today a cycle is: reading passage → questions → listening → questions →
speaking → writing → grammar → wordlists. 11.4 pages, 238 words of
teaching. The learner is tested eight times and taught once.

The rebuilt cycle, in order:

| # | beat | new? | what it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lead-in** | v1 | 3–4 questions on the sub-topic, from the learner's own life. 80 words |
| 2 | **Preview input** | v1 | a 180–260-word text (reading cycles) or 90-second recording (listening cycles) on the same sub-topic, with 3–4 easy questions. Warms the topic, pre-loads the vocabulary |
| 3 | **Vocabulary** | **v2** | the words the main input needs: a `field` or `collocation` figure, 2 exercises, 6–10 items |
| 4 | **Skills builder** | **v2** | **the sub-skill, taught and practised on a short text before any exam task.** 150 words of explanation, a worked example, 4–6 practice items that are *not* exam format |
| 5 | **Exam task** | existing | the 700–900-word passage or the full Part, unchanged, with its items |
| 6 | **Exam advice box** | **v2** | 40 words: what the examiner rewards here, what wastes time, the one trap |
| 7 | **Debrief** | v1 | why the wrong answers were tempting, keyed to the wrong-option lines we already wrote |
| 8 | **Outside the exam** | **v2** | one line: where this skill is used in a life that is not an exam |
| 9 | **Grammar, in context** | v1+v2 | rebuilt — see Part 2 |
| 10 | **Productive slot** | existing | speaking or writing, now with its own analysis figures |
| 11 | **Word list** | **v2** | the cycle's words, with the cycle each recycled word came from |

Beats 1, 2, 4, 6, 8 and 11 are the coursebook. Beat 5 is what we already
have, and it does not change.

### Why the Skills builder is the one that matters

A worked example from Unit 3, cycle 1, which currently sets eight
matching-headings questions with no preparation:

> **Skills builder · Matching headings**
> A heading names what a paragraph *does*, not what it mentions. A
> paragraph can mention money three times and still be about access.
> Read the first sentence and the last sentence; the middle is usually
> evidence for one of them.
> **Worked:** here is a 90-word paragraph and four candidate headings.
> Three name something the paragraph mentions. One names what it does.
> **Practice:** four short paragraphs, two headings each. Choose, then
> say in three words what the paragraph *does*.

Then the exam task. The learner now has a procedure, not a hope. Repeat
that across 60 cycles and 12 question types and the book has taught the
exam rather than administered it.

### The beats are distributed, not repeated

This is the cost control, and it is the main structural change from
version 1. **Not every beat appears in every cycle.** Per unit of five
cycles:

- **Lead-in, exam advice, outside-the-exam, word list, grammar** — every
  cycle, all five. They are short.
- **Preview input** — one per cycle, alternating: reading preview in
  cycles 1, 3, 5; listening preview in cycles 2, 4.
- **Skills builder** — one per cycle, following the preview's skill:
  reading sub-skill in cycles 1, 3, 5; listening sub-skill in 2, 4.
- **Vocabulary figure + exercises** — every cycle. Word-family table
  from Volume 2 upward, one a cycle.
- **Paraphrase bridge** — twice a unit, cycles 2 and 4.
- **Writing-stage work** (`taskread`, `langbank`, `ladder`) — on the
  writing-heavy cycles 4 and 5.
- **Scene, puzzle, unitmap, signpost, matrix, tracker** — once a unit.

Version 1 put every beat in every cycle and cost 28.4 pages a unit with
no skills lesson. Version 2 adds the skills lesson, the vocabulary strand
and the paraphrase strand and still costs about 34, because the
once-a-unit beats are once a unit.

## Part 2 · The grammar slot, rebuilt — and given a back-matter reference

What it is now: a part bar, an explanation, a pattern box, six items,
sometimes a fix-the-fault exercise. The explanation averages 61 words.
There is no text the grammar came out of, and no practice between the
pattern and the test.

What it becomes:

1. **In context** — 60–90 words of text, usually lifted from this
   cycle's own passage or recording, with the target structure in it.
   The learner meets the structure doing its job before meeting its name.
2. **Notice** — two questions about the text that cannot be answered
   without seeing the structure.
3. **Explain** — 120–160 words, with the meaning first and the form
   second, and the *contrast* named (why this and not the other one).
4. **Pattern box** — unchanged. It works.
5. **Practice, three graded exercises** — recognition, then controlled
   production, then a short free production tied to this unit's topic.
   8–12 items total. This is the "practice exercises inside the grammar
   section" you asked for.
6. **Pointer** — `→ Language reference, page N`.

### The Language reference, and why it is new in version 2

Benchmark factor 23: Complete IELTS has a grammar reference at the back
with fuller explanations and extra practice; we have nothing. Every
grammar slot in all twelve units points to it. It carries, per structure:
the full paradigm, the three errors learners at this band actually make,
and eight more items with answers.

Volume 3 teaches 60 grammar points, grouped into about 24 reference
entries. **24 pages.** The same reference, re-levelled, serves every
volume — which is why it is the pilot's job to design it.

Two more references follow the same logic and the same justification:

- **Writing reference** — the four task shapes, a model at band 6 and
  band 7 of each, the sentence patterns that move between them. 10 pages.
- **Speaking reference** — the three parts, the answer shapes, the
  functional language for agreeing, hedging, speculating, repairing.
  8 pages.

Total new back matter: **42 pages**, and every one of them is pointed at
from inside a unit, so none of it is decoration.

## Part 3 · Teacher notes, in a Teacher's Edition

Your instruction: teacher notes per cycle, "to force you to consider the
pedagogy and the relationship to the test itself, so the unit and each
cycle must have a clear justification in terms of how it serves the test."

The mechanism is a required field in the content, not prose in the book.
Every cycle declares:

```python
notes = dict(
    serves  = 'Matching headings, Reading Part 1-3; transfers to Listening matching',
    because = 'Band 6 learners match on repeated words, not paragraph function',
    skill   = 'Identify paragraph function from first and last sentence',
    evidence= 'Audit: 113 of 124 inputs unprepared; this cycle prepares one',
    watch   = 'Learners will pick the heading with the shared noun. Expect it',
    time    = '75 minutes, or 50 without the free grammar production',
)
```

`gate11.py` fails the build when any cycle lacks `notes`, when `serves`
names a task type `qtypes.py` cannot find in that cycle, or when
`because` is under 40 characters. **The justification becomes a build
dependency, not a good intention.** That is the real reason to do it this
way: a note in a margin can be wrong forever; a note cross-checked
against the classifier cannot.

The notes print in a **Teacher's Edition** — the same 12 units, same
pagination, with the notes, the answers and the audio scripts on the
page. It is generated from the same `content/` modules by a `--teacher`
flag on `book.py`, so it cannot drift from the student book.

## Part 4 · Front matter: contents, preview, diagnostic, progress

The audit's flattest finding: **there is no contents page in any of the
eight books.** A reader cannot see what is in front of them. Version 1
added contents in three tables and a preview. Version 2 adds the two
things the benchmark showed were still missing.

### 4.1 Contents, in three tables (v1, kept)

- **Table A · by unit** — unit, topic, the five cycle sub-topics, pages.
- **Table B · by exam task** — every task type down the side, the cycles
  that teach it across the top, generated from `qtypes.py` so it is true
  by construction.
- **Table C · by language** — grammar point and vocabulary field per
  cycle, with the reference page.

Table B is the one no competitor has, because they cannot generate it. We
can: the classifier already knows every item's type.

### 4.2 The preview, justified (v1, kept)

Six pages: what the test is, what a band means in plain words, what a
cycle is and why it has that shape, what the five skills in a cycle are
for, how to use the book alone and in class, and a worked cycle walked
through end to end with the pedagogy named.

### 4.3 Per-unit preview (v1, kept, now a figure)

One page opening each unit: the `unitmap` figure (five cycles, what each
does, which task types each carries), the `scene` figure, the `puzzle`
figure, and 60 words on why this topic is in an IELTS book at all.

### 4.4 Diagnostic test (v2, new)

Benchmark factor 65. Eight pages at the front: a short reading, a short
listening, one writing task, four speaking questions, and a scoring table
that maps each wrong answer to **the unit and cycle that teaches it**.
Bridge to IELTS and Complete IELTS both open this way, and it is the
single most useful page in a self-study book: it tells the learner where
to start.

### 4.5 Progress record (v2, new)

Two pages: the `tracker` grid — twelve units down, the five skills
across, the learner's own confidence mark and date. Mirrored by a
one-line version at each unit's end. Factor 68.

## Part 5 · The vocabulary strand (new in version 2)

Benchmark factors 24–27 are four of our worst scores: semantic fields 1,
word formation 0, collocation 1, recycling 1. The audit measured it:
**50 of 400 sampled words recur anywhere else in the volume.** A learner
meets a word once and never again, which is not how anyone learns a word.

The strand, per cycle:

1. a **`field`** or **`collocation`** figure for the cycle's topic;
2. a **`family`** table — verb, noun, person, adjective, adverb, stress
   marked — because Reading gaps test exactly this;
3. two exercises, 6–10 items, on the words the main input will use;
4. a **word list** at the cycle's end that marks every recycled word
   with the cycle it came from.

And a measured target, enforced by a new gate: **50% of each unit's
target words must reappear in a later unit of the same volume.** We can
measure this — `pedagogy.py` already does — so it can be a gate rather
than a wish. Current: 12.5%.

## Part 6 · The paraphrase strand (new in version 2)

Factor 31, where we score 0 and Cambridge scores 3. Paraphrase
recognition is the most testable single skill in IELTS: it is how
Reading gaps work, how Listening distractors work, how matching works,
and half of what Lexical Resource measures in Writing.

- **Twice a unit** (cycles 2 and 4), a **`bridge`** figure: question
  language on the left, text language on the right, the link drawn, the
  transformation named — synonym, nominalisation, voice, negation,
  hypernym.
- A 6-item exercise beside it, built from the *actual* stems and passage
  lines in this unit, so it is never generic.
- A progression across the volume: synonym and hypernym in units 1–4,
  nominalisation and voice in 5–8, negation and multi-word reformulation
  in 9–12.

24 bridges in Volume 3. It is cheap — 1.5 pages a unit — and it is
probably the highest return per page in this entire document.

## Part 7 · Subject diversity: two new units

The audit found Volume 3's ten topics repeat Volume 1's ten-for-ten.
Education, work, health, environment, technology, travel, food, money,
city life, media — the standard list, at a harder band. A learner working
up the series meets the same ten subjects six times.

### Unit 11 · How Animals Find Their Way

Animal navigation: magnetic sense in birds, the desert ant's step
counter, salmon returning by smell, the monarch's multi-generation
migration. Five cycles: the problem of navigation; birds and magnetism;
insects and counting; smell and memory; what we built instead (GPS).

Carries: a **`route`** map figure (migration with distances and dates),
process diagrams, a classification task, Task 1 line and bar data on
population and timing. Science with a story in it, which is exactly what
Academic Reading Part 3 looks like.

### Unit 12 · What the Ground Remembers

Archaeology: stratigraphy, dating methods, a dig as a decision process,
what gets preserved and what does not, who owns the past. Five cycles:
reading the layers; dating; one site, from survey to publication;
preservation and loss; whose heritage.

Carries: a **`section`** cross-section figure (stratigraphy), a
**`timeline`**, a site plan, a process flow for the dating chain, and —
the reason it is here — a genuine Part 3 and Task 2 argument about
ownership and repatriation that is not the tired "technology good or
bad".

Both units are science-with-a-narrative, both carry map, process and
classification tasks that the existing ten topics under-use, and both
give Part 3 and Task 2 something to argue about that is not on every
other IELTS book's topic list.

### The numbering decision (unchanged from v1)

Volume 3 is units 21–30 and the ten-unit arithmetic is hard-coded in six
places: `book.py` VOLS, `check.py:100`, `check.py:849`,
`density.py:153`, `density.py:216`, `freeze.py:26`, `gate10.py:41`.
Renumbering 30 units across six volumes to make room is rejected — it
would touch every paper, every answer key and every cross-reference for
no pedagogic gain.

Instead the new units are **u81 and u82**, and a single table replaces
the arithmetic:

```python
VOL_UNITS = {1: list(range(1,11)),  2: list(range(11,21)),
             3: list(range(21,31)) + [81, 82],
             4: list(range(31,41)), 5: list(range(41,51)),
             6: list(range(51,61)), 7: list(range(61,71)),
             8: list(range(71,81))}
```

Six edits, one source of truth, and every later volume can take its own
new units by appending to its row.

## Part 8 · The gates, written before the content

Eleven gates today. Three more, and every one of them written and
failing before the first unit is touched — which is how the last four
phases of this project were done and why they held.

### `gate11.py` — pedagogy

1. every cycle has `notes` with all six fields;
2. `notes['serves']` names a task type `qtypes.py` actually finds there;
3. `notes['because']` ≥ 40 characters;
4. every cycle has a lead-in, an exam-advice box and an
   outside-the-exam line;
5. every cycle has a **Skills builder** whose practice items are not in
   exam format (checked: they carry `qkind='skill'` and are excluded
   from the item census);
6. every unit has a preview reading **and** a preview listening across
   its five cycles;
7. teaching prose ≥ 35% of each cycle's words (audit measured 15.1%);
8. ≥ 6 cross-references per unit (audit measured 2 in the whole volume).

### `gate12.py` — visuals

Every figure declares what it `teaches`, from a closed vocabulary; a unit
has an activation figure; a 600+-word passage has a text-structure
figure; a cycle has a vocabulary figure; a unit has two paraphrase
figures; every question type taught has an anatomy figure somewhere.
Full rules in `IELTS_Visual_System.md`, Part 4.

### `gate13.py` — syllabus and recycling

1. **50% of each unit's target words reappear later in the volume**;
2. no grammar point taught twice in a volume without being flagged as
   revision;
3. every grammar slot points at a Language reference entry that exists;
4. the contents tables regenerate identically from the content (so they
   cannot go stale);
5. subject progression: no two units in a volume share a topic field,
   and no volume repeats more than 6 of its predecessor's 10.

Rule 5 is the stated rule for subject progression the benchmark asked
for, and it is what forces units 11 and 12 to exist rather than making
them optional.

## Part 11 · What this plan does not do

1. **No photographs, no human faces.** No image library is reachable
   from this container. Everything is generated vector art. Stated at
   length in `IELTS_Visual_System.md`, Part 6. It is a real loss against
   Cambridge and it buys gating, which no photograph has.
2. **No audio.** 273 cue cards and 1,021 texts have scripts and no
   recordings. The production spec is in the series plan; the recordings
   need voices we do not have.
3. **No native-speaker error corpus.** `notes['watch']` predicts the
   error a band-6 learner will make. That prediction is reasoned, not
   measured against learner data.
4. **The page counts in Part 10 are a design estimate.** The only
   measured numbers in this document are 569 pages, 457 figures, 15.1%
   teaching prose, 113 of 124 unprepared inputs, 2 cross-references,
   12.5% recycling, and 82/222 on the benchmark.
5. **One volume.** This is the pilot. Seven books and a bridge level
   follow, in `IELTS_Series_Rebuild_Plan.md`, and they only start once
   phase 26 has shipped 3A and 3B green.
