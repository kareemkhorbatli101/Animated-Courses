@EX Part 1: Reading - Policing Career Path
@INSTR Read the text and answer the questions.
_80
@PANEL
Many people start their policing careers as trainees. Trainees work during their studies for a small payment, but they gain important experience. After training for one or two years, graduates may become register clerks or assistant commanders. Junior officers receive training to improve their reporting and their languages. Some work on the visitor desk as desk supervisors, running a shift. Others work in the records room as register clerks or in permits as licence officers. The most experienced officers may become post commanders who answer to the directorate and train other officers. Working in policing offers opportunities to learn, to grow, and to keep work that others depend on moving.

@EX Part 4: Reading - Tourism Police Team Structure
@INSTR Read the text and answer the questions.
_80
@PANEL
Most directorates have a hierarchical structure. At the top is the Post Commander who agrees the patrol plan with the directorate and answers for the result. Below the commander are section heads who run specific areas such as records, the visitor desk, permits and equipment. Each section head supervises constables and assistants who carry out the daily work. Compliance officers check whether the rules were followed, and report to the directorate directly.
Posts also employ support staff like storekeepers, drivers, and cleaners. While these roles may seem less visible, they are essential. For example, a driver makes sure a patrol reaches the site on the promised day. A cleaner makes sure the post is safe to work in. Every role is important for the organisation to function effectively.
Recent years have seen changes in policing structures. Smaller posts operate more flexibly, with fewer management layers. This allows a decision to be taken faster. However, keeping records complete and equipment accounted for remain challenges even with simpler structures.

@PART 7 | Part 7: Culture
@EX Part 7: Policing Sector in Türkiye and the Region
@INSTR Read about policing work in Türkiye and the Middle East region, then answer questions.
_80
@PANEL
The policing sector in Türkiye has changed significantly in response to the need to serve visitors at many sites at once. Large directorates, ministries, museums and small site posts all employ officers. These officers serve both their own sites and visitors from abroad. Working in Türkiye's posts requires persistence because crowds, equipment and spare parts are all uncertain.
In neighboring countries like Bulgaria and Georgia, directorates employ officers who serve both local visitors and foreign groups. The work is challenging but essential. These organisations demonstrate how dedicated professionals keep working in hard times. Turkish officers are known in the region for their resourcefulness and persistence.
Many Turkish professionals who studied abroad chose to return to serve at posts at home. They sacrifice higher salaries in wealthy countries to protect the sites of their own towns. This shows deep commitment to the country and to the reliability of its institutions. Their choices inspire others to consider policing careers.
