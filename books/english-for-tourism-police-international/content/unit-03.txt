@PANEL
Commanders face difficult decisions when a post cannot meet every obligation at once. In any post, older arrangements rarely match current requirements. Commanders must prioritize obligations based on risk to people and on the number of parties affected. Safety and statement obligations receive priority over filing formalities. Matters affecting visitors and sites receive the strictest attention. Decisions with the highest legal exposure are examined first. Operational pressure is considered but never overrides the law. Commanders maintain transparency by explaining their reasoning to the directorate. This transparency builds trust and understanding.

_80
@PASSAGE
Policing practice is guided by core principles: legality, fairness, competence, and independence of judgement. Legality means acting only in ways the law allows, even where enforcement is weak. Fairness requires treating every visitor, site and supplier by the same rules. Competence means signing only what you are qualified to understand, and taking advice on the rest. Independence of judgement means a report rests on the facts, not on who is asking. These principles guide every professional decision and ensure ethical practice.

@PART 7 | Part 7: Culture
@EX Part 7: Law and Compliance in Mexican Contexts
@INSTR Read about law and compliance in Mexico and answer questions.
_80
@PANEL
Mexico has a long legal tradition and also a demanding compliance situation. An officer must be appointed under the Ministry of Security and Civilian Protection before taking a post, and the Ministry of Tourism and the National Institute of Anthropology and History set the rules on permits, excavation and access. Foreign missions add their own requirements on top, so a post may need a certificate from the state government, a certified translation, and a mission's own consent forms at the same time. Compliance is complicated by records held in several registries at once, permits that expire at different times, and the cost of professional advice. Lawyers and accountants work to keep posts registered alongside the tax authority. Missing paperwork affects organisations across the country. Officers demonstrate extraordinary persistence and resourcefulness. Their work includes statement drafting, record reconstruction, and the documentation a foreign mission will demand. International recognition of Mexican officers acknowledges their skill and dedication. Their resilience and procedural knowledge inspire support from partners abroad.

1. Why is compliance for a post serving foreign groups particularly challenging?
2. What is the scale of Mexico's missing-paperwork problem?
