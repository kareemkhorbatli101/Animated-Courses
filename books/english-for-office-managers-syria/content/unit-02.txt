@EX Part 1: Reading - Office Career Path
@INSTR Read the text and answer the questions.
_80
@PANEL
Many people start their office careers as trainees. Trainees work during their studies for a small payment, but they gain important experience. After training for one or two years, graduates may become registry clerks or assistant managers. Junior staff receive training to improve their filing and their correspondence. Some work at the front desk as reception supervisors, running a shift. Others work in the registry as records clerks or in personnel as staff officers. The most experienced staff may become office managers who answer to the directors and train other staff. Working in administration offers opportunities to learn, to grow, and to keep work that others depend on moving.

@EX Part 4: Reading - Office Structure
@INSTR Read the text and answer the questions.
_80
@PANEL
Most organisations have a hierarchical structure. At the top is the Office Manager who agrees the office plan with the directors and answers for the result. Below the manager are section heads who run specific areas such as the registry, reception, personnel and supplies. Each section head supervises clerks and assistants who carry out the daily work. Compliance officers check whether the rules were followed, and report to the directors directly.
Offices also employ support staff like storekeepers, drivers, and cleaners. While these roles may seem less visible, they are essential. For example, a driver makes sure a document reaches the authority on the promised day. A cleaner makes sure the registry is safe to work in. Every role is important for the organisation to function effectively.
Recent years have seen changes in office structures. Smaller firms operate more flexibly, with fewer management layers. This allows a decision to be taken faster. However, keeping records complete and supplies accounted for remain challenges even with simpler structures.

@PART 7 | Part 7: Culture
@EX Part 7: Office Sector in Syria and the Region
@INSTR Read about office work in Syria and the Middle East region, then answer questions.
_80
@PANEL
The office sector in Syria has changed significantly in response to the need to reconstruct records that were lost or damaged. Large manufacturers, ministries, clinics and small family firms all employ office staff. These staff serve both their own departments and customers abroad. Working in Syria's offices requires persistence because power, equipment and spare parts are all uncertain.
In neighboring countries like Lebanon and Jordan, companies employ staff who keep records for both local use and export documentation. The work is challenging but essential. These organisations demonstrate how dedicated professionals keep working in hard times. Syrian office staff are known in the region for their resourcefulness and persistence.
Many Syrian professionals who studied abroad chose to return to run administrations at home. They sacrifice higher salaries in wealthy countries to keep records in order in their own towns. This shows deep commitment to the country and to the reliability of its institutions. Their choices inspire others to consider office careers.
