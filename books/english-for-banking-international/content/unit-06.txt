@TASK 2A | Notice the grammar
@INSTR Read the short dialogue. Underline the indirect questions. What happens to the word order?
@SCRIPT

@NUM
“what today’s rate is” — where is the verb? ____
“how long the transfer takes” — is there do/does? ____
whether — what kind of question does it introduce? ____
@GF
@GRAMMAR
Form | Example
Could you tell me + question word + subject + verb | Could you tell me what the rate is? |
Do you know + question word + subject + verb | Do you know how long it takes? |
May I ask + whether / if + subject + verb | May I ask whether you have the IBAN? |
@REMEMBER in an indirect question the subject comes before the verb, and do / does / did disappears. Use whether or if when there is no question word.
@WATCH
✗ Could you tell me what is the rate? → ✓ Could you tell me what the rate is?
✗ Do you know how long does it take? → ✓ Do you know how long it takes?

@TASK 5C | How a transfer moves
@INSTR Read the short note. Which banks touch the money?
@READ Three Banks, One Payment | About 120 words
When money is sent from one country to another, it usually passes through three banks, not two. The sender’s bank takes the money and converts it. A correspondent bank in the middle moves it between the two countries, because the first and last banks may have no direct link. The beneficiary’s bank then credits the account. Each bank checks the payment against its own country’s rules, and each check takes a little time. This is why an international transfer takes a day, while a payment inside one country can be instant. It is also why a missing BIC is serious: without it, the middle bank does not know where to send the money next.

@PART Part 8 · Global Case Study — How Money Crosses Borders | Practitioner track · read then discuss
@INSTR Before you read: why does money sent abroad cost more and take longer than money sent at home?
@READ The Cost of Sending Home | About 205 words · a made-up example based on real practice
Every year, people working away from home send money back to their families. For many countries these payments, called remittances, are larger than all foreign aid together. The people who send them are usually not rich, and the amounts are small — often two or three hundred at a time. That is the problem. A fee of twelve is small on a transfer of five thousand. On a transfer of two hundred, it is six per cent of the money, and the spread on the exchange rate may cost as much again. An international body set a target: the average cost of sending money home should fall below three per cent. One bank tried to reach it. It could not cut the fee, because each payment is checked by three banks and each check costs money. Instead it changed the product. Customers who send money to the same person every month pay one fee for twelve payments, set up once. The checks are done at the start, not twelve times. The cost for those customers fell from six per cent to just under two. The bank did not make the transfer cheaper. It made it fewer transfers.
@GLOSS
remittance — money sent home by someone working abroad
spread — the difference between the buy rate and the sell rate
foreign aid — money given by one country to another
standing transfer — the same payment, set up once and repeated

@PART Part 9 · Branch Case Study — Manila in Focus: Money from Abroad | Practitioner track · read then decide
@INSTR Before you read: what should a branch do when a payment from abroad goes missing?
@READ The Payment That Stopped | About 185 words
It is the first week of the month at the Harbour Bank branch in Manila, and the counter is busy with people collecting money sent from abroad. Mrs Reyes has been waiting since the branch opened. Her son works overseas and sends two hundred on the same day every month. This month it has not arrived, and she needs it today to pay her rent. Priya Raman checks the system. The payment left her son’s bank eight days ago, but it is sitting at a correspondent bank, because the name on the transfer is “M. Reyes” and the account is held as “Maria Reyes Santos”. The correspondent bank will not release it until the names match. Fixing it will take two more days, and the request must come from the sending bank, not from Manila. Mrs Reyes is calm but close to tears. The branch has a small hardship fund for exactly this kind of case, but it needs the manager’s approval, and the manager is away until tomorrow.
