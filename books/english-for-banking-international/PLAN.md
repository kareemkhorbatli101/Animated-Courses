# English for Banking & Finance · International Edition — Adaptation Plan

Adapting **English for Banking & Finance · Professional Student's Book · Level B1**
(Operator & Practitioner tracks, Syria edition) into an **International Edition**.

Same method as the three books before it: measure the source first, then transpose. This
is a structural twin, built to the source's own measurements.

### The two tracks

Parts 1–6 are the **Operator track** (lower B1), for staff in their first months.
Parts 7–10 are the **Practitioner track** (upper B1), for staff with a few years'
experience. Parts 1, 2, 3 and 5 are marked *Pre-class input*; Parts 4 and 6 are
*In-class / homework output*; Part 10 is *Homework / self-study*. The book is designed
for a flipped classroom and says so in its front matter.

## 2 · The transposition

The source teaches banking English inside **one country**. It says so plainly: the
warm-up discussion is *Banking in Syria*, the company profile is a Syrian bank, and
Part 9 is *Local Case Study — Syria in Focus*. Part 8, by contrast, is already a
**Global Case Study**, so the book's own architecture contains the move the International
Edition makes: it takes the global/local pairing and puts the whole book inside it.

| | Source | International Edition |
|---|---|---|
| The bank | **Al Aman Bank**, a leading Syrian bank | **Harbour Bank**, an international bank |
| Where it works | branches across Syria | a head office in **Rotterdam**, branches in **Nairobi · Istanbul · São Paulo · Manila · Warsaw**, and correspondent partners elsewhere |
| Customers | people and small businesses in one country | the same, plus people who are paid in one country and send money to another |
| Money | one currency | several currencies, and the rate between them |
| The rules | one rule book | one rule book per country, plus the international ones everybody shares |
| W-B discussion | *Banking in Syria* | *Banking where you live* — the learner's own country is the comparison, in any country |
| Part 9 | *Local Case Study — Syria in Focus* | *Branch Case Study — <city> in Focus*, rotating through the bank's six locations |

This is the one decision the book turns on. Making the bank **international** does not add
a topic; it gives every topic the source already teaches a second dimension, at no cost to
the syllabus:

* a **transfer** is not only to another branch but to another country and currency — so
  Unit 6 teaches the vocabulary a cashier actually needs;
* a **customer** may be served in their second language by staff in their third — so
  Unit 2's politeness and clarity have a reason beyond manners;
* **fraud** arrives from outside the country and the money leaves it — Unit 7;
* **compliance** is not one rule book but several that must all be satisfied at once —
  Unit 8;
* **corporate banking** means trade: a buyer in one country, a seller in another, and a
  letter of credit between them — Unit 10.

And the learner the book is written for changes in the way the brief asks: from a person
working in a bank in one country to a person working in a bank that reaches across them.

### The syllabus (unchanged)

| Unit | Title | Grammar |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Getting Started in International Banking | present simple vs present continuous |
| 2 | Customer Service and Communication | modals for politeness and offers |
| 3 | Everyday Banking Transactions | quantifiers and countable/uncountable |
| 4 | Banking Products and Services | comparatives, and forms for advising |
| 5 | Digital and Online Banking | the passive |
| 6 | Currency Exchange and Money Transfer | indirect questions |
| 7 | Security and Fraud | past tenses for reporting an incident |
| 8 | Rules and Compliance | obligation and prohibition |
| 9 | Saving and Investing Basics | conditionals for advice |
| 10 | Corporate Banking | future forms and formal correspondence |

## 3 · Fidelity criteria

Items marked **[auto]** are checked by `qa/check_parity.py`; the rest by read-through.

**Architecture**
1. 10 units, same order, same grammar sequence. **[auto]**
2. 44 named tasks per unit in the source's exact code sequence; 47 in Unit 1, 45 in
   Units 2 and 10. **[auto]**
3. Warm-Up + Parts 1–10 in every unit, with the source's part titles and track notes. **[auto]**
4. Parts 1–6 Operator track, Parts 7–10 Practitioner track, labelled as the source labels them. **[auto]**
5. Five illustrations per unit, at the source's five slots. **[auto]**
6. Four audio tracks per unit, numbered n.1–n.4. **[auto]**
7. 6–8 matching tables per unit, in the source's Column A / Column B form. **[auto]**
8. Every unit closes: spiral review → Can-Do checklist → glossary box. **[auto]**

**Exercise system**
9. Every matching task has one extra option, and says so.
10. Every task that can be exemplified opens with a worked `Example: 0. …`.
11. Every writing task gives a model, a plan or frame, a phrase bank and a ☐ check list.
12. Every speaking task gives a model and role cards.
13. Every grammar point has a Grammar Focus table, a Remember line and a ⚠ Watch out! box.
14. Part 2 ends with a Tip pointing forward to where the grammar reappears in Part 5.

**Language and level**
15. Operator parts at lower B1, Practitioner parts at upper B1, at the source's density.
16. British spelling; the source's typography (`·`, `—`, `→`, `☐`, `✎`, `⚠`, `🔊`, curly quotes).
17. No unit uses its grammar in a text before the unit that teaches it, except as recognition.

**Texts**
18. 5A company profile 127–169 words, declared "About 200 words" as the source declares it. **[auto]**
19. 5C team-member profile 67–109 words. **[auto]**
20. Part 8 global case 187–214 words; Part 9 branch case 136–177. **[auto]**
21. Listening scripts: 3B 48–80 · 3C 48–79 · 3D 80–107 · 7B 67–91 words. **[auto]**
22. Writing models 7–49 words, six per unit. **[auto]**
23. Glossary box 70–97 words per unit. **[auto]**

**Visuals**
24. 52 illustrations: five per unit, a cover, and one extra in Unit 10. **[auto]**
25. 6.5 in wide, from 3200 px artwork, in the source's flat banner style. **[auto]**
26. Every illustration carries the unit's own subject.

**Integrity**
27. Everything about Harbour Bank is invented and declared invented.
28. The banking facts are true and general: IBAN and BIC, correspondent banking, KYC,
    sanctions screening, exchange rates and spreads, letters of credit.
29. No real bank, regulator, payment network or person is given invented words or figures.
30. No country is characterised; the six cities are settings, not subjects. The learner's
    own country is invited into the discussion tasks, never described for them.

**Production**
31. The source's design system, decoded from its OOXML: Calibri, the navy part bars, the
    five boxed-table types, the matching and word-bank geometry, A4 with its margins. **[auto]**
32. Total length within ±8 % of the source, and the page count within ±8 %. **[auto]**

## 4 · Build

A new toolchain, because this is a fourth design system: `build/render.py` (the decoded
design system), `build/compile.py` (DSL → .docx), `build/render_md.py` (DSL → .md),
`build/figures.py` (the drawing toolkit) and `build/make_figures.py` (the 52 illustrations).
