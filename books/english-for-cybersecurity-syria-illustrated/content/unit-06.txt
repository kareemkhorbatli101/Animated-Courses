@EX Part 2: Grammar Focus Box
@GRAM 1700,2450,2200,2676
 | Form | Use | Example

@IMG fig-06-4.png

@IT Before you read: is it more important for an officer to be fast, or to be honest?
@GL routine = regular, done again and again at the same interval    ·    due = ready to be done, because the interval has passed    ·    trust = believing that somebody will tell you the truth
@BX Routine maintenance is the regular care that keeps a system safe and working. It is dull work, and that is why it is skipped. The schedule is based on how important the system is, not on how old it is: the machine holding the staff records is checked monthly even if it is new, and the store’s spare twice a year even if it is ancient. At every monthly visit the updates are installed, the accounts are reviewed, and one file is brought back from the backup, because a backup nobody has tested is only a hope. A good officer also knows what not to do. There is always a temptation to sell a new machine when an hour’s work would do, or to ask for a bigger disk when the real problem is ten years of films somebody saved on the server. An officer who says “this does not need replacing yet” is worth a great deal, because the next time that officer says “this does need replacing”, the manager believes it. Honest advice builds trust, and trust is what brings a user to the help desk on the first day of a problem instead of the tenth.

@EX Part 5: Honest Maintenance in Damascus
@IT Before you read: why is a trusted officer so valuable to a small company?
@GL tight = not much (money)    ·    word of mouth = people telling each other about good work
@BX In Damascus, many companies keep the same machines for eight or ten years, because new ones are expensive and hard to import. Money is tight, and every company has heard a story about somebody who was sold three new computers and a server they did not need. Maher’s rule at Cham Systems is simple: recommend only the work the system really needs, and say plainly when something can wait. Last spring a company came to him after another firm had told them their whole system had to be replaced, at a price that would have taken their profit for a year. Lina spent one afternoon with the machines and found the real problem: a failing disk in the server, and a backup that had quietly stopped eleven months before. The disk cost very little. She replaced it, restarted the backup and tested it in front of the owner. Cham Systems earned nothing that day. But that company has sent four other businesses to Maher since, and in Damascus, word of mouth is worth more than any advertisement.

@PART Part 8  ·  Global Case Study — The Maintenance Plan That Kept Customers
@TRK [PR] Practitioner track  ·  pre-read / in-class discuss
@IT Before you read: why do some companies only call for help when something has already broken?
@GL maintenance plan = paying a fixed price for regular care    ·    unplanned = happening suddenly, with no warning    ·    downtime = time when people cannot work
@BX An IT company once lost many customers, and it could not understand why: its engineers were good and always came quickly. The new manager looked at two years of records and saw the pattern. Every customer they had lost had called them three or four times in a panic, with something badly broken, and had paid a large bill each time. The customers did not blame the engineers; they simply felt that the company only appeared when it was expensive. So the manager offered something new: a maintenance plan at a fixed price each month, which included the updates, a tested backup, an account review, and one visit a quarter whether anything was wrong or not. The first year was harder work for less money. But unplanned downtime among those customers fell by about two thirds, the panic calls nearly stopped, and almost none of them left. The manager said afterwards that they had started selling the thing customers actually wanted, which was not repairs: it was not having to think about it.

@PART Part 9  ·  Local Case Study — Syria in Focus: Only the Work the System Needs
@TRK [PR] Practitioner track  ·  pre-read / in-class decide
@IT Before you read: why might a company be worried when it is told its whole system must be replaced?
@BX A young accountant, Samer, runs a small firm in Aleppo with five machines and one old server. Another company told him the server was finished and he needed a new one, three new machines and a new licence for everything — a price that would have taken a year of his profit. He came to Cham Systems expecting to be told the same thing. Lina spent an afternoon with them.
