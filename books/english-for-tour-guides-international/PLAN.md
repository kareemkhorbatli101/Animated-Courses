# English for Tour Guides — Student Book (A2) · International Edition

This is the International Edition. Its twin, the Syrian Edition, is in
`books/english-for-tour-guides-syria/`. The two are exact structural twins —
all 30 metrics, all 5,455 elements, the same 251 pages, every one of 856 section
openings on the identical page — and differ only in where each unit is set. The
plan below covers both, and §3 gives this edition's ten countries.

## 3 · The two settings

The **Syrian Edition** is set in Syria throughout. Its spine is keeping a
guiding office working while the sites it interprets are themselves being
repaired: the Ministry of Tourism that licenses every guide before they may work
with a group, the Tourist Guides Syndicate that sets the professional rules, the
Directorate-General of Antiquities and Museums that issues the site permits, and
the caravan guides who led pilgrims and merchants from khan to khan on the
caravan roads for four hundred years before any of it. Its case office is the
Barada office in Damascus, whose lead guide is Rana; its sites are the Old City
of Damascus, Palmyra, Bosra, the Aleppo citadel and Krak des Chevaliers. Its
constraints are real ones: interrupted power in the middle of a long site walk,
vehicles that are hard to repair, headsets too few to cover a large group,
certified translation that costs more than the tour, and sites damaged or looted
in the conflict whose records have to be reconstructed.

The **International Edition** moves each unit to a different country, keeping
every element in place:

| ---: | --- | --- | --- |

| 2 | **Türkiye** | Culture and Tourism · Union of Tourist Guides Chambers · Cultural Heritage and Museums | serving guests at many sites at once; guides returning from abroad |

| 4 | **Morocco** | Tourism · National Federation of Tourist Guides · National Foundation of Museums | coach hire, repair costs, certified translation, long seasonal peaks |

| 6 | **Brazil** | Tourism · National Union of Tourist Guides · National Historic and Artistic Heritage Institute | *respeito*; the duty to declare a fault; the trail guides |

## 7 · Template quirks reproduced

1. The instruction-box tint rotates out of order in the last three parts: Part 6
   takes Part 8's tint, Part 7 takes Part 6's and Part 8 takes Part 7's, in every
   unit.
2. Unit 1 has no Part 6, and so 37 exercises where every other unit has 41.
3. Unit 2 carries four one-off dialogue elements that appear nowhere else, the
   same narrator text printed twice in two different treatments.
4. Every exercise heading carries a second, undefined `[object Object]`
   paragraph style, which is what makes the headings render as body text.
5. Short multiple choices lay their options out inside the stem paragraph,
   behind fifteen spaces and a line break, instead of as option paragraphs.
6. The answer-key strip sizes its cells from how many answers it carries and
   leaves the unused columns as 26-twip slivers.
7. Speaker voice colours are assigned per track rather than per role, so the
   same job title is blue in one unit and purple in another.
8. `docDefaults` names Times New Roman and leaves `pPrDefault` empty; the single
   list defines nine bullet levels cycling ● ○ ■ as literal characters.

## 8 · One deliberate divergence

The template's contents page runs one topic behind its units: Unit 3 is called
"International Organizations" but is about humanitarian crises, Unit 7 is called
"Working with Communities" but is about health and education, and so on through
Unit 10. Reproducing that offset in a new book would mean shipping ten units
whose titles describe the wrong content. Both Tour Guides editions therefore
name each unit for what it actually contains. Everything structural is
unchanged: the contents page still has ten entries, each unit still opens with
one Heading1, and all 30 metrics match.

## 9 · Where the editions necessarily differ from the template

- **+5,600 words** (71,245 and 71,300 against 65,644), almost all of it in
  Appendix B, where answers were written to the template's per-answer lengths
  and a few run long.
- **106 em dashes against 126** — the template's prose reaches for the dash more
  often than guiding description does.
- **251 pages against 257**: with the same elements and the same spacing,
  guiding prose fills its lines slightly more completely than the template's.
  Both editions land on the same 251, which is the number that matters.

## 10 · How the editions were built, and what QA found

The Syrian Edition was derived from the Tourism Police A2 book, whose structure
it shares element for element. The profession was moved across in five ordered
passes over the DSL — a vocabulary map (post → office, officer → guide,
commander → lead guide, patrol → tour, visitor → guest, site → agency), a
cleanup pass, a sense pass, an institution pass and a role pass — with the DSL
skeleton diff and the per-line length check run after every pass, so no pass was
allowed to move an element or stretch a line.

Five classes of defect were found and fixed:

- **Substring collisions in the vocabulary map.** `site → agency` corrupted
  *visited* and *opposite*; `gate → meeting point` corrupted *investigate*,
  *navigate* and *delegate*; `patrol → tour` corrupted *patrolling*; `unit →
  office` corrupted *community*. A scan for the new words embedded mid-word
  found all nine and they were repaired.
- **Collateral damage from the article fix.** Rewriting `an guide → a guide`
  also hit *Syrian guides* → *Syria guides*, *Syrian tour* → *Syria tour* and
  *can lead guides* → *ca lead guides*. A scan for a bare `a` before the new
  nouns found every instance.
- **Job titles swept up by the generic role map.** *Post services officer*,
  *permit clerk* and *purchasing officer* had been protected, but *licence
  officer*, *operations officer* and the *Purchasing Officer* matching item had
  not, and each briefly became a "guide". A scan for `<noun> guides` caught them.
- **Place senses of "site" turned into "agency".** The heritage-site, site-pass
  and site-permit senses were protected up front, but *protects the sites*,
  *sites that were damaged* and *site protection* in the pronunciation list were
  not; they were restored by hand along with the institution panels, which were
  rewritten so that a guide is *licensed* rather than *appointed*.
- **Two section openings a page out.** In the International Edition the Unit 5
  reading section ran short and the Appendix B Unit 9 section ran long, moving
  two headings by one page in opposite directions. Length was added to Unit 5's
  country one-offs and trimmed from Unit 8's, the edition was re-derived from a
  fresh copy, and both editions now render to 251 pages with all 856 section
  openings on the identical page.
