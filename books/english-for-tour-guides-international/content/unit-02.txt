@EX Part 1: Reading - Guiding Career Path
@INSTR Read the text and answer the questions.
_80
@PANEL
Many people start their guiding careers as trainees. Trainees work during their studies for a small payment, but they gain important experience. After training for one or two years, graduates may become register clerks or deputy lead guides. Junior guides receive training to improve their reporting and their languages. Some work on the guest desk as desk supervisors, running a shift. Others work in the records room as register clerks or in permits as licence officers. The most experienced guides may become lead guides who answer to the company and train other guides. Working in guiding offers opportunities to learn, to grow, and to keep work that others depend on moving.

@EX Part 4: Reading - Tour Guide Team Structure
@INSTR Read the text and answer the questions.
_80
@PANEL
Most companies have a hierarchical structure. At the top is the Lead Guide who agrees the tour plan with the company and answers for the result. Below the lead guide are section heads who run specific areas such as records, the guest desk, permits and equipment. Each section head supervises guides and assistants who carry out the daily work. Compliance officers check whether the rules were followed, and report to the company directly.
Offices also employ support staff like storekeepers, drivers, and cleaners. While these roles may seem less visible, they are essential. For example, a driver makes sure a tour reaches the agency on the promised day. A cleaner makes sure the office is safe to work in. Every role is important for the organisation to function effectively.
Recent years have seen changes in guiding structures. Smaller offices operate more flexibly, with fewer management layers. This allows a decision to be taken faster. However, keeping records complete and equipment accounted for remain challenges even with simpler structures.

@PART 7 | Part 7: Culture
@EX Part 7: Guiding Sector in Türkiye and the Region
@INSTR Read about guiding work in Türkiye and the Middle East region, then answer questions.
_80
@PANEL
The guiding sector in Türkiye has changed significantly in response to the need to serve guests at many sites at once. Large companies, ministries, museums and small agency offices all employ guides. These guides serve both their own agencies and guests from abroad. Working in Türkiye's offices requires persistence because crowds, equipment and spare parts are all uncertain.
In neighboring countries like Bulgaria and Georgia, companies employ guides who serve both local guests and foreign groups. The work is challenging but essential. These organisations demonstrate how dedicated professionals keep working in hard times. Turkish guides are known in the region for their resourcefulness and persistence.
Many Turkish professionals who studied abroad chose to return to serve at offices at home. They sacrifice higher salaries in wealthy countries to interpret the sites of their own towns. This shows deep commitment to the country and to the reliability of its institutions. Their choices inspire others to consider guiding careers.
