# English for Business · International Edition — Adaptation Plan

Adapting **EFB · Al Manzil Restaurant, Dubai — Illustrated Student's Book (Units 1–10)**
into **English for Business · International Edition**.

Same method as the two journalism books: measure the source first, then transpose. This is
a structural twin, built to the source's own measurements — not a book in its spirit.

## 2 · The transposition

The source teaches business English through **one restaurant in one city**: Al Manzil,
Dubai, one currency, one tax rule, one ledger. Every piece of accounting content it
teaches — recording a sale, closing a month, forecasting, an audit — is taught inside
that single frame.

The International Edition keeps the frame and makes it **cross-border**: the same five
jobs, the same ten grammar points, the same accounting syllabus, now in a restaurant
group that trades in five countries and five currencies and consolidates into one set
of books.

| | Source | International Edition |
|---|---|---|
| The business | **Al Manzil Restaurant**, Dubai | **Blue Door Group** — five restaurants in five countries |
| Head office | the accounts office upstairs | the **group accounts office in Lisbon** |
| Branches | *(none — one site)* | Lisbon · Nairobi · Istanbul · Singapore · Montréal |
| Currency | dirhams (AED) | branch currencies (EUR · KES · TRY · SGD · CAD), reporting in **euro** |
| Tax | one VAT rule | a different VAT/GST rule in every country |
| The owner | Mr Khalid, who opened in 2016 | **Mr Alvarez**, who opened the first Blue Door in 2016 |
| The month-end | one office closes its books | five branches close, then the group consolidates |
| The audit | an auditor visits the office | a group auditor reviews five sets of records |

This is the one decision the book turns on. Making the business a **group** means every
accounting idea the source teaches acquires a second, international dimension that costs
the syllabus nothing and teaches the learner far more:

* a **sale** is recorded in Singapore dollars and reported in euro — so the present tenses
  are practised on exchange rates as well as routines;
* a **mistake** happens in one branch and has to be explained to another — so the past
  tenses are practised across a time difference;
* a **price** can be compared between five countries — so comparatives have real data;
* a **month-end** has five deadlines in five time zones — so the first conditional and
  time clauses have something real to be conditional about;
* **compliance** means five different rules, not one — so the modals of obligation matter.

| Supplier and invoice clerk | **Sami** | **Can**, accounts payable, Istanbul |

Blue Door, its five restaurants, its staff and every figure in the book are invented, for
practice. The business facts — how VAT and GST work, what an exchange difference is, what
an auditor asks for, what the five values of an honest accountant are — are true.

### The Five Values

The source's closing idea is five values serving one purpose: **accuracy, honesty, good
records, confidentiality, compliance → trust**. They are kept exactly, and each one gains
a cross-border edge: accuracy *in every currency*, honesty *between branches*, records
*that a group auditor in another country can follow*, confidentiality *under five data
rules*, compliance *with five tax authorities*.

## 3 · Fidelity criteria

Items marked **[auto]** are checked by `qa/check_parity.py`; the rest by read-through.

**Architecture**
1. 10 units, same order, same grammar sequence. **[auto]**
2. 53 named task slots per unit, 55 in Unit 10, in the source's exact order and naming. **[auto]**
3. Unit head: `Unit n: <title>` + `<grammar> · B1.1–B1.2 · <setting>`. **[auto]**
4. Warm Up and Part 10 carry the orange banner; Parts 1–9 the blue banner. **[auto]**
5. 11 illustrations per unit, one per section, each with its `[OP]`/`[RA]` track label
   above and an italic caption below. **[auto]**
6. 15 tables per unit: 7 matching tasks in the source's exact shapes, plus the grammar
   focus box. **[auto]**
7. 10 multiple-choice stems per unit, each with four options A–D. **[auto]**
8. 5 audio tracks per unit, numbered n.1–n.5. **[auto]**
9. Part 7 is the `[RA]` Restaurant track; every other part is `[OP]` Accounts track. **[auto]**

**Exercise system**
10. Every matching task has one more meaning than terms ("One meaning is not needed").
11. Every task that can be exemplified opens with a worked `0.` item marked `(example)`.
12. Word banks have four items and the gapped text has four gaps. **[auto]**
13. Every Part 6 task and 7E has a model, a plan frame where the source has one, and a
    `Check before you finish:` line of three ☐ items. **[auto]**
14. Part 9 always: a scenario, three questions, a three-way choice, an answer frame, a
    model decision and a phrase bank.
15. Part 10 always: spiral review (4 gaps), a 4-item Can-Do checklist, and the glossary.

**Language and level**
16. CEFR B1.1–B1.2 throughout, at the source's sentence length and density.
17. British spelling; the source's typographic conventions (`·`, `—`, `→`, `☐`, `○`, `🔊`,
    curly quotes, en-dashes in ranges).
18. Grammar is explained in the source's voice: a prose *Notice*, then the four-row focus
    box, then *Remember*, then *Watch out!*.
19. No unit uses its grammar in a text before the unit that teaches it, except as
    deliberate recognition; earlier units' grammar is freely recycled.

**Texts**
20. Warm-up reading 181–203 words. **[auto]**
21. Part 5 reading A 197–241 words; reading B 173–183. **[auto]**
22. Part 8 reading 197–222 words; Part 9 scenario 176–205. **[auto]**
23. Part 3 dialogues 158–196 / 127–157 / 127–150 words; 7B 151–168. **[auto]**
24. Writing models 94–121 words, six per unit. **[auto]**
25. Glossary 117–157 words per unit. **[auto]**

**Visuals**
26. 110 illustrations, one per section, in the source's flat-vector language: pale blue
    ground, thin border, dashed rule ticks, centred navy title, rounded cards with
    coloured borders and gradient fills, flat figures with round heads and coloured
    torsos, a floor line near the foot. **[auto]**
27. Artwork 3000 px wide, placed 5.42 in wide and 2.60–3.38 in tall. **[auto]**
28. Every caption teaches, in the source's register.

**Integrity**
29. Everything about Blue Door is invented and declared invented.
30. The business and accounting facts are true: VAT and GST, exchange differences,
    accruals, an audit trail, the five values.
31. No real company, bank, tax authority or person is given invented words or figures.
32. No country is characterised; the five cities are settings, not subjects.

**Production**
33. The source's design system, decoded from its OOXML: Calibri throughout, the exact
    colour set, the exact shaded-paragraph vocabulary, A4 with 1-inch margins. **[auto]**
34. Total length within ±8 % of the source, and the page count within ±8 %. **[auto]**

## 4 · Build

A new toolchain, because this is a different design system from the journalism books:
`build/render.py` (the decoded design system), `build/compile.py` (DSL → .docx),
`build/render_md.py` (DSL → .md), `build/figures.py` (the drawing toolkit) and
`build/make_figures.py` (the 110 illustrations).
