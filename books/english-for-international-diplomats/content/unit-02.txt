The Senegal–Türkiye Infrastructure Cooperation Working Group held its inaugural session at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Dakar on a Tuesday morning in March 2025. The session had been in preparation for six weeks. Aïssatou Diallo, the ministry's Protocol and Liaison Officer, coordinated all logistical arrangements with Emre Yıldırım, the Turkish Embassy's logistics officer, through a series of phone calls and formal emails. The agenda was circulated to both sides five days before the session.
The Turkish delegation, led by Dr. Elif Kaya, Deputy Director of the Turkish Cooperation and Coordination Agency, arrived at the ministry's executive entrance at nine-thirty for a pre-meeting briefing in the protocol lounge. The host delegation was led by Moussa Sarr, Economic Affairs Specialist, who had prepared a detailed protocol brief for the minister's office covering the Turkish team's key positions and the three main areas of discussion: road network upgrades in priority regions, water infrastructure grants, and the establishment of a joint technical monitoring committee.
The session opened formally at ten o'clock with a statement by Moussa Sarr on behalf of the ministry, followed by a response from Dr. Kaya. The morning was dedicated to road network upgrades, with technical presentations from both sides. After a working lunch at twelve-thirty, the afternoon session addressed water infrastructure. At four o'clock, the two delegation leads signed an aide-mémoire recording the agreed framework for the joint technical committee. A brief press statement was issued jointly, and both sides expressed commitment to a second working group session within three months.
Aïssatou Diallo distributed the post-meeting summary to all participants within 24 hours, as required by ministry protocol. She noted in her internal report that the most critical coordination decisions had been the switch of conference rooms two days before the session and the last-minute arrangement of Turkish interpretation for two technical specialists — both resolved without the visiting delegation experiencing any disruption.

@INSTR In coordination calls, three types of information must be perfectly clear: names, dates, and times. A misheard name or wrong date can cause serious coordination failures. // Practice these techniques: // 1. For names: slow down and stress each syllable clearly. "Dr. E-lif KA-ya." @EX Role-Play: Opening a Meeting with a European Environmental NGO
@INSTR Work in pairs. Student A plays Moussa Sarr, host delegation lead. Student B plays Lars Virtanen, team lead of a Finnish environmental NGO visiting to discuss the Coastal Restoration Program. // Context: The meeting starts on time. Lars introduces his team informally: "Please, call me Lars." The agenda has three items and two hours. The Finnish team uses first names throughout. // Practice: // 1. Open the meeting formally, then accept the informal register gracefully // 2. Work through the first agenda item: reviewing the current Restoration Program scope // 3. A moment of silence after your first proposal — do not rush to fill it; allow Lars to respond // Guidance phrases: // • "Thank you, Lars. On behalf of the ministry, we are very pleased to have you here today." // • "I suggest we follow the agenda we circulated — does that work for your team?" // • "I would welcome your team's perspective on this proposal before we proceed."
@EX Role-Play: Opening a Meeting with a North African Ministerial Delegation
@INSTR Work in pairs. Student A plays Aïssatou Diallo, welcoming the visiting Moroccan delegation to the protocol lounge before the formal session. Student B plays His Excellency Youssef Benali, Deputy Foreign Minister of Morocco. // Context: The delegation has just arrived. There is a social welcome in the protocol lounge before the formal session begins. Mint tea and pastries have been arranged. His Excellency is accompanied by two advisers. // Practice: // 1. Welcome the delegation with full protocol: title, warmth, appreciation for the visit // 2. Offer hospitality without rushing to business // 3. His Excellency begins with social conversation — respond warmly and follow his lead // 4. Only transition to the session when His Excellency signals he is ready // Guidance phrases: // • "Your Excellency, on behalf of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, we are deeply honored by your visit to Dakar." // • "Please make yourself comfortable. We have arranged tea — may I offer you a cup?" // • "We look forward to a very productive session whenever Your Excellency is ready."
