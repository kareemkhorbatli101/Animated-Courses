@EX Part 1: Reading - Security Career Path
@INSTR Read the text and answer the questions.
_80
@PANEL
Many people start their security careers as trainees. Trainees work during their studies for a small payment, but they gain important experience. After training for one or two years, graduates may become log clerks or deputy security officers. Junior officers receive training to improve their reporting and their English. Some work at the help desk as desk supervisors, running a shift. Others work in the records section as log clerks or in purchasing as licence officers. The most experienced officers may become security officers who answer to the company and train other officers. Working in security offers opportunities to learn, to grow, and to keep work that others depend on moving.

@EX Part 4: Reading - Security Team Structure
@INSTR Read the text and answer the questions.
_80
@PANEL
Most companies have a hierarchical structure. At the top is the Security Officer who agrees the security plan with the company and answers for the result. Below the officer are section heads who run specific areas such as records, the help desk, licences and equipment. Each section head supervises officers and assistants who carry out the daily work. Compliance officers check whether the rules were followed, and report to the company directly.
Offices also employ support staff like storekeepers, electricians, and cleaners. While these roles may seem less visible, they are essential. For example, an electrician makes sure the generator starts when the mains fails. A cleaner makes sure the server room is free of dust. Every role is important for the organisation to function effectively.
Recent years have seen changes in security structures. Smaller offices operate more flexibly, with fewer management layers. This allows a decision to be taken faster. However, keeping records complete and machines accounted for remain challenges even with simpler structures.

@PART 7 | Part 7: Culture
@EX Part 7: Security Sector in Syria and the Region
@INSTR Read about security work in Syria and the Middle East region, then answer questions.
_80
@PANEL
The security sector in Syria has changed significantly in response to the need to rebuild systems and records that were damaged or lost. Large companies, ministries, universities and small company offices all employ security officers. These officers serve both their own sections and customers from abroad. Working in Syria's offices requires persistence because power, machines and licences are all uncertain.
In neighboring countries like Lebanon and Jordan, companies employ officers who serve both local users and foreign customers. The work is challenging but essential. These organisations demonstrate how dedicated professionals keep working in hard times. Syrian officers are known in the region for their resourcefulness and persistence.
Many Syrian professionals who studied abroad chose to return to serve at offices at home. They sacrifice higher salaries in wealthy countries to protect the records of their own towns. This shows deep commitment to the country and to the reliability of its institutions. Their choices inspire others to consider security careers.
