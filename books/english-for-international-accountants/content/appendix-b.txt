@H2 Listening Passage Title: The Morning Briefing
@H2 Context:~The audio takes place in an open-plan accounting office at 8:45 AM. Mr Harrison, the Senior Accountant, is walking Alice, a new Junior Accountant, through the daily procedures while dealing with a current issue.~
@BSCRIPT
Mr Harrison: "Good morning, Alice. Welcome to your first full week. As you can see, the office is already quite busy. Usually, the finance team starts at 8:30 AM sharp, local time. I want to walk you through the standard routine versus what is happening right now."
Alice: "Good morning, Mr Harrison. I am ready. I have my notebook."
Mr Harrison: "Excellent. So, let’s talk about the daily rhythm. Generally, every morning, the first thing we do is check the bank feed. Alice: "Understood. Alphabetical filing. And what is everyone doing at the moment? It looks very chaotic."
Mr Harrison: "Yes, well, today is an exception. Currently, we are upgrading our accounting software. Look over there at Tom. Usually, he sits at his desk and enters data into the ledger. However, at the moment, she is meeting with the IT consultants in the conference room. They are discussing the data migration."
Alice: "I see. And what about me? What am I doing today?"
Mr Harrison: "Good question. For your first week, you are shadowing me. Usually, I review the general ledger entries before lunch. But right now, I am looking for a discrepancy in the petty cash. There is a small error, and I am trying to find the missing voucher. So, while I am searching for that, I want you to observe the filing system. Are you understanding the difference between the routine tasks and the temporary projects?"
Alice: "Yes, I think so. The routine tasks happen every day, like checking emails and filing, but the software upgrade is just happening this week."
Mr Harrison: "Exactly. Accuracy is vital. Even though we are busy with the upgrade, we verify every figure, in every currency. Currently, I am double-checking these invoices before I post them. We never let the chaos interrupt our accuracy. Now, let’s go to the archive room."

Sarah: "Well, it seems the problem started last Tuesday. I was checking the ledger entries when I noticed a gap in the invoice numbers. It looked like someone deleted a batch of data. I investigated further. It turns out, the intern, Jason, was entering the invoices while the system was updating. He didn’t realize the server was unstable."
Mike: "Oh no. So, while he was posting the entries, the system was crashing?"
Sarah: "Exactly. He posted about fifty invoices. The system recorded the debit side of the entry, but it failed to record the credit side. That’s why our trial balance was off by such a large amount. I asked him what he was doing at 3 PM. He said he was listening to music on his headphones while he was typing, so he didn’t hear the IT announcement about the server maintenance."
Mike: "That explains a lot. I was trying to trace the source documents yesterday morning, but I couldn’t find the physical copies. Did you locate them?"
Sarah: "Yes. After I found the digital error, I went to his desk. He had filed the receipts in the wrong binder. He was filing them by date, but he put them in the 2023 folder instead of 2024. I took the box, and I re-entered all fifty transactions correctly. I finished the work at 8 PM, and then I finally went home."
Mike: "Good work, Sarah. It sounds exhausting. So, the discrepancy is resolved?"
Sarah: "Yes. The closing balance matched the bank statement this morning, in all three currencies. But we need to be careful. While we were fixing this, the auditors were asking for the reports. I told them we were correcting a recording error and that we would submit it today."

@H2 Unit 8: The Accounting Cycle
@H2 Listening Passage Title: Training the New Hire
@H2 Context:~A Senior Accountant, Lisa, is explaining the closing process to a trainee, Alex. She is describing the steps in the passive voice.~
@BSCRIPT
Lisa: "Alex, sit down. Today we are going to review the month-end closing cycle. It is important that you understand the sequence because strict procedures must be followed in every one of our offices."
Alex: "I’m ready. Where does the process start?"
Lisa: "It starts with the source documents. Throughout the month, all invoices and receipts are collected and scanned. Then, the transactions are analyzed and journalized. That means the data is entered into the system daily. Nothing is left until the last minute."
Alex: "Okay. And after they are entered?"
Lisa: "Once the data is entered, it is posted to the General Ledger. This updates the account balances. At the end of the month, the unadjusted trial balance is generated. This document is checked for errors. If the debits and credits do not match, the discrepancy is investigated immediately."
Alex: "And what about things like depreciation? They aren’t on the invoices."
Lisa: "Exactly. That is the next step. Adjusting entries are made for depreciation, prepaid expenses, and accruals. These figures are derived from our internal schedules. After the adjustments are posted, the adjusted trial balance is prepared."
Alex: "And then we create the financial statements?"
Lisa: "Yes. The Income Statement and Balance Sheet are prepared from the adjusted figures. Local balances are translated into the reporting currency before they are consolidated. Finally, the books are closed. The temporary accounts—revenue and expenses—are closed to Retained Earnings. The final report is then reviewed by the Group CFO. The entire package must be approved before we send it to the investors."
