@PART 2 | 3 | GRAMMAR – Modals of Request and Offer (Could, Would, May, Can)
@TASK 2A. Grammar Context: Professional Politeness
@LINE Modals are used in accounting communication to ensure professionalism and politeness when asking for approvals, offering help, or making formal requests.
@SCRIPT
Accountant: "May I ask you to sign this petty cash voucher? Could you please attach the original receipt? I can offer to process your reimbursement immediately, but would you mind filling in the expense form first? You can find the form on the shared drive."
@BLABEL Analysis
@BULLET
Underline the *modal verbs* (e.g., ~May, Could, Can, Would~).
Which modals are used for making a request? Which are used for making an offer?
Which modal is the most formal way to ask for permission?
What is the difference in formality between ~Can you...?~ and ~Could you...?~

@TASK 2B. Grammar Focus Box: Modals of Request and Offer
@TABLEB Function | Modal | Structure | Formality/Usage | Example (Accounting Context)
Formal Request | May | May I + base verb...? | Very formal. Asking permission. | "*May* I *request* the expense report by 4 PM?"
Polite Request | Could | Could you + base verb...? | Polite and professional. | "*Could* you *verify* the vendor details on this invoice?"
Stronger Request | Would | Would you + base verb...? Would you mind + -ing? | Polite. Often used for favor or commitment. | "*Would* you *mind* signing the *voucher* now?"
Informal Request | Can | Can you + base verb...? | Informal/Friendly. Also used for ability. | "*Can* you *check* the cash balance now?"
Offer/Willingness | Can | I can + base verb... | Offering assistance or capability. | "I *can offer* to *process* the *reimbursement* today."
