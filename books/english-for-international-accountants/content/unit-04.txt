@TASK 2B. Grammar Focus Box: Quantifiers
@TABLEB Quantifier | Use with C/U | Meaning | Example (Accounting Context)
Many, Too many | C | Large amount (Too many = excessive) | We have *too many* *laptops*.
Much, Too much | U | Large amount (Too much = excessive) | There *is* *too much* *money* in the petty cash box.
A few, Few | C | Small amount (*A few* = positive, *Few* = negative) | We *only* have *a few* *invoices* left.
A little, Little | U | Small amount (*A little* = positive, *Little* = negative) | We have *very little* *paper* left.
A lot of / Lots of | C & U | Large quantity (General) | The company *spends* *a lot of* *money* on *equipment*.
Some / Any | C & U | Non-specific quantity (*Any* in questions/negatives) | *Do* we have *any* *ink*? Yes, we have *some*.

@TASK 5B. Reading Passage 1: The Definition of Consumables
@LINE Read the internal memo on consumables.
@LINE Consumables are defined as supplies that are used up quickly in the course of business operations. These are treated as an expense when purchased, not an asset. This category includes a lot of items: printer toner, a few boxes of paper clips, cleaning supplies, and general stationery. We must note that equipment is not a consumable. Since we use too much paper, we have set a low reorder point for it. Employees may request new supplies using the online form. We cannot have too little paper or ink, or productivity will fall.

@TASK 5C. Reading Passage 2: Variance Reporting
@LINE Read the procedure for handling inventory variance.
@LINE Inventory variance is the difference between the recorded amount in the ledger and the physical count. There should not be much variance if stocktaking is performed correctly. If the variance is higher than 5% of the total value, the custodian must perform a re-count. If the variance persists, an investigation will be launched. We are looking for any evidence of misplacement or theft. We have too many unexplained variances in the last two quarters, in three different offices. We need a lot of improvements in our inventory control procedures.
@BLABEL Comprehension Questions
@NUM

@TASK 5D. Reading Passage 3: Obsolete Assets
@LINE Read the policy on obsolete equipment.
@LINE Assets become obsolete when they are no longer useful to the business, often due to technological changes. A few old computers and a large printer are currently considered obsolete assets. These must be disposed of according to strict environmental guidelines, which differ from country to country. We do not have much storage space, so they must be removed quickly. We do not want too many old items taking up space. How much money can we get from recycling them? That is the final question we must answer.
