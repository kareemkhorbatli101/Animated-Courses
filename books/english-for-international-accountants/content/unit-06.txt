@PART 2 | 6 | GRAMMAR – First and Second Conditionals
@TASK 2A. Grammar Context: Predicting Consequences
@LINE The Senior Accountant is training a new team member.
@SCRIPT
Marta: "If you don't cross-check the invoice against the PO, you will make a duplicate payment (First Conditional). If we made duplicate payments regularly, we would lose thousands of euros (Second Conditional). Also, if the bank statement does not match the ledger, we will not close the books this month. If I were responsible for that kind of error, I would implement a new internal control immediately."
@BLABEL Analysis
@BULLET
Underline the main *consequence* in each conditional sentence.
Which conditional *is used* for *real* or *probable* future consequences?
Which conditional *is used* for *hypothetical* or *unlikely* present/future situations?

@TASK 2B. Grammar Focus Box: Conditionals
@TABLEB Conditional Type | Form | Use | Example (Accounts Context)
First Conditional | If + Present Simple, Will + Verb | Real or probable future outcomes. | If we *miss* the *deadline*, we *will* *incur* a *penalty*.
Second Conditional | If + Past Simple (or ~were~), Would + Verb | Hypothetical or unreal situations now or in the future; giving advice. | If I *were* you, I *would* *reverse* the *entry*.
