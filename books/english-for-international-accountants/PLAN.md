# English for Accountants · International Edition — Adaptation Plan

Adapting **English for Accountants (B1) · Practical English for the Modern Accounting
Office** into an **International Edition**.

Same method as the five books before it: measure the source first, then transpose. This is
a structural twin, built to the source's own measurements.

   Task 3  <think–pair–share>           Think / Pair / Share
🧱 UNIT n: PART 1: VOCABULARY – <title>
   1A  <term matching>                  7×2 to 9×2 table

   1C  Vocabulary in Context: <title>   a document with six numbered gaps

   5A  Pre-Reading Discussion            three questions

   5C  Reading Passage 2 <email>         the email + 5 questions + a vocabulary match

Unit 1 adds **6E Peer Review Checklist**, and Units 1–5 put a matching table in the
Warm-Up where Units 6–10 use a plain list. Both local variations are reproduced, not
normalised.

## 2 · The transposition

This source is unusual among the six: nine-tenths of it is already country-neutral. The
units and the first two appendices are set in a generic accounting office with invented
firms (Apex Logistics, GreenLeaf Retail) and an international cast. **Appendix C is not.**
Its ten case studies are set in one country, during one moment in that country's history:
a Ministry of Finance hub in Aleppo, the Homs Municipality, a field office in Raqqa, a
medical depot in Idlib, a payroll audit in Deir ez-Zor, a report to the Minister in
Damascus, reconstruction funds, "the new free government".

So the International Edition has two jobs rather than one.

| | Source | International Edition |
|---|---|---|
| The workplace | a generic accounting office, firm unnamed | **Northwind Group**, a mid-size manufacturer and distributor |
| Where it works | one office, implied one country | a shared service centre in **Kraków** and operating companies in **Dublin · Toronto · Singapore · Nairobi · Santiago** |
| The money | one currency, written `$` | EUR, CAD, SGD, KES, CLP and USD, with the rate between them |
| The books | one ledger | one group ledger, six local ledgers, and the intercompany entries between them |
| The rules | "the rules" | the group's reporting manual, plus each country's own tax and filing rules |
| Appendix C | ten case studies in one country's reconstruction | ten case studies across the group's six locations |

Making the firm **international** does not add a topic; it gives every topic the source
already teaches a second dimension, at no cost to the syllabus:

* a **daily routine** runs across five time zones, so Unit 1's present continuous describes
  a handover as well as a desk;
* a **past transaction** may sit in another country's ledger and another country's
  quarter — Unit 2;
* **petty cash** is held in six currencies, so a float is converted as well as counted —
  Unit 3;
* **inventory** sits in warehouses under two sets of customs paperwork — Unit 4;
* **payment terms** differ by country, so Unit 5's future forms answer "when will we be
  paid?" differently in each;
* an **error** is often an intercompany mismatch, where both sides are sure they are right
  — Unit 6;
* **performance** cannot be compared until the figures are in one currency — Unit 7;
* the **cycle** closes twice: once under local rules, once for the group — Unit 8;
* **qualifications and software** travel differently across borders — Unit 9;
* **reporting** means relaying a message from a CFO who is three time zones away — Unit 10.

And the learner the book is written for changes in the way the brief asks: from a junior
accountant in one office to a junior accountant in a finance team that spans several.

## 3 · Fidelity criteria

Items marked **[auto]** are checked by the three suites in `qa/`; the rest by read-through.

**Architecture**
1. 10 units, same order, same titles, same grammar sequence. **[auto]**
2. 38 named tasks per unit in the source's exact slot order; 39 in Unit 1. **[auto]**
3. Warm-Up + Parts 1–8, with the source's emoji headings and part titles. **[auto]**
4. Three appendices, each with one entry per unit, in unit order. **[auto]**
5. Front matter: the course description, the unit-structure guide and the contents. **[auto]**
6. A hard page break before each unit head and each part. **[auto]**

**Exercise system**
7. 6–7 tables per unit, in the source's shapes. **[auto]**
8. Part 3's three passages each carry a Context line and an Audio Script. **[auto]**
9. 3B's True/False set has five `[ ]` items; 8E has four or five. **[auto]**
10. Every writing task names a scenario and a word count where the source does.
11. Every role play names Student A and Student B.
12. The Grammar Focus Box carries a note on the point's common trap.

**Language and level**
13. B1 throughout, at the source's density.
14. The source's own conventions: `–` in headings, `📘📍🧱🎧🗣📖✍🔊🧾`, `[ ]`, `↗ ↘`.
15. No unit uses its grammar in a text before the unit that teaches it, except as recognition.

**Texts**
16. Appendix A entries 470–620 words. **[auto]**
17. Appendix B entries 385–580 words. **[auto]**
18. Appendix C entries 230–400 words. **[auto]**
19. Part 3 audio scripts and Part 5 passages at the source's lengths. **[auto]**

**Integrity**
20. Everything about Northwind Group is invented and declared invented.
21. The accounting is true and general: double entry, accruals, reconciliation,
    intercompany balances, FX translation, the close, variance analysis.
22. No real company, regulator, standard-setter or software vendor is given invented words
    or figures.
23. No country is characterised. The six locations are settings, not subjects. The
    learner's own workplace is invited into the discussion tasks, never described for them.
24. Nothing of the source's one-country Appendix C survives. **[auto]**

**Production**
25. The source's design system, decoded from its OOXML: Letter page with its margins, the
    theme font at 11 pt, bold headings with an emoji run in Segoe UI Emoji, Word bullet and
    decimal lists, and tables with single 6-pt borders and the source's cell margins. **[auto]**
26. Each unit within ±12 % of its source unit, the book within ±8 %, and the page count
    within ±8 %. **[auto]**

## 4 · Build

A sixth toolchain, because this is a sixth design system — and the second with no
illustrations:

```
python3 build/compile.py   # content/*.txt -> dist/…_StudentBook.docx
python3 qa/check.py        # element-by-element parity against the source's OOXML
python3 qa/tasks.py        # per-task-slot list formatting, slot by slot
python3 qa/outline.py X    # dump any document in reading order with its real formatting
```

`build/render.py` is the decoded design system, rebuilt from scratch with `zipfile`.
`build/compile.py` maps a line-oriented DSL onto it. The DSL's tags are the source's own
elements: `@UNITHEAD @WARMUP @PART @TASK @TABLE @TABLEB @NUM @BULLET @SUB @BULLETLABEL
@SUBLABEL @BOX @TFBOX @LBOX @SCRIPT @BSCRIPT @LABEL @BLABEL @BOLD @LINE @H2 @PB`.
Inside any text, `*bold*` and `~italic~` carry the inline emphasis.

### Source quirks reproduced rather than normalised

The source is inconsistent in small ways, and the twin keeps them:

- the warm-up Task 2 is a matching table in Units 1–5, a list in 6–10, and a *numbered*
  list in 6–7 only;
- the 1B gap-fill prompts are plain lines in Unit 1 and numbered in Units 2–10;
- the 1C word bank is set in bold in Units 1, 3 and 9 and plain in the other seven;
- the true/false boxes are numbered, the self-assessment checklists bulleted, and Unit 1's
  peer-review checklist alone puts its `[ ]` first;
- Unit 3 and Unit 5 omit the page break after their warm-up; Unit 7 omits two more;
- Unit 1's 1E is not bold-columned while Units 2–8's are;
- Unit 3's 1B word box carries a stray double comma, and its 1B answers are already filled
  in;
- Unit 6's 8B grammar quiz is garbled in the source, and stays garbled;
- Unit 7's 2D is labelled "Conditional:" although the unit teaches comparatives;
- Units 8 and 9 end their 8E checklist after four items, not five.

### Two deliberate divergences

1. **The source's stray inline bold is not copied.** Beyond the designed emphasis — bold
   first columns in the grammar boxes, the target form inside each worked example, the
   grammar term in each Analysis bullet, bold cue labels — the source also bolds 960
   further spans, most of them single words (`is` 27 times, `If` 14, `a`, `or`, `e.g.,`)
   and some of them artefacts (`willclear`, `________`). That is typing noise, not
   typography, so the twin reproduces the design and not the accidents. This is the one
   metric that does not match: 947 source paragraphs carry mixed bold against 548 here.
2. **Apostrophes.** The source mixes straight and curly apostrophes in the same word
   (`company's` six times straight, twice curly), with the curly ones clustered in
   Appendix B and Unit 10 where Word's autocorrect happened to be on. The twin uses curly
   in exactly those two places and straight elsewhere, which matches the pattern without
   copying the randomness.

The source's stray footnote-marker digits (`8:30 AM sharp1111`, `bank feed2`, `fraud21`)
are citation-tool debris from the original drafting and are not reproduced.
