# English for International Journalists · Book 2 — Adaptation Plan

Adapting **English for Journalists · Syria Edition · Book 2: Across Borders** into
**English for Journalists · International Edition · Book 2: Across Borders**.

Same method as Book 1: measure the source first, then transpose. This is a structural
twin, built to the source's own measurements — not a book in its spirit.

Book 2 is markedly more regular than Book 1: one template, applied ten times. Every unit
carries the same section grammar (MODEL → NOTICE → PRACTISE → PRODUCE, with CHECK in
Part 7), the same two-reading/two-listening/two-writing/two-case/two-dialogue spine, and
the same closing sequence — Answer Key → Open tasks → note on real and invented content →
italic colophon.

### What is new in Book 2, relative to Book 1

* A fifth warm-up item (**W5**), always a discussion.
* A framing paragraph under the unit head — *Where we are now*, *A correspondent asks for
  the backstory*, *Three outlets, one project* — replacing Book 1's *The story so far*.
* **Part 3 carries a grammar box of its own** in several units (time clauses in Unit 5).
* Longer vocabulary tables (18 rows), longer readings, and a 1G/1J/1K tail in Part 1 that
  recycles the vocabulary twice more.
* A declared **Across Borders strand**: about a fifth of the book, with at least one part
  per unit involving a partner abroad, and Units 4, 7 and 10 wholly cross-border.

## 2 · The transposition

Book 2's engine is a **three-tier partnership**: a small local newsroom (Al-Manara) learns
to work with a regional network (Al-Ufuq) and an international wire (Meridian Newswire).
Every ethics case in the book turns on the asymmetry between the tiers — a bigger partner
wants a brighter angle, a rounder number, a sharper quote, an earlier run, a bigger share
of the credit.

The International Edition keeps that engine intact, and keeps **Atlas Wire** — the
protagonist of Book 1 — in the middle tier, so the asymmetry works in both directions.

| Tier | Syria Edition | International Edition |
|---|---|---|
| Local | *(Al-Manara itself)* | **Kalina Daily**, the newsroom in the town the story is about; **Dren** is its reporter |
| The learner's newsroom | **Al-Manara**, Damascus | **Atlas Wire**, the world desk in Geneva |
| Regional | **Al-Ufuq**, a regional network | **the River Basin Network (RBN)**, covering the Vara basin; **Ines Duarte** edits |
| International | **Meridian Newswire** | **the Meridian Post**, a large international daily; **Omar Faris** edits |

This is the one decision that makes the whole book work. Putting Atlas Wire in the middle
means it has a **bigger partner above it** (so every case about a big partner's pressure
transposes unchanged) and a **smaller partner below it** (so the book also teaches the duty
an international outlet owes the local newsroom whose story it is carrying). The source
teaches that asymmetry from below; the International Edition teaches it from both sides,
which is the honest version for a journalist who works across borders.

### The continuous story

Book 1 ended with the front page *A crossing comes home*. Book 2 opens a year later: that
coverage has been noticed, and it grows into a cross-border co-production about the whole
**Vara basin** — the other crossings, what reopened and what did not.

| Unit | Grammar | Story beat | Strand |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | present perfect vs past simple (+ ever/never/just/already/yet) | A year on; the Meridian Post writes to Atlas Wire | partner |
| 2 | used to · would · past continuous | Omar asks for the backstory of the valley | partner |
| 3 | will · going to · present continuous | Three newsrooms plan one project across time zones | partner |
| 4 | reported speech · say/tell · according to | Filing to the Meridian Post's desk | **wholly cross-border** |
| 5 | zero & first conditionals · time clauses | The conditions for running the story | partner |
| 6 | might/may/could · should/ought to | A claim spreads across the basin | partner |
| 7 | the passive across tenses | Writing for the wire | **wholly cross-border** |
| 8 | comparatives · superlatives · as…as, too/enough | An explainer for a reader far away | partner |
| 9 | quantifiers · articles · connectors | Co-writing with Kalina Daily and RBN | partner |
| 10 | gerunds and infinitives | The co-production goes out | **wholly cross-border** |

## 3 · Fidelity criteria

Items marked **[auto]** are checked by `qa/check_parity.py`; the rest by read-through.

**Architecture**
1. 10 units, same order, same grammar sequence. **[auto]**
2. Warm-Up (W1–W5) + 11 Parts + Answer Key in every unit. **[auto]**
3. 98 lettered exercises per unit, distributed 11·8·9·12·12·8·9·10·10·9. **[auto]**
4. Exercise codes run A–K, A–H, A–I, A–L, A–L, A–H, A–I, A–J, A–J, A–I. **[auto]**
5. Unit head: *BOOK 2 · UNIT n*, title, Beat + CEFR line, four numbered aims, cast figure,
   then a framing paragraph in the source's own voice. **[auto]**
6. 28 figures per unit (29 in Unit 10). **[auto]**
7. Part 1 vocabulary: 15 rows in Unit 1, 18 in Units 2–10. **[auto]**
8. Each unit closes: Answer Key → Open tasks → note on real and invented content → colophon.

**Exercise system**
9. Part 1 always: match → gap-fill → collocations → odd-one-out → paragraph gap-fill →
   definitions → a second collocation paragraph (1G) → personal → discussion → a third
   gap-fill (1J) → creative (1K).
10. Part 2 always: three grammar boxes, structured practice (2A–2D), *Your own words*
    (2E–2F), a fix-it (2G) and a free item (2H).
11. Part 3 always: model → notice (3A–3B) → build (3C–3D) → an ordering task (3E) →
    produce (3F) → three discussion items (3G–3I).
12. Part 4 always: two audios, each with prediction, detail, a form-identification task and
    a notes grid; then a both-audios summary, a reflective question, a comparison note and
    a discussion (4I–4L).
13. Part 5 always: Reading A → four comprehension tasks; Reading B (four numbered rules) →
    three tasks; then words-from-the-readings, a both-readings grammar task, and three
    discussion items (5J–5L).
14. Part 6 always: two writing pieces, each model → notice → produce, then a fix-it (6F),
    a plan-it (6G) and a peer-check (6H).
15. Part 7 always: model dialogue → notice → controlled (7B–7C) → role table → pair work →
    role-play → second dialogue → controlled (7F) → self-check grid → role-play (7G) →
    plan-it (7H) → discussion (7I).
16. Part 8 always: two cases, each with a binary choice, a classification, a rule gap-fill
    and a discussion; plus a written response (8H), a second classification (8I) and a
    closing discussion (8J).
17. Part 9 always: model artefact → parts-matching → find → fix → build → write → headline
    → reply → a fairness line → a two-line thread → discussion.
18. Part 10 always: vocabulary (8 items), three grammar tasks, a read-and-answer, the
    Can-Do list, reflection, a correct-the-sentence review, a mixed review and a final
    reflection (10I).
19. Part 11: assignment title, task, Remember box, five success criteria, and a *Keep this*
    forward link naming the next unit's grammar.
20. Every exercise is either answered in the key or listed under *Open tasks*. **[auto]**
21. Every closed, enumerated exercise has one answer per item. **[auto]**

**Language and level**
22. CEFR lower B1 → medium B1 throughout, above Book 1's A2 → lower B1.
23. Longer sentences and more subordination than Book 1, at the source's own density.
24. British spelling; the source's typographic conventions (curly quotes, `·`, `→`, `☐`).
25. Grammar boxes use the two-colour convention and are explicit about the contrast that
    each unit turns on (perfect vs past; used to vs would; will vs going to vs present
    continuous; zero vs first conditional; might vs should; active vs passive).
26. No unit uses its grammar in a text before the unit that teaches it, except as
    deliberate recognition; Book 1's grammar is freely recycled.

**Texts**
27. Warm-up story: 86–102 words. **[auto]**
28. Reading A: 288–375 words, with 3–6 named quotations.
29. Reading B: a numbered four-rule text of 73–129 words — five tips in Unit 1 and a
    four-rung *rumour → fact* ladder in Unit 6, exactly as the source varies it.
30. Listening scripts: 8–12 turns, two speakers (three in Unit 3's call).
31. Ethics cases: 94–138 words, each ending in a question to the student.
31a. Answer key: 890–1,132 words per unit, in the source's clipped register.

**Visuals**
32. 28 figures per unit (29 in Unit 10); ~281 placements. **[auto]**
33. The source's flat-vector language: 1500 px, navy title bar, pale ground, the same palette.
34. Figure types mirror the source: cast board, four-key-words card, grammar contrast
    diagrams, partner/consortium diagrams, time-zone clock, audio cards, case cards,
    phone-screen cards, Can-Do card, assignment template.
35. Every caption teaches.

**Integrity**
36. Every real fact attributable to a named organisation and dated.
37. Everything invented declared invented, in the front matter and at the end of every unit.
38. No invented quotation attributed to any real person or body.
39. No real state, border or population characterised; the geography is invented.
40. The asymmetry between partners is taught honestly from both sides.

**Production**
41. Same design system as Book 1, decoded from the source's OOXML. **[auto]**
42. Total length within ±8 % of the source. **[auto]**
