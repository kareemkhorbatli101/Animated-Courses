Four newsrooms have agreed a single hour of publication, and a short list of things that must be true before that hour: two sources for every figure, a document for every claim, a right of reply for everyone named, and a lawyer's reading in each country. @PART 2 | Grammar: rules and real conditions | The zero conditional for rules; the first conditional for real futures; unless, as long as, provided that.
@FIG u5-gA | Zero conditional = a rule that is always true. First conditional = one real future.
@GRAMMAR 1 · zero conditional — a rule
Form: {{If}} + present simple, present simple. "{{If}} a figure {{has}} one source, we {{do not publish}} it."
For something always true: a rule, a law, a standing practice.
You can swap the halves: "We {{do not publish}} a figure {{if}} it has one source."

@GRAMMAR 2 · first conditional — one real future
Form: {{If}} + present simple, {{will}} + verb. "{{If}} the office {{confirms}} it, we {{will run}} it on Sunday."
For one real possibility in the future, and its result.
[[Never 'if…will']] in the if-half: say *if it confirms*, NOT *if it will confirm*.

@FIG u5-gB | unless = if not · as long as / provided that = only on this condition.
@GRAMMAR 3 · unless · as long as · provided that
{{Unless}} = if not: "We will not publish {{unless}} we have two sources."
{{As long as}} / {{provided that}} = only on this condition: "We will run it {{as long as}} the method comes."
[[Not with a second not]]: say *unless we have*, NOT *unless we don't have*.

@PART 3 | Setting the conditions | A conditions note says what must be true before publication, and when each thing happens.
@FIG g-timezones | One hour, four countries. Every condition has to be met before it, not after.
@FIG u5-skill | A conditions note: what must be true, who checks it, and by when.
@GRAMMAR 4 · time clauses — saying when
After {{when, until, as soon as, before, after, by the time}}, use the {{present}} for the future.
"We will run it {{when}} the method {{arrives}}." "We will hold {{until}} it {{is}} confirmed."
[[Not 'will' twice]]: say *as soon as it arrives*, NOT *as soon as it will arrive*.

@PART 5 | Reading: the rules they agreed first | Two readings. Read for the conditions, and for why they come before the calendar.
@FIG g-reach | A story that runs in four countries can only be as strong as its weakest condition.
@SUBHEAD Reading A | · what must be true before Sunday
@READING Nothing runs until it is confirmed
Before the four newsrooms agreed an hour, they agreed a list. It is one page long, it was written in a morning, and it is the reason the project will survive its own publication.

The first condition is arithmetic. If a figure has one source, it does not run. "That is not a judgement we make story by story," says Priya Nair, who leads verification at Atlas Wire. "It is a rule. Rules are what you have when you are tired."

The second is paper. Every claim about the crossings has to rest on something a reader could in principle see: a register, a council minute, a photograph with a date. Where a document exists but naming its keeper would put her at risk, the partners publish the document's substance and withhold her name.

The third is the right of reply. Everyone named gets the chance to answer before publication, in their own language, with a real deadline. "If somebody answers us at the last hour, we will still carry it," says Omar Faris of the Meridian Post. "If they do not answer, we say that too."

The fourth is law. Each partner runs its own legal check, in its own jurisdiction, because what is safe to print in one country is not always safe in another. Nobody signs off until all four have read the piece in their own jurisdiction.

And then, last of all, comes the hour. The embargo holds until 07:00 GMT on the last Sunday of the month, and no partner may move it alone. "If a rival publishes first, we all move together, within the hour," says Ana Ferreira of Atlas Wire. "The one thing we never do is leave the smallest partner standing in the open on their own."

@SUBHEAD Reading B | · the conditions, in short
@READING Four conditions before you publish
**1. Two independent sources, or it does not run.** A rule, not a decision: *if a figure has one source, we do not publish it.* Rules survive tiredness.
**2. Something a reader could see.** A register, a minute, a dated photograph. If naming its keeper would put them at risk, publish the substance and withhold the name.
**3. A real right of reply.** Everyone named is asked before publication, with a deadline they can meet. If they answer late, carry it; if not, say so.
**4. One hour, agreed by all.** Hold until the agreed time. If it breaks elsewhere, move together — never leave the smallest partner exposed.

@SUBHEAD A first case | · the temptation to run early
@CASE Omar hears that a rival may have the story. If the Post runs on Saturday morning, it gets the credit and the traffic; if it waits, a competitor may take both. Nobody would be publishing anything false — only early. An embargo is not a courtesy; it is the only thing that makes a partnership of unequal outlets possible. The Code says: a promise to a partner is a promise.
