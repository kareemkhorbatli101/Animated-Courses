@BIGLABEL WELCOME | About Book 2
@P
English for Journalists, Book 2, continues the newsroom English course. In Book 1, you learned to report a story with Atlas Wire, an invented independent news agency with a world desk in Geneva, and to carry it from the crossing at Kalina to a front page. In Book 2, that story grows: Atlas Wire begins to work with other newsrooms — Kalina Daily, the local paper in the town itself; the River Basin Network, a regional network; and the Meridian Post, a large international daily — and learns to make one story with partners who are bigger and smaller than itself, in English, without losing anybody's truth.
The course climbs from lower B1 to medium B1 — the first half of B1. Each unit teaches one new piece of grammar and one part of cross-border journalism: recapping a running story, briefing a colleague abroad, planning across time zones, filing to a foreign desk, setting the conditions for publication, weighing a claim, writing for the wire, explaining a place to a distant reader, co-writing, and finally co-producing a package for the world.

@H The cross-border strand
@P
About a fifth of the book is the **Across Borders** strand: in every unit, at least one part involves working with a partner in another country, and Units 4, 7 and 10 are wholly cross-border. It prepares a journalist to work inside an international partnership — with the outlets above them, who reach further, and the outlets below them, whose story it is.

@H How to use this book
@NUM
Each unit has a Warm-Up and eleven parts: Words, Grammar, a Skill, Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking, Ethics, Digital, Review, and an Assignment.
Every part builds up step by step: you see a model, notice how it works, practise it, and then produce your own.
Grammar is taught half as controlled practice and half in your own words. Every part mixes exercises you can check with open tasks to discuss and create.
The Ethics part in every unit builds the Code — accurate, fair, independent, and kind — now tested across borders, and in both directions.
The Answer Key at the end of each unit gives the answers to the closed exercises. Open tasks (discussion, role-play, and your own writing) are marked.
