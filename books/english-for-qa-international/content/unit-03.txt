@INSTR Read the text and answer questions about compliance choices.

@PANEL
Certification bodies face difficult decisions when a company does not fully comply. In any factory, older equipment rarely matches current requirements. Bodies must prioritize findings based on risk to the consumer and the number of people affected. Safety and allergen requirements receive priority over documentation style. Products consumed by children or patients receive the strictest review. Processes with the highest hazard are examined first. Commercial pressure is considered but never overrides consumer safety. Bodies maintain transparency by explaining their classification of each finding to the company. This transparency builds trust and understanding.

@EX Part 4: Text Comprehension - Professional Ethics
@INSTR Read about professional ethics and choose the best answer.
_80
@PASSAGE
Quality practice is guided by core principles: safety, impartiality, competence, and independence. Safety means protecting the health of the people who will use the product. Impartiality requires applying the same standard to every batch regardless of who is waiting for it. Competence means reporting only tests you are trained and qualified to run, and calling in a specialist for the rest. Independence means that QA gives its conclusion based on evidence, not on commercial pressure. These principles guide every professional decision and ensure ethical practice.

The International Organization for Standardization and the Codex Alimentarius Commission publish requirements used worldwide. They cover management systems, food safety, laboratory competence and test methods. These bodies employ technical staff and depend on volunteer committee members from industry. Some standards have been adopted into national law in dozens of countries. Standards bodies are funded by member subscriptions, document sales, and government contributions. @PART 7 | Part 7: Culture
@EX Part 7: Standards and Certification in Mexican Contexts
@INSTR Read about standards and certification in Mexico and answer questions.
_80
@PANEL
Mexico has a long manufacturing tradition and also a demanding certification situation. The General Directorate of Standards publishes the national standards, holds the national measurement references, and operates the conformity mark that many products must carry. Export markets add their own requirements on top, so a food exporter may need a national certificate, a customer audit, and a third-party food safety certification at the same time. Certification is complicated by overlapping export schemes, difficulty importing reference materials, and the cost of maintaining accreditation. Laboratories work to keep their scopes alive alongside the national metrology service. Reference shortages affect factories across the country. QA staff demonstrate extraordinary persistence and resourcefulness. Their work includes method validation, supplier auditing, and the documentation an importing country will demand. International recognition of Mexican analysts acknowledges their skill and dedication. Their resilience and technical knowledge inspire support from the global profession.
