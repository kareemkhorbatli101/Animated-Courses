@EX Part 1: Reading - Quality Career Path
@INSTR Read the text and answer the questions.
_80
@PANEL
Many people start their quality careers as trainees. Trainees work during their studies for a small payment, but they gain important experience. After training for one or two years, graduates may become junior inspectors or analysts. Junior staff receive training to improve their technique and their record-keeping. Some work on the line as inspectors, sampling during production. Others work in the laboratory as analysts or in documentation. The most experienced staff may become quality managers who sign releases and train other staff. Working in quality assurance offers opportunities to learn, grow, and protect customers at the same time.

@EX Part 4: Reading - Quality Department Structure
@INSTR Read the text and answer the questions.
_80
@PANEL
Most quality departments have a hierarchical structure. At the top is the Quality Manager who approves the plans and signs each release. Below the manager are section heads who run specific areas such as the laboratory, the line inspection team, and documentation. Each section head supervises inspectors and analysts who take the samples and run the tests. Auditors check the system rather than the product, and report to the manager directly.
Departments also employ support staff like store keepers, sample couriers, and glassware technicians. While these roles may seem less visible, they are essential. For example, a courier makes sure a sample reaches the laboratory before it degrades. A glassware technician makes sure the equipment is clean enough for the result to mean anything. Every role is important for the department to function effectively.
Recent years have seen changes in department structures. Smaller companies operate more flexibly, with fewer management layers. This allows decisions to be made faster. However, keeping instruments calibrated and records complete remain challenges even with simpler structures.

@PART 7 | Part 7: Culture
@EX Part 7: Quality Sector in Türkiye and the Region
@INSTR Read about quality assurance in Türkiye and its neighbours, then answer questions.
_80
@PANEL
The quality sector in Türkiye has grown significantly in response to the need to prove that output meets fast-growing export markets' needs. Large pharmaceutical plants, food exporters, and small family workshops all employ quality staff. These staff serve both the domestic market and customers abroad. Working in Türkiye's departments requires persistence because references, accredited time and analysts are all scarce.
In neighbouring countries like Bulgaria and Georgia, departments employ analysts who test for both local sale and export. The work is challenging but essential. These organizations demonstrate how dedicated professionals keep working in hard times. Turkish QA staff are internationally recognized for their resourcefulness and persistence.
Many Turkish professionals who studied abroad chose to return to work in quality departments at home. They sacrifice higher salaries in wealthy countries to protect customers in their own market. This shows deep commitment to the country and to the reputation of what it makes. Their choices inspire others to consider quality careers.
