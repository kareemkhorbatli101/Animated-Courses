Reviewers conduct a code review by comparing the drawings against the regulations and measuring setbacks and heights. Required amendments are then issued. @EX Part 4: Reading - Regulatory Decision-Making
@INSTR Read the text and answer questions about code choices.
_80
@PANEL
Approval authorities face difficult decisions when a building does not fully comply. In any city, existing buildings rarely match current codes. Authorities must prioritize requirements based on risk to life and the number of people affected. Structural and fire requirements receive priority over comfort standards. Buildings used by many people, such as schools and clinics, receive the strictest review. Districts with the highest hazard are examined first. Cost is considered but never overrides life safety. Authorities maintain transparency by explaining their decisions to owners and architects. This transparency builds trust and understanding.

@INSTR Read about professional ethics and choose the best answer.
_80
@PASSAGE
Architectural practice is guided by core principles: safety, impartiality, competence, and independence. Safety means protecting the health and lives of the people who will use a building. Impartiality requires treating every user equally regardless of wealth, tenure, or ability. Competence means working only within what you actually know and calling in specialists for the rest. Independence means that architects give technical advice based on evidence, not on commercial pressure. These principles guide every professional decision and ensure ethical practice.

@PANEL
The International Organization for Standardization and the European standards committees publish technical requirements used worldwide. They cover structures, fire safety, materials and energy performance. These bodies employ thousands of technical staff and volunteer committee members. Some standards have been adopted into national law in dozens of countries. Standards bodies are funded by member subscriptions, document sales, and government contributions. Many national bodies struggle to keep their codes up to date despite clear need. Architects often report that reading standards carefully saves far more time than it costs.

@PART 7 | Part 7: Culture
@EX Part 7: Codes and Approvals in Peruvian Contexts
@INSTR Read about building regulation in Peru and answer questions.
_80
@PANEL
Peru has one of the oldest continuous building traditions in the Americas, and also one of the most demanding regulatory situations. Lima and Cusco contain protected historic centres where every intervention needs heritage approval as well as a building permit. The Peruvian Arab Standards and Metrology Organization publishes national technical standards, and the Order of Peruvian Engineers and Architects registers the professionals who may stamp drawings. Approval is complicated by lost records, informal land title, and buildings extended without permission. Municipal engineering departments review submissions alongside heritage authorities. Earthquakes and self-built construction affect buildings across the country. Architects demonstrate extraordinary patience and persistence. Their work includes seismic assessment, structural strengthening, and heritage conservation. International recognition of Peruvian conservation architects acknowledges their skill and dedication. Their resilience and craft knowledge inspire support from the global profession.
