@ANSHEAD Exercise 31: Part 5: Discussing Tour Guiding and Its Work
@QHEAD Question 1 - Sample Answer:
@ANS
A lead guide is a trained professional who sets objectives, arranges the staff, space and equipment to reach them, keeps the work moving, and reports the result. A assistant guide does one part of the work and finishes it. Unlike the assistant guide, who is judged on today's route, the lead guide is judged on the quarter's objectives. The key difference is that the lead guide decides what will be covered, while the assistant guide gets it covered.
_100
@QHEAD Question 2 - Sample Answer:
@ANS
There are five functions of tour guiding. Planning decides in advance what the office will cover, for whom, and at what cost. Organising arranges the staff, space and equipment so the plan can be carried out, and decides who may approve what. Communicating circulates a decision, answers an enquiry, and keeps everyone informed when the plan meets reality. Controlling compares what was done with what was planned and finds the cause of any difference. Reviewing looks back at a quarter rather than one shift.
_100

Reporting badly also removes the only chance to correct a problem while the promise can still be met. In Syria, where equipment is scarce, that early warning is the most valuable thing an office produces.

@ANSHEAD Exercise 1: Warm-Up: What Is Procedural Compliance?
@QHEAD Sample Answer:
@ANS
Antiquities regulation is the body of rules an office must satisfy before it may operate at an agency and while it continues to work. It covers licensing, permits, retention, access, guest records and guest protection. These rules are made by legislation and applied by the Tourist Guides Syndicate, the Directorate-General of Antiquities and Museums and the courts. Examples include being licensed before working with a group, obtaining written permits, and keeping the registers. Their goal is to protect everyone who deals with the office. Antiquities rules exist because failures in the past left guests, agencies and authorities with no remedy.

@ANSHEAD Exercise 18: Part 2: Grammar Summary - Compliance Communication
@QHEAD Sample Answer:
@ANS
Guides should use clear, factual language to describe requirements. They must present accurate information about what the rules demand and what the permits show. Using passive voice makes reports more formal and professional. They should employ conditional statements like "If the return is late, the permit may be suspended." @ANSHEAD Exercise 1: Warm-Up: Performance and Reporting Discussion
@QHEAD Sample Answer:
@ANS
Measurement and reporting serve several crucial purposes. First, accountability—the management want to know what their tour budget produced.
