@PANEL
Nepali NGOs and Humanitarian Organizations
Nepal has a long tradition of community organizations and charitable work. The Nepal Red Cross Society is one of the oldest and largest NGOs in Nepal. It provides healthcare, emergency relief, and community support services across the country.
Many smaller NGOs in Nepal focus on specific needs. Some work in education, providing schools and training programs for children and adults. Others focus on women's rights, environmental protection, and community development. During earthquakes and landslides, these organizations have been critical in providing food, shelter, and medical care to affected populations.
International NGOs also work in Nepal alongside local organizations. They bring funding, technical expertise, and resources to support community programs. Partnerships between Nepali NGOs and international organizations strengthen the capacity to help people.
NGO workers in Nepal often face challenges including limited funding, difficult mountain access, and hard working conditions. Despite these obstacles, they remain committed to their missions of helping communities and building a better future for all Nepalis.

What is a Non-Governmental Organization (NGO)?
An NGO is an independent organization that is not controlled by the government. NGOs work to solve problems and help people without making profit. They focus on social, environmental, or humanitarian goals. 
NGOs exist in almost every country in the world. They range from small local organizations to large international groups. Some famous international NGOs include Red Cross, Doctors Without Borders, and Save the Children.
NGOs are funded by donations from individuals, foundations, and sometimes international organizations. They employ staff and rely heavily on volunteers. Volunteers work without payment because they believe in the NGO's mission.
The mission of an NGO is its main goal or purpose. For example, a health NGO's mission is to improve healthcare. An education NGO's mission is to improve education. Environmental NGOs protect nature. All NGOs work to make the world a better place for communities they serve.
NGOs are essential for meeting needs that governments cannot address alone. They provide direct services, advocate for change, and respond quickly to emergencies. Communities benefit greatly from NGO work.

NGOs in Nepal: History and Current Work
Nepal has a rich history of community organizations and humanitarian work. The Nepal Red Cross Society, founded in 1963, is one of the oldest NGOs in South Asia. It has helped Nepalis during both natural disasters and times of hardship.
Today, many NGOs work in Nepal. Some are local organizations founded and run by Nepalis. Others are branches of international NGOs. Together they address critical needs in education, healthcare, food security, and emergency relief.
Education NGOs help Nepali children continue learning despite challenges. They provide schools, scholarships, and training programs. Health NGOs run clinics in villages without hospitals. They treat diseases and teach prevention.
During emergencies, humanitarian NGOs provide immediate help. They distribute food to hungry families, set up shelters, provide medical care, and reunite separated families.
Women's groups help women and girls get education and protection. Environmental organizations work to protect Nepal's water and land.
The work of NGOs is challenging. They operate with limited funding and often in difficult conditions. But they remain committed to helping Nepalis improve their lives. International partnerships help local NGOs expand their capacity to serve communities.

NGO Values and Humanitarian Principles
NGOs are guided by important values and ethics in their work. Humanitarian principles include humanity, neutrality, impartiality, and independence.
Humanity means treating all people with respect and dignity. NGO workers believe every person has worth and deserves help. This is why NGOs work with all people regardless of their religion, ethnicity, or politics.
Impartiality means helping people based on need, not on who they are or what they believe. NGOs help the most vulnerable people first. They do not discriminate.
Neutrality means NGOs do not take sides in conflicts. They stay independent politically so they can help all people. This allows them to work safely in conflict areas to help everyone.
Independence means NGOs make decisions based on their mission and values, not government pressure. This independence allows NGOs to speak up for vulnerable people and challenge injustice.
These principles guide NGO workers every day. They explain why NGOs are trusted and respected in communities. Following these values makes NGO work effective and ethical.
