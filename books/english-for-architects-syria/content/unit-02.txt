@EX Part 1: Reading - Architecture Career Path
@INSTR Read the text and answer the questions.
_80
@PANEL
Many people start their architecture careers as interns. Interns work during their studies for a small payment, but they gain important experience. After interning for one or two years, graduates may become junior architects in an office. Junior architects receive training to improve their drawing and detailing skills. Some work as site architects, visiting construction to check the work. Others work in the office as designers or coordinators. The most experienced architects may become project directors who train and manage other staff. Working in architecture offers opportunities to learn, grow, and shape places at the same time.

@EX Part 4: Reading - Architecture Office Structure
@INSTR Read the text and answer the questions.
_80
@PANEL
Most architecture offices have a hierarchical structure. At the top is the Principal who makes major decisions and signs the drawings. Below the principal are project architects who manage specific jobs like housing, schools, and repair work. Each project architect supervises junior architects and draughtsmen who prepare the drawings. Site architects deliver the work on site, checking setting out, materials and quality.
Offices also employ support staff like administrators, drivers, and model makers. While these roles may seem less visible, they are essential. For example, drivers make sure survey equipment reaches the site. Administrators manage permit applications and records. Every role is important for the office to function effectively.
Recent years have seen changes in office structures. Smaller practices operate more flexibly, with fewer management layers. This allows decisions to be made faster. However, managing fees and ensuring drawing quality remain challenges even with simpler structures.

@PART 7 | Part 7: Culture
@EX Part 7: Architecture Sector in Syria and the Region
@INSTR Read about architecture in Syria and the Middle East region, then answer questions.
_80
@PANEL
The architecture sector in Syria has grown significantly in response to war damage and the need to rebuild whole districts. Offices like the large Damascus practices, international conservation teams, and small local studios employ thousands of staff. These staff serve both urban families and returning communities. Working in Syria's offices requires courage because staff visit sites that are still unsafe.
In neighboring countries like Lebanon and Jordan, offices employ architects who design housing for refugees and host communities. The work is challenging but essential. These organizations demonstrate how dedicated professionals keep working in hard times. Syrian architects are internationally recognized for their skill and resilience.
Many Syrian professionals who studied abroad chose to return to work in offices at home. They sacrifice higher fees in wealthy countries to rebuild their own cities. This shows deep commitment to nation-building and helping people return to their districts. Their choices inspire others to consider architecture careers.
