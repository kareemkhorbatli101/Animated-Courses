@INSTR Read about architecture in Syria and answer the factual questions.

@PANEL
Syrian Architects and Building Institutions
Syria has a long tradition of building and of training builders. The Order of Syrian Engineers and Architects is the professional body that registers architects and engineers. It sets the rules for practice, keeps the register, and issues the stamp that drawings must carry before a permit is given.
Many smaller offices in Syria focus on specific kinds of work. Some design housing, preparing plans for apartment blocks and family houses. Others work on schools, clinics and offices. Since the conflict, many offices have worked on assessing damaged buildings and designing repairs, which has become one of the largest areas of practice in the country.
International consultants also work in Syria alongside local offices. They bring funding, technical expertise, and specialist knowledge of conservation and structural repair. Partnerships between Syrian architects and international organizations strengthen the capacity to rebuild.
Architects in Syria often face challenges including shortages of materials, interrupted power, and difficult access to sites. Despite these obstacles, they remain committed to their work of repairing cities and building a better future for all Syrians.

@PASSAGE
What is Architecture?
Architecture is the design of buildings and of the spaces between them. An architect decides how a building is arranged, how it stands up, and how it looks. Architects work on social, technical, and environmental goals at the same time. 
Architecture exists in almost every country in the world. It ranges from a single family house to a whole new district. Some famous examples include the Umayyad Mosque in Damascus, the Citadel of Aleppo, and the colonnaded streets of Palmyra.
Architects are paid a fee by the client, which may be a family, a company, or a public institution. They employ staff and work closely with engineers and surveyors. Students work as interns because they want to learn how real projects are made.
The brief of a project is its statement of purpose. For example, a school brief says how many classrooms are needed. A hospital brief sets out the departments. A housing brief gives the number and size of flats. All briefs describe what the people who use the building will need.
Architects are essential because buildings must be safe, usable and lasting. They prepare drawings, obtain permits, and supervise construction. Communities benefit greatly from careful architectural work.

Types of Buildings and Their Design
Architects design many different kinds of building. Residential buildings provide homes. They include family houses and apartment blocks. Their design must give privacy, daylight and ventilation to every room.
Educational buildings include schools and universities. They need classrooms of the right size, quiet circulation, and safe outdoor space. Some educational buildings also include workshops and laboratories.
Healthcare buildings must be planned carefully. Clinics and hospitals need clear routes for patients and staff, and rooms arranged so that clean and dirty traffic do not cross. Equipment and services take a lot of space.
Public buildings serve the whole city. They include museums, libraries and municipal offices. They work to be welcoming, accessible and easy to find.
Commercial buildings house shops and offices. They need flexible floors and good access for deliveries and for customers.
All these buildings work together to make a city. They employ thousands of builders and depend on the skill of many trades.

@PANEL
Architecture in Syria: History and Current Work
Syria has a rich history of building. Damascus has been continuously inhabited for thousands of years, and the Umayyad Mosque, completed in 715, is one of the oldest great buildings in the Islamic world. Syrian builders have worked in stone, brick, timber and mud brick for many centuries.
Today, many kinds of architectural work are carried out in Syria. Some offices are small practices founded and run by Syrians. Others work with international consultants. Together they address critical needs in housing, schools, healthcare, and the repair of damaged buildings.
Housing architects help Syrian families return to their districts. They design apartment blocks, assess damaged structures, and prepare repair drawings. Conservation architects work in the old cities of Damascus and Aleppo. They repair vaults, domes and stone walls.
After the conflict, architects have prepared damage assessments for whole neighbourhoods. They record what remains, decide what can be repaired, set priorities, and design temporary shelter where rebuilding will take years.
Landscape architects help restore public squares and gardens. Planning offices work to protect Syria's historic quarters and archaeological sites.
The work of architects is challenging. They operate with limited materials and often in difficult conditions. But they remain committed to helping Syrians rebuild their lives. International partnerships help local offices expand their capacity to serve communities.

Architectural Values and Professional Principles
Architects are guided by important values and ethics in their work. Professional principles include safety, honesty, fairness, and independence.
Safety means that the health and lives of the people who will use a building come before appearance or cost. Architects believe every user deserves a structure that will stand, and this is why drawings are checked and permits are required.
Fairness means serving the people who will use the building, not only the person who pays. Architects think about the tenant as well as the owner. They do not design spaces that exclude.
Independence means architects give honest technical advice even when a client would prefer a cheaper answer. They stay professionally independent so their certificates can be trusted. This allows them to refuse work that would be unsafe.
Honesty means architects state what they know and do not claim expertise they lack. This honesty allows architects to speak up about risk and to protect people who have no voice in the project.
These principles guide architects every day. They explain why architects are trusted and respected in communities. Following these values makes architectural work effective and ethical.
