# English for Media and Communications 2 · International Edition — Adaptation Plan

Adapting **English for Media and Communications 2 · Student's Book · B1.1–B1.2**
(Officer & Manager tracks, Care Foundation, Damascus) into an **International Edition**.

Same method as the four books before it: measure the source first, then transpose. This is
a structural twin, built to the source's own measurements.

### The two tracks

Parts 1–6 and 8–10 are the **Officer track**, marked `[OP] Officer track · <phase>`; Part 7
is the **Manager track**, marked `[PR] Manager track · leading the team`. The phase label
changes part by part: *in-class starter · pre-class input · grammar focus · listening ·
speaking · extended reading · writing · leading the team · consolidation · judgement ·
review*.

### The 42-task unit template

and each of those varies by a single item (a fourth gap, a fifth can-do line).

## 2 · The transposition

The source teaches communications English inside **one organisation in one country**. It
says so in every unit's subtitle: *Care Foundation, Damascus*. Every reading, every
dialogue and every decision happens there, and three tasks a unit are named for the
country — *Telling Syria's Story*, *Consent and Community in Syria*, *A Steady Voice in
Syria*.

| | Source | International Edition |
|---|---|---|
| The organisation | **Care Foundation**, a humanitarian organisation in Damascus | **Meridian Relief**, an international humanitarian organisation |
| Where it works | across Syria | a communications hub in **Geneva** and country offices in **Amman · Dhaka · Nairobi · Lima · Freetown** |
| The team | one office, one language | one team across five time zones, working in English because it is the language they share |
| The audience | the Syrian public and donors | the people in the stories, the public in several countries, and donors elsewhere |
| The rules | one set of standards | one global standard, plus what each country's law and each community's custom require |
| Part 5's third text | *Telling Syria's Story* | *Telling the Story from Where You Are* — the learner's own country is the comparison |
| Part 9 | a decision at the Damascus office | the same decision, at a named country office, rotating through the five |

This is the one decision the book turns on. Making the organisation **international** does
not add a topic; it gives every topic the source already teaches a second dimension, at no
cost to the syllabus:

* **consent** is not only a form but a form that means different things in different
  places, and must still mean one thing to the organisation — Unit 3;
* a **channel** that reaches everyone in one country reaches almost nobody in another, so
  Unit 4's comparatives have a reason beyond grammar;
* an **interview** is often done through an interpreter, or in the speaker's second
  language — Unit 6;
* **data protection** is one rule book at head office and another in each country —
  Unit 8;
* a **crisis** arrives at three in the morning in somebody's time zone — Unit 9;
* a **quote** has to survive translation and still be the person's own words — Unit 10.

And the learner the book is written for changes in the way the brief asks: from a
communications officer in one country to a communications officer in a team that spans
several.

### The syllabus (unchanged)

The ten unit titles and the ten grammar points are the source's own, verbatim.

| Unit | Title | Grammar |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Working in Media and Communications | present simple, present continuous and state verbs |
| 2 | The Newsroom and Its Tools | quantifiers and 'there has been' |
| 3 | Consent, Safeguarding and Do No Harm | imperatives, sequencers and the first conditional |
| 4 | Channels and Audiences | comparatives, superlatives and 'as … as' |
| 5 | Writing the Story | the passive across tenses |
| 6 | Interviews and Asking Questions | modals: requests, advice and deduction |
| 7 | Planning a Campaign | future forms and the first conditional in plans |
| 8 | Standards, Style and Data Protection | obligation, permission and reported rules |
| 9 | Crisis and Response Communications | present perfect continuous and the past as background |
| 10 | Telling It to the World | reported speech, attribution and the values of the work |

## 3 · Fidelity criteria

Items marked **[auto]** are checked by the three suites in `qa/`; the rest by read-through.

**Architecture**
1. 10 units, same order, same grammar sequence, same unit titles. **[auto]**
2. 42 named tasks per unit in the source's exact slot order; 43 in Unit 10. **[auto]**
3. Warm Up + Parts 1–10 in every unit, with the source's part titles and track lines. **[auto]**
4. Officer track and Manager track labelled as the source labels them, phase by phase. **[auto]**
5. The bar colours the source uses: orange for Warm Up and Part 10, blue for Parts 1–6
   and 8–9, teal for Part 7. **[auto]**
6. Five audio tracks per unit, numbered n.1–n.5, at the source's five slots. **[auto]**
7. 15 tables per unit, in the source's Column A / Column B and Grammar Focus shapes. **[auto]**
8. 10 multiple-choice stems per unit, four options each. **[auto]**
9. Every unit closes: spiral review → Can-Do checklist → unit glossary. **[auto]**

**Exercise system**
10. Every matching task has one option that is not needed, and says so.
11. Every task that can be exemplified opens with a worked `0.` example.
12. Every writing task gives a model; four of the five also give a plan or a check list.
13. Every speaking task gives a model and role cards.
14. The Grammar Focus box carries a Remember line, a Watch out! line and a Gloss.
15. Five Gloss lines per unit, at the source's five slots. **[auto]**

**Language and level**
16. B1.1–B1.2 throughout, at the source's density.
17. British spelling; the source's typography (`·`, `—`, `→`, `☐`, `○`, `🔊`, curly quotes).
18. No unit uses its grammar in a text before the unit that teaches it, except as recognition.

**Texts**
19. Warm-up reading 96–112 words. **[auto]**
20. Part 2 Notice 86–119 words. **[auto]**
21. Listening scripts: n.2 134–155 · n.3 134–154 · n.4 118–131 · n.5 144–166. **[auto]**
22. Part 5 main text 207–234 words; Part 5 place text 170–203. **[auto]**
23. Part 6 models 68–111 words, four per unit; 7E model 92–109. **[auto]**
24. Part 8 reading 207–225 words; Part 9 case 165–193; model decision 86–97. **[auto]**
25. Unit glossary 113–158 words. **[auto]**

**Integrity**
26. Everything about Meridian Relief is invented and declared invented.
27. The communications practice is true and general: informed consent, safeguarding, do no
    harm, attribution, embargo, right of reply, corrections, data minimisation.
28. No real organisation, government, news outlet or person is given invented words or figures.
29. No country is characterised. The five offices are settings, not subjects. The learner's
    own country is invited into the discussion tasks, never described for them.
30. The source's humanitarian ethic — protect people first, never publish without consent,
    tell the truth, correct mistakes openly — is carried over unchanged.

**Production**
31. The source's design system, decoded from its OOXML: Calibri 11 pt on A4 with 1-inch
    margins, shaded paragraphs rather than boxed tables, the two-table Column A / Column B
    matching pattern, the 5×4 Grammar Focus table. **[auto]**
32. Each unit within ±10 % of its source unit, the book within ±8 %, and the page count
    within ±8 %. **[auto]**

## 4 · Build

A fifth toolchain, because this is a fifth design system — and the first with no
illustrations at all, so there is no figure work:

```
python3 build/compile.py       # dist/EMC2_International_StudentBook.docx
python3 build/render_md.py     # dist/EMC2_International_StudentBook.md
python3 qa/check_parity.py     # the 42-slot template, bars, tables, MCQ, audio
python3 qa/check_texts.py      # every text block the source sets a range for
python3 qa/check_content.py    # typography, spelling, scaffolding, transposition
```
