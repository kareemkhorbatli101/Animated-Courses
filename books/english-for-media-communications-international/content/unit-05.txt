@TASK Part 2: Grammar Focus Box
@GRAMMAR
 | Form | Use | Example
Present simple passive | am / is / are + past participle | a routine or a rule
Past simple passive | was / were + past participle | a finished action
Present perfect passive | has / have been + past participle | finished, and it matters now
Future and modal passive | will be / must be + past participle | a plan or an obligation
@REMEMBER Use the passive when the doer is obvious, unknown or not important. Add 'by + the doer' only when it matters: 'The draft was written by Tomás.'
@WATCH Watch out: do not forget the past participle. Say 'The story was checked', not 'The story was check'.
@GLOSS past participle = the third form of a verb (write → written, check → checked)    ·    doer = the person or thing that does the action

@TASK Part 5: How a Story Is Written
@RUBRIC Read the text and choose the correct answer.
@TEXT
A humanitarian story is not written in the way a news report is written, and the difference is not style but consequence. A news reporter is accountable to readers; a communications officer is accountable to readers and to the people in the story, who will still be living in that village long after the article has been forgotten. For this reason the writing process is built around checking rather than speed. The draft is written by whoever did the interview, and it is written quickly, while the conversation is fresh. It is then given to a colleague who was not present, because a reader who was not there asks the questions a distant reader will ask: who is this person, what happened first, why does this number matter? Figures are checked against the project report, never against memory, because memory rounds numbers upwards. Quotes are read back to the speaker in the language they used, and if the speaker wants a word changed, the word is changed; a quote is a record of what somebody chose to say, not a trophy. Captions are written by the photographer, who alone knows who is in the frame. Finally, an editor signs the piece off, and nothing goes out without that signature. Teams that skip a step usually skip the read-back, because it is the slowest and the least visible. It is also the one that protects the person who trusted you.

@TASK Part 5: Telling the Truth, Protecting the Person
@RUBRIC Read about truth and protection in writing. Then mark each sentence True (T) or False (F).
@TEXT
Two duties sit beside each other in every humanitarian story, and sometimes they pull in different directions. The first is to tell the truth: not to exaggerate, not to invent, not to let a strong sentence carry a weak fact. The second is to protect the people in the story, who are rarely in a position to protect themselves once a text has been published. Most of the time these duties agree. A true story, carefully told, is also a safe one. But occasionally they do not. A detail may be true and important and still dangerous: the name of a street, a scar, a job, the fact that a family was the only one in the village to receive something. When that happens, the rule is not to choose between truth and protection. It is to find the version that is both. Usually it exists. The street can become 'a district in the north'. The scar can simply not be mentioned. What is never acceptable is to publish a false detail in order to protect somebody, because a reader who later discovers it will stop believing the parts that were true.

draft = an early version of a piece of writing    ·    edit = to improve a text by changing and cutting    ·    headline = the short, clear line at the top    ·    lead = the first paragraph, which says what happened    ·    quote = a person's exact words, inside quotation marks    ·    attribution = saying clearly who said or wrote something    ·    fact-check = to confirm that every detail is true    ·    read-back = reading a quote to the speaker to confirm it    ·    sign-off = the final approval before publication    ·    house style = the writing rules one organisation uses everywhere    ·    proofread = to check a text for small mistakes    ·    past participle = the third form of a verb (write → written)    ·    accountable = responsible to somebody for what you do    ·    consequence = what happens as a result    ·    remove = to take something out completely
