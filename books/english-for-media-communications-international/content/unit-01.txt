@TASK Part 2: Notice
@TEXT
Ingrid usually starts the day by reading the news from five countries. Tomás writes articles and often interviews people. Aisha takes photographs, and she always asks for consent first. These are the things that the team does every day. But today is different. Right now, the whole team is preparing the winter campaign. Notice the difference: we use the present simple for routines and facts, and the present continuous for actions that are happening now.
@LINE Look at the verbs in your teacher's copy. Which verbs describe a routine? Which describe an action happening now?

@TASK Part 2: Grammar Focus Box
@GRAMMAR
 | Form | Use | Example
Present simple | Aisha always asks for consent. | routines, facts, and frequency
Present continuous | Right now, she is choosing photographs. | actions happening now or these days
State verbs | I know the answer. (not 'I am knowing') | feelings and states, not usually continuous
Adverbs of frequency | always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never | before the main verb; after the verb 'be'
@REMEMBER Use the present simple for routines and facts, and the present continuous for actions happening now. State verbs (know, believe, need, want, prefer) are not usually used in the continuous.
@WATCH Watch out: put the adverb of frequency before the main verb ('she often writes') but after the verb 'be' ('she is often busy').
@GLOSS fact = something that is always true    ·    temporary = lasting for a short time, not permanent

@TASK Part 5: What a Communications Team Does
@RUBRIC Read the text and choose the correct answer.
@TEXT
The communications team at Meridian Relief has an important job: it helps the public understand the work of the organisation. The team does many different things. Writers prepare posts and short articles, photographers take pictures of the projects, and social media officers share the news online. Although the tasks are different, and although the team sits in six different cities, they all have the same goal — to tell true stories with respect. Every morning, the team meets online to plan the day. They decide which stories to tell and which photographs to use. Usually, they work on several projects at the same time. This week, for example, they are preparing a campaign about winter aid, because thousands of families need warm clothes and heating. Good communication is not easy. The team must think carefully about every word and every image, and about how each one will be read in a different country. They always ask for consent before they publish a photograph, and they never share private information. When people trust an organisation, they are more likely to support it. For this reason, honesty is the most valuable tool the team has. The work is demanding, but it is also rewarding. At the end of a long day, the team knows that its words have helped real people, and that is why they do this job.

@RUBRIC Write about why communicators must always ask for consent. Give reasons and an example.
@MODEL
Consent is one of the most important rules in communication. When we ask for consent, we show respect for the people in our stories. We also protect them, because a photograph or a name can put someone at risk. For example, if we share a family's address without asking, we could put that family in danger. Some people think that a strong photo is more important than a rule, but they are wrong. A story that harms a person is never a good story. For this reason, a responsible communicator always asks first, and never publishes without permission.

communication = sharing information and ideas with people    ·    campaign = a series of actions to reach a goal    ·    consent = permission to use someone's words or photo    ·    audience = the people who read or watch your work    ·    journalist = a person who reports the news    ·    caption = the short text under a photograph    ·    press release = an official statement sent to the news    ·    feature = a longer article about one topic    ·    platform = a place online where you share content    ·    spokesperson = the person who speaks for an organisation    ·    appeal = a public request for help or money    ·    state verb = a verb about a state or feeling, not an action    ·    routine = something you do regularly    ·    dignity = the respect that every person deserves    ·    communicator = a person whose job is to share information and stories    ·    dignified = shown with respect and self-respect    ·    exaggerate = to make something seem bigger or better than it really is
