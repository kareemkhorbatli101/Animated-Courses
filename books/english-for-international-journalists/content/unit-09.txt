@PART 2 | Grammar: advice and rules | Should for advice, must and have to for rules.
@FIG u9-gA | should / shouldn't — for advice.
@GRAMMAR 1 · should — advice
{{You should}} + verb: {{You should}} check every fact.
{{You shouldn't}} + verb: {{You shouldn't}} guess.
We use *should* to give advice — a good idea, not a strong rule.

@GRAMMAR 2 · must / have to — rules
{{must}} = a strong rule: You {{must}} protect your source.
{{have to}} = it is necessary: You {{have to}} meet the deadline.
[[must not]] = it is forbidden: You {{must not}} file a rumour.

@FIG u9-gB | must = a strong rule · have to = it is necessary.
@GRAMMAR 3 · Which one?
Advice (a good idea): You {{should}} ask a follow-up question.
A rule (you have no choice): You {{must}} name your source.
Not necessary: You {{don't have to}} file it today, but you can.

@PART 5 | Reading: the rules | Two readings. Read for the advice and the rules.
@FIG g-reach | Advice for a new correspondent — what to do, and what will follow.
@SUBHEAD Reading A | · advice for a new correspondent
@READING Ten minutes of advice: a note for a new correspondent
On Sofia's first day at Atlas Wire, Mira gave her a short note of advice. "I have been a correspondent for many years," she wrote, "and this is what I have learned."
"First, you should check every fact. If you check, your story will be strong. If you rush and guess, you will make mistakes, and readers will not trust you. So take your time — it is better to be right than to be first."
"Second, there are rules you must follow. You must protect your source. You have to be honest, and you must not file a rumour as a fact. If you break these rules, you will lose the one thing a journalist really has: trust."
"Third, when you work in a country that is not your own, you should listen before you write. Learn how the place works. Get your accreditation early. And remember that the local journalist beside you knows more than you do — you should ask them, and you must credit them."
"And last: if you make a mistake, you must say sorry and correct it. Everyone makes mistakes. A good reporter is not the one who never fails, but the one who always tells the truth about it."

@FIG u9-read | Four rules that hold in every newsroom, in every country.
@SUBHEAD Reading B | · four things you must do
@READING The rules of the newsroom
**1. You must check every fact.** If you are not sure, ask, or leave it out. Do not guess.
**2. You must protect your source.** If a source is not safe, you must keep their name out of the story.
**3. You have to be fair.** Give both sides. If you only give one side, the story is not fair.
**4. You should fix your mistakes.** If you make a mistake, say sorry and correct it. Readers will trust you more, not less.
