@PART 2 | Grammar: asking politely | Can and could for requests, would for offers, and the order of a question.
@FIG u4-gA | Can, could and would make a question polite.
@GRAMMAR 1 · can / could — requests
{{can}}: {{Can I}} ask you a question?   {{Can you}} say that again?
{{could}} is more polite: {{Could you}} tell me your name, please?
Use them to ask for permission and to make a request.

@GRAMMAR 2 · would — offers and polite requests
{{Would you like}} to talk to me?   (an offer / a soft request)
{{Would you}} say a little more, please?
*would* is gentle and very polite.

@FIG u4-gB | A question: (wh-) + do / can / is + subject + verb + ?
@GRAMMAR 3 · Question forms
Yes / no: [[Do]] you live here?   [[Is]] the crossing open?   [[Can]] I record this?
Wh-: [[What do]] you do?   [[Where do]] you live?   [[Why did]] you come back?
Order: (wh-) + do / does / can / is + subject + verb + ?

@FIG g-reach | A short interview, written up for the wire.
@SUBHEAD Reading A | · an interview, written up
@READING 'My mother is on the other side': a family comes back to Kalina
This week, correspondent Mira Sandoval sat down with Elena Marku and her family at their new café, at the end of the bridge at Kalina. The family came back to the town last month, and they are already part of the street.
"Could you tell me why you came back?" Mira asked. Elena smiled. "It is our home," she said. "We were away for seven years, but we always wanted to come back. When we heard that the crossing was open again, we knew it was time. My mother is on the other side of the river. Now I can see her every day."
Mira asked what they were going to do. "We are going to run this little café," Elena said. "My husband makes the coffee, and I make the bread. Our children help after school. Every day, more neighbours come and sit with us."
The café is small — only six tables — but it is always full. "People do not only come for coffee," Elena said. "They come to talk, and to see old friends. The valley is waking up, and we are happy to be a part of it."
Dren, a journalist from the town, sat with them and helped when the English was difficult. "People here want to tell their own story," he said afterwards. "They only need somebody to ask properly."
Before she left, Mira asked one more question. "Would you like to say anything to your neighbours?" Elena laughed. "Yes," she said. "Please come and visit us. The door is always open, and the coffee is always hot."

@FIG u4-read | Prepare, be polite, listen, and get the quote exactly right.
@SUBHEAD Reading B | · preparing for an interview — four tips
@READING Four tips for a good interview
**1. Prepare your questions.** Write three or four questions before you go. Mix wh- questions and yes/no questions.
**2. Be polite.** Start with a request: 'Can I ask you a question?' Use *could* and *would*. Say please and thank you.
**3. Listen well.** Do not only read your questions. Listen to the answer, and ask a follow-up: 'Could you say more about that?'
**4. Get the quote right.** Write the person's exact words. If you work with an interpreter, ask them to translate word for word, and check anything you are not sure of. Do not change what the guest said.
