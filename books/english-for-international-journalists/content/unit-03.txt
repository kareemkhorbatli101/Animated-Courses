@PART 2 | Grammar: now and next | Present continuous for now, going to for plans, and prepositions for place.
@FIG u3-gA | Present continuous — am / is / are + verb-ing = happening now.
@GRAMMAR 1 · Present continuous — happening now
Form: {{am / is / are}} + verb{{-ing}}.
Mira {{is reporting}}.   People {{are crossing}}.   The stalls {{are opening}}.
We use it for things that are happening now, at this moment.

@GRAMMAR 2 · going to — plans and the future
Form: {{am / is / are}} + {{going to}} + verb.
She {{is going to}} interview a stall-holder.   We {{are going to}} cover the story.
We use it for plans and what we think will happen next.

@FIG u3-gB | Prepositions of place — in, at, next to, in front of.
@GRAMMAR 3 · Prepositions of place
{{in}} a place: The town is {{in}} the valley.
{{at}} a point: Mira is {{at}} the crossing.
{{next to}} / {{in front of}} / {{behind}}: The café is {{next to}} the bridge.

@FIG g-notes | Live updates in brief — short items from the field.
@SUBHEAD Reading A | · a live report, written down
@READING A busy morning at the Kalina crossing
It is nine o'clock, and the crossing at Kalina is waking up. The barrier is up, the stalls are open, and a big crowd is here. Correspondent Mira Sandoval is standing at the old gate with a microphone.
"This is a very busy morning," Mira says. "People are carrying bags and boxes across the bridge. Families are coming from both sides of the river, and children are running ahead of them. Next to the gate, a young family is opening a small café. This is their first day."
Behind Mira, a stall-holder is putting fruit on his table. He is smiling. "The people are coming back," he says. "Every morning, more families cross. It is good for business, and it is good for the valley."
At the end of the bridge, a group of workers is painting the rail. "We are getting the crossing ready," one worker says. "Next week, there is going to be a small festival here, on both sides."
A little further on, two teachers are standing in front of the new school bus. They are waiting for the children. "We are starting again today," one teacher says. "Forty children are coming this morning, and more are coming next week. It is a very happy day for us."
Near the barrier, an old man is watching the crowd. He is not shopping; he is just looking. "For seven years, this bridge was closed and quiet," he says slowly. "Now it is full of people again. I am very happy. I am coming here every morning now, just to see the families."
Mira looks at the camera. "So the news this morning is simple and happy: Kalina is open. In a moment, I am going to talk to the family at the new café. This is Mira Sandoval, live from the crossing. Back to you in the studio."

@FIG u3-read | Say where you are, say what is happening, say what is next — then stop.
@SUBHEAD Reading B | · reporting from the field — three tips
@READING Three tips for a live report
**1. Say where you are.** Start with the place: 'This is Mira Sandoval, live from the crossing at Kalina.' The listener is far away and needs to see the scene.
**2. Say what is happening now.** Use the present continuous: 'The stalls are opening. A crowd is crossing.' Talk about this moment, not last week.
**3. Say what is next.** Use *going to* for the plan: 'In a moment, I am going to talk to a stall-holder.' This keeps the listener with you.
And always: stay calm, speak clearly, and only say what you can see. Do not guess.

@PART 8 | Ethics: live and fair | Live news is fast. A journalist is still accurate and kind.
@FIG u3-ethA | On air, still say only what you can see — a fact, not a guess.
@SUBHEAD A first case | · a number you cannot see
@CASE Mira is live at the crossing. The studio asks her: "How many people are there?" Mira can see a big crowd, but she did not count them. A voice in her ear says, "Just say ten thousand — it sounds good." The report is live, and listeners in many countries are hearing it now. But Mira did not count ten thousand people. She is a journalist, and she knows the Code: report only what you can see, and do not guess a number as a fact. What does Mira say on air?
