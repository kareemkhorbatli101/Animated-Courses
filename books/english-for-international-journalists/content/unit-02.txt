@PART 2 | Grammar: questions & describing | Ask about a story, and describe it. Half the practice is controlled; half is your own words.
@LABEL MODEL | The grammar
@GRAMMAR 1 · wh- question words — the five Ws + How
{{Who}} = people     {{What}} = the thing / event
{{When}} = the time     {{Where}} = the place
{{Why}} = the reason     {{How}} = the way

@GRAMMAR 2 · asking a question
With *be*:  [[Wh + is/are + …?]]   What is the news?   Where is the crossing?
With other verbs:  [[Wh + do/does + subject + verb?]]   When does the crossing open?   Why do families come back?

@GRAMMAR 3 · adjectives — describing a story
Before the noun:  a {{big}} story · an {{important}} event · {{international}} news
After *be*:  The story is {{important}}. The bridge is {{busy}}.

@WATCHOUT In a question, the wh- word comes first: *What is the news?* (not *The news is what?*). The adjective does not change: two big *storys* → two big *stories*, but *big* stays *big*.

@PART 5 | Read the story | Two readings. Reading A is about news around the world. Reading B is 'news in brief'.
@SUBHEAD Reading A | · factual · current
@LABEL NOTICE | Before you read
@FIG u2-read | Four tests a story has to pass before it is news.
@EX 5A | What kinds of stories are in the news in your country? Name two.
@READING What is news around the world today?
Open any international news website, and the stories are not all the same size. Some news is about big change. People move. Borders open and close. Governments meet, and agree, or do not agree. This is important news for many people, in many countries at once.
Other news is about daily life. Prices go up and down. A new clinic opens. A school starts again. A bridge reopens, and a market comes back with it. People want this news, too — and often they want it more.
There is also soft news — sport, music, and culture. A football match, a new song, a festival: these are stories too, and they cross borders easily.
For a journalist, the job is the same in every country. Find the story. Ask the five Ws. Check the facts. Name the source. The subject changes; the work does not.

@PART 8 | Ethics & the Code: get the facts right | A story must be true. Check every fact before you report it.
@LABEL MODEL | A case

@WHITE
The Code says: **be accurate**. A **fact** is true. A **rumour** is a story with no proof. An **opinion** is what someone thinks. Report facts. Check them with a source.
