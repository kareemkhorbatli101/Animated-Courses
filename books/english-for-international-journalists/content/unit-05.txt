@PART 2 | Grammar: reporting what people said | say and tell, reported speech, and attribution.
@FIG u5-gA | say (that)… / tell + a person + (that)…
@GRAMMAR 1 · say and tell
{{say}} (that)… — no person: She {{said}} (that) the crossing {{was}} open.
{{tell}} + a person + (that)…: She {{told me}} (that) it {{was}} open.
Remember: we **tell somebody**, but we **say something**.

@FIG u5-gB | Direct words become reported speech — the tense moves back.
@GRAMMAR 2 · Reported speech
Direct: "The crossing {{is}} open."  →  Reported: He said the crossing {{was}} open.
Direct: "We {{are}} happy."  →  She said they {{were}} happy.
The tense moves back: [[is → was]], [[are → were]], [[am → was]].

@GRAMMAR 3 · Attribution
Always say who a fact comes from.
{{According to}} the Crossing Office, a hundred families have crossed back.
The spokesperson {{said}} that the bridge {{was}} open.  A stall-holder {{told}} Mira it {{was}} busy.

@SUBHEAD Reading A | · a report with quotes
@READING Voices from the Kalina crossing
This week, correspondent Mira Sandoval spoke to many people at the crossing at Kalina. They all had something to say about the bridge that is open again.
Elena Marku, who owns the new café at the end of the bridge, told Mira that business was good. "The café is always full," she said. "People do not only come for coffee. They come to talk, and to see old friends."
A stall-holder on the bridge said that families crossed every morning. According to him, the market was busy from early until late. "Every day, I see new faces," he added.
The Crossing Office made a short statement. According to the office, a hundred families have now returned to the valley, and the school will open next week. The office also said that a small festival is planned for Friday.
Not everyone agreed on the numbers. One worker told Mira that he thought there were more than a hundred families. Another said there were fewer. Mira wrote down both, and she used the office's figure, with attribution, for the story.
Priya Nair, the verification journalist at Atlas Wire, said that this was the right thing to do. "We report what people say," she explained, "but we always name the source. If two people say different things, we say so. We do not choose one and hide the other. On an international story that goes out in six languages, that matters even more: a number with no name behind it travels a very long way."

@FIG u5-read | Name the source, use say and tell correctly, move the tense back, keep it exact.
@SUBHEAD Reading B | · say it right — four rules for quotes
@READING Four rules for quotes
**1. Name your source.** For every fact and every number, say who said it: 'according to…' or '… said'.
**2. Use say and tell correctly.** You *say* something; you *tell* somebody. 'She said that…' or 'She told me that…'.
**3. Change the tense.** In reported speech, the tense moves back: 'is' becomes 'was', 'are' becomes 'were'.
**4. Keep it accurate.** Use the person's exact words in a quote. Do not change what they said. If the interview was in another language, keep the translation close and say that it is a translation. If two sources disagree, report both.
