@PART 2 | Grammar: the present perfect | Has/have + done for a past action with a result now.
@FIG u8-gA | Present perfect — a past action with a result now.
@GRAMMAR 1 · Present perfect — what has happened
Form: {{has / have}} + past participle.
The crossing {{has reopened}}.  Families {{have returned}}.  The valley {{has changed}}.
We use it for a past action with a result now — the crossing is open now.

@FIG u8-gB | since + a point in time · for + a length of time.
@GRAMMAR 2 · for and since
{{since}} + a point in time: The valley has changed {{since}} the spring.
{{for}} + a length of time: Families have been back {{for}} six months.
Both go with the present perfect — from the past until now.

@GRAMMAR 3 · just, already, yet, ever, never
{{just}}: The school has {{just}} reopened.   {{already}}: Families have {{already}} returned.
{{yet}} (– / ?): The clinic has not reopened {{yet}}.
{{ever}} / {{never}}: Have you {{ever}} visited?  I have {{never}} seen it so busy.

@SUBHEAD Reading A | · how the valley has changed
@READING How Kalina has changed
Kalina has changed a great deal since the spring. A year ago, the bridge was closed and the road was empty; today, people cross it from six in the morning. The market has reopened, and, according to the Crossing Office, a hundred families have returned to the valley.
Life has improved in many small ways. The road has been repaired, the old gate has been replaced, and new lamps have been fitted along the river. The school itself has just reopened, and forty children have already gone back to class on the other side.
"I have had my stall here since the spring," said Bekim, who sells fruit and vegetables on the bridge. "Every week is better than the last. The valley has become a home again. I have never been so busy."
But not everything has changed yet. The clinic is still closed, and the bus does not cross the bridge yet. "We have done a lot," the Crossing Office said, "but we have not finished. There is still work to do."
For the people of Kalina, the change is easy to see. In one year, a quiet, empty crossing has become a busy road again — and, they say, the best is still to come.

@SUBHEAD Reading B | · present perfect or past simple? — four tips
@READING Four tips for the present perfect
**1. Use the present perfect for a result now.** 'The crossing has reopened.' (and it is open now).
**2. Use the past simple for a finished time.** 'The crossing reopened last April.' (a finished time — last April).
**3. Use since and for.** 'since the spring' (a point) / 'for six months' (a length). Both link to now.
**4. Report both sides.** Say what has improved — and what has not changed yet. That is fair and accurate.
