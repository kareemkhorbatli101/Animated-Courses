@PART 2 | Grammar: the passive | Was/were + done for the past, is/are + done for now.
@FIG u7-gA | Active → passive: the result comes first.
@GRAMMAR 1 · Past passive — what was done
Form: {{was / were}} + past participle.
The bridge {{was rebuilt}}.  The road {{was repaired}}.  New lamps {{were fitted}}.
We use it to say what was done, when the result matters more than who did it.

@GRAMMAR 2 · Active and passive
Active: Workers {{rebuilt}} the bridge.  (who + did + what)
Passive: The bridge {{was rebuilt}} ({{by}} workers).  (what + was done)
Add {{by}}… only if the doer matters. Often, we do not need it.

@FIG u7-gB | Present passive — for what is done again and again.
@GRAMMAR 3 · Present passive — what is done
Form: {{is / are}} + past participle.
The wire {{is filed}} every hour.  The stalls {{are cleaned}} each morning.
We use it for things that happen again and again.

@SUBHEAD Reading A | · a report on the rebuilding
@READING A crossing rebuilt: the work at Kalina
A great deal of work was done at the Kalina crossing this year. According to the Crossing Office, the bridge was rebuilt and the road was repaired last April.
The rail was painted, and the old gate was replaced. New lamps were fitted along the river, and the riverside path was cleared after seven years. Most of this work was done by local volunteers, with help from the councils on both sides.
"Every week, something new is finished," said one volunteer, who worked on the bridge. "The gate was the hardest job. It was rusted shut for years, but now it opens every morning. Children walk through it on their way to school."
Some work is still being done. This week, the school is being painted, and the riverside path is being cleaned. "The project is not complete," the office said, "but most of it is finished, and the crossing looks like a crossing again."
The office asked to thank the volunteers by name. "The bridge was rebuilt by our neighbours — from both sides of the river," one worker said. "This was done by us, together. That is why it means so much."

@FIG g-model | A report reaches readers who want to know what was done — and by whom.
@SUBHEAD Reading B | · active or passive? — four tips
@READING Four tips for the passive
**1. Use the passive for the result.** When the work matters more than the worker: 'The bridge was rebuilt.'
**2. Use 'by…' when the doer matters.** 'The bridge was rebuilt by local volunteers.' Name people who should get credit.
**3. Do not hide who did it.** 'Mistakes were made' hides the person. If readers should know who, say who.
**4. Keep it clear.** The active voice is often shorter and clearer. Use the passive when it helps — not everywhere.

@PART 8 | Ethics: who did it? | The passive can hide the doer. A fair report says who — when it matters.
@FIG u7-ethA | 'Mistakes were made' hides the person. If readers should know who, say who.
@SUBHEAD A first case | · hiding the doer
@CASE A part of the new river wall fell down a month after it was built. The building company sends Mira a line for the story: "A mistake was made, and the wall will be repaired." But the passive hides who made the mistake. Readers — and the families who live beside the wall — have a right to know which company built it. Mira can file the company's soft line, or she can find out who did the work and name them. The Code says: be accountable — do not use the passive to hide who is responsible. What does Mira do?

@SUBHEAD A second case | · who gets the credit
@FIG u7-ethB | Give credit fairly — to the people who did the work, not only the officials.
@CASE The bridge was rebuilt mostly by local volunteers, who worked for four months. But an official asks Mira to write only that "the bridge was rebuilt by the councils." The councils helped and paid for materials, but the volunteers did most of the work. If Mira writes only 'by the councils', the volunteers get no credit — and the story goes out in six languages. The Code says: be fair and accurate — give credit to the people who really did the work. What does Mira do?
