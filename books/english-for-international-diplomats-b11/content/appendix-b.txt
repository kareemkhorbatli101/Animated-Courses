@SPOKENHEAD Spoken Sample Answer  🔊
@SPOKEN
~Linh: Okay, let us go through the five visitor types. Let us start with diplomats and ambassadors.
~Huy: That is the Protocol Department. When a new ambassador arrives, the most important event is presenting credentials to the minister. Everything about that visit — the ceremony, the timing, the seating — is managed by the protocol team.
~Linh: What about journalists?
~Huy: The Press and Information Office. Journalists need official press accreditation before they can cover ministry events. They also need clear access to the spokesperson for official quotes and statements.
~Linh: And investors?
~Huy: Economic Affairs handles them. Investors coming to Viet Nam now are usually interested in manufacturing, energy, and infrastructure. The ministry helps connect them with the right government bodies and provides the regulatory overview.
~Linh: UN officials and NGO workers?
~Huy: They usually meet with Political Affairs or the Humanitarian Liaison team. They coordinate their field programmes with the government, share updates on their operations, and work with the relevant administrative offices to ensure safe and effective access to the areas where their programmes operate.
~Linh: And citizens living abroad?
~Huy: Consular Section. They may need a new passport, a document certified, or guidance on their legal status if they want to return and resettle or invest. This section deals with very personal situations and needs a particularly professional and empathetic approach.

@ANSHEAD Exercise 36: Reflection Discussion
@QHEAD Question 1 - Sample Answer:
@ANS
B1 Target: With a German investor, the most important adaptation is precision — specific answers, documents, and dates. With a Gulf investor, it is patience: the relationship is part of the due diligence — not a courtesy before it.
@SEP
@ANS
B2 Extension: With a German investor the adaptation is precision and pace: move to the agenda quickly, answer narrow questions narrowly, and never estimate a figure. With a Gulf investor the adaptation is patience and genuine hospitality — because the relationship is part of the assessment rather than a preliminary to it. What does not change is the content — the same legal framework, the same arbitration clause, the same honest admission of what has not been tested. Only the sequence and the register change, and treating either adaptation as optional is how a meeting is lost.
@QHEAD Question 2 - Sample Answer:
@ANS
B1 Target: The signal usually comes from the visitor — not the clock. They ask a specific question, or they refer to a document, or their adviser opens a folder. When that happens, move to specifics immediately and do not return to the social register.
@SEP

B2 Extension: The transition signal almost always comes from the guest rather than from the agenda. It may be a specific question, a reference to a document, an adviser opening a file, or simply a change in posture. When it comes, move to the specifics without hesitating and without going back to the social register — because retreating into courtesies at that point reads as unpreparedness. If no signal comes and time is genuinely short, the professional move is to offer rather than to switch: "whenever you would like to turn to specifics, I am ready" leaves the decision where it belongs.
