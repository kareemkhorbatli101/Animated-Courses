@PANEL
The Kazakhstan–France Bilateral Cooperation Framework, signed in Astana in June 2025, represents one of the most comprehensive bilateral agreements concluded by Kazakhstan with a European partner. The Framework was signed by both foreign ministers at a ceremony attended by senior officials from both countries. It covers three primary areas of cooperation: cultural and heritage exchange, academic and scientific partnership, and infrastructure and digital development.
In the cultural area, the Framework establishes a Joint Cultural Commission that will meet annually to design and oversee cultural exchange programs. Kazakh cultural heritage exhibitions are going to be hosted in Paris, Lyon, and Marseille over a three-year cycle, while French cultural programs will be presented in Astana and Almaty. A Kazakhstan–France Film and Literature Festival shall be established as an annual event, alternating between the two capitals.
In the academic area, the Framework provides for a student exchange program that will enable fifty Kazakh students per year to undertake postgraduate studies in France, and ten French academic specialists to conduct research in Kazakhstan. A joint research program in renewable energy is going to be launched in September 2025, with funding from both governments. Kazakh university partnerships with four French institutions are being formalized this year.
In the infrastructure and digital area, a joint working group is going to develop a Kazakhstan–France digital connectivity roadmap by December 2025. French technical expertise will support Kazakhstan's e-government platform development. An annual bilateral committee, comprising six members from each side, shall oversee the overall Framework implementation and report to both foreign ministries. The Framework is designed for an initial five-year period with a review mechanism at three years.

@INSTR Diplomatic speeches and toasts require a tone that is both warm (showing genuine feeling) and dignified (maintaining authority). The intonation pattern is: slightly elevated and expressive on warm phrases; falling and settled on formal declarations. // Practice the contrast with these three toast openings: // 1. Too flat (robotic): "This evening. We celebrate. A partnership." — monotone; sounds like a list, not a toast // 2. Too animated (informal): "This evening we CELEBRATE this AMAZING partnership!" — rising intonation; sounds like a business conference, not a diplomatic dinner // 3. Correct (warm and dignified): "This EVENING — [slight pause] — we celebrate a partnership..." — slight elevation on "evening," fall and pause, steady warm pace on "partnership" // Practice all three versions, then record your own using version three as the model. // Additional toast openings to practice: // • "It is my HONOR to propose a toast this evening..." // • "To friendship, to cooperation, and to a FUTURE that will be built together."

@EX 8B:  Three-Culture Formal Dinner Comparison Table
@INSTR Complete the table comparing formal dinner norms across three cultural contexts. Use the choices provided.
@GRID
Aspect | European (French / German) | Gulf Arab | East Asian (Japanese / Korean)
Formal greeting | Firm handshake; direct eye contact; first names often offered after introduction |  | Slight bow; two-handed business card exchange (receive with both hands, study briefly before putting away); formal title plus family name
Toasts |  | No alcohol; non-alcoholic alternative must be provided with dignity; participation in toast is valued even without alcohol | Sake or juice; follow the senior person's lead; do not pour your own drink — pour for your neighbor and they will pour for you
Seating hierarchy | Official rank guides seating; host to the right of guest of honor at formal dinners; less rigid than Asian or Gulf models | Strictly hierarchical; most senior sits closest to host; clear deference to the delegation lead throughout the evening |
Dinner conversation |  | Warm, personal, relational — family, hospitality, the country's history; business is discussed indirectly or reserved for the next formal session | Reserved; avoid direct personal questions; appreciate shared cultural topics (art, history, food); do not fill every silence — pauses are acceptable
Gift protocol | Gifts are optional at formal dinners; if given, usually opened immediately with appreciation; wine or a cultural item is appropriate |  | Gifts are received with both hands and a slight bow; typically not opened at the table; cultural or artisanal gifts are appropriate
Leaving | Guests leave after the guest of honor; clear signal when the event ends is appreciated; extended farewell conversation is normal | Guest of honor departs first; others follow in seniority order; farewell is warm and includes expressions of hospitality and future meeting intentions |
@CHOICES
a)  Direct, substantive conversation is welcomed; topics: culture, food, travel, bilateral relationship; business references are acceptable at the table in German culture, less so in French
b)  Wine or champagne toasts expected; host proposes, guests follow; eye contact during the toast is important in French and German culture
c)  Gifts are warmly appreciated; quality craftsmanship and cultural significance matter more than expense; avoid gifts with alcohol
d)  Warm handshake (may linger); hand over heart gesture for senior officials; titles always ('Your Excellency'); social warmth immediately
e)  Very strict hierarchy; most senior sits farthest from the door (place of honor); age and institutional rank determine all seating positions
f)  Most senior guest departs first; others follow in order; farewell is formal and reserved — extended warmth is expressed through the bow, not through prolonged physical departure
