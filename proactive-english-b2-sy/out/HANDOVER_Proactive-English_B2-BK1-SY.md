## 2 · The twelve units

| # | title | function | grammar |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | What We No Longer Have to Explain | saying what you no longer have to explain | ellipsis on shared ground · _as you know_ · when not to say _obviously_ |
| 2 | Taking the Person Out | turning a clause into a noun phrase, and back again | nominalisation — _-ion · -ment · -al · -ance_ and zero |
| 3 | The Essay | stating a position in one sentence that can be disagreed with | the thesis sentence · _X, rather than Y, is what…_ |
| 4 | Speculating at Range | saying how sure you are in nine steps instead of three | _must · may well · could conceivably · is unlikely to · cannot_ |
| 5 | Not Only, But | fronting the part of the sentence you want read first | fronting · _of the eleven, nine_ · _what matters here is_ |

| 7 | Cause at a Distance | tracing a consequence back to the cause, naming every step | _which in turn_ · _with the result that_ · _and so_ |
| 8 | Reviews | giving a verdict in one sentence and saying who it is for | _for anybody who …, this is …_ / _… it is not_ |
| 9 | Concession That Costs Something | conceding the best objection in its strongest form | _granted that_ · _there is no answer to_ · _that is simply true_ |

## 7 · Register rules

- **Hedging:** one per sentence, never two. **Approximators** (_about · roughly · between X and Y ·
  on average · out of_) are not hedges: they say how a figure was arrived at. Unit 10 is where
  they become the subject.
- **Reported speech:** B1's house rule carries over — write _said_ unless you can say out loud
  why you have chosen something else.
- **Nominalisation is taught in Unit 2 and then policed in both directions** for the rest of the
  book: the noun version is the one you cannot argue with, which is the reason to use it and the
  reason to take it apart.
- **A figure never appears without its base, its period and its counter** from Unit 10 on. This is
  checked by eye in every model and report after Unit 10.
- **No Americanisms, no policy vocabulary, no currency symbols.** `qa.sh` sweeps all three with
  `\b` boundaries — a substring match flags _accommodating_ for _dating_.
- ⚠ **`lines(N)` prints one rule, not N.** Consecutive identically-bordered empty paragraphs merge
  in the renderer, so a `lines(20)` block renders as white space with a **single** rule at its
  foot. Verified at 50 and 110 dpi on both tests. `lib.js` is frozen and byte-identical across the
  A2, B1 and B2 trees, so this is the as-built behaviour of **the whole series** and must not be
  "fixed" here. **A ruled writing block therefore reads as a blank page to any text-based sweep.**

### The page-count levers, in the order to try them
1. **A page with only a header and a page number is one of three things.** Read the page before it.
   - A **genuinely blank page**: the cycle's content ended exactly at a page foot and the next
     `pb()` pushed past it. **Remove the redundant `pb()`** — this saves a page and is the
     commonest single lever. Found in Units 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 (before 9.5), 10 (before 10.2 and 10.3),
     11 (before 11.6 and 11.7), 12 (before 12.7), and in ak07, ak08, ak09 (before 9.6) and ak10
     (before 10.7).
   - A **correct `lines(N)` ruled writing block** (see §7). Leave it. Five such pages are in the

3. ⚠ **Removing a structural `pb()` to save a page is not a lever.** Dropping the one before the

## 9 · Faults found and fixed — watch for these again

1. **Exam items with more than one valid answer.** Unit 9 check item 6 admitted _insistence_ and
   _insisting_; Unit 11's open cloze gap 1 admitted _to_ and _with_. Both were reframed so only one
   word fits. **Check every gap and every option by hand, against the stem, after the build.**
2. **Listening items the audio does not support.** Unit 9 item 2 had no sound answer among its
   three options. Unit 10 Part 2 item 10 asked students to complete a sentence the script ends
   before — **dishonest, not merely hard.** Read each extract alone and answer the item from it
   with the rest of the page covered.
3. **A printed key word the candidate's answer cannot contain**, and **answer-letter patterning**.
   Reorder **options**, never answers.
4. **Facts attributed to the wrong character.** Unit 9 extract 7 gave Adnan a falsifier that is
   Yara's; extract 8 was moved to Lubna with her own subject.
5. **Surname collisions across the whole series**, not just this book. See the Mr Kabbani note in §5.
6. **Cross-unit series claims.** The mediation ordinals, §6.
7. **Exam-task coverage across the whole book.** Part 7 was taught in 7.7 and tested nowhere, and
   this was found only by checking the plan's own coverage table against the twelve units. **Build
   that table from the files, not from the plan, at the end of every book.**
8. **A key that describes a test.** `ak12.js` listed the End-of-Book Test's Reading parts as
   "1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6" and had to be corrected when Part 7 was added. **Grep for every
   description of a test before changing the test.**
9. **Out-of-band house figures.** Every unit was first built outside at least one band — Unit 9 at

   49–50. **Fix by adding or trimming real content, never by waving a figure through.**
