Reading · Entry requirements and contextual offers

Multiple choice and sentence completion

Entry requirements and contextual offers

The standard offer for this course is BBB at A level, or an equivalent qualification. We also accept the International Baccalaureate at 30 points, and a range of vocational qualifications at merit or above.

We make contextual offers, normally one grade below the standard offer, to applicants who meet at least one of our contextual criteria. These include attending a school whose results are below the national average, living in an area where few people go on to higher education, and having spent time in local authority care.

A contextual offer is made automatically. You do not apply for one and you do not need to mention it in your personal statement; the data comes from your application and from your school. Applicants are told in the offer letter if a contextual offer has been made.

All offers are conditional until results are published. If you narrowly miss your conditions we will still consider you, and in practice we confirm a majority of near misses. An offer is only withdrawn where the shortfall is substantial or where a required subject has not been passed.

Part 3

Reading · How applications are assessed

Sentence completion

How applications are assessed

Applications arrive through the central admissions service and are checked first for completeness. An application missing a reference or a predicted grade is held, and the applicant is contacted; it is not rejected, but it does not move forward until the missing item arrives.

Complete applications are read by two assessors working independently. Each gives a score for academic attainment and a score for the personal statement, and the two scores are added. Assessors do not see each other’s marks while they read.

Where the two assessors disagree by more than three points, the application goes to a third reader, whose decision is final. About one application in nine takes this route, and the rate is published each year in our admissions report.

Decisions are released on a single date for all applicants, which is why an early application does not receive an early answer. Interviews are held only for courses with a professional registration requirement, and those applicants are notified separately in February.

Part 7

Writing · the task, the model, and your turn

You are writing to the admissions office you have been reading about, and asking the questions the page left open.

You have applied for a course and have not received a reply. Write a letter to the admissions office. In your letter: give the details of your application; explain why you are writing now; ask what will happen next. Write at least 150 words.

Model answer

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing about my application for the BSc in Physiotherapy, reference PHY/2291. I applied on 14 October and received an acknowledgement on 16 October, but I have heard nothing since.

I am writing now for two reasons. The first is that your website says decisions are released on a single date, and that date was two weeks ago. The second is that I have been invited to interview by another university on 3 March, and I would prefer to make a decision knowing where I stand with you.

I would be grateful if you could tell me three things: whether my application is still being considered; whether I have been selected for interview, since I understand that physiotherapy is an interview course; and the date by which I can expect a decision. If any part of my application is missing, I will supply it immediately.

Thank you for your help.

Yours faithfully,

A. Okonjo

Why it works
  • The second bullet gives two reasons and the second one is the real one. Putting the weaker reason first and the stronger second is worth doing deliberately, because the last thing a reader sees is the thing they act on.
  • The three questions are numbered in the sentence. A letter that asks "please let me know what is happening" gives the reader nothing to answer; three specific questions get three specific answers.
  • Dear Sir or Madam with Yours faithfully. The writer does not know a name, and inventing one to use "sincerely" is a mistake that costs marks for no reason.

Now write your own. Write at least 150 words in about 20 minutes.
