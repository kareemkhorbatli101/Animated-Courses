**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline every superlative.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | comparative | superlative |
|---|---|---|
| short words | cheap**er** | **the** cheap**est** |
| ending in *-y* | eas**ier** | **the** eas**iest** |
| long words | **more** expensive | **the most** expensive |
| irregular | better · worse · further | **the** best · **the** worst · **the** furthest |

> **Grammar Note** — Superlatives almost always take **the**, and often an *in* or *of* phrase:
> *the highest point **in** the city*, *the best **of** the three*. Not *of the city*.

### 2 · Comparative or superlative?

**2A  Study the chart.**

| | |
|---|---|
| The pool is **cheaper than** the cinema. | two things |
| The pool is **the cheapest** of the five. | three or more |
| It's **one of the cheapest** places in the city. | careful, and usually safer |
| It's **by far** the cheapest. | emphasis |
| It's **not quite** the cheapest. | nearly |

> **Grammar Note** — Use a comparative for two, a superlative for three or more. *One of the
> cheapest* is the most useful phrase here, because it is almost always true and almost never

> ## You have finished Wayfinder 2
>
> You began this book able to say what you **were**. You finish it able to say what you **did**,
> what you **had to** do, what was **happening** when something else happened, and which of two
> things is better and which of nine is best.
>
> That is the whole of ordinary conversation. Everything else is detail.
>
> In **Wayfinder 3** the tenses start to overlap. Nadia is on the nursing course and will be asked
> what she **has** done, not just what she did. Ahmad runs a team and has to say what **has been**
> happening since Monday. You will learn the present perfect, which is the tense English uses when
> the past has not finished mattering — and almost nothing in this book has finished mattering.
