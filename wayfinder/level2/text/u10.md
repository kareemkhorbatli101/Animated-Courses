> **Reader's Tip** — Official pages mix three things: what you **must** do, what you **should** do,
> and what you **may** do. Mark them differently as you read. They have very different

> ## Newly arrived in Portmere
> *Portmere City Council · information page 3*
>
> **1. Register your address.** Everybody over 16 **must** be registered at the address where they
> live. You must do this **within 21 days** of moving. It is free.
>
> **2. Council tax.** Most households **must** pay. Full-time students are **exempt**, but you

>
> **4. Library.** Free. Bring one proof of address. A tenancy agreement is enough.

### 1 · *must* and *must not*

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W3-10-05%%

> **Clerk:** You must register within twenty-one days of moving.
> **Oksana:** I moved in August.
> **Clerk:** Then you're late. It's not a fine, but you must do it today.
> **Oksana:** I tried online. The portal wants a code that comes by post.
> **Clerk:** To the address. Yes. That's why we have a desk.
> **Oksana:** So I must come in person because the online system can't prove I live where I live.
> **Clerk:** That is exactly the situation. I must see one document. What have you got?
> **Oksana:** A tenancy agreement.
> **Clerk:** Then we're finished in four minutes.

**Your opinion:** The clerk says *"That's why we have a desk."* Is the desk a failure of the
system, or part of it?

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline every *must*.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | |
|---|---|
| You **must** register. | obligation — a rule |
| You **must not** work on this visa. | prohibition — forbidden |
| You **don't have to** pay. | not necessary — a choice |
| You **should** register with a doctor. | advice — wise, not required |

> **Grammar Note** — The dangerous pair is **must not** and **don't have to**. *You must not pay*
> means paying is forbidden. *You don't have to pay* means paying is optional. They look similar
> and mean opposite things.

**1D  Practice with a partner. Choose the right one.**

1. You ______ register within 21 days. (must / should)
2. Students ______ pay council tax, but they must apply. (mustn't / don't have to)
3. You ______ smoke in the building. (mustn't / don't have to)
4. You ______ see a doctor before you need one. (should / must)
5. You ______ bring two documents — one is enough. (mustn't / don't have to)

**1E  Write three rules for somebody arriving here.** One *must*, one *must not*, one *should*.

### 2 · *must* with frequency, and *must* vs *should*

**2A  Study the chart.**

| | |
|---|---|
| You must renew it **every year**. | how often |
| You must tell them **within 21 days**. | by when |
| You must **always** carry it. | frequency adverb before the verb |
| You **should** do it early. **You must** do it by Friday. | advice, then obligation |

> **Grammar Note** — *must* has no past. For the past, use **had to**: *I had to register in
> August.* And it has no *-s*: *she must*, never *she musts*.

**2A  Listen.** %%AUD W3-10-08%%

| | |
|---|---|
| You **must** go. /məst/ or /mʌst/ | the *t* often disappears before a consonant |
| You **mustn't** go. /ˈmʌsnt/ | the *t* in the middle disappears too |
| You **must** **n**ot go. (two words, formal) | both sounds come back |

> **Why this matters:** in fast speech *must* and *mustn't* differ by one nasal sound in the
> middle. When the sentence is a rule, say the *n*.
