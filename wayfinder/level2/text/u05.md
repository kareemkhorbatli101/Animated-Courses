%%SUB The briefing, the timesheet, and asking before you guess%%

Samira starts at Northgate Distribution on a Monday. By Wednesday she has been given a scanner, a
vest, a locker and eleven pieces of information, four of which contradict each other. Nobody is
being unhelpful. There is simply no first week anywhere that makes sense while you are in it.

### 1 · *might* and *should*

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W3-05-05%%

> **Samira:** The scanner says two pallets and there are three.
> **Supervisor:** Then the note might be wrong, or the third one might belong to the next
> delivery.
> **Samira:** Which is it?
> **Supervisor:** I don't know yet. That's why you shouldn't sign it.
> **Samira:** Should I wait for you?
> **Supervisor:** Sign for two. Write *third pallet unlabelled* underneath. That's accurate and it
> takes ten seconds.
> **Samira:** And if the driver's in a hurry?
> **Supervisor:** He might be in a hurry. He still waits ten seconds.

**Your opinion:** The supervisor says *"That's accurate."* Why is accurate better than right here?

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline *might*, *should* and *shouldn't*.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **might** | It **might** be wrong. | possible — maybe yes, maybe no |
| **might not** | He **might not** come. | possibly not |
| **should** | You **should** write it down. | advice, or what is expected |
| **shouldn't** | You **shouldn't** sign it. | advice against |

> **Grammar Note** — All of these take a plain verb with no *to* and no *-s*: *she might come*,
> *he should ask*. There is no *mights* and no *to might*.

**1D  Practice with a partner. Choose *might* or *should*.**

1. I don't know where he is. He ______ be in goods-in.
2. You ______ tell your supervisor the same day.
3. It ______ rain later — take a coat.
4. You ______ sign for something you haven't counted.
5. She ______ be late; the traffic was bad.

**1E  Write three sentences: one *might*, one *should*, one *shouldn't*, about your own work or
study.**

### 2 · Requests with *can* and *could*

**2A  Study the chart.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Can you** show me? | ordinary, friendly |
| **Could you** show me? | slightly more polite or more careful |
| **Could you possibly** …? | formal, or a big favor |
| **Do you mind if I** …? | asking permission |
| **Would it be all right if I** …? | careful permission |

> **Grammar Note** — *Could* is not the past here. It is politeness. *Can you* and *could you* are
> both fine with colleagues; *could* is safer with somebody you do not know.

|---|---|

**2C  Practice with a partner.** Ask for four things, twice each — once as a request, once as an

> **Why this matters:** in English, politeness lives mostly in the tune and the question mark, not
> in extra words. The same four words can be a favor or an order.

### 3 · The conversation

**3A  Read and listen.** %%AUD W3-05-10%%

%%FIG w05-f9 | The goods-in desk. Samira with a scanner and a clipboard; a delivery driver opposite holding a phone; three pallets behind him, one without a label.%%

> **Samira:** I've got two on the note and three on the floor.
> **Driver:** The third one's probably for the next drop.
> **Samira:** It might be. Could you check your run sheet?
> **Driver:** I'm already late.
> **Samira:** I know. It'll take ten seconds and it saves us both a phone call tomorrow.
> **Driver:** … It's for Morley Road.
> **Samira:** Then I'll sign for two. Can you initial this line?

> **Samira:** Monday.
> **Driver:** Most new ones sign for three.

### 💼 ON THE JOB

**Asking about your own hours.** %%AUD W3-05-12%%

> **1** — **Samira:** Could I ask about Tuesday and Thursday? I worked through both breaks.
> **Supervisor:** Did you write it on the sheet?
> **Samira:** I did, on the day.
>
> **2** — **Supervisor:** Then it's already fixed. The system deducts and the note puts it back.
> **Samira:** I didn't know that.
> **Supervisor:** Nobody tells anybody. That's the actual problem here.
>

> **Need help?** Could I ask about …? · I worked through … on … · I wrote it on the sheet. ·

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some workplaces a new person is expected to watch for a week and say nothing. In others, a new
person who asks no questions is assumed to be hiding something they do not understand.

**Talk about it in groups.**

1. In your first job anywhere, were you expected to ask or to watch?
2. Is it acceptable to ask a manager to repeat something? Twice? Three times?
3. The driver says *"Most new ones sign for three."* What is he actually saying about Samira?

> No right answer — but signing for something you have not counted is the same mistake in every
> country.
