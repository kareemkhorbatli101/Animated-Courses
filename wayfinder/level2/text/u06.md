### 1 · Present simple and present continuous

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W3-06-05%%

> **Assistant:** What are you using at the moment?
> **Linh:** Forty gigabytes.
> **Assistant:** That's what you're paying for. What are you using?
> **Linh:** … About eleven.
> **Assistant:** Every month?
> **Linh:** I use about eleven most months. This month I'm using more, because the wifi at home
> isn't working.
> **Assistant:** Then don't choose a plan based on this month. What usually happens?
> **Linh:** Eleven. Twelve in December.
> **Assistant:** Then the fifteen is yours, and you'll save a hundred and twenty a year.

**Your opinion:** The assistant says *"Don't choose a plan based on this month."* Why is that good
advice beyond phones?

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline every present simple and every present continuous.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| **present simple** — usually, always, every month | **present continuous** — now, this week, temporarily |
|---|---|
| I **use** about eleven. | I**'m using** more this month. |
| She **works** at the library. | She**'s working** late all week. |
| The wifi **works** fine. | The wifi **isn't working**. |
| **Do** you **use** much data? | **Are** you **using** it now? |

> **Grammar Note** — Some verbs are almost never continuous: *know, want, need, like, believe,
> mean, understand*. You say *I know*, not *I am knowing*.

**1D  Practice with a partner. Choose the right form.**

1. I ( work / 'm working ) in a café every Saturday.
2. She ( doesn't answer / isn't answering ) her phone today.
3. We usually ( cook / 're cooking ) on Sundays.
4. I ( don't understand / 'm not understanding ) this bill.
5. He ( lives / 's living ) with his cousin until April.

week.**

### 💼 ON THE JOB

**Messaging that you will be late.** %%AUD W3-06-12%%

> **1** — **Grace:** You messaged at six forty. Your shift starts at six.
> **Tomás:** The bus didn't come.
> **Grace:** When did you know?

>
> **2** — **Grace:** Then that's when I needed the message. Not when you'd given up.

> **Need help?** The bus hasn't come — I'll be about … late. · I'll update you at … ·
> I might still make it, but you should know now.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some places a phone call is normal and a message is cold. In others, calling somebody without
messaging first is an intrusion. Work and family often have opposite rules in the same person's
life.

**Talk about it in groups.**

1. Do you call or message your family? Is it the same with friends here?
2. Is it rude to leave a message unanswered for a day? For a week?
3. Is a voice message warmer than a written one, or more demanding?

> No right answer — but with an employer here, a short written message beats a perfect spoken
> excuse delivered late.

> ### Forty and Eleven
> *by Linh Pham*
>
> I pay for forty gigabytes a month and I use eleven. I have known this since August and I did
