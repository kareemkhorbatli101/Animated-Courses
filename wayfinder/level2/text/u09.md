### 2 · Read

> **Reader's Tip** — In a complaints page, find the sentence that says **what we will do and when**.
> That is the only promise on the page.

%%FIG w09-f5 | A bank's complaints page: a short introduction, three numbered steps, a timescale box, a line about the ombudsman, and a note about goodwill refunds.%%

> ## If something has gone wrong
> *Tidewell Bank · complaints*
>
> **1. Tell us.** In the app, by phone, or in a branch. Say what happened, when, and what you would
> like us to do. That last part matters and most people leave it out.
>
> **2. We will acknowledge it within three working days** and give you a reference number. Keep it.
>
> **3. We will give you a final answer within eight weeks.** Most cases are settled in under two.
>
> If you are not happy with our final answer, you can take it to the **Financial Ombudsman**, free
> of charge, within six months. We will tell you how.
>
> We sometimes refund a charge as a **goodwill gesture** even where the charge was applied
> correctly. Asking is not rude.

### 1 · Purpose: *to* and *because*

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W3-09-05%%

> **Adviser:** How can I help?
> **Adeel:** I'm here to ask about a fee from November. I came in because the app only shows three
> months.
> **Adviser:** Which fee?
> **Adeel:** Thirty-two, on the thirtieth. I was four pounds overdrawn for two days, because my
> rent left on the twenty-seventh and my wages arrived on the twenty-ninth.
> **Adviser:** That's an unarranged overdraft fee. It's in the terms.
> **Adeel:** I know. I'd like you to refund it anyway, and I'd like to move my rent date so it
> doesn't happen again.
> **Adviser:** … Most people only ask for the first one.

**Your opinion:** Adeel asks for two things. Why is the second one the stronger request?

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline *to*, *because* and *would like*.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | |
|---|---|
| I came **to ask** about a fee. | purpose — why you did it |
| I came **because** the app only shows three months. | reason — the cause |
| I'm saving **to buy** a car. | purpose |
| I'm saving **because** I want a car. | reason |

> **Grammar Note** — **to** + verb answers *what for?* **because** + a whole clause answers
> *why?* Both are correct; *to* is shorter and sounds more confident.

4. He left early. His bus only runs every hour. → ______________________

### 2 · *too* and *not … enough*

**2A  Study the chart.**

| | |
|---|---|
| It's **too** expensive. | more than is acceptable |
| It's **not** cheap **enough**. | less than is needed |
| There isn't **enough** time. | before a noun |
| I'm **too** tired **to** argue. | *too* + adjective + *to* |
| It's **not** big **enough to** fit. | |

> **Grammar Note** — *enough* goes **after** an adjective (*cheap enough*) but **before** a noun
> (*enough time*). This catches everybody for about a year.

### 3 · *would like*

**3A  Study the chart.**

| | |
|---|---|
| I**'d like** a refund. | + noun |
| I**'d like to** move the date. | + *to* + verb |
| I**'d like you to** refund it. | + person + *to* + verb |
| **Would you like** …? | the polite offer |
| I **want** a refund. | true, but blunter |

> **Grammar Note** — *would like* is not more honest than *want*; it is more negotiable. In a

**2C  Practice with a partner.** Ask for three things with *I'd like*, then offer three things with

> **Why this matters:** *I'd like* said slowly sounds like a demand. Said at normal speed it is the
> most useful polite request in the language.

### 3 · The conversation

**3A  Read and listen.** %%AUD W3-09-10%%

%%FIG w09-f8 | A bank desk. The adviser at a screen; Adeel opposite with a printed statement, one line marked with a pen. He is holding a small piece of paper with three lines on it.%%

> **Adviser:** The fee was applied correctly, I'm afraid. It's in the terms.
> **Adeel:** I understand that. I'm not saying it's an error.
> **Adviser:** Then what are you asking for?
> **Adeel:** Three things. I'd like the thirty-two refunded as a goodwill gesture. I'd like my
> rent date moved to the thirtieth. And I'd like to know whether an arranged overdraft would have
> cost me anything on four pounds for two days.
> **Adviser:** About fourteen pence.
> **Adeel:** Fourteen pence instead of thirty-two.
> **Adviser:** I can do the date today and I'll put the refund request in now. You'll hear within
> three days.
> **Adeel:** Could I have the reference number?

4. He asks for (a) a manager (b) the reference number

**3C  Think about the grammar.**

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some countries you negotiate a fee by talking to a person and it depends on the relationship.
In others everything is a written process, and the person at the desk genuinely cannot change it —
but a form behind them can.

**Talk about it in groups.**

1. Where you are from, who would you speak to about a bank charge?
2. Is complaining seen as making trouble? Would it affect how you are treated afterwards?
3. Adeel asked for a *goodwill gesture*. Would that phrase exist in your language?

> No right answer — but in a written system, the person who asks politely and keeps the reference
> number usually gets further than the person who shouts.

### 2 · Read the model

**2A  Listen and read.** %%AUD W3-09-14%%

> **Thirty-two Pounds**
> *by Adeel Khan*
>
> In November my rent left on the twenty-seventh and my wages arrived on the twenty-ninth. For two
> days I was four pounds and ten pence below zero. The bank charged me thirty-two pounds, which is
> about eight times what I had borrowed, for two days.
>
> I found it in January, because the app only shows three months and I had not looked since August.
> I went in because of that, and because I wanted to say it to a person.
>
> I did not argue that the fee was wrong. It is in the terms and I agreed to them, in a flat, on a
> phone, two years ago. I asked for three things instead: the thirty-two back as a goodwill
> gesture, my rent date moved to the thirtieth, and an answer to one question — what would an
> arranged overdraft have cost on four pounds for two days?
>
> Fourteen pence. The refund arrived on Friday. The date is moved. The question was the part that
> worked.

> **Writer's Tip** — End on the smallest fact, not the biggest. *Fourteen pence* does more work
> than *thirty-two pounds*, because the reader does the arithmetic themselves.
