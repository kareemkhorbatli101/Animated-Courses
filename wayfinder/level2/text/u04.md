# Unit 4 · Find Work
%%SUB The tense that lets you say what you did%%
%%GOALS
Say what you did, at work and before
Use the simple past of regular verbs, and ask questions about it
Read an online job board and write about what you have done
%%

Until this unit you could say what you **were**. From here you can say what you **did** — and that
is the difference between *I was a cleaner* and *I cleaned forty rooms a night for two years*. One
of those sentences gets you an interview.

### 1 · The simple past: regular verbs

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W3-04-05%%

> **Priya:** This line says *"warehouse operative."* That's not a sentence.
> **Adeel:** It's what I was.
> **Priya:** What did you actually do?
> **Adeel:** I checked deliveries. I packed orders. I trained two new people last winter.
> **Priya:** Then write that. How many orders a day?
> **Adeel:** About two hundred.
> **Priya:** Did you count that, or are you guessing?
> **Adeel:** I counted it. We recorded it every shift.
> **Priya:** Then it goes in. Numbers survive the second reader.

**Your opinion:** Priya says *"Numbers survive the second reader."* What do you think she means?

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline every past-tense verb.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| most verbs | work → work**ed** · check → check**ed** | add **-ed** |
| ending in *-e* | train → train**ed** · serve → serv**ed** | add **-d** |
| consonant + *-y* | carry → carr**ied** · study → stud**ied** | *y* → **ied** |
| short vowel + consonant | stop → sto**pped** · plan → pla**nned** | double it |
| **negative** | I **didn't** check. (no *-ed* after *didn't*) | |
| **question** | **Did** you check? — Yes, I did. / No, I didn't. | |

> **Grammar Note** — The *-ed* ending has three sounds: /t/ after *walk**ed***, /d/ after
> *train**ed***, and /ɪd/ after *want**ed***. You already do this. Lesson 4 makes it conscious.

**1D  Practice with a partner. Put the verb in the past.**

1. I ______ (check) deliveries every morning.
2. She ______ (train) two new starters.
3. We ______ (not finish) before six.
4. ______ you ______ (work) weekends?
5. He ______ (carry) the heavy boxes.
6. They ______ (stop) at half past four.

### 3 · Adverbs of manner

**3A  Study the chart.**

| | |
|---|---|
| careful → careful**ly** · quick → quick**ly** | add **-ly** |
| easy → eas**ily** | *y* → **ily** |
| good → **well** · fast → **fast** · hard → **hard** | irregular |
| I worked **carefully**. · She learned **quickly**. | after the verb |

> **Grammar Note** — An adjective describes a thing (*a careful worker*). An adverb describes how
> something is done (*she worked carefully*). On a CV, the adverb is usually the more useful one.

### 2 · Say it clearly — the three sounds of *-ed*

**2A  Listen.** %%AUD W3-04-08%%

| /t/ | /d/ | /ɪd/ |
|---|---|---|
| work**ed** | train**ed** | want**ed** |
| check**ed** | clean**ed** | start**ed** |
| pack**ed** | carri**ed** | report**ed** |

After *t* or *d*, the ending is a whole syllable: *want-ed*. Everywhere else it is one sound.

**2B  Listen. Write /t/, /d/ or /ɪd/.** %%AUD W3-04-09%%

1. ______  2. ______  3. ______  4. ______  5. ______

**2C  Practice with a partner.** Say five things you did yesterday. Your partner says which ending
each one had.

> **Why this matters:** English speakers add a syllable only after *t* and *d*. Adding one
> everywhere — *work-ed*, *clean-ed* — is the clearest sign of a learner and the easiest to fix.

### 3 · The conversation

**3A  Read and listen.** %%AUD W3-04-10%%

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some countries a CV is a list of employers and dates, and saying what you personally achieved
would seem boastful. In others, a CV with no achievements is read as a CV with nothing in it.

**Talk about it in groups.**

1. What goes on a CV where you are from? A photograph? Your age? Your marital status?
2. Is it normal to say *I trained two people*, or would you say *we trained*?
3. Adeel said *"I did. Two, last winter."* Would that sentence feel comfortable to you?

> No right answer — but here, nobody will ask what you achieved. The CV has to say it.
