### 1 · Irregular past verbs

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W3-08-05%%

> **Nurse:** What happened?
> **Yusuf:** She fell off a wall at about half past nine. She landed on her right arm and she
> hasn't used it since.
> **Nurse:** Did she hit her head?
> **Yusuf:** No. I saw it happen.
> **Nurse:** Did she lose consciousness at any point?
> **Yusuf:** No. She cried straight away, which I understand is good.
> **Nurse:** It is. Has she eaten?
> **Yusuf:** She had breakfast at eight. Nothing since.
> **Nurse:** You've done this before.
> **Yusuf:** I wrote it down in the car.

**Your opinion:** The nurse says *"You've done this before."* What has Yusuf actually done?

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline every past verb that does not end in *-ed*.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| be → **was / were** | go → **went** | have → **had** | do → **did** |
| fall → **fell** | come → **came** | see → **saw** | take → **took** |
| get → **got** | give → **gave** | eat → **ate** | drink → **drank** |
| break → **broke** | hit → **hit** | put → **put** | cut → **cut** |
| feel → **felt** | lose → **lost** | leave → **left** | tell → **told** |

> **Grammar Note** — After *did* and *didn't*, every verb goes back to its plain form.
> You say *She **fell***, but *Did she **fall**?* and *She didn't **fall***.
> The irregular form appears only in the positive statement.

**1D  Practice with a partner. Put the verb in the past.**

1. She ______ (fall) off a wall.
2. He ______ (not eat) anything since eight.
3. ______ she ______ (hit) her head?
4. They ______ (take) her for an X-ray.
5. I ______ (write) it down in the car.
6. We ______ (wait) four hours and they ______ (say) ninety minutes.

**1E  Write four sentences about last Sunday.** Use at least three irregular verbs.

### 2 · *have to* and *had to*

**2A  Study the chart.**

| | |
|---|---|
| I **have to** wait. | it is necessary — now |
| She **has to** bring her card. | third person |
| I **had to** wait four hours. | it was necessary — past |
| **Did** you **have to** wait? | question |
| I **didn't have to** pay. | it was not necessary |

> **Grammar Note** — *have to* is about necessity that comes from outside — a rule, a situation,
> somebody else. *should* is advice. *I have to wait* means there is no choice; *I should wait*
> means it would be wise.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some countries you go straight to a hospital for anything and are seen in an hour. In others
you must see a family doctor first, and arriving at a hospital without a referral is seen as
jumping a queue.

**Talk about it in groups.**

1. Where would you take a child with a hurt arm in your country? On a Sunday?
2. Is it normal there to wait four hours? Would anybody complain? To whom?
3. Here you are seen by clinical need, not by arrival. Is that the fairest system you have lived
   with?

> No right answer — but every system in the world is unbearable in a waiting room and defensible
> on paper.

> *by Yusuf Dalmar*
