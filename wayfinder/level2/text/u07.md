twenty minutes. The same meal from Marrow & Son costs about four and takes an hour, including
getting there. Tomás thinks that settles it. Mei, who works forty-eight hours and studies, does
not.

### 2 · Read

> **Reader's Tip** — *Use by* and *best before* are not the same thing, and the difference is the
> single most expensive misunderstanding in any kitchen.

%%FIG w07-f5 | A consumer leaflet about food dates and waste: a short introduction, two boxes explaining use by and best before, three storage tips, and a line at the bottom giving a world waste figure with its source.%%

> ## Dates, and what they mean
> *Portmere Community Kitchen · leaflet 4*
>
> **Use by** is about safety. Do not eat it after this date, even if it looks fine. This is for
> meat, fish and ready meals.
>
> **Best before** is about quality. After this date it is still safe; it may simply be less good.
> Rice, pasta, tins and biscuits are often fine for months. Bread is fine toasted.
>
> **Three things that save the most:**
>
> 1. **Freeze on the day you buy, not the day it expires.** Bread, cheese, cooked rice and most
>    meat freeze well.
> 2. **Move older food to the front.** Almost all waste is food that was behind something else.
> 3. **Check before you shop, not after.** Two minutes looking in the fridge beats any list.
>
> Worldwide, about a fifth of the food that reaches people is thrown away. Households are
> sixty percent of that — around **79 kilos per person a year**. Nobody means to.
>
> *Figures: UN Environment Programme, Food Waste Index Report 2024.*

<sub>**use by** — the last safe day · **best before** — after this it is still safe but worse ·
**portion** — the amount for one person</sub>

### 1 · Count and noncount, *How much* and *How many*

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W3-07-05%%

> **Tomás:** Eleven for two people. That's absurd.
> **Mei:** How much is the rice in it?
> **Tomás:** Three hundred grams.
> **Mei:** And how many tins?
> **Tomás:** Two small ones.
> **Mei:** So about four from the market, and an hour of my evening. I did fifty-one hours last
> week.
> **Tomás:** So the box is buying you an hour.
> **Mei:** The box is buying me an hour. You keep saying it like it's a crime.
> **Tomás:** I think I'm the one who can't afford it and is cross about it.
> **Mei:** That's more honest.

**Your opinion:** Tomás changes his argument halfway through. Was that a weakness?

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline *how much*, *how many* and the amounts.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | I count them | I don't count them |
|---|---|---|
| examples | tins, onions, portions, packets | rice, oil, water, time, money |
| **+** | some · a few · several · three | some · a little · a lot of |
| **−** | not any · not many | not any · not much |
| **?** | **How many** tins? | **How much** rice? |
| amounts | two tins · a packet of | 300 grams of · half a liter of |

> **Grammar Note** — Some nouns are both: *a coffee* (a cup) and *coffee* (the substance);
> *two chickens* (birds) and *some chicken* (meat). The word does not change; the meaning does.

**1D  Practice with a partner. Choose the right word.**

1. How ( much / many ) oil does it need?
2. How ( much / many ) portions does it make?
3. There isn't ( much / many ) rice left.
4. We've got ( a few / a little ) tomatoes.
5. I don't have ( much / many ) time this week.

**1E  Write three sentences about tonight's dinner.** Use *How much*, *a little* and *a few*.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some kitchens a recipe is a set of measurements and following it exactly is the point. In
others, a recipe is a suggestion from somebody's grandmother and measuring is slightly insulting.

**Talk about it in groups.**

1. Did anybody in your family measure when they cooked?
2. Is there a dish you can make that has no written recipe anywhere?
3. Mei bought the kit to save an hour. Would your family understand that, or think it was a waste?

> No right answer — but every cuisine in the world has a word for *enough*, and nobody can define
> it.

### 2 · Read the model

**2A  Listen and read.** %%AUD W3-07-14%%

> **How Much an Hour Is Worth**
> *by Mei Lin Chen*
>
> The meal kit costs eleven and takes twenty minutes. The same meal from the market costs four
> twenty and takes an hour, because the market is twenty minutes away and I am not fast with a
> knife.
>
> Tomás says the kit is absurd. He is right about the arithmetic. Eleven for two portions is nearly
> three times the market, and there are only two tins and three hundred grams of rice in it. But I
> worked fifty-one hours last week and I have an assignment due on the ninth, and the thing the box

> I will have seven hundred grams of rice left over, which Hana says is Thursday.

> **Writer's Tip** — Concede the other person's best point in full before you make yours: *He is
> right about the arithmetic.* Then the *but* lands.
