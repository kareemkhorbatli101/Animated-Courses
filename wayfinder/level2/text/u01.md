### 2 · Read

> **Reader's Tip** — When a text says *most people think X, but*, the sentence after *but* is the
> point of the whole text. Find it first.

%%FIG w01-f5 | A study-skills handout from the college: a short introduction, three numbered methods with the evidence for each, and a boxed line at the bottom about what does not work.%%

> ## Three things that work
> *Northside Technical College · Study skills, handout 2*
>
> Most people revise by reading their notes again. It feels productive and it is the weakest method
> we know of. Here is what the research actually supports.
>
> **1. Test yourself instead of re-reading.** Close the book and write down what you remember. It
> is uncomfortable, and that discomfort is the learning. Students who test themselves remember
> roughly half as much again as students who re-read.
>
> **2. Spread it out.** Four twenty-minute sessions across a week beat one eighty-minute session
> the night before, even though the total time is the same.
>
> **3. Mix the topics.** Studying one thing until you are sure, then moving on, feels better and
> works worse than moving between three topics in one session.
>
> **The one that does not work:** highlighting. It feels like studying. It is coloring.

<sub>**recall** — bring something back from memory · **spaced** — spread out over days ·
**passive** — reading or listening without doing anything with it</sub>

### 1 · *want to*, *need to*, *like to*

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W3-01-05%%

> **Tutor:** How much are you studying?
> **Nadia:** The app says eleven hours.
> **Tutor:** That isn't what I asked.
> **Nadia:** … Three. Maybe three.
> **Tutor:** Good. Three real hours is a lot. What do you want to get out of this course?
> **Nadia:** I want to be a nurse. I need to pass the first assignment.
> **Tutor:** Those are different things and you need to treat them differently. What do you like
> doing?
> **Nadia:** Flashcards. I like testing myself.
> **Tutor:** Then do more of that and stop coloring in your book.

**Your opinion:** The tutor says *"Three real hours is a lot."* Why does she say that?

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline *want to*, *need to* and *like*.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| I **want to** | be a nurse. | what you are aiming at |
| I **need to** | pass the assignment. | what has to happen |
| I **like to** study · I **like studying** | | what you enjoy — both forms are correct |
| **Do you want to** …? | Yes, I do. / No, I don't. | |
| **What do you need to** do? | Finish the reading. | |

> **Grammar Note** — After *want* and *need*, always **to** + verb: *I want **to** go*. After
> *like*, you can use either *to* + verb or *-ing*: *I like **to** swim* = *I like swimm**ing***.

**1D  Practice with a partner. Finish each sentence truthfully.**

2. This week I need to ______________ .
3. I don't like ______________ .
4. I'd like to ______________ , but I don't have time.
5. My tutor wants me to ______________ .

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some education systems the student's job is to listen and remember, and asking the teacher a
question in front of the class suggests the teacher explained badly. In others, a silent class is
a failed class.

2. Was it rude to disagree with a teacher where you studied? Is it here?
3. Nadia told her tutor she didn't understand. Was that easy? Would it have been easy at home?

> No right answer — but a tutor cannot help with a problem nobody has said out loud.
