# Cited public sources

Gate 12: every cited source is real, public, reachable, and current at the time of authoring.
These are the sources the Level 3 reading texts cite. Each was checked by search in
November 2026; figures are quoted as the publisher states them, and nothing here is
rounded in the book's favour.

The setting (Portmere) is unplaced, so a source is never cited as if it described Portmere.
A reading text uses a world figure the way a real leaflet does: *"Worldwide, households throw
away about 79 kilos of food per person each year."*

| Book 4 unit | Source | What is cited |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Back to School | OECD, *Trends in Adult Learning* (June 2025), from the 2023 Survey of Adult Skills | Two in five adults take part in adult learning in a year; the range runs from 58% (Norway) to 13% (Korea); about half of adults neither took part nor wanted to; one in four reports a barrier |
| 2 Fun Times | OECD Time Use Database (country data to 2024, 30 countries) | "Leisure and personal care" averages about 15 hours a day for full-time workers — a figure that includes sleeping, which is the point of the lesson |
| 3 Office Life | OECD, *Digital Economy Outlook 2024, Volume 2* — "Skills for the digital age" | 25% of adults across OECD countries lack the most basic digital skills; a further 14% can perform only basic functions |
| 4 The Interview | ILO, *Global Employment Trends for Youth 2026* (August 2026) | Global youth unemployment 12.4% in 2025, about 67 million people aged 15–24; Arab States 26.2%, Northern Africa 22.6% |
| 5 Lend a Hand | ILO / UNV, *State of the World's Volunteerism Report 2026* (December 2025); ILOSTAT | About 2.1 billion people volunteer each month, 34.5% of the global working-age population; the statistical threshold is one hour in a four-week period |
| 6 In the Kitchen | UNEP, *Food Waste Index Report 2024* | 1.05 billion tonnes of food wasted a year; households are 60% of it, 631 million tonnes, about 79 kg per person per year |
| 7 Money Moves | INTERPOL, *2nd Global Financial Fraud Threat Assessment* (March 2026) | USD 442 billion lost to financial fraud in 2025; AI-enabled schemes estimated about 4.5 times more profitable than conventional ones |
| 8 Healthy Habits | WHO, "Nearly 1.8 billion adults at risk of disease from not doing enough physical activity" (26 June 2024) | 31% of adults — about 1.8 billion — do not meet the guideline; up from 23.4% in 2000; projected 35% by 2030; the guideline is 150 minutes of moderate activity a week |
| 9 On the Road | WHO, *Global status report on road safety* (current edition) | About 1.19 million road deaths a year; the leading cause of death for people aged 5–29; 53% of those killed are pedestrians, cyclists and riders |
| 10 Keeping Safe | ILO / Lloyd's Register Foundation World Risk Poll, as reported by ILOSTAT (2025) | 16% of the global workforce reported serious harm at work in the previous two years, down from 18% in 2023 and 19% in 2021 |
| 11 Big Moments | UN DESA Population Division, International Migrant Stock 2024 | 304 million international migrants in 2024, about 3.7% of the world's population, nearly double the 1990 figure |
| 12 Speak Up | Eurostat, *Living conditions in Europe — housing* (2024 data); *Migrant integration statistics — housing* | 8.2% of people in the EU spend 40% or more of disposable income on housing; for non-EU citizens the figure is 18.8%, against 7.6% for nationals |

## Also corrected in an earlier book

Wayfinder 2 (Book 3), Unit 7, used an invented household food-waste figure in a council leaflet.
It now carries the UNEP figure and names the source. An invented number in a document that looks
official is exactly the thing the Real-life Reading lesson teaches learners to check, so it had no
business being there.

is recorded here and the reading text says so too.

## Notes on honesty

Three of these need a caveat, and each is carried into the book rather than hidden:

- **Unit 5.** The ILO's 2.93 million figure is an estimate for 2019, published in 2023. It is the
  most recent global estimate that exists; it is not a 2025 figure, and the text says so.
- **Unit 9.** The 12-billion-days figure comes from WHO/ILO work published in 2022 and repeated in
  WHO's current fact sheet. It is still the current official figure.
- **Unit 11.** The base of the 66% fake-review figure is not stated in the published summaries.
  Rather than drop the figure or pretend it is clear, the reading prints it and the task asks the
  learner to say what they would need to know before quoting it. This is the skill the unit is
  for.
