u01-a02Scene · Lesson 1, slot 1B

The front desk, 8:40 on a Monday

The unit's establishing shot. Six affordances are labeled by the picture alone, so a learner who cannot yet read the rubric can still answer the gist question: where is Nadia? The clock reads 8:40, the board behind the desk already answers Lesson 2's questions, and the queue is three deep because that is why nobody wants to ask twice.

Alt text: A woman stands behind a desk with a tablet. Three students wait in a line. Nadia is second. Behind the desk a board shows two groups and two room numbers.

u01-a03Vocabulary set · Lesson 1, slot 2A

Nine things, one scale

The matching set. Every item is drawn at a consistent scale and lit from the same side, because a learner matching a word to a picture should be deciding about the word, never about the drawing. Numbers sit outside the objects so they never cover the thing being named.

Alt text: An open bag on a desk with the classroom behind. Nine things are numbered: a book; a notebook; a pen; a pencil; the bag; a chair; a table; the board and a computer.

u01-a04 · u01-a06Realia · Lesson 1 AUG-a, Lesson 2 slot 4

The screen that filled itself in, and the form that did not

Realia is designed, not photographed: a real layout with invented data, typeset so that the reading skill the slot teaches is genuinely exercised by the page. On the left, the passport scanner has put the family name in First name and the given name in Last name — the learner's job is to catch it, because the only person who can is the person whose name it is. On the right, the paper form has one field left empty on purpose: emergency contact is the line newcomers cannot fill in yet.

u01-a07Grammar cue · Lesson 3, slot 3A

Singular and plural, drawn so the answer is unarguable

One board, two computers, three chairs, four pens, one bag, one notebook. The picture is composed around the grammar: every count is unambiguous at a glance, so the pair asking what's number 4? argues about a pen versus pens and never about what they can see. This is the series' most reused cue type and it is re-earned at five levels.

Alt text: A cutaway view of a classroom with seven numbered groups of things. Some groups are one thing like the board and some are several like the two computers, the three chairs and the four pens.

u01-a12Story panels · Lesson 5, slot 1B

8:35 outside, 9:05 inside

The Real Lives model text is seeded by two panels, thirty minutes apart. The clock is in both, because the time is the story: she is early, she is checking the number on the door against the number on her phone, and then she is in the room with a new notebook open. Same character construction in both frames — that consistency is what a recurring cast buys, and why fifteen assets per unit is affordable at all.

Alt text: Two pictures. In the first Nadia stands outside the school at 8:35 holding up her phone to check the address. In the second she sits in Room 4 at 9:05 with a new notebook open and another student beside her.

u01-a14Data figure · Team Up & Review, slot E–F

The chart the class builds, and the total that does not add up

One series, so no legend box — the title names it, and every bar is labeled directly. Bars are sorted by value, not alphabetically. The table beside it is not a courtesy: at this level reading the table is the exercise, and it is also the accessibility path. The numbers are deliberately wrong: five countries sum to twelve, and there are thirteen students in the room. Item F3 asks the class to find the missing one. That is three minutes of numeracy, data literacy and a register check at once.

u01-a05 · u01-a11Realia · Lesson 2 reading, Lesson 5 slot 1A

The welcome sheet and the badge

The reading text is a document the center would really produce, which is why it cites no outside source: it makes no claim about the world. Inventing a citation for a 63-word welcome sheet would be worse than having none, so external sourcing starts at Level 2, where the articles start making claims. The badge is the same name again, now correct, which is the unit's whole arc in one object.

How this system is built

  • Six level accents, each validated in light and dark. The hues interleave warm and cool rather than running a warm-to-cool gradient, because a gradient put Starter's red-orange beside Level 1's amber and those two collide for a deuteranopic reader. Interleaving lifts the worst adjacent pair from ΔE 1.9 to 16.1.
  • No six-hue set clears the all-pairs floor, so color never carries level identity alone: every cover and running head also carries the level name, numeral and glyph.
  • Data graphics use the project's already-validated categorical palette, and the level accent is withdrawn from the inside of any chart so a figure never reads as page furniture.
  • Every asset exists in both themes from tokens, never by inversion. Every one has authored alt text written as a teaching sentence, because a learner on a screen reader is doing the same exercise and not a reduced one.
  • Characters are built once per band and reused. That is what makes roughly 2,400 assets across 78 units affordable, and what makes the books feel written rather than assembled.
