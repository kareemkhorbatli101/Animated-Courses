# Wayfinder English — Series Bible

Everything authored for this series obeys this file. It is the answer to "what is this place, who
lives there, and how does the text sound?" so that 78 units written over months read as one book.

---

## 1. Portmere

A mid-size coastal city. A university on the hill, a teaching hospital, a logistics park out past
the ring road, a market quarter by the water, a technical college on the north side, and the bus
routes that stitch them together.

**Portmere is deliberately unplaced.** No country is named. No national institution appears. No flag,
no anthem, no president, no federal agency, no national holiday that belongs to one state. The
currency is "a unit" written as a number plus the word. The name itself is spelling-neutral, so it
reads identically in the American and British editions (§4.1 of the plan).

This is not squeamishness. It is what makes the book sellable in forty markets and recognizable in
each of them: every learner has arrived somewhere and found that the bus map assumes you already
know the city.

## 2. The cast — Starter and Level 1

Step Forward uses fresh names every unit and no recurring characters. Wayfinder does the opposite,
and it is the series' biggest single upgrade: continuity learners care about, grammar that recycles
inside a story instead of a grid, and art that is drawn once and reused 300 times.

### 2.1 Casting law

1. **Nobody is defined by their nationality.** Origins are mentioned where a learner would mention
   them and never carry a trait. There is no "the hardworking X" and no accent played for laughs.
2. **Nobody is comic relief and nobody is a victim.** People are embarrassed, tired, wrong, and
   funny on purpose.
3. **No institution rescues anybody.** Problems are solved by the person, a friend, a form filled in
   correctly, or persistence. Where a problem is left open, it stays open — the learner solves it.
4. **Women hold technical and supervisory roles at the same rate as men.** The supervisor, the
   pharmacist and the bus driver are not all one gender.
5. **The person who misunderstands is as often a local as a newcomer.** Confusion runs both ways.
6. **Ages 18–60**, spread, with real bodies and real jobs.

### 2.3 How the cast ages

Level 2 and 3 are the same people one and two years on — Nadia on a part-time contract, Tomás a
shift supervisor, Mei on placement, Yusuf studying logistics at night — joined by new arrivals
(Oksana, Adeel, Linh, Samira). By Level 5 Unit 1, a learner retells in narrative tenses the arrival
they first described, at A1, in six present-tense sentences. That payoff is the reason the cast
exists, and it is designed in from Book 1.

## 3. Voice

The difference between a course people finish and a course people abandon is whether the sentences
are worth reading. Rules, not aspirations:

1. **Every sentence carries information.** No "In today's fast-paced world." No "Let's explore."
   No sentence whose only job is to introduce the next one.
2. **Every character wants something concrete in every text.** Not "Maria likes her job." Maria wants
   Thursday off and has not asked yet.
3. **Specific beats general, always.** Not "transportation." The 14 bus, every twenty minutes, less
   often on Sunday.
4. **Numbers are real and they add up.** A payslip's deductions total correctly. A timetable's
   intervals are consistent. A learner who checks the arithmetic finds it sound, because some of them
   will check.
5. **Problems that the slot says are unresolved stay unresolved.** The *Think It Through* case ends
   without a solution. Resisting the urge to tidy it is the whole exercise.
6. **Humor is allowed; cruelty is not.** Nobody is the butt of a joke about their English.
7. **Nobody is pitied.** A learner reading about a learner should recognize themselves, not be
   consoled.
8. **Model texts end on a decision or a next step, never a moral.** "Finally, I am going to ask her
   on Monday" — not "Now I know that hard work pays."
9. **Rubrics are imperative, 4–10 words, and say exactly one thing.** "Listen again and check your
   answers." Not "Now, let's listen one more time to see how you did."
10. **No sentence exists to demonstrate grammar at the expense of sense.** If the only reason a
    character says it is the target structure, the situation is wrong and gets rewritten.

## 4. Content doctrine — what makes it current

The architecture is from 2017. The content is from now. Every unit is checked against this list, and
a unit that could have been written in 2010 is sent back.

- **Most official processes are an app with a queue behind it.** Registering an address, booking a
  clinic slot, proving who you are, getting paid: the learner's real difficulty is a screen in a
  second language with a deadline, and the book shows the screen.
- **Machine translation is in every learner's pocket and it fails in specific, teachable ways.** It
  drops politeness, it guesses gender, it flattens register, it is confident when wrong. AUG-a teaches
  learners to check it rather than to avoid it, because they are not going to avoid it.
- **AI assistants are a study tool learners already use badly.** The strand teaches writing a prompt
  that gets a usable answer, and then verifying it — a 2026 literacy, not a 2010 one.
- **Scams arrive by text message and they are good now.** The phishing task uses messages that are
  actually plausible, because the implausible ones teach nothing.
- **Work is increasingly shift-booked by app, and hours are contested.** The timesheet unit is about
  a discrepancy, not about telling the time.
- **Money is mobile-first.** Cards, apps, instant transfers, a fee discovered afterward.
- **Heat, storms and power cuts are ordinary now, not exotic.** The emergency unit reflects that.
- **Study is hybrid.** A class has people in the room and people on a screen, and the classroom
  language has to work for both. The Pre-unit teaches both from Starter.
- **Qualifications earned elsewhere are often not recognized**, and that is one of the defining adult
  experiences of migration. Ahmad carries it. It is never a lecture.

**Sourcing.** Every reading article cites a real, public, reachable source dated 2024 or later unless
the point is historical. Sources are verified at authoring time and recorded in the unit's `META`
file with the date checked. We cite our own sources; we never inherit a citation.

## 6. What never appears

No real brand, company, product or logo. No real person. No national institution, agency, document,
emergency number or holiday of any single country. No religion presented as default or as other. No
politics of a named state. No currency symbol in body text. No cross-reference to any picture
dictionary or to any other publisher's material. No character whose difficulty is their accent. No
sentence, rubric or dialogue line sharing a six-word run with any source held by the project.
