Ms Nkemelu has written one claim on the board and eight ways of saying it. All eight are
grammatical. One of them would get you sued, one would get you ignored, and the six in between are
what the rest of this book is for.

%%FIG z00-f1 | Room 7, five years on. Eleven adults around a long table, most with notebooks open. Ms Nkemelu at the board, which carries one sentence at the top and eight numbered variations beneath it.%%

### 1 · Eight distances

**1A  Read the eight. Then say each one aloud, and decide how far the speaker is standing from it.**

> The claim: *the policy caused the fall in applications.*
>
> **1** The policy caused the fall.
> **2** The policy has caused the fall.
> **3** The fall appears to have followed the policy.
> **4** The fall is consistent with the policy having had an effect.
> **5** It has been suggested that the policy caused the fall.
> **6** Some have argued that the policy caused the fall.
> **7** It is not impossible that the policy contributed.
> **8** The report does not establish that the policy caused anything.

**1B  Listen. Four speakers use four of the eight. Which, and why?** %%AUD W6-00-01%%

**1C  Discuss in threes.**

1. Which of the eight would you use in a complaint? In an essay? In a meeting with your manager?
2. Sentence 5 has no author. Who said it? Does the sentence care?
3. Sentence 8 is the only one that refuses the claim. Is it the strongest or the weakest?

### 3 · Tense and aspect

**3A  Say what each sentence adds that the one above it does not.**

1. I worked there.
2. I was working there.
3. I had worked there.
4. I had been working there.
5. I have worked there.
6. I have been working there.
7. I will have worked there for ten years in March.
8. I will have been working there for ten years in March.

**3B  Listen. Eight speakers. Write the number each one matches.** %%AUD W6-00-02%%

**3C  Numbers 7 and 8 are the only ones left in the series.** With a partner, say which of the two
describes a length of activity and which describes a completed total, and invent a situation where
only one of them is true.

> **Grammar Note** — **Tense** places an event in time. **Aspect** says how you are looking at it:
> as complete, as continuing, or as having consequences now. Every pair in 3A is the same tense in
> two aspects. English learners reach B2 with the tenses secure and the aspects approximate, and
> the approximation is invisible until the moment somebody needs to know whether you have
> *finished*.

### 4 · What this book will ask of you

**4A  Read, then discuss in your group.**

> This book will ask you to read three things that disagree. Two academic studies of the same
> object, using different methods, arrive at five point six minutes and seventeen minutes for the
> same quantity. Two summaries of one report give two different numbers for the same projection.
> Nobody in any of those cases is lying.
>
> It will also ask you to say, out loud and in writing, exactly how far you are standing from a
> claim. That is the whole of academic and professional English and it takes about a year.

1. When did you last read two sources that disagreed? What did you do?
2. Is it possible to be honest and wrong? What is the difference between that and lying?
3. What would you need to see before you repeated a number you had read once?
