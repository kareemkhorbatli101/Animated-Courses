### 2 · Read

> **Reader's Tip** — When a figure is given as a round number in one place and a precise one in
> another, the precise one usually comes from the statistical agency and the round one from the
> organization writing about it. Neither is dishonest. But only one of them can be checked against
> a method.

%%FIG z05-f5 | A five-page report extract: a cover with a goal number and a year, a page of indicator boxes each with a figure and an arrow, a double page with a stacked bar chart by region and a photograph caption strip, and a final page with a definitions box and a note on data availability.%%

> ## One in four, and what gets done about it
> *Portmere Civic Forum · note for the housing group*
>
> 1  Our last session divided fairly sharply between people who thought global housing conditions
> 2  were improving and people who thought they were not. Both positions can be defended from the
> 3  same statistics, which is the honest and unsatisfying answer, and it is worth seeing exactly
> 4  how.
>
> 5  **The figure.** According to United Nations statistics for 2024, about **1.16 billion urban
> 6  residents — almost one in four of the world's urban population — live in slums or informal
> 7  settlements**. UN-Habitat's own reporting rounds this to 1.1 billion. The rounding is not a
> 8  disagreement; it is a different house style, and both numbers refer to the same estimate.
>
> 9  **Why the two positions are both arguable.** The *share* of urban residents living in these
> 10 conditions has fallen over the last two decades in most regions. The *number* has risen,
> 11 because cities have grown faster than conditions have improved. A speaker who says things are
> 12 getting better is quoting the share; a speaker who says they are getting worse is quoting the
> 13 number; and the two speakers will never resolve it, because they are not disagreeing.
>
> 14 **How the work actually gets done.** Very little of it is done by clearing and rebuilding,
> 15 although that is what most people picture. The dominant approach is **incremental upgrading**:
> 16 water and sanitation are connected, paths are surfaced and drained, electricity is brought in,
> 17 and — the part that is least visible and matters most — some form of tenure is recognized, so
> 18 that a household will not be removed from a house it has just improved. Where tenure is not
> 19 secured first, the improvements are often made and then lost.
>
> 20 **Who does it.** Rarely one body. A typical upgrading program involves a national ministry,
> 21 a municipal authority, one or more contractors, a development bank, and a residents'
> 22 association which usually has no formal standing and does most of the organizing. When a
> 23 project fails, the published account of it is almost always written in the passive voice, and
> 24 the reason is not grammatical.
>
> 25 **What we would ask about any such program.** Who commissioned it. Who was consulted before
> 26 it was designed. Whether tenure was addressed before or after the building work. And who signs
> 27 the completion certificate, because that is the one question whose answer is always a named
> 28 person.
>
> *Figures: UN DESA, SDG Goal 11 indicator reporting, 2024 data; UN-Habitat.*

basis of occupation · **incremental upgrading** — improving housing in stages, in place</sub>

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Tense | Passive form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Present simple** | is / are + -ed | Maintenance **is contracted out**. |
| **Present continuous** | is being + -ed | Parts **are being ordered**. |
| **Present perfect** | has / have been + -ed | The lift **has been taken** out of service. |
| **Past simple** | was / were + -ed | The survey **was carried out** in March. |
| **Past continuous** | was being + -ed | The roof **was being retiled** at the time. |
| **Past perfect** | had been + -ed | The works order **had been issued** in January. |
| **Future** | will be + -ed | The certificate **will be signed** next week. |
| **Modal** | must / can be + -ed | It **can't be repaired** on site. |
| **Infinitive** | to be + -ed | It needs **to be inspected** first. |

> **Grammar Note** — Use the passive when the doer is unknown, obvious, or genuinely not the point
> — *the survey was carried out in March* is fine if the firm's name adds nothing. Use it also to
> keep the topic at the front of the sentence: *the lift has been taken out of service* keeps the
> lift as the subject across four sentences. The question to ask yourself is whether a reader who
> wants the agent can find it anywhere.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Asking who decided something is routine in some systems and reads as a challenge in others. In
some administrative cultures, decisions are deliberately collective and naming an individual is
considered unfair to them; the correct address is the office, not the person. In others, every
decision carries a named signature and a refusal to supply it is itself a complaint. Tenants'
groups, unions and residents' associations exist in part because an individual asking the question
is easier to deflect than eleven signatures asking it.

**Discuss:** where you have lived, how do you find out who decided something? Does asking cost you
anything?

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Letter to the housing office
>
> I am writing about the lift at Cranbourne House, which has now been out of service for eleven
> days, and about the notice that was posted on the eighth.
>
> The notice contains four sentences and no names, no dates and no telephone number. Residents
> were told that the lift had been taken out of service, that a contractor had been appointed,
> that parts were being awaited and that patience was requested. I do not doubt that every one of
> those statements is true. All four, however, are written in a form which allows a reader to
> establish nothing and act on nothing, and there are fourteen households in this building,
> including two wheelchair users and a woman on the ninth floor who has had her shopping carried up
> by neighbors for a week and a half.
>
> Your officer has since told me, in person and without being pressed, that the inspection was
> carried out on the eighth by an engineer from Harwell Lifts, that the works order was raised the
> following day and given to the same firm, and that the parts are being shipped from Italy. That
> is three of the four things the notice should have said, and it took him ninety seconds.
>
> On the fourth point I was told that the decision to leave an eleven-story building without a
> lift for three weeks was taken at the area meeting. I would be grateful if you could tell me
> who chaired that meeting. I am not asking in order to complain about an individual. I am asking
> because I intend to write to whoever it was about the arrangements for the two wheelchair users,
> and a letter addressed to a meeting will not be answered by anybody.
>
> Two things would resolve most of this. Have the notice replaced with one that carries the firm,
> the dates and an extension number — your officer has already offered to do it — and have the
> works order copied to the residents' association as a matter of course in future. Neither
> requires a decision above the level of the person who answers this letter.

> **Writer's Tip** — Do not attack the passive voice; attack the missing information. "This notice
> allows a reader to establish nothing" is a complaint anybody can act on. "This notice is written
> in the passive" is a complaint about grammar, and it will be answered by somebody explaining why
> the passive is sometimes appropriate.

**1D  Circle the correct word.**

1. The lift has been out for (eleven / nineteen) days.
2. The notice contains (four / fourteen) sentences.
3. The parts are coming from (Spain / Italy).
4. The writer wants to write to (the meeting / whoever chaired it).

### 3 · Write

Write **250 to 350 words**: a letter about something that has been done, not been done, or been
decided, where you live or work.

**Use at least four different passive tenses and one *have something done*. Ask for one named
person and one concrete change.**

**Editing checklist — check your own, then swap.**

| | ✓ |
|---|---|
| Is every passive I used there for a reason I could state? | |
| Have I used *have / get something done* where the work was arranged, not done, by me? | |
| Have I asked for a name without attacking a person? | |
| Are my two requests things one person could actually agree to? | |

**Peer feedback.** Write one sentence saying whether your partner's letter could be acted on by
the person who opens it, and name the one sentence that would make an officer defensive.

### B · Write a team summary

> This is a courtyard on a weekday morning. Fifteen people are in view. A panel has been opened at
> ground level and three people in work jackets are standing round it.

Write eight sentences. Use at least four passives. One person writes, everybody checks.

### C · Report

Read your summary to another team. They name one sentence where you could have supplied an agent
and didn't.

### 🧩 THINK IT THROUGH

**Listen and read.** %%AUD W6-05-16%%

%%FIG z05-f16 | A small site office with a desk, two hard hats on a shelf and a wall of plans. A woman standing with a sheaf of papers, a man seated at the desk with a laptop and a printed schedule. Through the window, a half-built low-rise block and a line of existing houses close behind it.%%

> Adaeze works for a contractor delivering an upgrading scheme: water, drainage and surfaced paths
> for about four hundred households. The paths were finished last month and the drainage will be
> done by March. What has not happened, despite being in the original program, is the tenure
> step: no household has yet been given any document recognizing its occupation, and the item has
> been moved twice in the schedule. Her firm is not responsible for tenure — a ministry is — and
> her contract will be signed off as complete whether or not it happens. She has seen, in two
> previous schemes, what follows: improvements are made, land becomes worth more, and some of the
> households that improved it are no longer there two years later. Her site manager has told her,
> not unkindly, that it is not her job and that raising it will not help the firm win the next
> contract.

1. What is the problem, and which part of it is Adaeze's to act on?
2. Her contract will be signed off either way. Does that settle the question of responsibility?
3. What are three things she could do? Which one puts something in writing that will still exist
   after her firm has left?
