# Wayfinder 5
%%SUB Communication for Study, Work and Life · CEFR B2%%

## How to use this book

This is the last book in the Wayfinder series. It assumes you can narrate, compare, report,
concede and disagree. What remains is precision: choosing the verb that reports a claim at the
right distance, putting the emphasis where you need it, saying what would have been true if
something years ago had gone otherwise, and reading a document closely enough to find the
obligation hidden in a single quantifier.

**Each unit has sixteen pages and five lessons.** The shape is the one you know.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Forty to forty-eight new items, plus an academic set, with a team research task |
| **2 · Real-life Reading** | A 400–550 word text, True/False/Not Given, word study and a graph |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures, a *Language connection* box, and a team error hunt |
| **4 · Everyday Conversation** | Pronunciation, a long dialogue, a workplace box, and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A 250–350 word model, then your own, with an editing checklist |
| **Team Up & Review** | A team project with four roles, a class survey, and a hard case |

**The audio.** 🔊 marks a track. 194 tracks, numbered `W6-unit-track`.

## What is different about this book

**It was written from the CEFR B2 descriptors, not from anybody's syllabus.** Every other book in
this series was built on an architecture we cloned from an existing one. At B2 there is no
scaffolding left to borrow, and none is needed.

**Every reading text in this book is about reading.** That is deliberate. At this level the
difficulty is no longer the language; it is working out what a document establishes, what it
merely implies, and what it quietly leaves to you. Unit 3 gives you one report summarized two ways
with two different numbers. Units 9 and 11 give you two academic studies of the same object that
disagree by a factor of three, both honestly. Nothing in this book asks you to decide who is
lying, because nobody is.

**The last unit is not a test.** Unit 12 asks for one extended piece of writing and one sustained
turn of talk, which is what B2 means. If you can do those two things, the series has finished its
job and you do not need a book six.

## The people

Five years have passed in Portmere. **Nadia Rahman** is a nurse. **Tomás Alvarez** manages a site
and is training the person who will replace him. **Mei Chen** comes back twice a year and is
briefly a visitor in a place she helped make. **Priya Raghavan** chairs things. **Lucia Pham** is
a tenants' representative who knows exactly which clause to turn to. **Ahmad Rahim** employs four
people. **Oksana Koval**, **Linh Pham**, **Adeel Khan**, **Samira Haddad** and **Yusuf Osman** are
not characters in a story about arriving any more; they are the people the town is made of.

Everybody in this book is invented. So is Portmere. Every figure quoted in a reading text is real
and its source is named, including the three that contradict each other.
