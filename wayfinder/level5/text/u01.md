Nadia has told the story of her first week here perhaps forty times, and the forty tellings have
polished it into something smooth, funny and not quite true. It now has three jokes in it. The

### 2 · Read

> **Reader's Tip** — A projection is not a prediction and it is not a story. It says: *if these
> assumptions hold, this follows.* The assumptions are always stated and almost never read, and
> every argument about a projection that does not mention them is an argument about something
> else.

%%FIG z01-f5 | A five-page report summary: a cover with a title and a year, a page carrying a world map with countries shaded by whether population has peaked, a page with a line chart and a fan of projection variants, and a final page with an assumptions box and a source line.%%

> ## Sixty-three countries have already turned
> *Portmere Civic Forum · briefing for community organizations*
>
> 1  We are asked about population more often than about anything except housing, usually in the
> 2  form of a sentence beginning *everyone says*. The underlying figures are published, they are
> 3  not controversial among the people who produce them, and they do not say what the sentence
> 4  beginning *everyone says* usually says.
>
> 5  **The headline that is not a headline.** The world's population stood at about **8.2 billion
> 6  in 2024**. It is projected to go on growing for another fifty or sixty years, to peak at
> 7  around **10.3 billion in the mid-2080s**, and then to decline slowly to about **10.2 billion**
> 8  by the end of the century. That is a forecast of growth followed by decline, and both halves
> 9  of the sentence get quoted separately by people arguing opposite cases.
>
> 10 **The figure nobody quotes.** Population has **already peaked in 63 countries and areas**,
> 11 including China, Germany, Japan and Russia. In 126 others — among them India, Indonesia,
> 12 Nigeria, Pakistan and the United States — it is projected to go on rising through 2054. Both
> 13 of those groups are living in the same decade and the experience inside them is not remotely
> 14 the same, which is why a global figure answers almost no question anybody actually has.
>
> 15 **What changed in ten years.** The projection for 2100 is now about **700 million lower** —
> 16 six per cent — than the one made a decade earlier. The revision is largely driven by lower
> 17 fertility in some of the largest countries, China in particular. A projection that moves by
> 18 six per cent in ten years is not unreliable; it is doing what a projection does, which is
> 19 respond to data. The people who find that disturbing are usually people who were quoting it
> 20 as a fact.
>
> 21 **Why this is in a community briefing.** Because almost every argument we hear about housing,
> 22 school places and services rests on an assumption about how many people there will be, and
> 23 almost nobody making the argument has looked. Look. It takes four minutes and the answer is
> 24 rarely the one that was being assumed.
>
> *Figures: UN DESA, World Population Prospects 2024.*

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Tense | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Past simple** | carries the events, in order | I **left** the meeting and **sat** down. |
| **Past continuous** | sets the scene around an event | It **was raining** when I came out. |
| **Past perfect** | fixes something as earlier | By then I **had decided** not to go back. |
| **Past perfect continuous** | a run of activity up to a past point | I'**d been sitting** there for an hour when he came down. |
| ***would* / *used to*** | repeated past habit | Every Tuesday I **would** take the long way round. |

> **Grammar Note** — The past perfect continuous answers *how long had it been going on?* and the
> past perfect answers *was it finished?* **I had worked there for a year** when they told me is a
> completed total; **I had been working there for a year** is a stretch you were still inside. In
> narrative the continuous is what makes a listener feel the duration rather than be informed of
> it.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

What a personal story is *for* varies. In some traditions an account of hardship is offered as
instruction and is expected to end with what it taught you. In others it is offered as
companionship and an ending that draws a lesson sounds like a sermon. In others again, telling it
at all to people you do not know well is a claim on them that has not been earned.

**Discuss:** in your family or community, who tells stories like this, to whom, and how do they
