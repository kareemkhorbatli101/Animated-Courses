%%SUB Building 2, and the deadline nobody mentioned%%

A college is a building full of information that nobody says out loud. The room is on a board, the
deadline is on a door, the test is in an email, and the one thing you actually need is written in
a corridor you have not walked down yet. Ahmad has three weeks to find all of it.

### 1 · Prepositions of time

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W2-02-05%%

> **Ahmad:** Excuse me. Is the English test on Thursday?
> **Priya:** It's on Thursday at six. In Room 12, not Room 7.
> **Ahmad:** Room 12. Is that in this building?
> **Priya:** It's in Building 3. Five minutes' walk, in the rain.
> **Ahmad:** And the deadline for the form?
> **Priya:** The thirtieth. That's in nine days.
> **Ahmad:** Are you sure? The board says the twenty-eighth.
> **Priya:** Then the board is old. Go by the email.

**Your opinion:** Priya says *"Go by the email."* Why the email and not the board?

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline *on*, *at* and *in*.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| **at** + clock time | **on** + day or date | **in** + month, year, period |
|---|---|---|
| at six | on Thursday | in September |
| at half past seven | on 30 September | in 2027 |
| at night | on Monday evening | **in** nine days · **in** five minutes |

> **Grammar Note** — **in** also means *after this much time*: *in nine days* = nine days from now.
> That is not the same as *on the ninth*. Learners mix these up and miss deadlines.

**1D  Practice with a partner. Say it with *at*, *on* or *in*.**

1. The test is ______ six o'clock.
2. The deadline is ______ the thirtieth.
3. The course starts ______ October.
4. I'll be there ______ ten minutes.
5. There's no class ______ Friday.
6. She finishes ______ half past nine ______ the evening.

**1E  Write three sentences about your week.** Use *at*, *on* and *in* once each.

### 2 · Yes/no questions with *be*

**2A  Study the chart.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Is** the test on Thursday? | Yes, it is. / No, it isn't. |
| **Are** you in Room 7? | Yes, I am. / No, I'm not. |
| **Is** that in this building? | Yes, it is. / No, it's in Building 3. |
| **Are** you sure? | I'm not, actually. |

> **Grammar Note** — A short answer repeats the verb, not the whole sentence: *Yes, it is.* Saying
> only *yes* is not wrong, but the short answer sounds more certain, and in a corridor
> conversation that matters.

**2C  Practice with a partner.** Say *Are you sure?* with the voice going up, then going down.
What is the difference in meaning?

> **Why this matters:** *Are you sure?* ↗ is a question. *Are you sure.* ↘ is an accusation.
> The words are identical. Only the tune tells them apart.

### 3 · The conversation

**3A  Read and listen.** %%AUD W2-02-10%%

%%FIG v02-f9 | Ahmad at the information desk in Building 2. A member of staff points down a corridor with one hand and writes on a slip of paper with the other. Ahmad holds his phone with the email open.%%

> **Ahmad:** Excuse me, can you help me? I'm looking for Room 12.
> **Staff:** Room 12 is in Building 3. This is Building 2.
> **Ahmad:** Ah. Is it far?
> **Staff:** Five minutes. Out of this door, turn right, past the canteen, across the car park.
> **Ahmad:** Sorry — past the canteen, then …?
> **Staff:** Across the car park. The gray building. Shall I write it down?
> **Ahmad:** Yes please. And is the test at six?

### 💼 ON THE JOB

**Asking before you get it wrong.** %%AUD W2-02-12%%

> **1** — **Ahmad:** Grace, can you help me? Is this pallet for aisle nine or aisle nineteen?
> **Grace:** Nine. Why?
> **Ahmad:** The label is smudged. It could be either.
>
> **2** — **Grace:** Good. Ask every time.
> **Ahmad:** Every time? It feels slow.
> **Grace:** It's slower to move forty boxes twice.
>
> **3** — **Grace:** Ahmad, is the new man asking you things?
> **Ahmad:** All morning.
> **Grace:** Then he'll be fine.

**A  Practice all three in pairs.**

**B  Think about it.**

1. Ahmad says why he is asking — the label is smudged. Why does that matter to Grace?
2. Grace says *"It's slower to move forty boxes twice."* What is she really saying about asking?
3. You are not sure about an instruction at work. What do you say? Say it out loud.

> **Need help?** Can you help me? · Is this … or …? · I want to check before I start. ·
> Sorry — did you say …?

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some workplaces and classrooms, asking a lot of questions shows you are interested. In others
it shows you were not listening, and people stay quiet to look competent.

**Talk about it in groups.**

1. In your country, is it good or bad to ask your teacher many questions?
2. Is it different at work? Is it different with a manager you do not know well?
3. Here, Grace says *"ask every time."* Would your last manager have said that?

> No right answer — but a question costs seconds and a wrong guess costs a morning.
