### 1 · *be*: statements, negatives and contractions

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W2-01-05%%

> **Mrs Oyelaran:** Nadia. You're back.
> **Nadia:** I'm back. Is Ms Nkemelu still here?
> **Mrs Oyelaran:** She is. She's upstairs. You're not in her class now, though — you're in Room 7.
> **Nadia:** Room 7? That's the access course.
> **Mrs Oyelaran:** It's the English for it. You aren't on the course yet.
> **Nadia:** Not yet.
> **Mrs Oyelaran:** No. But you're nine days early, which is new for you.

**Your opinion:** Mrs Oyelaran says *"which is new for you."* Is she being unkind? Say what you think.

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline every short form of *be*.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | long | short | negative |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | I am | **I'm** | I'm not |
| you / we / they | you are | **you're** | you aren't / you're not |
| he / she / it | she is | **she's** | she isn't / she's not |

> **Grammar Note** — In speaking, use the short form almost always. In a form or a formal letter,
> use the long one. *Yes, I am* never becomes *Yes, I'm*.

**1D  Practice with a partner. Say it with the short form.**

1. I am not on the course. → *I'm not on the course.*
2. She is upstairs. → ______________
3. They are not here yet. → ______________
4. We are in Room 7. → ______________
5. He is not a student. → ______________

### 💼 ON THE JOB

**Asking for a reference.** %%AUD W2-01-12%%

> **1** — **Nadia:** Grace, can I ask you something? Would you write me a reference?
> **Grace:** For what?
> **Nadia:** A nursing course. The college wants one from an employer.
>
> **2** — **Grace:** How long have you worked here?
> **Nadia:** Six months. Is that long enough?
> **Grace:** It's enough for me to write what you're like. Send me the form.
>
> **3** — **Grace:** What do they want me to say?
> **Nadia:** Whether I turn up and whether I can be trusted.
> **Grace:** Then it's a short letter. Both yes.

**A  Practice all three in pairs.**

**B  Think about it.**

1. Nadia says *"Can I ask you something?"* before she asks. Why not just ask?
2. Grace asks what the college wants her to say. Why is that a useful question for both of them?
3. You need a reference. Who would you ask, and what would you say? Say it out loud.

> **Need help?** Can I ask you something? · Would you write me …? · They want to know … ·
> I can send you the form.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some countries a reference is a formal letter from a senior person, and asking for one is a big
thing. In others it is a phone number you write on a form, and nobody writes anything.

**Talk about it in groups.**

1. In your country, what is a reference? Who gives one?
2. Is it strange to ask your manager for a letter about yourself?
3. Somebody says yes but then does not write it. What do you do?

> No right answer — but here, asking is normal and most managers expect it.
