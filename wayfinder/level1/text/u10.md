### 1 · The past of *be*

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W2-10-05%%

> **Grace:** You were an engineer.
> **Ahmad:** I was. For eleven years.
> **Grace:** Were you managing people?
> **Ahmad:** I was. Six, in the last four years.
> **Grace:** And here you weren't anything for two years.
> **Ahmad:** I wasn't anything on paper. I was a father and I was learning English.
> **Grace:** That's a fair answer. Why didn't you say that on the form?
> **Ahmad:** There wasn't a box for it.
> **Grace:** There never is. Say it anyway.

**Your opinion:** Ahmad says *"I wasn't anything on paper."* What is the difference between what he
was on paper and what he was?

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline every *was*, *were*, *wasn't* and *weren't*.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| I / he / she / it | **was** / **wasn't** | an engineer. |
| you / we / they | **were** / **weren't** | late. |
| **Was** he …? | Yes, he was. / No, he wasn't. | |
| **Were** you …? | Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't. | |
| **There was** a problem. | **There were** six people. | |

> **Grammar Note** — This is the past of *be* only. The past of every other verb comes in
> Wayfinder 2. For now, you can say what you **were**, where you **were**, and how you **felt**.

**1D  Practice with a partner. Change to the past.**

1. I am a teacher. → *I was a teacher.*
2. They are late. → ______________
3. She isn't here. → ______________
4. Are you nervous? → ______________
5. There are six people. → ______________

### 2 · *and*, *too*, *but*

**2A  Study the chart.**

| | |
|---|---|
| I was an engineer **and** I managed six people. | adding |
| I was nervous. She was nervous **too**. | the same, at the end |
| He was late, **but** he was ready. | different, opposite |

> **Grammar Note** — *too* goes at the **end** of the sentence: *I was nervous too.* Putting it in

### 2 · Say it clearly — *was* and *wasn't*, weak and strong

**2A  Listen.** %%AUD W2-10-08%%

| weak (normal) | strong (answers and negatives) |
|---|---|
| I was /wəz/ an engineer. | Yes, I **was** /wɒz/. |
| They were /wə/ late. | No, they **weren't** /wɜːnt/. |

In a sentence, *was* and *were* almost disappear. In a short answer, they are the loudest word.

**2B  Listen. Circle what you hear.** %%AUD W2-10-09%%

1. was / wasn't  2. were / weren't  3. was / wasn't  4. were / weren't

**2C  Practice with a partner.** Ask *Were you…?* questions. Answer with short answers only. Make
the *was* or *weren't* loud.

> **Why this matters:** in an interview, the short answer carries the meaning. *Yes, I was* said
> quietly sounds like a guess.

### 3 · The conversation

**3A  Read and listen.** %%AUD W2-10-10%%

%%FIG v10-f9 | Grace and a second manager at the interview table. Ahmad is answering with one hand open; his small notebook is closed in front of him, unopened.%%

> **Grace:** Tell us about a time something went wrong on a shift.
> **Ahmad:** In September a pallet was in the wrong aisle and two orders were late.
> **Grace:** Were you responsible for it?
> **Ahmad:** No, I wasn't. But I found it, and I told the supervisor before the customer called.
> **Second manager:** What would you do differently now?
> **Ahmad:** I'd check the aisle numbers at the start of the shift, not when something's missing.
> **Grace:** Good. Any questions for us?
> **Ahmad:** Yes. Who trains the trainer? If I'm teaching six people, who teaches me?
> **Grace:** Nobody has asked that before.

**3B  Choose a or b.**

1. The problem was in (a) September (b) February
2. Ahmad (a) was (b) wasn't responsible
3. He told the supervisor (a) before (b) after the customer called
4. His question is about (a) pay (b) training

**3C  Think about the grammar.**

1. Ahmad says *"No, I wasn't. But I found it."* Why is the *but* sentence more important than the
   *no*?
2. Find two questions in the past.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some countries you talk about your achievements directly in an interview and modesty is a
mistake. In others, praising yourself is embarrassing, and you expect your references to speak
for you.

**Talk about it in groups.**

1. In your country, how do people answer *"What are you good at?"*
2. Is it normal to ask the interviewer questions? Which ones would be rude?
3. Ahmad says *"I wasn't anything on paper."* Is talking about the gap a weakness or a strength?

> No right answer — but here, nobody will guess what you did before. You have to say it.
