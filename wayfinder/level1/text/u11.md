# Unit 11 · Be Safe
%%SUB The signs you stop seeing after a week%%
%%GOALS
Understand safety signs and say what people should and shouldn't do

There are forty-one safety signs in the Kestrel Logistics warehouse. Ahmad counted them in his
first week, because they were the only English in the building he was certain he understood. By
his third week he had stopped seeing them, which is the actual danger.

> **Reader's Tip** — In a safety notice, find the sentence that says **who**. A rule with no person
> attached to it is a rule nobody does.

> ## Safety on the floor
> *Kestrel Logistics · Read this every time you read it*
>
> 1. **High-visibility vests** are worn by everybody past the yellow line. No exceptions, including
>    visitors and including managers.
> 2. **Walkways** stay clear. If you put something in a walkway, you move it before you leave.

### 1 · *should* and *shouldn't*

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W2-11-05%%

> **Ahmad:** Grace, the fire exit by aisle six has pallets in front of it.
> **Grace:** How many?
> **Ahmad:** Three, and a stack of boxes.
> **Grace:** How long have they been there?
> **Ahmad:** Since Monday, I think. Nobody should have put them there.
> **Grace:** No, and somebody should have said so on Monday.
> **Ahmad:** I walked past it four times.
> **Grace:** So did everybody. That's how it works — we all stop seeing it. Write it in the book
> and I'll move them now.
> **Ahmad:** Shouldn't I just move them?
> **Grace:** Move them **and** write it. The book is how it stops happening again.

**Your opinion:** Grace says *"we all stop seeing it."* Is that an excuse, or an explanation?

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline every *should* and *shouldn't*.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | |
|---|---|
| You **should** report it. | advice or a mild obligation |
| You **shouldn't** block the exit. | advice against |
| **Should I** move them? | asking for advice |
| **Shouldn't** we tell somebody? | suggesting, and expecting agreement |

> **Grammar Note** — *should* is the same for everybody: *I should, he should, they should.* No
> *-s*, and the verb after it is plain.

**1D  Practice with a partner. Give advice with *should* or *shouldn't*.**

1. The floor is wet and there is no sign. → *You should put a sign out.*
2. Somebody is lifting a heavy box alone. → ______________
3. The exit is blocked. → ______________
4. A cable is across the walkway. → ______________
5. A visitor has no vest. → ______________

**1E  Write three rules for a place you know, using *should* and *shouldn't*.**

### 2 · Questions: information and yes/no

**2A  Study the chart.**

| yes/no | information |
|---|---|
| **Is** the exit blocked? | **What** is blocking it? |
| **Should** I report it? | **Who** should I report it to? |
| **Was** it there yesterday? | **How long** has it been there? |
| **Do** you know where the assembly point is? | **Where** is the assembly point? |

> **Grammar Note** — A yes/no question gets you one bit of information. An information question
> gets you the thing you actually need. In an emergency, ask *where*, not *do you know where*.

**2A  Listen.** %%AUD W2-11-08%%

| | |
|---|---|
| **Don't** go through there. | the *don't* is loud |
| Don't go through **there**. | the place is the point |
| You should **report** it. | the action is the point |
| **You** should report it. | not somebody else |

**2B  Listen. Which word is stressed?** %%AUD W2-11-09%%

1. ______  2. ______  3. ______  4. ______

**2C  Practice with a partner.** Say *You shouldn't lift that alone* four times, stressing a
different word each time. Which version is a warning? Which is an accusation?

> **Why this matters:** on a loud floor, people hear one or two stressed words out of a sentence.
> Put the stress on the one that keeps them safe.

### 3 · The conversation

**3A  Read and listen.** %%AUD W2-11-10%%

### 💼 ON THE JOB

**Stopping somebody.** %%AUD W2-11-12%%

> **1** — **Ahmad:** Stop — don't lift that on your own.
> **Colleague:** It's not heavy.
> **Ahmad:** It's not the weight, it's the shape. Wait two seconds, I'll take the other end.
>
> **2** — **Colleague:** You sound like Grace.
> **Ahmad:** Grace stopped me in my first week. I was annoyed for about a day.
> **Colleague:** And now?
> **Ahmad:** Now my back is fine and Yusuf's isn't.
>
> **3** — **Grace:** Ahmad, I heard you stop somebody this morning.
> **Ahmad:** Was that all right?
> **Grace:** That's the job. That's actually the whole job.

**A  Practice all three in pairs.**

**B  Think about it.**

1. Ahmad says *"It's not the weight, it's the shape."* Why does giving the reason matter?
2. He tells a story about himself instead of quoting the rule. Why does that work better?
3. You see somebody about to do something unsafe. What do you say in the first three seconds?
   Say it out loud.

> **Need help?** Stop — · Wait two seconds. · Let me take the other end. · It's not …, it's … ·
> Somebody did this for me once.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some workplaces anybody can stop anybody, including a new person stopping a manager. In others,
correcting somebody senior in front of others is unthinkable, whatever the rule says.

**Talk about it in groups.**

1. Where you are from, could a new worker tell a manager to put a vest on?
2. Is it different if it is a safety rule? Should it be?
3. Ahmad says *"That's on us, not you."* Why does he take the blame for something he didn't do?

> No right answer — but every safety system in the world depends on somebody junior being willing
> to speak.

### 2 · Read the model

**2A  Listen and read.** %%AUD W2-11-14%%

> ### Forty-one Signs
> *by Ahmad Sultani*
>
> In my first week I counted forty-one safety signs. They were the only English in the building I
> was sure about. By my third week I wasn't reading them any more, and that is the dangerous part.
> On Monday somebody put three pallets in front of a fire exit. I walked past it four times before
> I said anything, and so did everybody else. Grace says we all stop seeing it. We moved the
> pallets and I wrote it in the book, and now there's a yellow box painted on the floor. You can't
> stop seeing paint.

> **Writer's Tip** — Admit your own part. *I walked past it four times* is what makes the last
> line land. A piece where only other people are at fault is not believed.

**2B  Circle the correct word.**

3. He walked past the pallets ( four / forty ) times.

**2C  Listen to Grace. Circle what you hear.** %%AUD W2-11-15%%

2. She says most accidents happen in week ( one / three ).
