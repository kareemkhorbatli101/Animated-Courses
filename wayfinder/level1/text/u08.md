# Unit 8 · Good Food
%%SUB Cooking for six on what four people can afford%%
%%GOALS
Talk about food, quantities and what things cost
Use count and noncount nouns, and quantity words
Read a recipe and a nutrition label, and plan a week of meals
%%

On the first Sunday of every month, the four in Apartment 4B cook together and whoever turns up
eats. It started as a way to use up what was in the fridge. It is now the only evening in the week
when all four of them are in the same room at the same time.

### 1 · Count and noncount

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W2-08-05%%

> **Linh:** How much rice?
> **Nadia:** A kilo. There are six of us.
> **Linh:** And how many onions?
> **Nadia:** Four. There are three in the cupboard, so buy one.
> **Linh:** Is there any oil?
> **Nadia:** There's a little. Not much.
> **Linh:** A little isn't a lot.
> **Nadia:** A little is enough. Mei is bringing bread and Priya is bringing people.

**Your opinion:** Nadia says Priya is *bringing people*. Is that a contribution? Say what you think.

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline *much*, *many*, *a little* and *any*.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | I count them | I don't count them |
|---|---|---|
| examples | onions, eggs, apples, bottles | rice, oil, milk, water, money |
| **+** | some · a few · a lot of · three | some · a little · a lot of |
| **−** | not any · not many | not any · not much |
| **?** | How **many**? Are there any? | How **much**? Is there any? |

> **Grammar Note** — *a few* and *a little* mean *a small amount, and that is fine*. *Not many*
> and *not much* mean *a small amount, and it is a problem*. Same quantity, opposite feeling.

**1D  Practice with a partner. Choose the right word.**

1. How ( much / many ) rice?
2. How ( much / many ) onions?
3. There isn't ( much / many ) oil.
4. There are ( a few / a little ) eggs left.
5. We don't have ( much / many ) money this week.
6. Is there ( some / any ) milk?

**1E  Write three sentences about your kitchen now.** Use *a little*, *a few* and *not much*.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some countries people shop every day and cook fresh. In others they shop once a week and freeze
half of it. Cooking for six on a Sunday is normal in some homes and strange in others.

**Talk about it in groups.**

1. How often did your family shop at home? How often do you shop here?
2. Do people eat together where you are from? Every day, or only on certain days?
3. Is there a food from home you cannot find here? What do you use instead?

> No right answer — but the question *"what do you use instead?"* is how most recipes survive a
> move.

### 2 · Read the model

**2A  Listen and read.** %%AUD W2-08-14%%

> ### The First Sunday
> *by Nadia Rahman*
>
> On the first Sunday of the month we cook for whoever comes. There are four of us and usually six
> plates. We buy a whole chicken because it costs about half as much as the pieces, and Linh cuts
> it up in the kitchen while the rice is on. There isn't much oil and there aren't any clean
> plates, but there is always enough food. It costs about four each. The point of it isn't the
> money. It's that this is the only evening when all four of us are in the same room.

> **Writer's Tip** — Save the real reason for the last sentence. Everything before it is rice and
> chicken; the last line is what the piece is actually about.
