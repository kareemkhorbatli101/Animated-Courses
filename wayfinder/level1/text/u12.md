Nobody can quite believe it. There is a listing page open on the table, six suggestions, four
opinions, and a bus that leaves at nine.

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| I | **'m going to** | sleep. |
| You / We / They | **'re going to** | meet at one. |
| He / She / It | **'s going to** | rain. |
| **Are** you **going to** …? | Yes, I am. / No, I'm not. | |
| negative | I **'m not going to** pay twelve. | |

> **Grammar Note** — Use *be going to* for a plan you have already made, and for something you can
> see coming: *It's going to rain* — look at the sky. In speech it often sounds like *gonna*.
> Write *going to*.

### 2 · *was* and *were* for what happened

**2A  Study the chart.**

| plan (before) | what happened (after) |
|---|---|
| We **are going to** meet at one. | We **were** at the market at one. |
| It **'s going to** rain. | It **was** sunny all afternoon. |
| I **'m not going to** pay twelve. | I **wasn't** going to pay twelve, and I didn't. |
| **Are** you **going to** come? | **Were** you there? |

> **Grammar Note** — You can say what you **were going to** do but didn't: *I was going to sleep,

> ## You have finished Wayfinder 1
>
> You started this book able to say who you are. You finish it able to say who you **were** — and
> that is the difference between describing yourself and telling your story.
>
> In twelve units you enrolled on a course, asked for help in a building that explains nothing,
> described the people you live with, furnished a room, learned a city, laid out a week, compared
> two prices, fed six people, prepared for a ten-minute appointment, sat an interview, stopped
> somebody doing something dangerous, and spent one Saturday doing nothing much.
>
> In **Wayfinder 2** the past tense opens properly. Nadia starts the nursing access course. Ahmad
> runs a team of six. Mei qualifies. Tomás stops delivering and starts managing. And you learn to
> say what happened — not just what you were, but what you **did**, which is the tense that
> everything interesting lives in.
