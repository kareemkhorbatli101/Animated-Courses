%%SUB Delivering for an hour, and learning a city properly%%

Tomás takes a delivery round two evenings a week. It pays badly and it has taught him the city
better than three years of living in it. He has learned that nobody uses street names, that *the
blue door* is an address, and that the app is wrong about one street in four.

### 1 · The present continuous

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W2-05-05%%

> **Tomás:** Hello? I'm outside, but I can't find the door.
> **Customer:** Where are you standing?
> **Tomás:** On the corner. There's a pharmacy opposite me.
> **Customer:** Then you're on Market Square. You want Bridge Lane. Are you walking or driving?
> **Tomás:** Walking.
> **Customer:** Right. Go past the pharmacy and turn right. I'm looking out of the window now.
> **Tomás:** I'm turning. … I can see you.
> **Customer:** You're waving at the wrong window, but you're close.

**Your opinion:** The customer asks *"Where are you standing?"* before giving any directions. Why
is that the right first question?

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline every *am / is / are* + *-ing*.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| I | **'m standing** | on the corner. |
| You / We / They | **'re walking** | the wrong way. |
| He / She / It | **'s looking** | out of the window. |
| **Are** you **walking**? | Yes, I am. / No, I'm not. | |
| **Where are** you **standing**? | On the corner. | |

> **Grammar Note** — Spelling: *walk → walking*, but *come → coming* (drop the *e*) and *get →
> getting* (double the consonant). Those three patterns cover almost everything.

**1D  Practice with a partner. Make the sentence.**

1. (I / stand / on the corner) → *I'm standing on the corner.*
2. (she / look / out of the window) → ______________
3. (they / not / wait / outside) → ______________
4. (? / you / walk or drive) → ______________
5. (he / cross / the road) → ______________

**1E  Write three sentences about what people in this room are doing now.**

### 2 · *Where* questions

**2A  Study the chart.**

| | |
|---|---|

**2C  Practice with a partner.** Say *It's the second door on the left* four times, stressing a

> **Why this matters:** English puts the new or important information in a loud, long syllable.
> If you say every word the same, the listener does not know what to hold on to.

### 3 · The conversation

**3A  Read and listen.** %%AUD W2-05-10%%

%%FIG v05-f9 | Tomás on Bridge Lane in the rain with a phone to his ear and a delivery bag on his shoulder. A blue unmarked door is a few meters away. A woman is leaning out of a first-floor window waving.%%

> **Tomás:** I'm on Bridge Lane now. There are three doors.
> **Customer:** Mine's blue. Are you walking towards the bridge or away from it?
> **Tomás:** Towards it.
> **Customer:** Then it's on your left. Second one.
> **Tomás:** The one with no number?
> **Customer:** That's the one. Nobody can ever find it.
> **Tomás:** Can I say something? Put a number on it.
> **Customer:** Everyone says that.
> **Tomás:** Everyone is standing in the rain.

**3B  Choose a or b.**

1. There are (a) two (b) three doors
2. Tomás is walking (a) towards (b) away from the bridge
3. The door has (a) a number (b) no number
4. Tomás suggests (a) a light (b) a number

**3C  Think about the grammar.**

1. The customer asks *"Are you walking towards the bridge or away from it?"* Why does the
   direction matter more than the street name?
2. Find two present continuous verbs.

### 💼 ON THE JOB

**Saying where you are.** %%AUD W2-05-12%%

> **1** — **Dispatcher:** Where are you?
> **Tomás:** Market Square, by the crossing. The traffic's stopped.
> **Dispatcher:** How long?
> **Tomás:** Ten minutes at least. There's an accident at the lights.
>
> **2** — **Dispatcher:** Can you go round by the bridge?
> **Tomás:** I'm walking, not driving. The bridge adds fifteen.
> **Dispatcher:** Then stay. I'll tell the customer.
>
> **3** — **Dispatcher:** Tomás, next time call before the ten minutes, not during.
> **Tomás:** Understood.

**A  Practice all three in pairs.**

**B  Think about it.**

1. Tomás says *where* he is and *why* he is stopped in one sentence. Why do both matter?
2. The dispatcher says *"call before the ten minutes."* What is she actually asking for?
3. You are going to be late for work or class. What do you say, and when? Say it out loud.

> **Need help?** I'm at … · There's a problem with … · It'll be about … minutes. ·
> I'll call you when I'm moving.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some cities people give directions by street name and number. In others, by shops, trees and
things that are no longer there. Some people would rather walk with you than explain.

**Talk about it in groups.**

1. In your city, how do people give directions?
2. Does anybody there use north, south, east and west? Do you?
3. Somebody gives you directions you do not understand, and they are being kind. What do you do?

> No right answer — but *"Can you show me on the map?"* works in every city.

### 2 · Read the model

**2A  Listen and read.** %%AUD W2-05-14%%

> ### The Blue Door
> *by Tomás Alcaraz*
>
> I'm standing on a corner in the rain and I can't find number fourteen. The app says I'm there.
> I'm not there. There are three doors on Bridge Lane and none of them has a number. I call the
> customer and she asks me a good question: am I walking towards the bridge or away from it?
> Towards. Then it's the second on my left, and it's blue. She says nobody can ever find it. I say
> put a number on it. When I leave, she's writing one on a piece of card.

> **Writer's Tip** — Write the present continuous when you want the reader **inside** the moment:
> *I'm standing on a corner in the rain.* Past tense would push them outside it.
