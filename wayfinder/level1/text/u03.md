### 1 · *have* and *has*

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W2-03-05%%

> **Nadia:** Linh, have you seen the rota?
> **Linh:** What rota?
> **Nadia:** On the fridge. Behind the photo.
> **Linh:** Behind the photo. So I have a week and nobody told me.
> **Priya:** We have a photo problem, not a rota problem.
> **Linh:** Has everybody got a key to the post box?
> **Priya:** Mei hasn't. She has the door key only.
> **Linh:** So four people, three keys and a hidden rota.
> **Nadia:** When you say it like that.

**Your opinion:** Linh says *"nobody told me."* Whose job was it to tell her?

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline *have*, *has*, *hasn't* and *got*.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| I / you / we / they | **have** / **don't have** | a key. |
| he / she / it | **has** / **doesn't have** | a key. |
| **Do** you **have** a key? | Yes, I do. / No, I don't. | |
| **Does** she **have** a key? | Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't. | |

> **Grammar Note** — You will also hear **have got**: *I've got a key. Has she got a key? She
> hasn't.* It means the same thing and it is very common in speech. Use whichever you like, but
> do not mix them: *Do you have got* is not English.

**1D  Practice with a partner. Say it two ways.**

1. (I / a key) → *I have a key.* / *I've got a key.*
2. (she / two jobs) → ______________
3. (they / no rota) → ______________
4. (he / a car) → ______________
5. (we / a problem) → ______________

**1E  Write two sentences: one thing you have, one thing you don't have.**

### 2 · Possessives

**2A  Study the chart.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **my · your · his · her · our · their** + noun | *her* key, *their* rota |
| name + **'s** | Mei**'s** key, Priya**'s** week |
| plural + **'** | the tenant**s'** kitchen |
| **Whose** is this? | It's mine. / It's Mei's. |
| **mine · yours · his · hers · ours · theirs** | That key is *hers*, not *mine*. |

> **Grammar Note** — *her key* and *hers* mean the same thing, but *hers* stands alone with no
> noun after it. *It's her.* is a different sentence entirely, and usually not the one you want.

### 3 · The conversation

**3A  Read and listen.** %%AUD W2-03-10%%

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some homes, jobs are shared by who is free. In others, by who is oldest, or youngest, or a
guest. A written rota can look cold to one person and fair to another.

2. Is a written rota a good idea, or does it make a home feel like an office?
3. You live with somebody who never does their share. What do you say first — and to whom?

> No right answer — but the conversation is easier before somebody is angry than after.
