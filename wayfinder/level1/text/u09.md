### 1 · Imperatives and *should*

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W2-09-05%%

> **Dr Haddad:** How long?
> **Yusuf:** Since August.
> **Dr Haddad:** August. And you've come in November.
> **Yusuf:** It isn't bad enough to stop working.
> **Dr Haddad:** That's not the test. Does it wake you at night?
> **Yusuf:** Sometimes.
> **Dr Haddad:** Then you should have come in September. Right — keep moving, don't rest
> completely, and change which shoulder you carry that bag on.
> **Yusuf:** That's it?
> **Dr Haddad:** For six weeks, that's it. If it's the same in December, come back and I'll refer
> you.

**Your opinion:** Yusuf says *"It isn't bad enough to stop working."* The doctor says that's not
the test. What is the test?

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline every imperative and every *should*.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **Imperative** | Keep moving. / Don't rest. | an instruction |
| **should** | You **should** come back. | advice |
| **shouldn't** | You **shouldn't** lift that. | advice against |
| **Should I** …? | Should I take something? | asking for advice |

> **Grammar Note** — *should* is advice, not an order. After it, the verb is plain: *you should
> come*, never *you should to come* or *you should comes*.

**1D  Practice with a partner. Change the imperative to advice with *should*.**

1. Keep moving. → *You should keep moving.*
2. Don't stay in bed. → ______________
3. Use heat for fifteen minutes. → ______________
4. Don't carry it on one side. → ______________
5. Come back in December. → ______________

### 2 · *have* and *has* for symptoms

**2A  Study the chart.**

| | |
|---|---|
| I **have** a headache. · She **has** a temperature. | the symptom |
| I've **had** it **since** August. | when it started |
| I've **had** it **for** three months. | how long it has lasted |
| **How long have you had** it? | the question that matters |

> **Grammar Note** — **since** + a point in time: *since August, since Monday, since I started the
> job.* **for** + a length of time: *for three months, for two weeks.* Mixing these is the most
> common mistake in a doctor's room.

### 2 · Say it clearly — *should* and *shouldn't*

**2A  Listen.** %%AUD W2-09-08%%

| | |
|---|---|
| You **should** go. /ʃʊd/ — short and quiet | You **shouldn't** go. /ˈʃʊdnt/ — longer, with a stop |
| I **should** rest. | I **shouldn't** rest. |

The *l* is silent in both. The difference is the *n't*.

**2B  Listen. Circle *should* or *shouldn't*.** %%AUD W2-09-09%%

1. should / shouldn't  2. should / shouldn't  3. should / shouldn't  4. should / shouldn't

**2C  Practice with a partner.** Give four pieces of advice, two positive and two negative. Your
partner says *do it* or *don't do it*.

> **Why this matters:** in a consulting room, *you should rest* and *you shouldn't rest* are
> opposite instructions that differ by one small sound at the end of a quiet word.

### 3 · The conversation

**3A  Read and listen.** %%AUD W2-09-10%%

%%FIG v09-f9 | Dr Haddad and Yusuf in the consulting room. Yusuf is holding out a folded piece of paper with three lines written on it. The doctor has taken it and is reading it.%%

> **Dr Haddad:** What can I do for you?
> **Yusuf:** Lower back. Since August. It's worse when I lift and it wakes me about once a week.
> **Dr Haddad:** You've written it down.
> **Yusuf:** My housemate told me to. She said you get ten minutes.
> **Dr Haddad:** Your housemate is right and I'd like to meet her. Any numbness down the leg?
> **Yusuf:** No.
> **Dr Haddad:** Good. Then it's muscular. Keep moving, don't stay in bed, and swap the shoulder
> you carry the bag on.
> **Yusuf:** And if it's the same in six weeks?
> **Dr Haddad:** Come back and we'll look further.

**3B  Choose a or b.**

3. Yusuf has (a) written his symptoms down (b) forgotten them
4. The doctor says it is (a) muscular (b) serious

**3C  Think about the grammar.**

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some countries you see a doctor the same week and they examine you for half an hour. In others
you wait a month for ten minutes. In some places you go early; in others going early for a small
thing is seen as wasting everybody's time.

**Talk about it in groups.**

1. How long do you wait for an appointment where you are from?
2. Is it normal to go to a doctor for a pain that does not stop you working?
3. Here, a ten-minute appointment means you must say the important thing first. Is that fair? Is
   it a skill you were ever taught?

> No right answer — but the three lines on Yusuf's paper are a skill, and nobody is born with it.
