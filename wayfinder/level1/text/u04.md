Yusuf's family is moving. The new place has one more room and one less problem, and on the
viewing day it is completely empty, which makes it very hard to tell whether it is big. His
daughter measures the bedroom in footsteps. His wife measures it in beds.

### 1 · *There is* and *There are*

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W2-04-05%%

> **Amina:** So? Is it big?
> **Yusuf:** There are two bedrooms. There's a living room and a kitchen.
> **Amina:** Is there a bath?
> **Yusuf:** There isn't a bath. There's a shower.
> **Amina:** And cupboards?
> **Yusuf:** Ah. There aren't any cupboards above the sink. The last tenant took them.
> **Amina:** Took the cupboards?
> **Yusuf:** Took the cupboards.
> **Amina:** Then there's one question. How much are cupboards?

**Your opinion:** Amina's last question is about money, not about cupboards. Why is that the right
question?

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline every *there is*, *there are* and their negatives.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **There's** | a shower. | one thing |
| **There are** | two bedrooms. | more than one |
| **There isn't** | a bath. | one thing, negative |
| **There aren't** | any cupboards. | more than one, negative |
| **Is there** a bath? | Yes, there is. / No, there isn't. | |
| **Are there** any cupboards? | Yes, there are. / No, there aren't. | |

> **Grammar Note** — Use **any** in negatives and questions with plural nouns: *There aren't any
> cupboards. Are there any shelves?* Use **some** in positives: *There are some shelves.*

**1D  Practice with a partner. Make the sentence.**

1. (two bedrooms) → *There are two bedrooms.*
2. (not / a bath) → ______________
3. (a shared garden) → ______________
4. (not / any cupboards) → ______________
5. (? / an elevator) → ______________

### 3 · The conversation

**3A  Read and listen.** %%AUD W2-04-10%%

%%FIG v04-f9 | Yusuf and the letting agent in the empty kitchen of the Market Street apartment. The agent points at the wall above the sink where cupboards used to be; the screw holes are visible. Yusuf has a phone out, photographing it.%%

> **Agent:** So. Two bedrooms, second floor, available from the first.
> **Yusuf:** There aren't any cupboards above the sink.
> **Agent:** No. The last tenant fitted those himself and took them.
> **Yusuf:** Is that on the inventory?
> **Agent:** It won't be. They were never the landlord's.
> **Yusuf:** Then can we write that down? That there weren't any when I moved in?
> **Agent:** We can. Photograph it as well.
> **Yusuf:** I already have.
> **Agent:** Good. You've done this before.

**3B  Choose a or b.**

4. The agent says Yusuf (a) has done this before (b) is being difficult

**3C  Think about the grammar.**

2. Find one negative with *any*.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some countries an apartment comes with everything, down to the spoons. In others *furnished*
means four walls and a light bulb, and tenants carry a kitchen from home to home for years.

**Talk about it in groups.**

1. In your country, what comes with a rented home?
2. Who pays for heating and water where you are from — the tenant or the landlord?
3. What is the strangest thing you have had to buy for a new place?

> No right answer — but ask for the **list** before you sign, in any country.
