### 1 · *have*, *want* and *need*

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W2-07-05%%

> **Salesman:** Twelve a month. Nothing today.
> **Priya:** How many months?
> **Salesman:** Thirty.
> **Priya:** So three hundred and sixty.
> **Salesman:** If you want to look at it that way.
> **Priya:** It's the only way to look at it. The cash price is 280.
> **Salesman:** Do you have 280 today?
> **Priya:** We don't. But we don't need it today either. We need it in March.
> **Salesman:** Most people need it today.

**Your opinion:** The salesman says *"Most people need it today."* Is that true, or is it a way of
selling? Say what you think.

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline *have*, *want*, *need* and their negatives.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| I **have** / **don't have** | enough. | what is in your hand |
| I **want** / **don't want** | the big one. | what you would like |
| I **need** / **don't need** | it today. | what you cannot do without |
| **Do** you **have** …? | Yes, I do. / No, I don't. | |

> **Grammar Note** — *want* and *need* are not the same, and the difference decides money.
> A shop will always talk about what you *want*. A budget is about what you *need*.

### 3 · The conversation

**3A  Read and listen.** %%AUD W2-07-10%%

%%FIG v07-f9 | Priya at the shop counter with her phone calculator visible. The salesman stands behind the counter beside a price card. Nadia is at her shoulder looking at the small print.%%

> **Salesman:** So, twelve a month. Everybody takes it.
> **Priya:** How many months is that?
> **Salesman:** Thirty.
> **Priya:** Thirty or thirteen?
> **Salesman:** Thirty. Three-zero.
> **Priya:** Then the total is 360. The cash price is 280.
> **Salesman:** You don't have 280.
> **Priya:** Not today. We have 180 and we get paid on the thirtieth.
> **Salesman:** Then come back on the thirtieth.
> **Priya:** That's what I wanted to hear.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some countries paying monthly for a fridge is completely normal and nobody thinks about the
total. In others, borrowing for anything except a house is something people avoid, or are ashamed
of.

**Talk about it in groups.**

2. Is borrowing money from a shop different from borrowing from family? How?
3. Would you tell a friend what you pay a month for something? Would you tell them the total?

> No right answer — but anybody who will not show you the total is telling you something.

### 2 · Read the model

**2A  Listen and read.** %%AUD W2-07-14%%

> ### Three Hundred and Sixty
> *by Priya Raghavan*
>
> The washing machine stops on a Tuesday. The shop says twelve a month and nothing today, which
> sounds like a gift until you ask how many months. Thirty. Thirty times twelve is three hundred
> and sixty, and the cash price is two hundred and eighty. So the gift costs eighty. We have one
> hundred and eighty between four of us and we get paid on the thirtieth. We wash things in the
> bath for eleven days. On the thirtieth we buy it with cash, and nobody owes anybody anything.

> **Writer's Tip** — Write numbers in words when they are the point of the sentence. *Thirty times
> twelve is three hundred and sixty* lands harder than *30 × 12 = 360*.
