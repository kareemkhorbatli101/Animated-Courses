### 1 · The present perfect, where it matters

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the opinion question.** %%AUD W4-04-06%%

> **Adeel:** I've applied for nineteen jobs since March.
> **Grace:** And how many did you apply for last March?
> **Adeel:** Four. But that was a different year and a different me.
> **Grace:** So say that. "I've applied for nineteen jobs this year" is a fact. "I applied for
> four last year" is a different fact. Only one of them is still happening.

*Is nineteen applications a sign of effort, or a sign that something in the approach is wrong?*

**1B  Underline the present perfect forms in 1A, and the past simple ones. What is the
difference between the two situations?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Started in the past, **still true now** | I'**ve worked** here **for** eleven months. |
| Experience, **time not stated** | I'**ve managed** a team before. |
| A result that **matters now** | I'**ve sent** it, so you should have it. |
| **Finished time** → past simple | I **worked** there **in 2023**. |

> **Grammar Note** — The present perfect is not a past tense in disguise. It is a present tense
> about a situation with a past in it. The moment you name a finished time — *yesterday*, *in
> March*, *last year* — the sentence is no longer about now, and the verb must go to the past
> simple.

### 3 · Contractions at speed

**3A  Study the chart.**

| Full | Contracted | Heard as |
|---|---|---|
| I have | I've | "ive" |
| he has | he's | "heez" |
| we have not | we haven't | "we havn't" |

> **Grammar Note** — *He's* is both *he is* and *he has*. Only what follows tells you which: *he's

> whole sentences to it.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some hiring cultures you are expected to state your achievements plainly, and modesty reads as
weakness. In others, stating them plainly reads as boasting and the expected move is to credit the

what you did, then say who you did it with.
