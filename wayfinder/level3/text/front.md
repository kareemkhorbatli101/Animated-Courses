# Wayfinder 3
%%SUB Communication for Study, Work and Life · CEFR B1%%

## How to use this book

This is the fourth book in the Wayfinder series. It is written for adults who are studying,
working, or doing both, in a language that is not their first. It assumes you can already handle
the past, the future and the present perfect in speech, and that what you now need is range:
longer texts, harder documents, and the confidence to disagree with somebody politely and in
detail.

**Each unit has sixteen pages and five lessons**, and each lesson takes about ninety minutes.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Twenty-eight to thirty-four new words, in situations, with a team research task |
| **2 · Real-life Reading** | A long authentic document, with True/False/Not Given, word study, and a graph |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures, a *Language connection* box, and a team error hunt |
| **4 · Everyday Conversation** | Pronunciation, a long dialogue, a workplace box, and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A model text by one named person, then your own, with an editing checklist |
| **Team Up & Review** | A team project with four roles, a class survey, and a hard case |

**The audio.** 🔊 marks a track. 194 tracks, numbered `W4-unit-track`.

**What is different at this level.** Three things, and all three are deliberate.

**The texts are real documents.** A tenancy law extract, a hospital letter, a bank's dispute
policy, a job description written by somebody who has never done the job. They are not simplified.
They are not meant to be read word by word. Lesson 2 teaches you to find what you need and ignore
the rest, which is what everybody does with these documents, including the people who write them.

**Every reading text cites a real source.** The numbers in this book are not invented. Where a
text says *one in four*, there is a published figure behind it and the text names it. You will be
asked, more than once, whether the number supports the sentence it is standing next to.

**The phrase banks are gone.** In the first three books, every discussion question came with the
language to answer it. You no longer need that, and carrying it any further would be pretending
you did.

## The people

The cast is the same one you met in Wayfinder Starter. Three years have passed in Portmere.

**Nadia Rahman** has finished the access course and started the nursing degree. **Tomás Alvarez**
runs a shift at Northgate. **Mei Chen** has a conditional offer. **Yusuf Osman** is training other
people. **Priya Raghavan** organizes things nobody asked her to organize, and they work.
**Lucia Pham** has a tenancy she now reads before she signs. **Ahmad Rahim** has a second-hand van
and an opinion about it. **Oksana Koval** is still waiting for one letter. **Linh Pham**, **Adeel
Khan** and **Samira Haddad** arrived two books ago and are no longer new.

Everybody in this book is invented. So is Portmere, so is every company in it, and so is every
document reproduced in these pages except where a source is named.
