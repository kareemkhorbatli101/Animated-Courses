### 2 · Read

> **Reader's Tip** — A rights summary tells you what you are entitled to. It rarely tells you how
> long it takes or what it costs you to claim it. Those two facts decide whether the right is
> real for you, and you almost always have to ask a person for them.

%%FIG x12-f5 | A four-page advice leaflet: a cover with a title and an organization name, two pages of numbered sections with a boxed warning, and a final page with a small comparison table and a source line.%%

> ## Knowing it and using it
> *Portmere Advice Center · leaflet 2 · for tenants, workers and students*
>
> 1  **Rights are not self-executing.** Almost nothing in this leaflet happens automatically. A
> 2  repair obligation does not repair anything. An entitlement does not pay anybody. Every right
> 3  described here becomes real only when a named person asks for it, in a prescribed way, within
> 4  a time limit. That is not a flaw in the system; it is the system.
>
> 5  **Order matters more than argument.** Raise it informally. If that fails, put it in writing,
> 6  dated, factual, one page. Then use the internal process. Only then go outside. A complaint
> 7  that skips a stage is usually returned, and the time it took to return it comes out of your
> 8  time limit, not theirs.
>
> 9  **Retaliation.** Being treated worse because you complained is unlawful in most of the
> 10 situations covered here, and it is also hard to prove and slow to remedy. We say this plainly
> 11 because leaflets that do not say it are read by people who then feel foolish. Keep dates. Keep
> 12 copies. Tell somebody at the time.
>
> 13 **Why housing comes first in this leaflet.** Across the EU in 2024, **8.2 per cent** of people
> 14 lived in households spending 40 per cent or more of disposable income on housing. For
> 15 non-nationals from outside the EU the figure was **18.8 per cent**, against **7.6 per cent**
> 16 for nationals. If you are reading this as a migrant tenant, the gap is not your imagination.
>
> *Figures: Eurostat, Living conditions in Europe — housing, and Migrant integration statistics,
> 2024 data.*

**1B  Underline the four verb + infinitive patterns in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Verbs followed by **to + base** | Example |
|---|---|
| decide, agree, offer, promise, refuse | She **refused to sign** it. |
| want, need, hope, expect, plan | I **expect to hear** by Friday. |
| manage, fail, arrange, afford | He **failed to reply** within ten days. |
| **+ object** — ask, tell, advise, remind, allow | They **advised me to write**. |

> **Grammar Note** — *Advise*, *allow*, *remind*, *tell* and *ask* take an object before the
> infinitive: *they advised me to write*, not *they advised to write*. Leaving the object out is
> the commonest error at this level and it makes the sentence read like a notice rather than a
> conversation.

### 2 · Verb + gerund, and verbs that take either

**2A  Study the chart.**

| Pattern | Verbs | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **+ gerund** | avoid, consider, suggest, deny, risk, keep | I **considered leaving**. |
| **+ gerund** | finish, mind, enjoy, miss, put off | He **kept promising**. |
| **+ either, same meaning** | start, begin, continue, prefer | It **started raining / to rain**. |
| **+ either, different meaning** | stop, remember, forget, try, regret | see the note |

> **Grammar Note** — *I regret telling him* means you told him and wish you had not. *I regret to
> tell you* means you are about to tell him and it is bad news. *I tried writing* means you tried
> it as a method; *I tried to write* means you attempted and may have failed. These pairs appear
> constantly in formal letters, which is why they are here.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Putting something in writing means different things in different places. In some systems it is
the normal, unremarkable way to be taken seriously. In others it is an escalation, close to a
declaration of war, and doing it early destroys a relationship that would have solved the problem
in a week. Advisers who do not ask which system you grew up in give the same advice to everybody
and are surprised by what happens.

**Discuss:** where you come from, what does a letter mean? Would you have written one sooner here,
or later?

> ### Real Lives — Lucia
>
> I spent four months being right on the telephone. I was right every time, and every call ended
> with a man saying he would send somebody, and nothing in the flat getting any warmer.
>

> the thing that works.

> **Writer's Tip** — Take every adjective out of a complaint and see what is left. If what is
> left is still a complaint, you have one. If it isn't, you have a feeling, which is real and
> belongs somewhere else.
