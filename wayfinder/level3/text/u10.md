At twenty past eleven on a Wednesday, a window went in at the back of the Fenner Street shop.
Priya heard it from two doors down. By the time anyone had decided what to do, four people had
seen four different things, and the only person who wrote anything down was the one who saw

is how memory works and not how evidence does.

### 2 · Read

> **Reader's Tip** — A report separates what was observed from what was concluded, and the join
> is usually a single word: *therefore*, *suggests*, *appears*, *consistent with*. Find those
> words and you have found the line between evidence and opinion.

%%FIG x10-f5 | A three-page incident review: a cover with a reference and a date, a page with a timeline down the left margin and observations beside it, and a final page with a short findings list, a recommendations box and a source line.%%

> ## Incident review: Fenner Street, 14 November
> *Portmere Business Watch · review note 3 · circulated to members*
>
> 1  **What is recorded.** At 23:20 a rear pane was broken at number 14. Four residents report
> 2  hearing it. One account was written at 23:22; the others were given to officers after 00:30,
> 3  by which time the four had spoken to one another. The camera bracket at the corner has held
> 4  no camera since March.
>
> 5  **What is observed.** Three of the four accounts mention a dark car. The written account
> 6  does not. Where accounts are taken after witnesses have conferred, detail converges; this is
> 7  well established and is not evidence of dishonesty. We therefore treat the contemporaneous
> 8  note as the stronger record, while noting it is also the shortest.
>
> 9  **What we conclude, and what we do not.** We conclude that entry was forced at the rear and
> 10 that the street lighting outage two doors away is relevant. We do not conclude anything about
> 11 a vehicle. Attributing an incident to a described car on the basis of accounts given after
> 12 discussion is how a street ends up watching for the wrong thing for a year.
>
> 13 **Context for members.** Harm at work remains common: survey data for 2025 indicate that
> 14 **16 percent of the global workforce** reported experiencing serious harm at work in the
> 15 previous two years, down from 18 percent in 2023 and 19 percent in 2021. Falling, slowly.
> 16 Recording things properly is part of why.
>
> *Figures: ILO / World Risk Poll data, reported by ILOSTAT, 2025.*

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| **As the subject** | **Writing it down** took two minutes. |
| **After a preposition** | He left **without saying** anything. |

> **Grammar Note** — A gerund subject is always singular. *Writing things down **is** useful*,
> not *are useful*, however many things there are. The subject is the activity, not the things.

### 2 · Gerund or infinitive?

**2A  Study the chart.**

| Verb | Followed by | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **avoid, enjoy, finish, suggest, mind** | gerund | I avoid **going** that way. |
| **decide, agree, refuse, offer, manage** | infinitive | She agreed **to write** it down. |
| **start, begin, continue, like, hate** | either, same meaning | It started **raining** / **to rain**. |
| **remember, forget, stop** | either, **different** meaning | see the note |

> **Grammar Note** — *I stopped smoking* means you gave it up. *I stopped to smoke* means you
> paused in order to. *I remember locking it* is a memory of the act; *I remembered to lock it*
> means you did not forget. Four sentences, four different events, one letter group apart.

**2B  Listen and repeat.** %%AUD W4-10-07%%
