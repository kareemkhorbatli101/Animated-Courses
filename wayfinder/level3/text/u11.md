Mei's offer came through on a Tuesday: a place on the degree, conditional on one grade she has
already got, starting in September, in a city two hundred kilometres away. Three people were told
within the hour and none of them was the one she most needed to tell. The letter uses the passive
eleven times, which is how letters like that are written, and every one of those eleven sentences
hides somebody who decided.

### 1 · The passive

**1B  Underline the passive forms in 1A. What is the auxiliary in each?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Tense | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Present** | am / is / are + participle | Requests **are considered** monthly. |
| **Past** | was / were + participle | The decision **was made** in June. |
| **Present perfect** | have / has been + participle | A place **has been offered**. |
| **Future** | will be + participle | It **will be confirmed** after acceptance. |
| **Modal** | may / must be + participle | It **may be granted**. |

> **Grammar Note** — The passive is not a way of being vague; it is a way of putting the
> important thing first. *The window was broken* is the right sentence when the window matters
> more than who broke it. It becomes evasive only when the person is being hidden on purpose,
> and you cannot always tell which is happening.

### 3 · *be able to*

**3A  Study the chart.**

| Tense | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Present** | am / is / are able to | I'm **able to** start in September. |
| **Past** | was / were able to | She **was able to** defer. |
| **Present perfect** | have / has been able to | I **haven't been able to** reach them. |
| **Future** | will be able to | You'**ll be able to** apply again. |
| **After a modal** | might / should be able to | They **might be able to** help. |

> **Grammar Note** — *Can* has no infinitive and no past participle, which is why *be able to*
> exists. *I have been able to* is the only way to say that in the present perfect; *I have
> could* is not English and never has been.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Who a big decision belongs to varies enormously. In some families it is the individual's, and
consulting everybody looks like indecision. In others the decision belongs to the household, and
announcing it alone is the insult, not the decision itself. People get this wrong in both
directions when they move, and the damage is rarely about the decision.

**Discuss:** in your family, who would be told first, and who would expect to be consulted? Has
moving changed that?
