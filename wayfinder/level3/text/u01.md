### 2 · Read

> **Reader's Tip** — A document that gives you a number and a sentence is making two claims, not
> one. Read the sentence, then read the number, then ask whether the second supports the first.
> They often do not match, and it is rarely a lie — it is usually somebody rounding an idea.

%%FIG x01-f5 | A four-page college guide spread out: the cover with a title and a photograph space, a contents page, a page of numbered sections on terms and credits, and a page with a shaded box containing a statistic and its source line.%%

> ## So you are coming back to study
> *Northside College · Guide for returning and part-time students · Section 2*
>
> 1  You are not unusual. Across the wealthier countries, about **two adults in every five**
> 2  take part in some kind of education or training in a given year. Most adults are studying
> 3  something. What is unusual is how little any institution explains itself to the people
> 4  it has just admitted, and that is what this section is for.
>
> 5  **Terms and credits.** The year is divided into two semesters of fifteen weeks. Each module
> 6  you pass is worth credits. You need 120 credits for the year, and you must pass the
> 7  mandatory modules — you cannot replace them with optional ones you happen to find easier.
>
> 8  **Who decides what.** Your personal tutor is responsible for you, not for any one subject.
> 9  Your module leader marks your work. Only the course administrator can change what is on
> 10 your timetable, and only student services can change what is on your record.
>
> 11 **Extensions.** Apply *before* the deadline. A retrospective extension is possible but you
> 12 must show that you could not have applied in time, which is a higher bar than being busy.
> 13 Work handed in late without an extension is capped at the pass mark.
>
> 14 **The thing nobody tells you.** Roughly half of all adults did not take part in any learning
> 15 last year and did not want to. One in four says something was in the way. If you are reading
> 16 this, you are already past the hardest part, which is deciding.
>
> *Figures: OECD, Trends in Adult Learning, 2025.*

<sub>**mandatory** — required; you cannot choose not to · **retrospective** — applying backwards,
to something already past · **capped** — not allowed to rise above a limit</sub>

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some systems, asking "who actually decides this?" is normal and efficient. In others it sounds
like you are challenging the person in front of you. The safe version almost everywhere is to ask
about the *process*, not the person: not *are you the one who decides?* but *what happens to this
form after I hand it in?*

**Discuss:** which version is normal where you come from? Has that caused you a problem here?
