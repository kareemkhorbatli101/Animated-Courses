> **Reader's Tip** — A report that lists what it found is only half the document. The other half
> is the list of what it did not check, and that list is usually shorter, quieter and more
> important.

>
> 1  **Summary.** No record of insurance write-off. No record of theft. No outstanding finance
> 2  recorded. One previous keeper. Roadworthiness certificate current, expires in four months.
> 3  **One item flagged for attention: a mileage discrepancy.**
>
> 4  **Recorded readings.** 2021: 48,900. 2022: 61,400. 2023: 74,100. 2024: **69,800**.
> 5  2025: 83,200. Today's declared reading: 91,000. The 2024 reading is lower than the 2023
> 6  reading by 4,300. A reading cannot fall. Either one entry is a keying error, or the
> 7  instrument has been altered, and this report cannot tell you which.
>
> 8  **What this report does not cover.** We hold recorded data only. We cannot tell you the
> 9  mechanical condition of the vehicle, whether it has been repaired after an accident that was
> 10 not claimed on insurance, whether the service history is genuine, or whether the person
> 11 selling it is the person entitled to sell it. A clean report is not a recommendation. It is
> 12 the absence of certain records, which is not the same as the absence of certain events.

### 1 · Time clauses

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the opinion question.** %%AUD W4-09-06%%

> **Ahmad:** Before I pay anything, I want the discrepancy explained.
> **Seller:** It's a typing error. Happens all the time.
> **Ahmad:** It probably is. When my brother-in-law gets back on the ninth, he'll look at it, and
> after he's looked at it I'll give you an answer.
> **Seller:** I've got two other people interested.
> **Ahmad:** Then I hope one of them buys it before the ninth.

*Is "I've got two other people interested" ever true, and does it matter?*

**1B  Underline the four time clauses in 1A. Which one is about the past?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Word | Order of events | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **before** | second thing named first | **Before I pay**, I want it explained. |
| **after** | first thing named first | **After he's looked at it**, I'll answer. |
| **when** | one follows the other | **When he gets back**, he'll look. |
| **as soon as** | immediately after | I'll call **as soon as I know**. |
| **until** | up to that point | I'm not paying **until it's explained**. |

> **Grammar Note** — The order of the clauses in the sentence is not the order of the events.
> *Before I pay, I want it explained* means the explaining comes first. Learners who translate
> clause by clause get the sequence backwards, and in a negotiation that is expensive.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some places haggling is expected and a buyer who pays the asking price is thought careless or
rude. In others, naming a lower figure insults the seller and ends the conversation. Both sides
usually think their own way is simply normal. The safe opening almost everywhere is a question
rather than a number: *is there any movement on the price?*

**Discuss:** how is it where you come from? What happened the first time you got it wrong here?

> ### Real Lives — Ahmad
>
> I bought the van on a Saturday and I nearly did not. The paperwork had a flag on it — one

> not the answer, it is the four minutes.

> everything is a story; a text that leaves one thing open is an account, and accounts are what
> people trust.
