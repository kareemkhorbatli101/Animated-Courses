> *Portmere Advice Center · winter briefing for volunteers*
>
> 1  Every October we are asked how many people cannot heat their homes. There is a figure, it is
> 2  reliable, and it needs three sentences of explanation before it is any use to anybody on a
> 3  doorstep.
>
> 4  **The figure.** In 2024, **9.2 percent** of people in the EU said they were unable to keep
> 5  their home adequately warm. That is about forty-one million people. It is **down 1.4
> 6  percentage points** from 10.6 percent in 2023.
>
> 7  **The first sentence.** It fell. That is genuinely good news and it is smaller news than it
> 8  looks, because the 2023 figure was a peak driven by an energy price shock, and a fall from a

### 1 · The present unreal conditional

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the opinion question.** %%AUD W5-07-06%%

> **Priya:** If we had fifteen thousand, this would be a short meeting.
> **Lucia:** We haven't.
> **Priya:** No. If the roof weren't leaking, I'd fund the club without thinking about it.
> **Lucia:** And if the club closes?
> **Priya:** Then forty children have nowhere on a Saturday and the roof is fine.

*When a group cannot afford everything it needs, is it better to half-fund two things or fully
fund one?*

**1B  Underline the three unreal conditionals in 1A. What tense follows *if*, and what follows
the comma?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **If + past, would + base** | the standard pattern | **If we had** more, we **would do** both. |
| ***were* for all persons** | formal and standard | **If I were** you, I'd apply for the grant. |
| **Negative** | | **If the roof weren't** leaking, … |
| **Question** | | **What would you do if** you had it? |
| ***could* / *might*** | ability, possibility | We **could** fund it **if** the grant came. |

> **Grammar Note** — The past tense here is not about the past. It marks the sentence as unreal:
> *if I had the money* says that I do not. *Was* and *were* are both heard after *if I*, and
> *were* is the form that will never be marked wrong, in speech or in writing.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Who may raise money in a group is not a neutral question. In some places asking neighbors
directly for a contribution is ordinary and refusing is easy. In others it creates an obligation
that outlasts the money, and people avoid asking precisely because they know what it would cost
the person who said no. Committees that mix the two traditions have the same argument every year
and never discover that it is not about the money.

**Discuss:** where you come from, who asks, and what happens to somebody who says no?
