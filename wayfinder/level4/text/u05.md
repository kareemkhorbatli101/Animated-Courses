### 2 · Read

> **Reader's Tip** — A global health or safety figure always has a reference year, and it is
> usually several years before the publication date. Find it before you quote the number, because
> somebody will ask, and "it's the latest figure" and "it describes this year" are not the same
> claim.

%%FIG y05-f5 | A four-page safety briefing: a cover, a page with one very large figure and a breakdown beneath it, a page with a part-whole diagram, and a final page with a method and reference-year note and a source line.%%

> ## The number, and what it counts
> *Portmere Business Watch · safety briefing for members*
>
> 1  Members ask for one figure they can put on a poster. Here it is, with the three things you
> 2  have to say alongside it, because the figure on its own will be challenged by the first
> 3  person who reads it properly.
>
> 4  **The figure.** Around **2.93 million** workers die each year from work-related causes. Of
> 5  those, **2.6 million — eighty-nine per cent — are from occupational disease**, and **330,000
> 6  from accidents**. A further **395 million** workers sustain non-fatal injuries.
>
> 7  **The first thing to say.** Most of it is disease, not accidents. Nine deaths in ten are from
> 8  something that took years: dust, chemicals, noise, shift patterns. A poster showing a fall
> 9  from height is showing you eleven per cent of the problem.
>
> 10 **The second thing to say.** The reference year is **2019** and the estimate was published in
> 11 **2023**. It is the most recent global estimate that exists. It is not a figure for this year
> 12 and anybody who presents it as one is wrong, including us if we are careless.
>
> 13 **The third thing to say.** These are estimates built from national reporting that is
> 14 incomplete, and underreporting runs in one direction only. Nobody records a death that did
> 15 not happen. The true figure is therefore higher than the published figure, by an amount
> 16 nobody can state.
>
> 17 **Why this matters to a member with eleven staff.** Because the thing most likely to harm
> 18 your people is not the thing on the poster. It is the thing that has been raised, closed as
> 19 *no action required*, and raised again.
>
> *Figures: ILO estimates of work-related mortality, published 2023 for reference year 2019;
> ILOSTAT.*

### 1 · *have to*, *have got to*, *must*

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the opinion question.** %%AUD W5-05-06%%

> **Oskar:** You have to report it. It's in the policy.
> **Samira:** I've got to report it by Friday or it doesn't count.
> **Oskar:** And I must stop pretending I didn't see it in March.
> **Samira:** That one's different.
> **Oskar:** That one's mine.

*If a rule exists and nobody enforces it, whose obligation is it?*

**1B  Underline the three forms. Which one is the speaker imposing on himself?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Form | Where the obligation comes from | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **have to** | outside: a rule, a law, a system | You **have to** report it. |
| **have got to** | outside, spoken, often urgent | I'**ve got to** do it by Friday. |
| **must** | the speaker: own judgement or authority | I **must** stop pretending. |
| **don't have to** | no obligation — it is optional | You **don't have to** sign it. |
| **mustn't** | prohibition — it is forbidden | You **mustn't** move the load. |

> **Grammar Note** — *Don't have to* and *mustn't* are opposites, not variants. *You don't have
> to come* means it is your choice. *You mustn't come* means do not. Learners who treat them as
> the same sentence end up in exactly the wrong room.

**1D  Ask and answer with a partner. Three each.**

1. What do you have to do at work or in study that you think is pointless?
2. What have you got to do this week?
3. What must you do that nobody is making you do?

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some workplaces raising a hazard is routine and expected, and a manager who was not told would
be the one in trouble. In others, raising one marks you as a complainer and the cost falls on the

they become quiet, which looks the same from a distance and is not.

**Discuss:** what happens where you have worked to the person who reports something? Has that
changed how you behave here?
