# Wayfinder 4
%%SUB Communication for Study, Work and Life · CEFR B1+%%

## How to use this book

This is the fifth book in the Wayfinder series. By now you can narrate, compare, explain and
disagree. What this book adds is **distance**: the ability to report what somebody else said
without becoming them, to describe what should have happened instead of what did, and to say
what you would do if circumstances were other than they are.

**Each unit has sixteen pages and five lessons**, and each lesson takes about ninety minutes.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Thirty-four to forty new words, plus an academic set, with a team research task |
| **2 · Real-life Reading** | A 300–400 word document, True/False/Not Given, word study and a graph |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures, a *Language connection* box, and a team error hunt |
| **4 · Everyday Conversation** | Pronunciation, a long dialogue, a workplace box, and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A 170–250 word model, then your own, with an editing checklist |
| **Team Up & Review** | A team project with four roles, a class survey, and a hard case |

**The audio.** 🔊 marks a track. 194 tracks, numbered `W5-unit-track`.

## What is new at this level

**Academic language.** Every unit carries a boxed set of seven to twenty-five academic items —
*constitute*, *subsequent*, *whereas*, *nonetheless*, *on the grounds that* — banded and glossed.
These are not decoration. They are the words that appear in every policy document, every report
and every formal letter you will ever have to read, and they are almost entirely absent from
conversation, which is why nobody picks them up by living here.

**You will be asked to report what you read.** Six of the twelve units require you to take
something somebody said or wrote and pass it on accurately. That is a harder skill than it looks
and it is the one that gets people into trouble when they get it wrong.

**The sources are harder to use.** In Book 3 the numbers were clear. In this book one of them has
a base nobody states, one is six years old and is still the best figure that exists, and one is
repeated everywhere while nobody checks what it counts. The tasks ask you about all three. A
B1+ reader is not somebody who believes documents; it is somebody who can say precisely what a
document does and does not establish.

## The people

Four years have passed in Portmere. **Nadia Rahman** is in her second year of nursing and has
started saying *we* about the ward. **Tomás Alvarez** manages a shift and has discovered what that
costs. **Mei Chen** went. **Priya Raghavan** is on a committee and has strong views about
committees. **Lucia Pham** took her landlord to a tribunal and won, slowly. **Ahmad Rahim** has a
van and two drivers. **Oksana Koval**, **Linh Pham**, **Adeel Khan** and **Samira Haddad** are no
longer anybody's idea of new arrivals.

Everybody in this book is invented. So is Portmere, and so is every company and document in it,
except where a source is named — and where one is named, the figure is real.
