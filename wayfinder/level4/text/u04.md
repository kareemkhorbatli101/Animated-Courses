Tomás was asked to write a five-year plan for his appraisal and could not do it. He had moved
four times in six years, none of it planned, and all four moves looked deliberate in hindsight and
had not been. The form wanted a destination. What he had was a direction, arrived at by taking
whatever was in front of him and noticing afterwards which ones had been worth it.

### 2 · Read

> **Reader's Tip** — Whenever you meet an average, ask what it is an average *of*. An average
> across countries, an average across people and an average across jobs are three different
> numbers, and they are routinely printed as if they were one.

%%FIG y04-f5 | A four-page labor market note: a cover, a page of three findings with figures, a page carrying a country-ranked bar chart with a dashed average line, and a final page with a definitions box and a source line.%%

> ## How long anybody stays anywhere
> *Portmere Employment Partnership · labor market note 4*
>
> 1  Advisers are asked constantly whether it is bad to have moved several times. The honest
> 2  answer needs three figures and a definition, and the definition is the part that is always
> 3  left out.
>
> 4  **The headline.** Average labor-force tenure across thirty-three countries was about **ten
> 5  years** in 2023. That sounds like a settled world. It is an aggregate across every worker in
> 6  every sector, including people who have been in the same post for thirty years and are
> 7  pulling the figure up very hard.
>
> 8  **The movement underneath.** Roughly **one worker in five** changes their employment
> 9  situation in any given year. The rate of job change has risen in **24 of the 27 countries**
> 10 for which there are data. Both of those things are true at the same time as the ten-year
> 11 figure, and they describe the same labor market.
>
> 12 **Why both can be true.** Tenure is heavily skewed. A small number of very long stays raise
> 13 the average well above what a typical worker experiences, which is why the median is a more
> 14 honest measure and is quoted far less often. Where national figures use the median instead,
> 15 they come out at roughly a third of the aggregate.
>
> 16 **What this means for advice.** Four moves in six years is not unusual, and it is not
> 17 evidence of instability. What an employer is actually looking for is not a plan; it is whether
> 18 you can say why each move happened and what you took from it. Those are different questions,
> 19 and only one of them can be answered honestly in advance.
>
> *Figures: OECD, Retaining Talent at All Ages, and OECD labor market statistics.*

<sub>**tenure** — length of time in a job · **skewed** — pulled out of shape by extreme values ·
**median** — the middle value, half above and half below</sub>

**1B  Underline the past perfect forms in 1A. What happened first in each sentence?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| **Earlier of two past events** | When they advertised it, I **had** already **done** it. |
| **With *by* + time** | **By** March I **had led** 240 shifts. |
| **Continuous**, for a run of activity | I'**d been doing** it since June. |
| **Negative** | They **hadn't asked** anybody who worked there. |
| **Question** | **Had** you **applied** before? |

> **Grammar Note** — The past perfect is not for every past event. It is for the *earlier* of two,
> when the order is not already obvious. Once you have fixed the order, you can go back to the
> past simple: *I had finished the course, then I applied, got an interview and started in May.*
> One past perfect does the work; four in a row is a reader's nightmare.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

The five-year plan is a cultural artifact, not a universal. In some systems it is a reasonable
question and an unanswerable one is read as drift. In others, announcing a five-year plan at work
would be thought presumptuous, or unlucky, or simply none of the employer's business. People
moving between the two systems get marked down for a difference in manners and told it is a
difference in ambition.

**Discuss:** would this question be asked where you come from? What would a good answer sound like
there?
