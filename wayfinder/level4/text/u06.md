**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the opinion question.** %%AUD W5-06-06%%

**1B  Underline the three adjective clauses in 1A. What word begins each?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Relative | Used for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **who** | people | a colleague **who** is good at his job |
| **which** | things | figures **which** arrive late |
| **that** | people or things, defining only | the report **that** depends on them |
| **where** | places | the office **where** the figures are produced |
| **when** | times | the week **when** it was four hours late |

> **Grammar Note** — A **defining** clause tells you which one and takes no commas: *the colleague
> who produces the figures* — there are several colleagues and this identifies one. A
> **non-defining** clause adds information about something already identified and takes commas:
> *Priya, who writes the report, stayed until eleven*. In a non-defining clause you cannot use
> *that*.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Raising a problem about a colleague's work differs enormously by workplace. In some, going to a
manager without speaking to the person first is a betrayal, and the manager will send you back. In
others, speaking to the person first is overstepping, and you are expected to use the line. The
person who gets it wrong is not thought to have made a procedural mistake; they are thought to be
the kind of person who does that.

**Discuss:** which is expected where you work? Who would you speak to first, and what would happen
if you got it the other way round?

> Dilnoza has covered the gaps left by a colleague for seven months. He is a good nurse who is
> going through something he has told her about and has told nobody else. The gaps are small —

> allergy was not recorded at handover and it was caught by a pharmacist. Nothing happened. Her
