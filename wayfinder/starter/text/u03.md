### 1 · *It's* + time

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W1-03-05%%

> **Yusuf:** Excuse me. What time is it?
> **Mr Bah:** It's twenty-five past nine.
> **Yusuf:** Is the 14 late?
> **Mr Bah:** No, it isn't. I'm the 14. I'm early.
> **Yusuf:** Early? The timetable says twenty past.
> **Mr Bah:** The timetable says twenty past on Monday. Today is Sunday.

**Your opinion:** Mr Bah says *"Get on."* Why? Say what you think.

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline every time.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **It's** | nine **o'clock**. | 9:00 |
| **It's half past** | nine. | 9:30 |
| **It's** (a) **quarter past** | nine. | 9:15 |
| **It's** (a) **quarter to** | ten. | 9:45 |
| **It's** twenty **past** nine. | **It's** ten **to** ten. | 9:20 · 9:50 |

> **Grammar Note** — Use **past** for the first half hour, and **to** for the second half. After
> half past, say the **next** hour: 9:50 is *ten to ten*, not *ten to nine*.

### 💼 ON THE JOB

**Asking about your hours.** %%AUD W1-03-12%%

> **1** — **Yusuf:** Grace, what time do I start on Sunday?
> **Grace:** Ten at night. It's on the board.
> **Yusuf:** Ten. Thank you.
>
> **2** — **Yusuf:** Is the shift still eight hours?
> **Grace:** It is. Ten to six.
> **Yusuf:** Six in the morning. OK.
>
> **3** — **Yusuf:** Grace, there's a problem. The last Sunday bus is at twenty to eleven.
> **Grace:** Ah. Then come at half past nine and start early.
> **Yusuf:** Thank you.

**A  Practice all three in pairs.**

**B  Think about it.**

1. Yusuf reads the board **and** asks Grace. Is that a waste of her time? Why not?
2. In the third conversation Yusuf does not say *"I can't come."* He says what the problem is.
   What is the difference?
3. Your shift and your bus do not fit. What do you say to your manager? Say it out loud.

> **Need help?** There's a problem with … · The last bus is at … · Can I start early? ·
> Is that all right?

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some places, *nine o'clock* means nine o'clock and people arrive at five to. In other places it
means *about* nine, and arriving at ten past is normal and polite.

**Talk about it in groups.**

1. In your city, what time do people arrive for a class at nine? For dinner at eight?
2. Is being early ever rude?
3. Your class starts at nine. You arrive at ten past. What do you say? Does it depend on who is
   there?

> There is no right answer. But a shift at ten means ten.
