### 1 · *How much?* and plurals

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W1-05-05%%

> **Nadia:** Excuse me. How much are the apples?
> **Hana:** Two fifty a kilo. After twelve it's two.
> **Nadia:** And this box?
> **Hana:** Those are tomatoes. Three a kilo — no, wait. Two twenty now.
> **Nadia:** Two twenty? The label says three.
> **Hana:** The old label. Look underneath.
> **Nadia:** Ah. Two kilos, please.
> **Hana:** Two kilos of tomatoes. Four forty.

**Your opinion:** Hana corrects her own price, and the new price is lower. Why does that matter?

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline *this*, *that*, *these* and *those*.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | one | more than one |
|---|---|---|
| **here** | **this** apple | **these** apples |
| **there** | **that** box | **those** tomatoes |

> **Grammar Note** — **How much is** + one thing. **How much are** + more than one thing.
> *How much is this melon? How much are these apples?*

**1D  Practice with a partner. Point at something and ask.**

1. (one apple, here) → *How much is this apple?*
2. (many bananas, there) → ______________
3. (one box, there) → ______________
4. (many onions, here) → ______________

### 2 · Plurals: *-s*, *-es*, and the odd ones

**2A  Study the chart.**

| add **-s** | add **-es** after s, sh, ch, x | change the word |
|---|---|---|
| apple → apples | box → boxes | person → people |
| note → notes | brush → brushes | child → children |
| coin → coins | watch → watches | man → men |

> **Grammar Note** — After a **consonant + y**, change *y* to *ies*: *city → cities*.
> But after a **vowel + y**, just add *s*: *day → days*.

### 💼 ON THE JOB

**At the till.** %%AUD W1-05-12%%

> **1** — **Customer:** How much is that altogether?
> **Tomás:** Six eighty, please.
> **Customer:** Here's ten.
> **Tomás:** Ten. Three twenty change. Thank you.
>
> **2** — **Customer:** Sorry, the shelf said two fifty.
> **Tomás:** Let me check. … You're right. Two fifty. I'll change it.
>
> **3** — **Tomás:** Grace, the label on the tomatoes is wrong.
> **Grace:** The old one or the new one?
> **Tomás:** Both are on the box.
> **Grace:** Take the old one off. Give the customer the low price.

**A  Practice all three in pairs.**

**B  Think about it.**

1. Tomás says *"Let me check."* He does not say *"No, it isn't."* Why is that better?
2. Grace says *"Give the customer the low price."* Why would a shop do that?
3. You are charged too much and you are not sure. What do you say? Say it out loud.

> **Need help?** Sorry — the shelf said … · Can you check, please? · I think there's a mistake. ·
> Can I see the receipt?

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some markets the price on the label is the price, and asking for less is rude. In other markets
the first price is the beginning of a conversation, and paying it without asking is strange.

**Talk about it in groups.**

1. In your country, where can you ask for a lower price? Where can you not?
2. Is it different for food, clothes, a taxi, a phone?
3. You ask for a lower price here and the answer is no. What do you say next?

> No right answer — but in a supermarket here, the label is the price.
