### 1 · *in*, *on*, *at* with dates

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W1-04-05%%

> **Mrs Oyelaran:** Lucia, your form is here. When can you come?
> **Lucia:** On Thursday?
> **Mrs Oyelaran:** Thursday is the third. I'm not here on Thursday.
> **Lucia:** Friday, then. In the morning.
> **Mrs Oyelaran:** At nine?
> **Lucia:** At nine. Friday the fourth, at nine, in the morning.
> **Mrs Oyelaran:** Write it down, Lucia.
> **Lucia:** I'm writing it down.

**Your opinion:** Mrs Oyelaran says *"Write it down."* Is she being unkind? Say what you think.

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline *in*, *on* and *at*.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| **in** + month, year, part of day | **on** + day, date | **at** + clock time |
|---|---|---|
| in April | on Friday | at nine o'clock |
| in 2026 | on 4 April | at half past three |
| in the morning | on Friday morning | at night |

> **Grammar Note** — Big goes with **in**, small goes with **at**: *in 2026 → in April → on
> Friday → at nine*. The shorter the time, the smaller the word.

**1D  Practice with a partner. Say the sentence with *in*, *on* or *at*.**

1. My birthday is ______ June.
2. The appointment is ______ Thursday.
3. We start ______ half past eight.
4. She was born ______ 2001.
5. The office is closed ______ Sunday.
6. I study ______ the evening.

**1E  Write three true sentences about your year.** Use *in*, *on* and *at* once each.

### 2 · Say it clearly — /t/ and /θ/

**2A  Listen.** %%AUD W1-04-08%%

| /t/ | /θ/ |
|---|---|
| **t**ree | **th**ree |
| **t**in | **th**in |
| **t**ank | **th**ank |
| ten**t** | ten**th** |

**2B  Listen. Circle the word you hear.** %%AUD W1-04-09%%

1. tree / three  2. tin / thin  3. tank / thank  4. tent / tenth

**2C  Practice with a partner.** Say a date: *the third, the fourth, the fifth, the sixth, the
tenth*. Your partner writes the number.

> **Why this matters:** *tree* and *three* are not close in meaning, but *the tent* and *the tenth*
> both make sense in a sentence about a date. Get the /θ/ right and nobody has to guess.

### 3 · The conversation

**3A  Read and listen.** %%AUD W1-04-10%%

%%FIG u04-f9 | Lucia at the college office window. Mrs Oyelaran is on the other side with a diary open. A wall calendar hangs behind her. Lucia has her phone in her hand.%%

> **Lucia:** Good morning. I'd like an appointment, please.
> **Mrs Oyelaran:** Of course. When can you come?
> **Lucia:** On Thursday?
> **Mrs Oyelaran:** Thursday is full. Friday the fourth?
> **Lucia:** Friday is fine. What time?
> **Mrs Oyelaran:** At nine, or at a quarter past three.
> **Lucia:** At nine, please.
> **Mrs Oyelaran:** Friday the fourth, at nine. Please arrive ten minutes early.

**3B  Choose a or b.**

1. Lucia wants (a) an appointment (b) a form
2. Thursday is (a) free (b) full
3. She chooses (a) nine o'clock (b) a quarter past three
4. She must arrive (a) on time (b) ten minutes early

**3C  Think about the grammar.**

1. Mrs Oyelaran says *on Thursday* but *at nine*. Why the different words?
2. Find one question with *When* and one with *What time*.

### 💼 ON THE JOB

**Asking for a day off.** %%AUD W1-04-12%%

> **1** — **Tomás:** Grace, can I ask about the eleventh?
> **Grace:** The eleventh. What about it?
> **Tomás:** It's my daughter's birthday. Can I finish early?
>
> **2** — **Grace:** What time do you need to leave?
> **Tomás:** At four.
> **Grace:** Four is fine. Tell me a week before next time.
>
> **3** — **Grace:** Tomás, I've put it on the board. The eleventh, finish at four.
> **Tomás:** Thank you, Grace.

**A  Practice all three in pairs.**

> **Need help?** Can I ask about …? · It's my …'s birthday. · Can I finish early? ·
> I can work on … instead.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some places you ask for a day off weeks ahead, in writing. In others you ask the day before and
nobody minds. Some workplaces write the date as 11/04; others as 04/11.

**Talk about it in groups.**

1. In your country, how far ahead do you ask for a day off?
2. Which holidays does everybody get? Which do you have to ask for?
3. You ask for a day off and your manager says *"We'll see."* What does that mean? Is it yes?

> No right answer — but if you are not sure whether *"We'll see"* was a yes, ask again.
