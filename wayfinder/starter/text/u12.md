### 1 · *can* and *can't*

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W1-12-05%%

> **Grace:** So. What can you do?
> **Ahmad:** I can use computers. I can read technical drawings. In Herat I am an engineer.
> **Grace:** You *were* an engineer. Can you lift fifteen kilos?
> **Ahmad:** Yes, I can.
> **Grace:** Can you drive a forklift?
> **Ahmad:** No, I can't. But I can learn.
> **Grace:** Good answer. We train that. Can you start at six?
> **Ahmad:** I can start at five.

**Your opinion:** Ahmad says *"No, I can't. But I can learn."* Why is that better than just *no*?

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline every *can* and *can't*.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| I / you / he / she / we / they | **can** | lift it. |
| I / you / he / she / we / they | **can't** | drive. |
| **Can** you | **drive**? | Yes, I **can**. / No, I **can't**. |

> **Grammar Note** — *Can* never takes **-s**: *she can*, never *she cans*. And the verb after it
> is plain: *she can drive*, never *she can drives* or *she can to drive*.

### 💼 ON THE JOB

**The first week.** %%AUD W1-12-12%%

> **1** — **Grace:** Ahmad, can you read this label?
> **Ahmad:** Yes. Four boxes, aisle nine.
> **Grace:** Good. That's the job.
>
> **2** — **Ahmad:** Grace, I can't lift this one alone.
> **Grace:** Then don't. Ask Yusuf. Nobody lifts alone here.
>
> **3** — **Grace:** Ahmad, next month there's a forklift course. Do you want it?
> **Ahmad:** Yes. Can I ask one thing? Is my engineering useful here?
> **Grace:** Not yet. In two years, when we need a supervisor, it will be.

**A  Practice all three in pairs.**

**B  Think about it.**

1. Ahmad says *"I can't lift this one alone"* instead of trying. Is that weakness or sense?
2. Grace says *"Nobody lifts alone here."* Who is that rule protecting?
3. Grace's answer about his engineering is honest, not kind. Is honest better here? Say what you
   think.

> **Need help?** Can you show me? · I can't do that alone. · Can I ask one thing? ·
> I can learn that.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some countries you say clearly what you are good at, and not saying so means you cannot do it.
In others, saying so is boasting, and you wait to be asked.

**Talk about it in groups.**

1. In your country, do people say what they are good at in an interview?
2. Who taught you how to answer *"What are you good at?"* Did anybody?
3. Ahmad says *"No, I can't, but I can learn."* Is that modest or confident? Can it be both?

> No right answer — but here, in an interview, nobody will guess what you can do. You have to say
> it.
