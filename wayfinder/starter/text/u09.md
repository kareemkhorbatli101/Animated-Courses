**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W1-09-05%%

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline every *am / is / are + -ing*.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| I | **am wearing** | a coat. |
| He / She / It | **is raining** | now. |
| You / We / They | **are standing** | by the door. |
| **Are** you **working** today? | Yes, I am. / No, I'm not. | |

> **Grammar Note** — Use it for **now**: *It's raining.* Compare with the simple present, which is
> for **always**: *It rains in March.* Both are true; they say different things.

### 2 · Adjective + noun, and *It's* + weather

**2A  Study the chart.**

| | |
|---|---|
| a **blue** shirt · **black** trousers · a **big** coat | the adjective comes **before** the noun |
| a blue**s** shirt ✗ · blue shirt**s** ✓ | the adjective never takes **-s** |
| **It's** raining. · **It's** cold. · **It's** a cold day. | weather starts with *It's* |

> **Grammar Note** — English puts the adjective **before** the noun, and never changes it: *two
> big coats*, not *two bigs coats*. If your language puts it after, this will feel backwards for a
> while. That is normal.

### 3 · The conversation

**3A  Read and listen.** %%AUD W1-09-10%%

%%FIG u09-f9 | Tomás at the Portmere Workwear counter. An assistant holds up a shirt against him. The size chart is on the wall behind the counter. Tomás holds a receipt.%%

> **Tomás:** Excuse me. This shirt doesn't fit. It's too big.
> **Assistant:** No problem. What size is it?
> **Tomás:** Large. I think I'm a medium.
> **Assistant:** Let me measure. … A hundred and three. That's between the two.
> **Tomás:** So which one?
> **Assistant:** Take the large if you wear something underneath. The medium if you don't.
> **Tomás:** I'm wearing a jumper under it in winter.
> **Assistant:** Then keep the large. Do you have the receipt?

**3B  Choose a or b.**

**3C  Think about the grammar.**

### 💼 ON THE JOB

**Asking for the right kit.** %%AUD W1-09-12%%

> **1** — **Tomás:** Grace, this shirt is too big.
> **Grace:** What size do you need?
> **Tomás:** A medium, I think. I'm a hundred and three.
> **Grace:** Then you need a large. Take both and try them.
>
> **2** — **Grace:** Are your shoes all right for the floor?
> **Tomás:** They're fine, but they're not waterproof.
> **Grace:** The door is open all day in March. Ask for the waterproof ones.
>
> **3** — **Tomás:** Who do I ask?
> **Grace:** Me. I'm asking you now, so ask me back.

**A  Practice all three in pairs.**

**B  Think about it.**

1. Grace says *"Take both and try them."* Why is that better than guessing?
2. Tomás says his shoes are *fine, but not waterproof*. Why mention it if they are fine?
3. Something you were given at work does not fit or is not safe. What do you say? Say it out loud.

> **Need help?** This doesn't fit. · It's too big / small. · Do you have a …? ·
> Can I try both?

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some workplaces everybody wears the same thing and you are given it. In others there is a dress
code in words — *smart*, *tidy*, *no open shoes* — and you have to work out what that means.

**Talk about it in groups.**

1. In your country, what do people wear to an office? To a café? To a first day anywhere?
2. What does *smart* mean where you are from? Is it the same here?
3. You are not sure what to wear on your first day. What do you do?

> No right answer — but on a first day, slightly too smart is a smaller mistake than the other one.
