### 1 · Possessive *'s*

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W1-10-05%%

> **Priya:** Is your radiator still cold?
> **Nadia:** Since October.
> **Priya:** October! Did you write to him?
> **Nadia:** No. I told him at the door.
> **Priya:** That's not the same. Mr Caruso's rule is: write it down.
> **Nadia:** It felt rude.
> **Priya:** It isn't rude. It's his system. Mei's window took three days because she wrote. My
> tap took two weeks because I didn't.

**Your opinion:** Nadia thinks writing is rude. Priya thinks it is the system. Who is right?

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline every *'s* that shows who owns something.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| one person | Mei**'s** window · Nadia**'s** room | add **'s** |
| a name ending in s | Mr Rivas**'s** key | add **'s** |
| more than one | the student**s'** kitchen | add **'** after the s |
| a thing, not a person | the door **of** the kitchen | use **of** |

> **Grammar Note** — Careful: *it's* means *it is*. The possessive is *its*, with no apostrophe.
> This catches everybody, including people who grew up here.

**1D  Practice with a partner. Make it shorter.**

1. the room of Nadia → *Nadia's room*
2. the window of Mei → ______________
3. the rule of Mr Caruso → ______________
4. the kitchen of the students → ______________
5. the number of my housemate → ______________

> **Why this matters:** English hears the strong syllable first. If you put it in the wrong place,
> people do not hear a wrong word — they hear no word at all.

### 3 · The conversation

**3A  Read and listen.** %%AUD W1-10-10%%

%%FIG u10-f9 | Nadia at Mr Caruso's door on the ground floor. He stands in the doorway with reading glasses pushed up and a pen in his hand. Nadia holds a folded note.%%

> **Nadia:** Mr Caruso? I have a note about my radiator.
> **Mr Caruso:** Good. Is the date on it?
> **Nadia:** Yes. And my room number.
> **Mr Caruso:** Room 4B. Since when?
> **Nadia:** Since October.
> **Mr Caruso:** October? Why didn't you write in October?
> **Nadia:** I told you at the door.
> **Mr Caruso:** Telling me is not writing to me. Now it is written, so now it starts. Two working
> days.

**3B  Choose a or b.**

1. Nadia brings (a) a note (b) a phone number
2. The radiator has been cold since (a) October (b) December
3. Mr Caruso says telling him at the door (a) is the same as writing (b) is not the same
4. The repair will take (a) 2 working days (b) 14 working days

**3C  Think about the grammar.**

1. Mr Caruso says *"Now it is written, so now it starts."* What starts?
2. Find one question with *Why* and one with *Since when*.

### 💼 ON THE JOB

**Reporting something broken at work.** %%AUD W1-10-12%%

> **1** — **Yusuf:** Grace, the light above aisle six doesn't work.
> **Grace:** Since when?
> **Yusuf:** Last night.
> **Grace:** Write it in the book. I can't order a part without a date.
>
> **2** — **Grace:** Is it dark enough to be a problem?
> **Yusuf:** For reading labels, yes.
> **Grace:** Then it's urgent. I'll mark it.
>
> **3** — **Yusuf:** I wrote it in the book.
> **Grace:** Good. Now it exists.

**A  Practice all three in pairs.**

**B  Think about it.**

1. Grace says *"I can't order a part without a date."* Why does she need the date and not just the
   problem?
2. Yusuf says it is a problem *for reading labels*. Why is that better than saying *"it's dark"*?
3. Something is broken at your work or college. What do you say, and who do you tell? Say it out
   loud.

> **Need help?** There's a problem with … · It doesn't work. · It's been like that since … ·
> Where do I write it down?

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some places you call your landlord and they come the same day, and writing a letter would seem
cold. In others nothing exists until it is in writing, and a phone call is forgotten by Thursday.

**Talk about it in groups.**

1. In your country, how do you tell a landlord something is broken?
2. Is writing it down unfriendly, or is it just safe?
3. Somebody promises to fix something and does not. What do you do next?

> No right answer — but here, writing it down is not rude. It is the only thing that starts a
> clock.
