### 1 · *my, your, his, her, our, their*

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W1-06-05%%

> **Mrs Oyelaran:** Nadia, your form has an empty box.
> **Nadia:** Which one?
> **Mrs Oyelaran:** Your emergency contact. Who is it?
> **Nadia:** My mother. But she lives in Tabriz.
> **Mrs Oyelaran:** And her English?
> **Nadia:** Not good. My housemate is here. Her name is Priya.
> **Mrs Oyelaran:** Then write Priya. Does she know?
> **Nadia:** Not yet. I ask her tonight.

**Your opinion:** Mrs Oyelaran asks *"Does she know?"* Why is that the important question?

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline *my*, *your* and *her*.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| I → **my** | we → **our** | *My* brother lives here. |
| you → **your** | you → **your** | *Your* form is here. |
| he → **his** · she → **her** · it → **its** | they → **their** | *Her* name is Priya. |

> **Grammar Note** — The word does **not** change for the thing after it: *her brother*, *her
> sister*, *her brothers*. It changes for the **owner**, not for the thing.

**1D  Practice with a partner. Change the sentence.**

1. I have a sister. → *My sister lives in Lima.*
2. He has a brother. → ______________
3. They have a son. → ______________
4. We have a teacher. → ______________
5. She has a phone. → ______________

### 3 · The conversation

**3A  Read and listen.** %%AUD W1-06-10%%

%%FIG u06-f9 | Nadia and Priya at the kitchen table in Apartment 4B. The form is between them. Priya holds a pen. Two mugs sit on the table.%%

> **Nadia:** Priya, can I ask you something?
> **Priya:** Of course.
> **Nadia:** The college wants an emergency contact. Can I write your name?
> **Priya:** My name? Yes. What does it mean?
> **Nadia:** They call you if something happens to me at the college.
> **Priya:** Then write my mobile, not the apartment. I'm at work in the day.
> **Nadia:** What's the best time to call you?
> **Priya:** Any time. It's an emergency, Nadia.

**3B  Choose a or b.**

4. Priya says call (a) in the evening (b) any time

**3C  Think about the grammar.**

2. Find two questions. Which one starts with *What*?

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some places *family* means your parents, your brothers and your sisters. In other places it
means forty people, and your mother's cousin is as close as your own brother. Forms usually assume
the small version.

**Talk about it in groups.**

1. In your language, is there one word for *cousin*, or several?
2. Who in your family would you call first in an emergency? Is that person on any form?
3. A form says *"next of kin"*. Who is that, for you?

> No right answer. But the person on the form should be the person you would really call.
