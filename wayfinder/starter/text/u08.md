Oskar Lindqvist has been the pharmacist on Fenner Street for nine years. He says the same sentence
most days: *"Read the label, and if you are not sure, come back."* Most people do not read the
label. Some of them come back.

>
> **Adults:** one 5 ml spoon, **twice a day**, after food.

### 1 · *have* and *has*

**1A  Listen to the conversation.** %%AUD W1-08-05%%

> **Oskar:** Good morning. What's the matter?
> **Mei:** I have a sore throat. I have a headache too.
> **Oskar:** Do you have a temperature?
> **Mei:** I don't know. I don't have a thermometer.
> **Oskar:** Then we have one here. … Thirty-seven eight. That's a little high.
> **Mei:** Is it bad?
> **Oskar:** No. But your friend has the same thing, yes? Half the college has it.
> **Mei:** How do you know?
> **Oskar:** Because I sell this every ten minutes.

**Your opinion:** Oskar checks her temperature before he sells her anything. Why does that matter?

**1B  Look at the conversation again. Underline *have*, *has* and *don't have*.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| I / you / we / they | **have** / **don't have** | a headache. |
| he / she / it | **has** / **doesn't have** | a temperature. |
| **Do** you | **have** | a thermometer? |
| **Does** she | **have** | a prescription? |

> **Grammar Note** — After *do* and *does*, always **have**, never *has*: *Does she have…?*
> never *Does she has…?*

### 3 · The conversation

**3A  Read and listen.** %%AUD W1-08-10%%

%%FIG u08-f9 | Mei at the pharmacy counter. Oskar holds up a box and points at the dose line with a pen. Mei holds her phone with the translation on the screen.%%

> **Oskar:** Here you are. Twice a day, after food.
> **Mei:** Twice? My phone says three times.
> **Oskar:** Your phone is translating the wrong line. Look — this line is the children's dose.
> **Mei:** Ah. So twice.
> **Oskar:** Twice. And not more than two spoons in a day.
> **Mei:** How many days?
> **Oskar:** Three. If your throat is the same on Thursday, see a doctor.
> **Mei:** Thank you. I write it down.

**3B  Choose a or b.**

1. The correct dose is (a) twice a day (b) three times a day
2. The phone translated (a) the adult line (b) the children's line
3. The most in one day is (a) two spoons (b) four spoons
4. If she is the same on Thursday, she should (a) take more (b) see a doctor

**3C  Think about the grammar.**

1. Oskar says *"not more than two spoons in a day."* Which words tell you the limit?
2. Find two frequency expressions in the conversation.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

In some countries you can buy many medicines over the counter and the pharmacist advises you. In
others almost everything needs a doctor's prescription, and the pharmacist will not discuss it.

**Talk about it in groups.**

1. In your country, what can you buy without a doctor?
2. Who do you ask first about a small health problem — a pharmacist, a doctor, your family, the
   internet?
3. Here, the pharmacist is free and you do not need an appointment. Does that change what you
   would do?

> No right answer — but free advice from a trained person is worth asking for.
