# Starter · Unit 1 · Teacher notes

**Unit at a glance.** Six lessons across 14 pages. 16 audio tracks. Grammar: plural nouns, subject
pronouns with *be*, *a / an*. Pronunciation: /ɪ/ vs /e/. The unit's spine is a single real task —
**give your name, have it written down, and check it was written down right** — which is the first
thing that happens to anybody anywhere and the first thing that goes wrong.

**Every lesson is staged:** Warm-up and review (books closed) → Introduction → Presentation →
Guided practice → Communicative practice and application → Evaluation.

**Three objectives per lesson,** differing by verb and not by topic. Pre-level learners *identify*
and *complete*; on-level learners *use* and *produce*; higher-level learners *extend* and *explain*.
Fixed tactics all book: extra time and a reduced item set for pre-level; extra original sentences
for higher-level learners who finish early; oral answers for pre-level where on-level learners write.

**A third of every lesson is talk.** The communicative-practice stage below is never cut for time.
If something has to go, cut an item from guided practice.

## Lesson 1 · Vocabulary — 60 min

| Stage | Min | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up, books closed | 5 | Say your own name. Write it on the board. Spell it aloud. Ask three students to do the same. Say nothing about the unit yet. |
| Introduction | 5 | Open to p. 4. Read the three UNIT GOALS aloud. Ask: *which of these three can you do already?* Hands. |
| Presentation | 10 | 1A, then 1B and 1C with the audio. Hold up the eight cards. Do not translate; point. |
| Guided practice | 15 | 1D, then 2A–2C. Monitor spelling of *address* and *email*. |
| Communicative practice | 20 | 1E (the spell-back is the task, not the names), 2D, then AUG-a. |
| Evaluation | 5 | CHECK YOURSELF, then the say-it step. |

**Objectives.** *Pre-level:* identify the eight personal-information words and match the nine
classroom words to pictures. *On-level:* use them to ask and answer, and spell a name back for
confirmation. *Higher-level:* explain why the machine-filled form in AUG-a is wrong and what the
person should say.

**The room should sound like** twenty people spelling names badly and correcting each other. That is
the lesson working.

**AUG-a is the heart of this lesson.** Do not rush it. The learners have all had a machine get their
name wrong. Let one of them tell that story — in any language if they must, then help them tell it
again in English. The point to land: *a machine filled this in, and the only person who can catch the
mistake is the person whose name it is.*

**Watch for:** learners who answer *"My name is Rahman Nadia"* because that is the order at home.
That is not an error to correct — it is Lesson 4's Culture Check arriving early. Note it and use it.

## Lesson 2 · Real-life Reading — 60 min

| Stage | Min | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up, books closed | 5 | *What did you do on your first day here?* Two volunteers. |
| Introduction | 5 | 1A, then the 1B poll. Take the count on the board — it is data you use again in Review. |
| Presentation | 10 | Reader's Tip: title and numbers first. Have them read **only** the title and the three numbers, then guess the content. Then read. |
| Guided practice | 15 | 2A audio, 3A, 3B. |
| Communicative practice | 20 | 4A partner Q&A from the form, then 4B. Brief TAKE IT OUTSIDE properly — show them a sign outside the window if you can. |
| Evaluation | 5 | Check 3B as a class. Ask who changed their poll answer after reading. |

**Objectives.** *Pre-level:* find four facts in the text and the empty field on the form.
*On-level:* answer the inference question in 4B with one concrete example. *Higher-level:* describe
what happens over the next month if the wrong name goes on the card.

**The form in 4A is the unit's best five minutes.** Put one learner in the chair and make the other
ask. The field that is blank matters: *emergency contact* is the one newcomers leave empty because
they do not yet know anyone, and that is worth two minutes of honest conversation.

**TAKE IT OUTSIDE needs a real brief.** Say exactly: one sign, any sign, copy the name exactly,
bring it Thursday. Learners who forget bring a name from their phone instead — accept it.

## Lesson 3 · Grammar — 75 min

| Stage | Min | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up, books closed | 5 | REMEMBER? aloud in pairs, then write. |
| Introduction | 5 | 1A audio twice. Take the opinion question seriously — thirty seconds, two answers. |
| Presentation | 15 | 1B underlining, then 1C chart. Then 2A chart with 2B audio. |
| Guided practice | 20 | 1D, 1E, 2C. |
| Communicative practice | 25 | 3A at the picture, 3B written, then 4A mingle and 4B report. |
| Evaluation | 5 | CHECK YOURSELF. |

**Objectives.** *Pre-level:* identify *am / is / are* in a text and complete the short forms.
*On-level:* use them in original sentences and ask three classmates for their information.
*Higher-level:* explain why *"Yes, I'm"* is wrong and write four sentences about the picture.

**1B has a deliberately soft answer.** The count is 8, 9 or 10 depending on whether short forms are
counted separately. **Do not give a number — ask which ones they counted.** The disagreement is the
learning. This is the first time the course asks learners to defend an answer, and it is on purpose.

**4A is a mingle, not a pair task.** Move the furniture. If you cannot, have half the class stand.

## Lesson 4 · Everyday Conversation — 75 min

| Stage | Min | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up, books closed | 5 | Spell a learner's name wrong on the board. Wait. Let them correct you. |
| Introduction | 10 | 1A from the class list, then 1B audio. |
| Presentation | 15 | 2A–2C pronunciation, then 3A dialogue with audio, 3B, 3C and the chart. |
| Guided practice | 10 | 4A at the sign-in screen. |
| Communicative practice | 30 | ON THE JOB in threes, CULTURE CHECK in groups, then 5A and 5B. |
| Evaluation | 5 | CHECK YOURSELF role-play. |

**Objectives.** *Pre-level:* hear /ɪ/ and /e/ apart and complete the gap-dialogue. *On-level:*
perform the front-desk conversation including the spell-back and the check. *Higher-level:* correct a
mis-heard name politely, and answer the Culture Check questions with a comparison.

**Note the order.** In odd-numbered units ON THE JOB comes **before** the gap-dialogue, so learners
meet the workplace version first and the gap-dialogue consolidates both. Even-numbered units reverse
it. This is deliberate; do not "fix" it.

**CULTURE CHECK has no right answer and the page says so.** Your job is to stay out of it. If one
learner says their teacher calling them by their family name feels cold, and another says it feels
respectful, you have the whole lesson. Do not resolve it.

**The CHECK role-play fails if Card B does not correct Card A.** Say so before they start. Politely
correcting someone more senior than you is the hardest thing in this unit and the most useful.

## Lesson 5 · Writing — 60 min

| Stage | Min | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up, books closed | 5 | *Spell your last name to your partner. Partner writes it. Check.* |
| Introduction | 5 | 1A badge, 1B picture story with audio. |
| Presentation | 10 | 2A model text and Writer's Tip. Point at every capital letter. |
| Guided practice | 15 | 2B, then 2C and the agree-with-a-partner step. |
| Communicative practice | 20 | 3A form, 3B role-play, then 4A writing and 4B peer check. |
| Evaluation | 5 | CHECK YOURSELF, said aloud. |

**Objectives.** *Pre-level:* complete the frame in 4A with their own information. *On-level:* write
the ten sentences and check a partner's against the three points. *Higher-level:* write without the
frame, and add two sentences about why they are here.

**2C is the first disagreement task in the course.** They listen, they differ, they argue, they
listen again, they agree. Let them be wrong for a minute before the third listening.

**Ahmad's text in 2C does real work.** *"In my country I am an engineer. Here I am a student again."*
Some of your learners are living that sentence. Do not gloss it and do not make a lesson of it —
just let it be in the book, read in an ordinary voice, like everything else.

## Team Up & Review — 60 min

| Stage | Min | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | 5 | Collect TAKE IT OUTSIDE signs. |
| A–C | 15 | Scene discussion, team summary, report. |
| D | 15 | The class list, four named roles. Rotate roles if you run it twice. |
| E–F | 15 | The country survey and the bar chart. |
| Case | 8 | THINK IT THROUGH in groups. |
| My Progress | 2 | Individually, in silence. |

**Objectives.** *Pre-level:* answer the four scene questions and contribute one line to the team
summary. *On-level:* take a named project role and report. *Higher-level:* chair the team, and argue
a case solution with a reason.

**Item F3 is the real item.** The country totals must add up to the number of students in the room.
When they do not — and they often do not — someone has been missed or double-counted. Make them find
who. That is numeracy, data literacy and a register check all at once, and it costs three minutes.

**THINK IT THROUGH has no solution in the book and must not get one from you.** If a group asks what
the right answer is, ask what they would do. Blanca's problem is not hard; being shy on your third
question of the week is. Groups who say *"just go and ask again"* are right, and should be asked why
Blanca has not.
