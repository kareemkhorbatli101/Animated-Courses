### 5 · Word study — non-defining relative clauses

**5A  Study the chart.**

%%FIG q07-f6 | A chart contrasting defining and non-defining relative clauses: two pairs of sentences, one with no commas and one with commas, with arrows showing that the commas turn essential information into extra information, and a note that that cannot be used in a non-defining clause.%%

| Type | Commas | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **defining** | no commas | The colleague **who handles customs** is leaving. |
| **non-defining** | commas | Ines, **who handles customs**, is leaving. |
| **non-defining, thing** | commas + *which* | The customs work, **which was absorbed in 2022**, has never been graded. |
| **whose** | either | Ines, **whose job this is**, wrote half the description. |
| **not *that*** | — | *Ines, ~~that~~ who handles customs, is leaving.* |

> **Grammar Note** — The commas change the meaning, not just the punctuation. *The coordinator who
> speaks Korean is leaving* implies there is more than one coordinator. *The coordinator, who
> speaks Korean, is leaving* says there is one, and adds a fact about her. In a job description or
> a reference, that difference matters.

**5B  Add commas where they are needed, or write *no commas*.**

1. Ines who has done the job for three years is leaving.
2. The person who handles customs must be trained before April.
3. The customs rules which changed last year affect one country only.
4. My manager whose name is on the offer is away this week.

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **person, who …,** | Ines, **who** trained both of them, is leaving. |
| **thing, which …,** | The customs work, **which** nobody graded, is 45% of the job. |
| **possession, whose …,** | Ines, **whose** notes these are, wrote them in 2022. |

| **place, where …,** | Ostberg, **where** all three borders are handled, has one specialist. |

> **Grammar Note** — *Which* can refer to the whole previous clause: *nobody replaced him, which is
> how she took it on*. This is extremely useful in reports and extremely easy to overuse; two in a
> paragraph is plenty.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Hiring is one of the most regulated and most culturally variable things a manager does. What may
legally be asked at interview differs sharply between countries — about age, family, health,
nationality, previous salary — and a question that is routine in one place is unlawful in another.
Notice periods range from days to six months. References mean different things: in some countries a
written reference is a factual statement of dates only, and in others a detailed personal
assessment is expected and its absence is read as a warning. Pay transparency also differs: in some
markets advertising a salary band is required, and in others discussing pay with a colleague is
treated as a disciplinary matter.

**Discuss:** Where you work, what may you ask a candidate? What does a reference contain? **And
find out:** what does the law where you are require about notice and about pay transparency?
