### 5 · Word study — articles

**5A  Study the chart.**

%%FIG q11-f6 | A chart of article use in four rows: a or an for one of many and for jobs, the for something already identified or unique, no article for plurals in general and for uncountable nouns, and no article with most proper names, each row with a right and a wrong example.%%

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| **a / an** — one of many; a job | We visited **a** supplier. She is **an** engineer. |
| **the** — already known, or the only one | **The** supplier we visited is in Porto. **The** plant manager met us. |
| **no article** — plurals in general | **Operators** know more than **managers** think. |
| **no article** — uncountable in general | **Safety** is the first item. **Travel** is expensive. |
| **the** + uncountable, specified | **The safety** of this line is good. |
| **no article** — most place names | We flew to **Porto**. *(but* **the** *Netherlands,* **the** *UK)* |

> **Grammar Note** — The hardest article decision in business English is the second mention.
> *We visited **a** supplier yesterday. **The** supplier has two lines.* First mention *a*, after
> that *the*. If you find yourself saying *the* before anybody knows what you are referring to, your
> listener is quietly lost.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Business hospitality is where the most expensive misunderstandings happen, because the rules are
invisible and nobody writes them down. Whether a meal is part of the work or separate from it
varies enormously: in many business cultures declining a lunch or dinner is close to declining the
relationship, and the real conversation happens there and nowhere else. Punctuality means different
things — early in some places, exactly on time in others, and a flexible window elsewhere, where
arriving precisely on time for a social event is odd. Business cards carry weight in some countries
and none in others. Gifts range from expected to prohibited. Photography is restricted in far more
places than visitors assume. And in every culture, asking your host what you should have done
differently is rare — and almost always answered.

**Discuss:** In your country, is a work lunch part of the work? What would a visitor get wrong?
**And find out:** what does your own company's policy say about gifts?
