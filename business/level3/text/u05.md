**1C  Study the chart.**

| Direct | Reported | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| "They **are** missing." | She said they **were** missing. | present → past |

> **Grammar Note** — You do not have to shift the tense if the thing is still true: *she says the

> describing the situation.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

What a customer expects from a complaint call differs widely. In some countries an explicit apology
is required before anything else and its absence is the complaint; in others an apology without a
remedy is an insult and the first sentence should be about the fix. Directness about a colleague's
error also varies: saying "nobody called you, and I'm not going to defend it" is honest in some
workplaces and a betrayal of the team in others, where the expected phrase protects the company and
the customer understands the code. In some cultures the customer expects to be given a named person
who owns the problem; in others a case number is the proper answer and asking for a name is
intrusive.

**Discuss:** Where you work, what must you say first on a complaint call? Would you name a
colleague's mistake to a customer?

> ### Case 44118 (44207 merged) — summary and what changes
> *S. Reyes · 11 March · copied to the customer*
>
> **What was reported.** On 2 March the customer said the pallet had arrived without the two seal
> cartons. On 4 March she was told that the order showed as delivered. On 6 March a second case,
> 44207, was opened for the same pallet and was not linked to the first. On 8 March she was told
> somebody would call her on Thursday. Nobody did. On 10 March she called for the fifth time.
>
> **Root cause.** The seal cartons were listed under the same pallet label as the main goods. The
> driver scanned the label once, so the system recorded everything on it as delivered. **This will
> happen again on any order where two items share a label**, and it is not a driver error.
>
> **What we found.** The cartons were in bay 4, behind the returns cage. They left this morning and
> arrive tomorrow.
>
> **What the customer asked for.** Not compensation. She asked to be told the same story by
> everybody she speaks to.
>
> **What changes.** 1. Cases are merged, not duplicated: I have asked for the duplicate-detection
> rule to be switched on, which takes a week. 2. Items under a shared label are scanned separately
> from Monday. 3. A promised callback now creates a task with an owner; if it is missed, it
> escalates automatically at the end of that day.
>
> **What I am not promising.** Point 3 will not stop callbacks being missed. It will stop them
> being missed silently.

> **Writer's Tip** — Write the case summary in reported speech and dated order, and the document
> becomes useful to three different people: the customer, who sees they were believed; the next
> agent, who sees the whole story; and whoever has to fix the system, who can see the exact
> sentence where it broke.
