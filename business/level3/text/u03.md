### 5 · Word study — quantifiers and approximation

**5A  Study the chart.**

%%FIG q03-f6 | A chart of approximation language arranged on a number line around the figure 3.7 million: just under, about, around, roughly, some, more than, just over, well over, each positioned where it applies, with a note that about and roughly are interchangeable and that just under and nearly are not the same as about.%%

| Expression | Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **about / around / roughly** | near the figure, either side | **about** 3.7 million |
| **just under / nearly** | a little less | **just under** four million |
| **just over / more than** | a little more | **just over** 600,000 |
| **well over** | a lot more | **well over** a million |
| **one in ten / seven in ten** | a proportion, said simply | **seven in ten** applications |
| **a percentage point** | the gap between two percentages | up 6.5 **percentage points** |

> **Grammar Note** — *Per cent* and *percentage point* are not the same. If a share goes from 13.5
> per cent to 20 per cent, that is a rise of **6.5 percentage points** and also a rise of about
> **48 per cent**. Both are true, one sounds enormous, and choosing between them without saying
> which you mean is the commonest dishonest thing done with numbers at work.

> **Grammar Note** — *Fewer* goes with things you count and *less* with things you measure: *fewer

**2A  Study the chart.**

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Expectations about presenting differ sharply. In some business cultures a short talk with one
figure reads as confident and senior; in others it reads as unprepared, and the full data is
expected to be shown as a mark of respect for the audience's judgement. In some places interrupting
a presenter with a question is normal and welcome; in others all questions wait until the end, and
an early question suggests the talk is going badly. There are differences in modesty too: in some
cultures you state your result plainly, and in others you present it as the team's and let somebody
else name you. And the slide itself carries meaning — a dense slide says *I have done the work* in
many organizations, however hard it is to read.

**Discuss:** Where you work, is a simple slide a strength or a worry? When may somebody interrupt?

> *C. Obi · 9 March*
>
> **The number.** **Twenty-two per cent** of our orders take eight working days or more to

>
> **What it is compared with.** Our published lead time is five days, and the median is 4.6, which

>
> **Where it comes from.** Our own despatch records for 1,841 orders between 1 August and 31

>

> days of somebody's time and which I would like to ask for.
>

> speed is the target. Those are different projects.
>
> **Appendix** (attached, not presented): lead time by product group, by carrier, by month; the
> full distribution; the eleven orders over twenty days.

> **Writer's Tip** — Every number you present should be followed, somewhere, by the sentence
