# Starting Point · Three Habits
%%SUB Before Unit 1: the three things that make a B1 speaker sound like a professional%%
%%GOALS
Review the tenses, modals and passives you already have
Say a number with its unit, its date and its source
Say what you do not know, in one sentence, without apologizing
%%

You can already do this. What follows is not new grammar. It is three habits that decide whether
people believe you.

### 1 · What you already have

**1A  Work with a partner. Say one sentence of your own in each row, out loud.**
%%AUD CL3-00-01%%

%%FIG q00-f1 | A one-page review sheet with six boxes, each headed with a structure: present perfect, present perfect continuous, past simple with a time, the passive, must and have to, and the first conditional. Each box holds one example sentence from a working day.%%

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| **Present perfect** | I'**ve sent** the revised quote. |
| **Present perfect continuous** | We'**ve been waiting** for their drawing **since** Tuesday. |
| **Past simple + time** | The batch **arrived** on the ninth. |
| **The passive** | The gauge **is calibrated** every six months. |
| ***must* / *have to*** | We **have to** keep the record for five years. |
| **First conditional** | If they pay late, we **will** be on the overdraft. |

**1B  Ask and answer with a partner.** One question in each structure. Then change roles.

### 2 · Habit one — a number is never alone

**2A  Listen and repeat.** %%AUD CL3-00-02%%

> **Weak:** *Patent applications are up.*
> **Better:** *Patent applications are up about five per cent.*
> **Professional:** *Patent applications were up 4.9 per cent in 2024 — that's WIPO, published
> last November.*

A number at work carries four things: **the figure, the unit, the date, and where it came from.**
Leave one out and somebody has to ask. Leave two out and nobody believes it.

**2B  Fix these. Say each one aloud with all four parts.**

1. Sales are better.
2. We're faster than them.
3. Most customers pay late.
4. The market is growing.

### 3 · Habit two — say what you do not know

**3A  Listen and read.** %%AUD CL3-00-03%%

%%FIG q00-f2 | A card taped inside a notebook with five sentences printed on it: I don't know, and I'll find out by Thursday; two sources disagree and I've used the lower one; that figure is from 2023; I saw it, but I wouldn't call it a test; and I can't answer that in this meeting.%%

> **Chiamaka:** What's the lead time?
> **Anders:** I don't know. I'll find out and tell you by four.
> **Chiamaka:** Thank you.

Three sentences. Notice what Anders does **not** do: he does not guess, he does not apologize for
three lines, and he gives a time.

**3B  Practice with your partner.** Ask each other four questions about your own work. Answer two
of them with "I don't know, and I'll find out by ______".

**3C  Talk about it.** Where you work, is it safe to say *I don't know*? What happens to people who
guess instead?
