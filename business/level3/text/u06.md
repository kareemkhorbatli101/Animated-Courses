### 5 · Word study — *by*, *until*, *within*

**5A  Study the chart.**

%%FIG q06-f6 | A chart of four time prepositions on a timeline: by pointing at a single deadline point, until showing a shaded stretch ending at a point, within showing a bracketed stretch from now, and in showing a gap then a point, with an example under each.%%

| Word | Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **by** | at or before this point | I'll send it **by** 16:00 Ostberg time. |
| **until** | up to this point, then stop | The window is open **until** 15:00. |
| **within** | inside this length of time | **Within** two hours of your message. |
| **in** | after this length of time | I'll call **in** twenty minutes. |
| **at / on / in** | clock time / day / month | **at** 13:30 · **on** Thursday · **in** March |
| **no later than** | a firm *by* | **No later than** 09:00 CT Friday. |

> **Grammar Note** — *By Friday* and *on Friday* are different promises, and *until Friday* is a
> third thing. The commonest and most expensive mistake in cross-border work is *end of day*, which
> is not a time at all: for a four-zone project it names four different moments, two of which are
> in the past.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Time is not experienced the same way everywhere, and across zones the differences compound. In some
working cultures a meeting outside office hours is normal and refusing one would be remarkable; in
others contacting a colleague after work is close to a breach of contract, and in several countries
it is literally regulated. Expectations about reply speed differ: an overnight silence reads as
rudeness in one place and as a healthy boundary in another. Public holidays are the quiet
troublemaker, because they feel universal from the inside: a go-live scheduled on a day that is a
national holiday somewhere else is one of the commonest and most avoidable failures in distributed
work. And who takes the bad hour is itself a status question — in many organizations it is always
the same time zone, and nobody has ever said so out loud.

**Discuss:** Where you work, who takes the inconvenient hour? Is it ever discussed?

**Read your draft aloud to your partner first.** Hearing it is the quickest way to find the sentence
that does not work. Then check your own against the list, and swap.
