### 5 · Word study — *used to* and *would*

**5A  Study the chart.**

%%FIG q10-f6 | A chart of past habit forms: used to with a note that it covers states and repeated actions, would with a note that it covers repeated actions only, and the past simple with a time expression, each with an example and a cross-marked wrong version.%%

| Form | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **used to + verb** | a past habit or state, now finished | We **used to** keep the log on paper. |
| **would + verb** | a repeated past action (not a state) | Every Friday he **would** check the calibration. |
| **didn't use to** | negative | We **didn't use to** record the reason. |
| **Did you use to …?** | question | **Did you use to** sign each page? |
| **not for states** | ✗ | *He ~~would~~ be the manager* → He **used to** be the manager. |

> **Grammar Note** — *Used to* works for both states and repeated actions; *would* only for
> repeated actions. And in the negative and question it loses its *d*: *didn't use to*, *did you
> use to*. In an audit this grammar matters because it is how people describe what the practice
> was before the current procedure — and the gap between *used to* and *now* is where findings
> live.

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|

> **Grammar Note** — When you are describing how things were, keep *used to* for the practice and
> the past simple for the single event: *we used to record only the reading, and in March 2024 we

> much more serious statement than you intended.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Audits mean different things in different places, and the differences are mostly about blame. In
some organizations a finding is a normal part of improvement and a department with no findings is
suspected of hiding things; in others a finding is a personal failure, is remembered at appraisal
time, and the whole audit becomes an exercise in showing as little as possible. Directness varies
too: in some cultures an auditor states the finding plainly in the room, and in others it would be
discussed privately with a manager first so that nobody loses face in front of their team. And
"evidence" itself is cultural: in some systems a senior person's word is evidence, and asking for a
document from them is close to an insult.

**Discuss:** Where you work, what happens to a department that gets findings? Could you ask your
director for a document?

> ### Audit finding IA-2026-03/1 — observation
> *Brightwell Pharma · scope: document control and data integrity · I. Costa · 14 April*
>
> **Statement of fact.** The opening slide of the management review of 11 March presented
> "1,474,118 valid ISO 9001:2015 certificates worldwide, 2024" as fact, sourced on the slide as
> "ISO Survey 2024".
>
> **Evidence examined.** (1) The slide itself. (2) A third-party infographic citing IAF CertSearch,
> giving 1,474,118 for 2024 and 1,250,243 for 2023. (3) A second third-party page, also citing the
> ISO Survey, giving 837,978 for 2023.
>
> **What could not be examined.** The ISO Survey results. ISO's own page states that the survey is
> produced and hosted on IAF CertSearch and that results are obtained by logging in; no public
> table was available during the audit.
>
> **The problem.** The two secondary sources disagree about 2023 by over 400,000 certificates, so
> it cannot be determined whether the 2024 figure reflects growth in certification or a change in
> counting. Both the presenter and the auditor consider the second explanation more likely. Neither
> can demonstrate it.
>
> **What this finding does not assert.** It does not assert that the figure is incorrect; that the
> presenter acted carelessly; or that any batch, quality or regulatory record is affected. The
> sample of batch records examined (12 of 214, selected by random number) was complete and
> traceable in every case.
>
> **Requirement.** Figures presented in management review shall be traceable to a source held on
> file.
>
> **Corrective action, agreed with R. Menon.** By 30 April, the slide will be re-presented with the
> figure, both conflicting prior-year figures, and a one-line statement that the primary data is
> not publicly accessible. **Agreed action is to state the limitation, not to remove the figure**,
> because the figure is the best available and a stated limitation is more useful to the reader
> than an absence.

> **Writer's Tip** — *What this finding does not assert* is the most valuable paragraph in any
> audit document. Every reader fills in the blanks uncharitably; this paragraph does it for them,
> accurately, and it is why a well-written finding makes people less defensive rather than more.

**1D  Circle the correct word.**
