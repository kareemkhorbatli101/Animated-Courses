# Clearline 2
%%SUB Business English · International Edition · CEFR B1%%

## How to use this book

Clearline 1 got the work done. This book is about **taking part**: speaking in a meeting you are
not chairing, asking for something and giving something back, standing up with a chart, telling
people a date has moved before they find out, and saying what you have learned in a year without
either boasting or apologizing.

**Twelve units**, fourteen pages each, the same six lessons in the same order as Books 1 and 2. If
you have used either of them, you already know where everything is.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Sixteen words, a second set of six, and a team research task |
| **2 · Business Reading** | A real document, and one real number from a real report |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures, taught as choices with consequences |
| **4 · Workplace Conversation** | Clear English, a long dialogue, a culture check and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A model of the unit's genre, then your own |
| **Team Up & Review** | A picture, a team project, a survey, and one problem with no easy answer |

**The audio.** 🔊 marks a track. 195 tracks, numbered `CL3-unit-track`.

## What is different about this book

**Every unit asks you to say something that is not comfortable.** A date has moved. The figure is
not the one you were hoping for. The customer is angry and also right. The person in front of you
has done the job for eleven years and you are about to change it. At B1 you have enough English to
say these things; what this book adds is the shape that stops them becoming an argument.

**One unit is built on a number that cannot be checked.** Unit 10 quotes a figure for the number
of quality certificates in the world, prints two different baselines for the year before, and says
plainly that it cannot tell you which is right. Then it asks you to write an audit finding about
exactly that. It is the most useful unit in the book and the only one with no answer key.

**Two true sentences can point in opposite directions.** In 2025 the airline accident rate fell and
the number of people killed rose. Both figures are in the same report. Unit 11 teaches you to say
both of them in one breath, because that is what honesty sounds like in a second language.

**Nothing assumes one country's law or one country's manners.** Notice periods, appraisal rules,
expenses, who may interrupt whom: all of these differ. Where it matters the book says *this differs
— find yours*, and makes that the task.
