### 5 · Word study — passive reporting structures

**5A  Study the chart.**

%%FIG r01-f6 | A chart of passive reporting structures in five rows: it is estimated that, is expected to, is said to, is thought to have, and is reported as, each with an active version beside it and a note on when each is honest and when it hides the source.%%

| Pattern | Example | What it hides |
|---|---|---|
| **it is estimated that** | **It is estimated that** savings will be 98,000. | who estimated it |
| **X is expected to** | Payback **is expected to** be 2y 7m. | who expects it |
| **X is said to** | The contract **is said to** be renewable. | who said it |
| **X is thought to have** | The seal **is thought to have** failed in July. | who thinks so |
| **X is reported as** | The rate **is reported as** 6.49 percent. | by whom, and when |

> **Grammar Note** — These structures are useful and dangerous. Useful: they let you report a
> figure without claiming it as your own. Dangerous: they can make a guess sound like a finding. The
> rule in this book is simple — use a passive reporting structure **and then name the source in the
> same sentence**: *it is estimated, by our own meter data over twelve months, that…*

| Pattern | Honest version |
|---|---|

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

How a case for money is made and judged differs a great deal. In some business cultures the written
page decides it and the meeting is a formality; in others the page is a courtesy and the real
decision is made in conversations beforehand, so a brilliant document presented cold will fail. The
place of uncertainty differs too: in some organizations naming your weakest assumption is the mark
of a professional, and in others it is read as a lack of conviction and the approver expects you to
argue for your own number. Hierarchy matters as well — in some places you may put a case directly
to the person who holds the budget, and in others it must pass through your own manager first, and
going round them is the real error, whatever the quality of the case.

**Discuss:** Where you work, is the decision in the document or in the room? May you name your
weakest assumption?

> ### AC-C09 — compressor and lighting replacement
> *C. Obi · 14 March · one page; appendix of 11 pages attached and not required reading*
>
> **The ask.** **400,000**: 384,000 capital, 16,000 of operating cost over five years.
>
> **Benefits, each with its source.**
> | | Per year | Source |
> |---|---|---|
> | Electricity | 98,000 | our own meter data, 12 months, **at today's price** |
> | Maintenance avoided | 34,000 | current contract, **net of 18,000 already claimed in AC-C07** |
>
> **Payback.** Central case **2 years 7 months**. **Range: 2 years 1 month to 3 years 4 months**,
> driven almost entirely by the electricity price. **Our hurdle is three years, and the low case
> does not meet it.**
>
> **What the answer depends on.** (1) The electricity price in years 2–5. (2) The maintenance
> contract being renewed on current terms. (3) Installation in one shutdown rather than two.
> (4) No change to shift patterns.
>
> **The assumption I am least sure about.** The electricity price in year three. It is not
> forecastable and I have not tried; the range above is what I can honestly offer instead.
>
> **What this case does not claim.** A downtime saving. The compressors fail about twice a year
> and I believe the saving is real, but nobody records what a failure costs us, so any figure would

> 30,000 a year of avoided downtime; **I am not asking you to approve that version.** I have asked
> A. Holm to start recording failure cost from April so that the next case can use a measured
> figure.
>
> **What I would like.** Approval of the 400,000, and a review in October against the actual
> electricity price, at which point the lighting half can be stopped if the low case has arrived.

> **Writer's Tip** — The two sections that get a case approved are *the assumption I am least sure
> about* and *what this case does not claim*. They cost you eleven lines and they move the page from
> something your reader has to check to something your reader can rely on. Every approver has been
> burned by an optimistic case; be the one who hands them the knife.

### 3 · Write

Write **120 to 160 words**: a one-page business case.

**Use at least two passive reporting structures and name the source inside each sentence. Give a
payback range, not a point. Name the assumption you are least sure about. Write what the case does
not claim.**

**Read your draft aloud to your partner first.** Hearing it is the quickest way to find the sentence
that does not work. Then check your own against the list, and swap.
