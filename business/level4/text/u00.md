# Starting Point · Four Habits for Carrying a Decision

At B1+ the English is not the hard part any more. What is hard is that the decisions are now
partly yours, and a sentence you write in March gets quoted in November.

### 1 · What you already have

**1A  Work with a partner. Say one sentence of your own in each row, out loud.**
%%AUD CL4-00-01%%

%%FIG r00-f1 | A one-page review sheet with six boxes, each headed with a structure: present perfect continuous, the passive, reported speech, the second conditional, cause and result linking, and non-defining relative clauses. Each box holds one example sentence from a working day.%%

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| **Present perfect continuous** | We'**ve been running** at ninety percent **since** March. |
| **The passive** | The rate **was reopened** when fuel moved eleven percent. |
| **Reported speech** | He said he **would** confirm by four. |
| **Second conditional** | If the line **stopped**, we **would** lose about ninety thousand. |
| **Cause and result** | Prices rose, **so** the margin fell. |
| **Non-defining clause** | Ines, **who audits now**, wrote the finding. |

**1B  Ask and answer with a partner.** One question in each structure. Then change roles.

### 2 · Habit one — a figure has four parts, and now a fifth

**2A  Listen and repeat.** %%AUD CL4-00-02%%

> **Book 2 and 3:** the figure, the unit, the date, the source.
> **This book adds:** the **scope**.
>
> *Electricity is 0.18 euros a kilowatt-hour* is not a fact until you say **for whom**: that figure
> is for businesses using between 500 and 2,000 megawatt-hours a year. A smaller company pays
> more and a steelworks pays less.

**2B  Add the scope. Say each one aloud.**

1. The average wait is four minutes. *(for whom? at what time of day?)*
2. Payment takes forty-one days. *(which customers? which invoices?)*
3. Staff turnover is nine percent. *(including or excluding fixed-term staff?)*

### 3 · Habits two, three and four

**3A  Listen and read.** %%AUD CL4-00-03%%

%%FIG r00-f2 | A card taped inside a folder with four short rules printed on it: say the scope; say the counter-example; say what you are not claiming; and write down the decision date. Beneath each rule, one example sentence in smaller type.%%

> **Two — say the counter-example.** *Investment rose six percent. In the countries we are
> talking about it fell five.* If your figure has a counter-example and you do not say it, somebody
> else will, and then it is theirs.
>
> **Three — say what you are not claiming.** *I am not saying the figure is wrong. I am saying we
> cannot trace it.* Two sentences. They prevent about half of all arguments.
>
> **Four — write down the decision date.** Not the deadline. The date on which **you will find out
> whether you were right**, and what you will look at then.

**3B  Practice with your partner.** Make a claim about your own work. Your partner asks for the
scope, the counter-example, and what you are not claiming. Then change roles.

**3C  Talk about it.** Which of the four habits would be hardest to use where you work? Why?
