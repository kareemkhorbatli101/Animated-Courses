### 5 · Word study — degrees of refusal

**5A  Study the chart.**

%%FIG r02-f6 | A chart of refusal forms arranged from softest to hardest: I'd rather not, I'm not able to, I can't, I won't be able to, that isn't possible, and I'm not willing to, each with a note on what it commits you to and one example.%%

| Form | Strength | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **I'd rather not** | a preference | I'**d rather not** take it on this month. |
| **I'm not able to** | a statement of capacity | I'**m not able to** do both. |

|---|---|

### 2 · *be able to*, and why it exists

**2A  Study the chart.**

| Form | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **can / can't** | present ability | I **can't** finish it today. |
| **will / won't be able to** | future ability | I **won't be able to** finish by Friday. |
| **haven't been able to** | up to now | I **haven't been able to** reach them. |
| **wasn't able to** | one past occasion | I **wasn't able to** get a price before the cut-off. |

> **Grammar Note** — *Can* has no infinitive and no perfect form, which is why *be able to* exists.

**3A  Study the chart.**

| Step | Example |
|---|---|
| **1 · Say no early and plainly** | I can't take this on as asked. |

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Refusing is one of the most dangerous things to get wrong in a second language, because the words
are easy and the social rules are not. In some business cultures a direct "I can't do that" is
normal, efficient and respected; in others it is close to unsayable to a senior person, and the
expected form is an acceptance followed by a renegotiation of the date, or an answer routed through
a third person. In some places "I'll try" means *I will try*; in others it is a recognized polite
no, and a colleague who takes it literally has misread the room. There are also differences in who
may refuse: in strongly hierarchical organizations a refusal is read as a challenge to authority
rather than as information about capacity, and the safe route is to make the arithmetic visible and
let the manager do the refusing.

**Discuss:** Where you work, can you say no to your manager? What does "I'll try" mean? **And find
out:** what does the law where you are say about working hours and overtime?

### 3 · Write
