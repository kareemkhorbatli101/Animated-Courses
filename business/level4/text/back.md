# Grammar reference

Every structure in this book, in unit order. The second column is the mistake it causes, which is
the column worth reading twice.

## Starting Point · four habits

| | |
|---|---|
| **A figure has five parts now** | the figure, the unit, the date, the source, and the **scope** |
| **Say the counter-example** | if you do not, somebody else will, and then it is theirs |
| **Say what you are not claiming** | two sentences that prevent half of all arguments |
| **Write down the decision date** | not the deadline — the day you find out whether you were right |

## Unit 1 · Passive reporting structures

| | |
|---|---|
| ***it is estimated that*** · ***is expected to*** · ***is said to*** | each one hides who said it |
| **Name the source inside the sentence** | *we estimate, from our own meter data, that…* |
| **Five degrees of certainty** | measured · estimated · a reasoned guess · unknown · not claimed |
| **A payback is a range** | a single figure invites the whole page to be doubted |

## Unit 2 · Degrees of refusal; *be able to*

| | |
|---|---|
| ***can't*** = capacity · ***I'm not willing to*** = a decision | people hear the difference |
| ***not as asked*** | refuses the version and keeps the thing on the table |
| ***be able to*** after *will*, *would like*, *have*, *want* | *I will can* · *I'd like to can* |
| **Arithmetic, not the word *busy*** | a total, a displacement, two offers, one question |

## Unit 3 · Quantity: *per*, *out of*, ratios

| | |
|---|---|
| **A quantity statement has three parts** | the number, the unit, and the **denominator** |
| ***per cent of what?*** | eight per cent of overhead, of total cost and of price are three claims |
| ***out of*** for counts · ***per*** for rates | *19 out of 27* · *0.18 per kWh* |
| **Nothing got dearer; there is less to divide it by** | the sentence that ends one specific argument |

## Unit 4 · The future perfect; *by the time*

| | |
|---|---|
| ***will have + participle*** | finished before a future point |
| ***by the time*** takes the **present** | *by the time it will arrive* |
| ***will have been + -ing*** | how long, at that point |
| **Itemize a deduction** | a deduction you cannot itemize is one you cannot negotiate |

## Unit 5 · Reporting verbs and *that*-clauses

| | |
|---|---|
| **Match the verb to the evidence** | *shows* and *confirms* for tables; *suggests* for your own reading |
| ***suggest*** + *-ing* or *that* | *suggest to raise* |
| ***attribute X to Y*** | *attribute to the slowdown travel disruption* |
| **Label every figure value or volume** | two words that prevent a whole category of argument |

## Unit 6 · *should have*; *be supposed to*

| | |
|---|---|
| ***should have*** = judgement · ***was supposed to*** = a rule | one takes responsibility, one blames the procedure |
| ***needn't have done*** ≠ ***didn't need to do*** | one happened, one did not |
| **Thanks, then the rule, then the solution** | in that order, with ***and*** rather than ***but*** |
| **Protect the decision, not only yourself** | recusal costs nothing and makes the review stronger |

## Unit 7 · Zero conditional; *shall*, *may*, *must*

| | |
|---|---|
| ***may*** = permission · ***shall*** = obligation on the organization · ***must*** = obligation | |
| ***should*** has **no force** | a clause that says *should* has promised nothing |
| **if + present, present / must** | rules use the zero conditional; *where* is its formal twin |
| **A role, a number of days, a written output** | add all three or the clause is decoration |

## Unit 8 · Past perfect; narrative sequence

| | |
|---|---|
| ***had + participle*** for what was already true | at the point you are looking back from |
| **Past simple for the timeline, past perfect for the gap** | and the present for what changes |
| ***by the time + past simple*** | names the point you look back from |
| **Put the finding above the timeline** | most readers stop at row six |

## Unit 9 · *unless*, *provided that*, *in case*

| | |
|---|---|
| ***unless*** = the stopping condition · ***provided that*** = the continuing one | |
| ***in case*** is a precaution taken **beforehand** | *in case it rains* ≠ *if it rains* |
| **A trigger has three parts** | a figure, a date and an action |
| **Lead with the figure against you** | it removes the weapon from whoever was going to find it |

## Unit 10 · Verb + *-ing* or *to*-infinitive

| | |
|---|---|
| *avoid, consider, involve, risk, delay* **+ -ing** | *avoided to tell* |
| *decide, agree, arrange, manage, aim* **+ to** | *decided closing* |
| ***stop + -ing*** ≠ ***stop + to*** | *we stopped making it* ≠ *we stopped to make it* |
| ***tell***, not *communicate* | one describes a person doing something difficult |

## Unit 11 · Noun phrases; un-nominalizing

| | |
|---|---|
| **Nominalize to define, un-nominalize to describe** | *an improvement has occurred* names nobody |
| **A definition has six parts** | term, numerator, denominator, exclusions, period, baseline |
| **The exclusions line** | the one most often left out and the cause of most arguments |
| **Name the actor** | if you cannot tell who did it, rewrite it |

## Unit 12 · Hedging and commitment

| | |
|---|---|
| **Six levels** | exploring · intend · aim · expect · have committed · have done |
| **Each level needs evidence the one below does not** | if you have not got it, move down a row |
| **A boundary is part of the figure** | not a footnote, and not a disclaimer |
| **Finished things first, with dates** | intentions after four facts are believed |

## Seven honesty notes, printed here because they are printed in the units too

- **Unit 11 is the clearest case in the whole series of one business measured two ways.**
  International parcel post tonnage fell 16 per cent while the number of items rose 38 per cent.
  Both are true and they describe the same traffic: people are sending more parcels and each one
  weighs less. A report quoting only the tonnage says the business is shrinking; one quoting only
  the items says it is growing. The unit prints both and makes the learner write the sentence that
  holds them together. It also keeps the UPU and the IPC figures visibly apart, because they are
  different organizations measuring different populations over different periods, and putting them
  in one table would be the error the unit is about.
- **Unit 5 separates value from volume**, which is the commonest honest-looking error in business
  reporting. The WTO's own October 2026 update has services trade up 14 per cent in value in one
  quarter and the year's **volume** forecast cut to 3.3 per cent. Neither figure is wrong. Quoting
  one as though it were the other is, and the unit's model note leads with the volume figure
  because the decision in question is a capacity decision.
- **Unit 9's headline contains its own counter-example.** Global foreign direct investment rose 6
  per cent; flows to lower-middle-income countries fell 5 per cent; and excluding conduit flows the
  rise was 4 per cent rather than 6. All three are in the same report. The unit's model proposal
  leads with the figure that argues against it.
- **Unit 7 scores one subject three times and the gaps are the finding.** The law averages 67, its
  enforcement 53, and the systems that would make it usable 47. Averaging the three would hide
  exactly what the report was built to show, and the unit's model policy is mostly procedure for
  that reason.
- **Unit 12 is a count that depends on what you are counting.** "Nearly 50 jurisdictions on the
  journey" and "21 jurisdictions have adopted" are both current and both defensible, because one
  counts intention and the other enactment — and even enactment is often partial. The unit prints
  both with their dates and asks which belongs in an annual report.
- **Unit 4's figure is a simple average of providers, not of money.** The World Bank computes its
  6.49 per cent by averaging the cost charged by each provider in its database, so a provider used
  by almost nobody counts as much as one used by millions, and the figure describes a 200-dollar
  personal transfer rather than a commercial payment. The unit says so, and uses the figure only
  as an order of magnitude.
- **Unit 10 mixes two vintages on purpose.** The enterprise birth and death rates are for 2024; the
  most recent EU-wide five-year survival figure follows businesses born in 2014. Both are the
  newest published figures for their own measure and they do not describe the same period. The unit
  prints the years beside the figures and makes the mismatch the task, because this is the kind of
  error that is invisible once it is in a sentence.

**Everything else in this book is invented.** Ostberg is not a real city. Lindmark Freight, Arvo
Components, Brightwell Pharma, Corvid Analytics, Juno Textiles, Novak Packaging, Kjeldsen,
Ostergaard, Vandermeer, Harbour Mutual, Terrace Hotels, Teval Transport, Baptista Metalúrgica and

payment, policy, incident, proposal, closure plan, service report and statement in these pages was
written for this book. Where a document would in reality depend on the law of a particular country

disclosure requirements — the book says so and makes finding your own answer the task.

# Closing

You began this book able to take part. You end it able to make a case for money and say what is
wrong with it; to refuse work and come out of the conversation with a decision rather than
sympathy; to cost something so that nobody can quote half of it back at you; to find out what a
payment actually costs and fix it across two companies; to tell value from volume and lead with the
figure the decision needs; to keep a relationship and protect a decision in the same sentence; to
write a rule that somebody could actually use and somebody could actually breach; to put a finding
above a timeline; to argue for a decision whose headline is against you, with a stopping rule you
would keep; to close something in the right order and admit the delay; to match a measure to a
promise; and to say what your company has done without claiming what it has not.

Four sentences to take with you. From Unit 1: **what this case does not claim.** From Unit 3: **at
a volume of.** From Unit 9: **unless, by month fifteen.** And from Unit 12, the one that costs
nothing and buys everything: **we intend to, and there is no plan behind it yet.**

None of the four is impressive English. All four are the sentences that make the difference between
being fluent and being trusted, and the second one is the one people remember you for.
