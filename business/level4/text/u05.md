### 5 · Word study — reporting verbs

**5A  Study the chart.**

%%FIG r05-f6 | A chart of reporting verbs grouped by what they commit the writer to: neutral with say, state and report; cautious with suggest, indicate and estimate; strong with show, demonstrate and confirm; and conceding with acknowledge, concede and admit, each with one example that-clause.%%

| Group | Verbs | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **neutral** | say, state, report, note | The update **reports that** value rose 14 percent. |
| **cautious** | suggest, indicate, estimate | The data **suggest that** price is most of it. |
| **strong** | show, demonstrate, confirm | The table **shows that** volume growth was revised down. |
| **conceding** | acknowledge, concede, admit | The authors **acknowledge that** travel disruption affected Q2. |
| **attributing** | attribute X to Y | They **attribute** the slowdown **to** transport disruption. |

> **Grammar Note** — Most reporting verbs take *that* and a clause: *the report states that…*. A
> few take an object and an infinitive: *they expect the market to grow*. And *suggest* never takes
> an infinitive in this sense: not *suggest to raise prices* but *suggest raising prices* or
> *suggest that we raise prices*.

### 1 · Reporting verbs and *that*-clauses

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the opinion question.** %%AUD CL4-05-06%%

> the volume forecast was cut.
> **Daniel:** "Confirms" is doing some work there.

*Daniel accepts "confirms" because it is in a table. When would "suggests" be the honest verb?*

**1B  Underline the reporting verbs. Which one is strongest?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **verb + that + clause** | The report **states that** volume grew 5.3 percent. |
| **verb + object + to-infinitive** | They **expect** the market **to grow** 3.3 percent. |
| **attribute X to Y** | They **attribute** the slowdown **to** travel disruption. |
| **according to + source** | **According to** the October update, value rose 14 percent. |
| **suggest + -ing / that** | I **suggest raising** the price · I **suggest that we raise** it. |

> **Grammar Note** — Choose the verb for the evidence, not for the effect. *Shows* and *confirms*
> belong to things printed in tables. *Suggests* and *indicates* belong to your own reading of the
> data. Using a strong verb for a weak inference is the most common dishonesty in professional
> writing, and it is almost always unintentional.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

What a report to senior management is allowed to contain differs sharply. In some business cultures
you are expected to bring the number and the recommendation and to defend both; in others bringing
bad news directly to a board is thought to be your manager's job, and doing it yourself is a breach
of order rather than an act of courage. There are differences in optimism too: in some
organizations a cautious figure is read as rigour, and in others as negativity that will be
remembered at appraisal time. Even the shape of a paper varies — a single slide is standard in
some companies and insultingly thin in others, where the same content is expected over six pages
with an annex.

**Discuss:** Where you work, who takes a cautious number to a board? What happens to them?

> ### Market note for the board — capacity decision
> *P. Moreau · 20 October · one page*
>
> **The figure this decision needs.** Services trade **volume** growth for 2026 is forecast at

>

>

> moved once already this year, by 1.5 points in three months, and may move again before March.

> repeat that sentence, you have done the job; if they can only repeat your number, you have not.

### 3 · Write

that does not work. Then check your own against the list, and swap.
