# Clearline 3
%%SUB Business English · International Edition · CEFR B1+%%

## How to use this book

Clearline 2 was about taking part. This one is about **carrying responsibility**: making a case for
money, saying no to work you cannot do, costing something honestly, reporting a number that could
be read two ways, and writing a rule that other people will have to follow.

**Twelve units**, sixteen pages each, the same six lessons in the same order as the earlier books.
If you have used any of them, you already know where everything is.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Sixteen words, a second set of six, and a team research task |
| **2 · Business Reading** | A real document, and one real number from a real report |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures, taught as choices with consequences |
| **4 · Workplace Conversation** | Clear English, a long dialogue, a culture check and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A model of the unit's genre, then your own |
| **Team Up & Review** | A picture, a team project, a survey, and one problem with no easy answer |

**The audio.** 🔊 marks a track. 195 tracks, numbered `CL4-unit-track`.

## What is different about this book

**Every reading text is a number that could mislead you.** Not one of them is wrong. Postal
tonnage fell sixteen percent while the number of parcels rose thirty-eight. Services trade grew
fourteen percent in value and its volume forecast was cut to three point three. Foreign investment
rose six percent worldwide and fell five percent in the countries that needed it most. Twelve
units, twelve figures, and in every one the honest sentence is longer than the headline.

**You will practice saying no.** Three units are about refusing something — work, a price, a
favor that is not quite a bribe — and the point of all three is that a refusal with a reason and
an alternative is a professional act, while a yes you cannot deliver is not.

**One unit asks you to write a rule.** Unit 7 reads a report that scores the same subject three
times: the law, its enforcement, and the systems that would make it work. The three numbers are
sixty-seven, fifty-three and forty-seven. Then it asks you to write a policy that admits the gap.

**Nothing assumes one country's law.** Gifts, expenses, notice, overtime, data, pay: all differ.
Where it matters the book says *this differs — find yours*, and makes that the task.
