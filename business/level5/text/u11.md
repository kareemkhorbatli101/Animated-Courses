### 5 · Word study — *whereby*, *insofar as* and the formal connectors

**5A  Study the chart.**

%%FIG s11-f6 | A chart of six formal connectors with their meanings in plain words: whereby meaning by means of which, insofar as meaning to the extent that, in respect of meaning about, notwithstanding meaning despite, thereby meaning and in that way, and to the extent that meaning as far as — each with one example sentence.%%

| Connector | Plain meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **whereby** | by means of which | a scheme **whereby** employees receive a top-up |
| **insofar as** | to the extent that | **Insofar as** direction is concerned, it is upwards |
| **to the extent that** | as far as | comparable **to the extent that** methods are unchanged |
| **in respect of** | about, concerning | a provision **in respect of** sick pay |
| **notwithstanding** | despite | **Notwithstanding** the break, the direction holds |
| **thereby** | and in that way | extending cover and **thereby** raising effective coverage |

> **Grammar Note** — *Whereby* means *by means of which* and nothing else. It is not a substitute for
> *where* or *in which*: *a market whereby wages are low* is wrong; *a scheme whereby wages are
> topped up* is right. These connectors belong in policy documents and contracts, where their
> precision earns their formality; in an email they make an ordinary sentence sound defensive.

**5B  Rewrite each sentence with the connector in brackets.**

2. As far as the direction goes, it is upwards. *(insofar as)*
3. Despite the series break, the direction holds. *(notwithstanding)*
4. This clause is about sick pay. *(in respect of)*

**1B  Underline the long noun phrase. How many words is it, and could it be shorter without losing

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| **noun + of-phrase + relative** | the share **of the rise** **that** is attributable to measurement |
| **noun + participle** | the population **legally covered** by comprehensive systems |
| **noun + prepositional chain** | coverage **in** the 50 countries most vulnerable **to** climate change |
| **nominalized verb + by** | the **extension** of protection **by** national schemes |
| **noun + whereby-clause** | a scheme **whereby** employees receive a top-up |
| **stacked modifiers** | **non-contributory working-age** coverage |

> **Grammar Note** — A complex noun phrase lets you make something precise the subject of a sentence,
> which is exactly what a policy document needs. Its danger is that three stacked modifiers before a
> noun — *non-contributory working-age coverage* — can be read two ways and nobody asks. If a phrase
> has more than two modifiers in front, move one behind with *of* or a relative clause.

### 2 · Qualifying a series break

**2A  Study the chart.**

| Function | Language |
|---|---|
| **Quote the publisher** | *as the publisher states*, with the sentence in their words |
| **Name the causes they name** | methodological enhancements, extended data, country revisions |
| **Refuse the unknowable split** | how much is real and how much is measurement cannot be determined |
| **Keep what survives** | the direction holds; the current figures are comparable with each other |
| **Show the break in the chart** | a gap in the line, each segment labelled |
| **Say what you removed** | my first draft drew the decade line; I have removed it |

> **Grammar Note** — *Not strictly comparable* is the publisher's phrase and it is weaker than *not
> comparable* and much stronger than *broadly comparable*. Do not paraphrase a caveat: quote it. Any
> rewording you choose will be either softer or harder than theirs, and the one you reach for will
> be the one that suits your argument.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Social protection differs so much between countries that a group benefits policy is one of the
hardest documents a company writes. In some systems a substantial pension and comprehensive medical
cover are provided by the state and funded by contributions, so an employer's supplementary scheme
is a small addition and sometimes an insult; in others almost nothing is provided and an employer's
package is the entire safety net for an employee's household. There are cultural differences in what
is expected from an employer at all: in some places housing, schooling and family support are
normal parts of employment, and in others they would be seen as intrusive. And discussing coverage
can be politically sensitive, since a company document that states plainly that a country's state
provision is inadequate may be read as criticism of the government by employees who agree with it.

**Discuss:** Where you work, what does the state provide and what does the employer? Would you write
down the difference?

> ### Benefits policy brief — the four new markets
> *N. Khalil · 9 September · one page*
>
> **The current figures, which are comparable with each other.** **52.4 per cent** of the world's
> population is covered by at least one social protection benefit — the first time the figure has
> passed half. **3.8 billion people** remain entirely unprotected. **33.8 per cent** of the
> working-age population is legally covered by comprehensive social protection. In the **50 countries
> most vulnerable to climate change**, effective coverage is about **25 per cent**.
>
> **The publisher's own statement on comparability, quoted.**
>
> > *These estimates are not strictly comparable with those of the previous report, owing to
> > methodological enhancements, extended data availability and country revisions.*
>
> **What I am claiming about the decade, and what I have removed.** The earlier figure was **42.8 per
> cent in 2015**. **Insofar as** direction is concerned, coverage has risen. **The share of the rise
> attributable to improved measurement rather than to extended protection cannot be determined from
> this report**, and the publisher does not determine it. **My first draft opened with a ten-point
> rise over a decade. It is struck through on the attached page rather than deleted, so that the
> board can see what I declined to claim and disagree if it wishes.**
>
> **The figure this policy turns on.** **33.8 per cent legally covered.** Legal coverage is the one
> thing an employer cannot create. Effective coverage is precisely what an employer can affect, by
> operating a scheme **whereby** employees receive what their state does not — which is why the
> global figure tells us what to expect and local law tells us what to fill.
>
> **Four markets, four packages.** A: state pension and sickness sound; nothing added. B: sickness
> only; pension top-up. C: sickness only in law and patchy in practice; top-up and private medical.
> D: among the 50 most vulnerable; full package, **costed separately so that it is never averaged
> into a group figure**.
>
> **One principle, identical in all four.** **Notwithstanding** the differences between the markets,
> no employee's household falls below the local subsistence threshold on our sick pay.
>
> *Source: ILO, World Social Protection Report 2024–26, for all global figures and for the quoted
> statement on comparability. Country-level provision is from local counsel and is not from this
> report.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Strike a claim through rather than deleting it, and write your reason beside it.
> A deletion is invisible, so the reader cannot tell whether you considered the claim and rejected it
> or never thought of it — and the second is much worse for your credibility than the first. This is
> the cheapest way there is of showing judgement rather than asserting it.
