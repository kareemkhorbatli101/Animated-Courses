### 5 · Word study — tentative and distancing language

**5A  Study the chart.**

%%FIG s12-f6 | A chart of tentative forms grouped by what they do: softening a claim with it may be that and I wonder whether; checking understanding with what I'm hearing is and have I got that right; leaving room with I could be wrong and correct me if; and inviting with would it be fair to say and how would you put it — each with one example.%%

| Function | Language | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Soften a claim** | it may be that · I wonder whether | **It may be that** the handover is part of it. |
| **Check understanding** | what I'm hearing is … have I got that right? | **What I'm hearing is** the rota changed in October. |
| **Leave room** | I could be wrong · correct me if I've misread this | **I could be wrong about this.** |
| **Invite a reframing** | would it be fair to say · how would you put it | **Would it be fair to say** nobody asked? |
| **Separate your view** | my reading is · from where I sit | **From where I sit** it looks like workload. |
| **Hold without deciding** | I'd want to understand · before I form a view | **I'd want to understand** October first. |

> **Grammar Note** — *It may be that* plus a clause is more tentative than *it may be* plus a noun,
> and much more tentative than *maybe*. In a difficult conversation the extra words are doing real
> work: they say that you have a hypothesis rather than a conclusion, and they make disagreeing with
> you cheap. A conclusion is expensive to disagree with, which is why people stop telling you things.

**5B  Rewrite each sentence to make it tentative.**

1. The handover is the problem.
2. You are overloaded.
3. They left because of the rota.
4. Nobody asked them anything.

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **Can you tell me + wh-clause** | **Can you tell me how** the handover works? |
| **Can you tell me + whether** | **Can you tell me whether** anyone was asked in October? |
| **I wonder + whether / wh-** | **I wonder whether** that is the right question. |

> **Grammar Note** — Indirect questions take statement word order after the introducing phrase: *can
> you tell me how the handover **works***, never *how does the handover work*. With *whether*, no
> *if* and no inversion: *I wonder whether anyone **was** asked*. This is the single most common
> structural error B2 learners make in exactly the conversations where it matters most.

**2A  Study the chart.**

| Move | Language |
|---|---|
| **Paraphrase** | What I'm hearing is … · So the position is … |
| **Check** | Have I got that right? · Is that fair? |
| **Hold the view back** | I'd rather not form a view until … |
| **Name your own limit** | I could be wrong · I wasn't here in October |

> **Grammar Note** — Paraphrase before you respond, every time, and use your own words rather than

### %%AUG c%% 2 · Clear English — asking without steering

**2A  Listen to the model.** %%AUD CL5-12-09%%

> *Can you tell me how the handover works now?* — rising on **how**, then flat; no stress on *problem*
> *What I'm hearing is … have I got that right?* — the second half quieter, genuinely a question
> *I could be wrong about this.* — said at normal speed, not as an apology

The steering is usually in the stress, not the words. *Is the **handover** a problem* and *is the
handover a **problem*** invite different answers. If you emphasize any word in your question, you
have told the other person what you expect.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Difficult conversations are among the most culturally specific things that happen at work. In some
business cultures a manager is expected to name the problem directly in the first sentence, and
circling round it through process questions is read as manipulation; in others opening with the
difficulty itself is so abrupt that the other person will say almost nothing for the rest of the
conversation. Hierarchy changes everything: where seniority is strongly marked, a junior colleague
may not contradict a senior one's hypothesis however tentatively it is offered, so a tentative form
can function as an instruction. Who may be present differs too — in some countries a works council
representative or union official would normally attend a conversation like this, and holding it
privately would itself be the problem. And the words differ: English hedges — *it may be that*, *I
wonder whether* — translate into several languages as either weakness or insincerity.

**Discuss:** Where you work, how does a difficult conversation open? Who else would be in the room?

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Note of a conversation — Ostberg coordinators
> *N. Khalil · 3 March · copied to A. Holm for correction before it goes anywhere*
>
> **Why I am writing this.** Two experienced coordinators resigned within five weeks. Both exit
> interviews record **"pay"**. Both were conducted by the person who sets the pay, three days before
> the leaver's last day, in a room with a glass wall. **I am treating the recorded reason as
> incomplete rather than untrue.**
>
> **What I asked, in order.** How the shift handover works now. When it changed. A walk through last
> Tuesday. How many Tuesdays like that in a month. What has been asked for since October, and of
> whom. **I did not open with the resignations, and I have recorded that deliberately.**
>
> **What Anders said, in his words as far as I can reproduce them.** The handover overlap was
> **halved from 30 minutes to 15 in October**, at the same time as the move to four shifts. The
> coordinators absorbed it. On a normal Tuesday a coordinator takes the handover in eleven minutes,
> finds misrouted loads from the night shift, fixes them, and starts **about forty minutes behind**.
> **Most Tuesdays are like that.** He asked operations for the thirty minutes back **twice**, was
> asked for a cost both times, and could not make it work because **the cost is a quarter of a
> person**.
>
> **My hypothesis, labelled as one.** **It may be that** what the two left over was **the asking
> rather than the fifteen minutes**. **I could be wrong.** Pay may well be a real part of it, and I
> note that both went to competitors at roughly the same money.
>
> **What I am changing, and from whose budget.** A paid 15-minute overlap on **both** sides of each
> handover — half a person across four shifts — **from my budget, not operations', for three
> months**.
>
> **What would show I was wrong.** Misroutes per week, measured from this week. If they do not fall
> materially in three months, the diagnosis was wrong and I will say so here in writing and try the
> next thing.
>
> **My own gap.** He asked operations and not me, twice. That is a gap in how I am reachable, not a
> failure of his.
>
> *Source for the context, not for anything about this depot: Eurofound, European Working Conditions
> Survey 2024 (first findings 3 September 2025; overview 14 April 2026), which gave me seven
> dimensions to ask about rather than one. **No figure from that survey is evidence about these eleven
> people and none is used as such here.***

> **Writer's Tip** — Write down what would show you were wrong, with a measure and a date, in the
> same note as your hypothesis. It is the only sentence that distinguishes a diagnosis from a
> preference, and it is the sentence that lets the other person believe you. It also means that in
> three months you will not be able to quietly reinterpret what you meant.
