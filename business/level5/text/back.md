# Grammar reference

Every structure in this book, in unit order. The second column is the mistake it causes, which is
the column worth reading twice.

## Starting Point · three habits for arguing in public

| | |
|---|---|
| **The figure and its qualification travel together** | if the publisher qualified it, the qualification is part of the figure |
| **Say what would change your mind** | *I am confident* tells the room nothing and invites a test |
| **Find the weakness in your own case first** | otherwise the person who finds it owns the meeting |

## Unit 1 · Cleft sentences and emphasis

| | |
|---|---|
| ***it*-cleft** · ***what*-cleft** · **all-cleft** | *It is the March assumption that matters* |
| **Clefts move the news to the end** | two in a paper persuade; six are a style |
| **Negative fronting inverts** | *not once the summary mentions it* |
| **Emphatic *do* concedes and holds** | *I do accept the figure — what I can't accept is using it twice* |

## Unit 2 · Concessive clauses

| | |
|---|---|
| ***even though*** = true · ***even if*** = may not be | *even though the budget doubled* when it has not |
| ***albeit*** takes a phrase | *albeit it is smaller* |
| ***however*** / ***no matter how*** + adjective | *no matter how the case is strong* |
| **Granted … Even so …** | one reversal is enough; never *but even so* |

## Unit 3 · Near and distant futures

| | |
|---|---|
| ***is set to*** / ***is due to*** claim a schedule | they look foolish at a ten-year horizon |
| ***will have been + -ing*** needs a stated point | *by 2036*, *by the time the rule lands* |
| ***by the time*** takes the **present** | *by the time it will take effect* |
| **Future in the past: *would*, *was going to*** | using *will* adopts somebody else's old forecast as your view |

## Unit 4 · Past modals of deduction

| | |
|---|---|
| ***needn't have*** = done and unnecessary | *didn't need to* usually means the need never arose |
| ***must have*** / ***can't have*** | inference, not accusation; *mustn't have* is not this |
| ***could have*** has two meanings | the option not taken, or the thing that nearly happened |
| **With hindsight at the front** | at the end it sounds like an excuse added late |

## Unit 5 · Relative clauses with quantifiers

| | |
|---|---|
| ***two of which*** · ***half of whom*** · ***none of which*** | *eleven countries, two of them are above* is a comma splice |
| **Always non-defining** | comma required; *that* impossible |
| **Verb agrees with the quantifier** | *none of which is*, *two of which are* |
| ***across*** countries vs ***within*** one | a sentence that does not say which describes nothing |

## Unit 6 · Like-for-like comparison

| | |
|---|---|
| ***on a like-for-like basis*** | say what is held constant, every time |
| ***on a constant sample of N*** | and give the N |
| ***as reported*** vs ***as restated*** | the figure then, or the figure republished |
| **Refuse the comparison, not the source** | *this index is unreliable* loses you a useful series |

## Unit 7 · Participle clauses

| | |
|---|---|
| **-ing active · -ed passive · *having* + -ed** | *Built from 79 indicators, the index is…* |
| **The participle takes the main clause's subject** | *Built from 79 indicators, we should not over-read it* |
| **Four-second test** | find the participle, find the noun after the comma, ask if it did the thing |
| **One per sentence** | two with different subjects is unreadable and nobody will say so |

## Unit 8 · Hypothetical and distancing forms

| | |
|---|---|
| **Inversion replaces *if*** | *if were we to arbitrate* |
| ***should + subject*** = if it happens | not obligation |
| ***but for*** + noun | *but for the clause, this would be in court* |
| ***I would be reluctant to*** | weaker than *I advise against*; choose the one you mean |

## Unit 9 · Markers of contrast and reconciliation

| | |
|---|---|
| **They are adverbials, not conjunctions** | *the figures differ, that said neither is wrong* |
| ***on the other hand*** needs a first hand | otherwise readers look back for a sentence they missed |
| **Cite the publication, not the institution** | four parts: institution, publication, month, scenario |
| **Never average two different measures** | the average is the only figure no publication supports |

## Unit 10 · The language of versions

| | |
|---|---|
| ***as published on*** + date · ***as published in*** + document | five words that keep a sentence true for ever |
| ***has been revised*** vs ***was revised in December*** | present perfect for what is now in force |
| ***as at*** fixes a measurement point | *as of* also means *from now on* |
| **Say what the survey measures** | *a day* is right; the silence after it is not |

## Unit 11 · *whereby*, *insofar as*, complex noun phrases

| | |
|---|---|
| ***whereby*** = by means of which | *a market whereby wages are low* |
| ***insofar as*** = to the extent that | not *insofar the direction* |
| ***notwithstanding*** = despite | not *notwithstanding of* |
| **Quote a caveat; never paraphrase it** | your rewording will lean the way your argument does |

## Unit 12 · Tentative and distancing language

| | |
|---|---|
| **Indirect questions take statement order** | *can you tell me how does the handover work* |
| ***I wonder whether***, no *if*, no inversion | *I wonder if whether anyone was asked* |
| **Paraphrase in your own words before responding** | repeating their words proves only that you heard the sounds |
| **Say what would show you wrong** | the sentence that separates a diagnosis from a preference |

# Sources

Twelve units, twelve different source bodies. Every figure quoted in a reading text in this book is
real, published, dated and named in the text where it is used, and no figure is reused from
Clearline Foundation, Clearline 1, Clearline 2 or Clearline 3.

What makes this book different from the three before it is that **every one of the twelve sources
qualifies its own figure** — with a published assumption, a revision, a definitional difference, a
series break, a growing sample, or a median that disagrees with its own average. The qualification
is printed in the unit, in the publisher's words where the publisher has words for it.

## Eight honesty notes, printed here because they are printed in the units too

- **Unit 4 is a published forecast that missed, by its own publisher's later figures.** The March
  2025 *sigma* said insured natural catastrophe losses were "on trend to 145 billion dollars"; the
  March 2026 *sigma* reports about 107 billion. Nobody did anything wrong: a projection made in
  March describes a year that has not happened, and the phrase *on trend to* is not *will be*. The
  unit prints both figures, shows that the substitution of *will be* was made by the company and not
  by the forecaster, and refuses the conclusion that forecasts are useless — because the alternative
  is planning the next renewal on instinct.
- **Unit 1 is a forecast whose assumptions are published and are the story.** The October 2026
  projections assume a shipping strait reopens in March 2027 and oil averages 96 dollars. The growth
  figure everybody quotes is downstream of two sentences almost nobody quotes. The unit's task is to
  put the assumption directly under the figure, and to notice when a plan has counted one assumption
  twice by calling it two reasons.
- **Unit 5 is an average and a median disagreeing about direction, in one report.** The OECD-wide
  unemployment rate was 4.9 percent and near a four-year low; the median across member countries
  rose from 5.4 to 5.7. The first is weighted by the size of each labor market, so large economies
  dominate it; the second treats every country alike. *Unemployment is flat* and *unemployment is
  rising in most countries* are both true, and eleven country managers live in the second sentence.
- **Unit 6 is a ranking whose sample is still growing.** B-READY covered 50 economies, then 101, with
  62 more planned and a target given as about 170 on one page and about 180 on another. 50 plus 62
  does not make 101. None of this is concealed — the publisher describes the 2025 edition as interim
  — and all of it means that no movement in rank between the two editions is a finding about a
  country. The unit's task is to say so and still give the reader something usable.
- **Unit 7 is a composite of 79 indicators across 139 economies**, in which a five-place move near
  the middle of the table can be produced by a small change in two or three inputs, by a country
  improving its own statistics, or by somebody else entering above or below. The unit accepts
  sixteen consecutive years at number one without hesitation and declines the five-place arrow, and
  notes that the publisher's own rounding of 139 to "about 140" is a fair guide to how precisely the
  ranks are meant to be read.
- **Unit 8 quotes what the statistics do not contain.** The 2025 figures give case numbers, values,
  sectors and award counts. They do not give how long a case takes, which is the only question a
  board asks. The unit makes the absence the first heading, allows one inference — pending cases
  rising faster than new ones — labels it as an inference, and reaches a recommendation anyway.
- **Unit 9 is one institution publishing two different versions of the same measure**, three months
  apart, on different scenarios and horizons. Both are correct. The unit's real subject is the
  averaging reflex: 22 percent is between two correct figures and is the only number on the page
  that no publication supports and nobody can check.
- **Unit 11 says in its own front pages that it cannot be compared with the report before it.** The
  rise from 42.8 to 52.4 percent is partly extended protection and partly better measurement, in
  proportions the publisher does not claim to know. The unit prints the publisher's sentence, keeps
  the direction, declines the rate, and strikes the decade claim through rather than deleting it, so
  that the reader can see what was declined.

# Closing

Twelve units, twelve sources, and in every one the honest sentence is longer than the headline.
That is the whole of this book.

If one habit survives, let it be this: **when a publisher qualifies a figure, the qualification is
part of the figure.** It travels with it into your slide, your paper, your minute and your
recommendation. Leaving it behind is not a shortcut; it is a transfer of the publisher's uncertainty
onto your own name, and you will be the one holding it when somebody checks.

The people in this book are invented. Ostberg is invented. Every figure in every reading text is
real, published, dated, attributed, and printed together with whatever its publisher said about it.

**Clearline 5** goes further out: strategy across borders, governance, dispute, and the long view.
