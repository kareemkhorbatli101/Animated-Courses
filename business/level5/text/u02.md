### 5 · Word study — concession

**5A  Study the chart.**

%%FIG s02-f6 | A chart of concession words arranged by strength and register: although and even though; while and whereas; granted and admittedly; albeit and for all; much as and even if — each with one short example sentence and a note on whether it is spoken or written.%%

| Form | Example | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **although / even though** | **Although** the case is sound, the engineers are not hired. | neutral |
| **while / whereas** | **While** finance is available, skills are not. | contrast, written |
| **granted / admittedly** | **Granted**, the sample is a year old. | spoken, concedes first |
| **albeit** | A strong case, **albeit** a smaller one. | formal, tight |
| **for all** | **For all** its detail, the survey has no 2026 figure. | formal, slightly literary |
| **much as** | **Much as** I want four cells, I am asking for one. | spoken, personal |
| **even if** | **Even if** the budget doubled, we still could not staff it. | hypothetical |

> **Grammar Note** — *Even though* concedes something true. *Even if* concedes something that may
> not be true. "Even though the budget is small" means it is small; "even if the budget doubled"
> means it has not. Learners swap these constantly, and the difference is the difference between
> admitting a fact and dismissing a hypothetical.

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **Although + clause, main clause** | **Although** the survey is a year old, the direction is clear. |
| **Main clause, although + clause** | The direction is clear, **although** the survey is a year old. |
| **While / whereas** | **While** finance is available, skills are not. |
| **Granted … Even so …** | **Granted**, the sample is small. **Even so**, I would act on it. |
| **albeit + phrase** | A smaller request, **albeit** the same project. |
| **Even if + clause** | **Even if** we doubled the budget, we could not staff it. |
| **No matter how / however** | **However** strong the case, the engineers are not hired. |

> **Grammar Note** — *Albeit* takes a phrase, not a clause: *albeit a smaller one*, never *albeit it
> is smaller*. *However* and *no matter how* take an adjective or adverb next: *however strong the
> case*, *no matter how quickly we hire*. And a concession placed first subordinates itself to what
> follows, which is why *although X, Y* argues for Y, while *Y, although X* ends on the doubt.

### 2 · Conceding and holding in two moves

**2A  Study the chart.**

| Move | Language |
|---|---|
| **Concede the fact** | Granted · Admittedly · It is true that · I accept that |
| **Limit the concession** | as far as it goes · on that point · in that respect |
| **Hold** | Even so · That said · Nonetheless · The point remains that |
| **Name the real question** | The question is not whether … but whether … |
| **Offer the condition** | which is why I am asking for X **subject to** Y |

> **Grammar Note** — *That said* and *even so* both reverse direction, but *that said* is softer and
> belongs after a long concession, while *even so* is short and lands harder. Neither takes *but*
> with it: *even so, but we should act* is wrong — one reversal is enough.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Arguing against your own proposal is admired in some business cultures and incomprehensible in
others. Where decisions are made by consensus before the meeting, a paper that contains its own
counter-case can be read as a sign the proposer is not ready, and the honest thing to do is to
resolve the objection privately first and bring a clean recommendation. Where meetings are
adversarial, pre-empting the objection is the single most effective move available, and a proposer
who has not done it looks unprepared. There are hierarchical differences too: in some
organizations only a senior person may name a weakness in a plan, and a junior colleague who does
it is read as criticizing their own manager. And the words differ — a direct *this is the weakness*
is plain speech in some languages and extraordinarily blunt in others, where the same meaning would
arrive as a question.

**Discuss:** Where you work, would a paper that argued against itself be read as strong or as
unfinished? Who would it be safe for?

>
> **Why it is material.** Our maintenance vacancy in Ostberg has been open for nine months at the
> band I may offer. **Even if** the full two million were approved today, that constraint would not
> move, because the constraint is not approval.
>
> **What would answer it.** Two engineers in post before delivery; a training clause in the supplier
> contract, drafted and attached; one cell rather than four.
>
> **What would not.** A larger budget, a faster delivery date, or an assumption that we will recruit
> later.
>
> **The figure that helps me, at the same weight.** The same survey reports that investment in
> **intangibles** — software, training, process — continues to grow across the EU, while being the
> part of investment most vulnerable to uncertainty. **Granted**, that is an argument for buying
> training before machines. **Even so**, one cell with trained people is a smaller and more
> defensible version of the same project, which is what I am now asking for.
>
> **Revised request, with its condition.** 640,000 now. The remaining three cells in eighteen
> months, **subject to** both engineers being in post and the training clause signed. If the
> engineers are refused, I withdraw the cell as well.
>
> *Source: EIB Investment Survey 2025 and EIB Investment Report 2025/2026, March 2026. Country
> figures are from the same survey. No EU-wide 2026 figure was published at the time of writing, and
> I have not estimated one.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Give the figure that helps you exactly the same number of words as the figure
> that damages you. Readers can feel the imbalance even when they cannot name it, and a counter-case
> that gets two lines next to your eight reads as a formality rather than an argument. If your
> weakness genuinely needs more space than your strength, you may be proposing the wrong thing.

### 3 · Write

Write **140 to 180 words**: a proposal that contains its own counter-case.

**Put the strongest objection in your second paragraph. Say whether it is material. Give what would
answer it and what would not. Give the helpful figure the same weight. End with a conditional
request.**

**Read your draft aloud to your partner first.** Hearing your own concession is the only reliable
way to tell whether it sounds confident or apologetic. Then check your own against the list, and
swap.
