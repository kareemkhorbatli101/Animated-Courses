# Starting Point · Three Habits for Arguing in Public
%%SUB Before Unit 1: how to be the person whose numbers hold up%%
%%GOALS

At B2 you can already say almost anything you need to say at work. What is hard now is being
believed by somebody who intends to check. This book is about the three sentences that make that
possible, and they are all short.

### 1 · What you already have

**1A  Work with a partner. Say one sentence of your own in each row, out loud.**
%%AUD CL5-00-01%%

%%FIG s00-f1 | A single review sheet with six headed boxes: hedging, reporting verbs, the passive of revision, unreal conditionals, nominalization and its opposite, and concession. Each box holds one example sentence taken from a working day.%%

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| **Hedging** | The figure **appears to** have stabilized, **although** the quarter is short. |
| **Reporting verbs** | The report **acknowledges that** the estimates changed method. |
| **Passive of revision** | The total **was revised** from 9.6 to 9.5 trillion. |
| **Unreal conditional** | **Had we known** the term was ninety days, we **would have** priced it differently. |
| **Un-nominalizing** | *Following the implementation of the policy* → **after we applied the policy** |
| **Concession** | **Granted**, the sample is small. **Even so**, the direction is consistent. |

**1B  Ask and answer with a partner.** One question using each structure. Then change roles.

### 2 · Habit one — the figure and its qualification travel together

**2A  Listen and repeat.** %%AUD CL5-00-02%%

> Earlier books in this series asked you to give a figure with its unit, its date, its source and
> its scope. This book adds the hardest part: **the thing the publisher said about it.**
>
> *Global growth is projected at 3.1 percent this year.* That is true, and it is incomplete,
> because the projection assumes a shipping strait reopens in March and oil averages ninety-six
> dollars a barrel. Those two assumptions are published. Almost nobody quotes them.
>
> **The rule:** if the publisher qualified the figure, the qualification is part of the figure. You
> do not get to leave it behind because it is inconvenient to say.

**2B  Add the qualification. Say each one aloud with a partner.**

1. Coverage rose from 42.8 to 52.4 percent. *(the method changed between the two reports)*
2. Insured losses reached about 107 billion dollars. *(the projection a year earlier was 145)*
3. Unemployment is 4.9 percent. *(that is weighted; the median rose)*

### 3 · Habits two and three

**3A  Listen and read.** %%AUD CL5-00-03%%

%%FIG s00-f2 | A card taped inside the cover of a folder, printed with three rules: the figure and its qualification travel together; say what would change your mind; and never let somebody else be the one who finds the weakness. Beneath each rule, one example sentence in smaller type.%%

> **Two — say what would change your mind.** This is the sentence that separates an argument from a
> position. *I would drop this if the volume forecast were cut again* tells the room you are
> reasoning. *I am confident in this* tells the room nothing at all, and invites somebody to test it.
>
> **Three — be the one who finds the weakness in your own case.** Unit 2 is built entirely on this.
> If there is a counter-example in your material and you do not raise it, the person who raises it
> owns the meeting, and they will raise it in the worst possible sentence.

**3B  Say it both ways. With a partner, give the weak version and then the strong one.**

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| *I'm sure about this.* | *I'd change my mind if __________ .* |
| *The data's good.* | *It's the best available; it was revised once, in __________ .* |
| *There isn't a counter-example.* | *There is one, and it's __________ . Here's why I still think __________ .* |

### 4 · One number, read three ways

**4A  Read. Then discuss the three questions with a partner.**

%%FIG s00-f3 | A chart with one bar and three different labels pointing at it, each giving a different reading of the same quantity, and a footnote in small type at the foot of the frame.%%

A trade body reports that 52.4 percent of the world's population has at least one social
protection benefit. Three people read it.

> The first says: **more than half the world is now covered.** True, and it is the first time that
> sentence has been available.
>
> The second says: **3.8 billion people are not covered at all.** Also true, from the same page.
>
> The third reads the front pages and says: **these figures are not strictly comparable with the
> last report's, because the method changed.** Also true, and stated by the publisher.

1. Which of the three is being dishonest? *(Careful.)*
2. Which one would you want in a meeting about where to spend money?
3. What does the third person have to add, so that the sentence is useful rather than only correct?

**4B  Think about it.** *Talk with a partner.* When was the last time you heard a figure given with
its qualification attached? Who was speaking, and did it make them more or less persuasive?
