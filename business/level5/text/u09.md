### 5 · Word study — markers of contrast and reconciliation

**5A  Study the chart.**

%%FIG s09-f6 | A chart of discourse markers in three groups: contrast with by contrast, conversely and on the other hand; reconciliation with that said, then again and at the same time; and precision with strictly speaking, in fact and more precisely — each with one example sentence.%%

| Group | Markers | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Contrast** | by contrast · conversely · on the other hand | **By contrast**, the electricity report uses 2025. |
| **Reconciliation** | that said · then again · at the same time | **That said**, neither figure corrects the other. |
| **Precision** | strictly speaking · in fact · more precisely | **Strictly speaking**, the multipliers measure different periods. |
| **Concession-to-point** | admittedly · granted · it is true that | **Admittedly** the gap looks like an error. |
| **Consequence** | accordingly · on that basis · it follows that | **Accordingly**, we cite the 2035 horizon. |

> **Grammar Note** — *On the other hand* needs a first hand. If you have not just given one side, it
> arrives from nowhere and readers look back for the sentence they missed. *That said* can stand
> alone, which is why it is the most useful of these and the most overused.

**5B  Join each pair with a marker from the chart.**

2. The two figures differ. Neither is a correction. *(reconciliation)*
3. The multipliers look contradictory. They measure different periods. *(precision)*
4. Our asset lasts fifteen years. We cite the longer horizon. *(consequence)*

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Marker | Position | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **by contrast** | front, after a comma | **By contrast**, the earlier report uses 2030. |
| **conversely** | front | **Conversely**, a shorter horizon gives a smaller ratio. |
| **on the other hand** | front, needs a first hand | **On the other hand**, the newer figure is newer. |
| **that said** | front, stands alone | **That said**, neither corrects the other. |
| **strictly speaking** | front or mid | The two figures, **strictly speaking**, measure different periods. |
| **in fact** | front or mid | They are **in fact** from different scenarios. |
| **accordingly** | front | **Accordingly**, we cite the longer horizon. |

> **Grammar Note** — These are adverbials, not conjunctions: they cannot join two clauses with a
> comma. *The figures differ, that said neither is wrong* is a comma splice. Use a full stop or a
> semicolon before them: *The figures differ. That said, neither is wrong.*

|---|---|

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Norms of citation differ more than people expect. In some business cultures naming a publication and
a month in a slide is standard and its absence is read as carelessness; in others it looks academic
and slightly distrustful, as though you expected to be doubted. Where seniority carries authority,
a figure given by a senior person may not be questioned at all, so the citation does no work in the
room and only matters later. There are differences in attitude to official institutions too: in some
countries an agency's number is treated as settled fact, and in others as one party's position.
Among engineers and accountants in any country, by contrast, the publication-level citation tends to
be expected — which means the right level of citation may depend less on the country than on who is
in the room.

**Discuss:** Where you work, would a four-part citation on a slide be read as rigour or as
pedantry? Who decides?

> ### Depot electrification — the figure on page one
> *T. Ek with W. Zhang · 7 May · one page*
>
> **Two figures, both correct.** The institution's **flagship outlook, stated policies scenario,
> autumn 2026** gives electricity's share of total final energy consumption rising from **23 percent
> today to nearly 30 percent by 2035**, with electricity demand growing **over four times faster**
> than total energy demand. Its dedicated **electricity report, February 2026**, gives the share
> rising from **21 percent in 2025 to 24 percent in 2030**, with demand growing **at least 2.5
> times as fast** as overall energy demand, and renewables and nuclear supplying about **50 percent
> of generation** by 2030.
>
> **Why they differ.** Different publications, scenarios, baseline years and horizons. **Strictly
> speaking** the two multipliers measure different periods, so they cannot be compared directly
> either. **Neither figure corrects the other.**
>
> **What we are not doing.** Our first draft averaged 23 and 21 and wrote **22 percent**. **That
> figure appears in no publication and cannot be checked against anything.** It is left crossed out
> on the attached working page so that the next person to do the same arithmetic finds it already
> done.
>
> **What chose the figure we are using.** The depot structure and its charging infrastructure have a
> **fifteen-year life**, so this is a **2035-horizon** decision. **Accordingly** we cite the flagship
> outlook: **23 percent today, nearly 30 percent by 2035, stated policies scenario, autumn 2026.**
>
> **The other figure, stated deliberately.** **By contrast**, the February electricity report's 24
> percent by 2030 implies a slower path on a shorter horizon from a lower baseline. We state it here
> so that a reader who has seen it knows we have too.
>
> **Where the newest numbers belong.** The **July mid-year update** reports demand growth of **3.6
> percent in 2026** and **3.8 percent in 2027**, after **3 percent in 2025**. These are growth
> rates rather than shares and they belong in the operating budget, not in a fifteen-year asset case.
>
> *Sources: IEA, World Energy Outlook 2026, stated policies scenario; IEA, Electricity 2026,
> February 2026; IEA Electricity Mid-Year Update, July 2026. All three publications are from the same
> institution.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Leave your own wrong working visible, crossed out, with a line saying why. It
> costs you two lines and a small amount of pride, and it stops the same error being made by the next

### 3 · Write

Write **140 to 180 words**: a page handling two correct figures from one institution.

**Give both with their publications, months, scenarios and horizons. Say why they differ. Say what you

deliberately. Use at least three discourse markers.**

**Read your draft aloud to your partner first.** Reading a citation out loud shows instantly whether
it is three short movements or an unreadable chain. Then check your own against the list, and swap.
