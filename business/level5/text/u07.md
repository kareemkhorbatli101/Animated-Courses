### 5 · Word study — participle clauses

**5A  Study the chart.**

%%FIG s07-f6 | A chart of participle clause types: present participle for an active meaning, past participle for a passive one, having plus participle for an earlier event, and a note on the dangling participle with one wrong example struck through, each with a short example drawn from a ranking.%%

| Form | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **-ing (active)** | **Covering** 139 economies, the index places countries in bands. | which covers |
| **-ed (passive)** | **Built** from 79 indicators, it is precise only at the top. | which is built |
| **having + -ed** | **Having risen** five places, the UAE is now 25th. | after it rose |
| **having been + -ed** | **Having been** revised, the figure now reads 101. | after being revised |
| **with + noun + -ing** | **With Europe holding** 14 of the top 25, the band is crowded. | adds a second subject |

> **Grammar Note** — A participle clause takes the subject of the main clause. *Built from 79
> indicators, the index is precise only at the top* is correct, because the index is what is built.
> *Built from 79 indicators, we should not read it closely* says that **we** are built from 79
> indicators. That is a dangling participle, it is the commonest error in formal English writing, and
> it is almost always produced by shortening a sentence that was correct.

2. The index is built from 79 indicators. It is precise only at the top.

4. Europe holds 14 of the top 25. The band is crowded. *(use* with *)*

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **-ing, main clause** | **Covering** 139 economies, it is broad rather than precise. |
| **-ed, main clause** | **Published** in September, the figures are a year old by autumn. |
| **having + -ed** | **Having entered** at 74th, Bhutan has no trend yet. |
| **main clause + -ing** | The index ranks 139 economies, **drawing** on 79 indicators. |
| **with + noun + -ing** | **With Europe holding** 14 places, the top band is crowded. |
| **not + -ing** | **Not having** salary data, the index cannot answer our question. |

> **Grammar Note** — Participle clauses are compression, and compression is what makes formal
> English readable or impenetrable depending on how much of it you use. One at the front of a
> sentence is elegant. Two in the same sentence, with different subjects, is unreadable, and nobody
> will tell you.

### 2 · Avoiding the dangling participle

**2A  Study the chart.**

| Wrong | Why | Right |
|---|---|---|
| **Built** from 79 indicators, **we** should not over-read it. | *we* are not built from indicators | **Built** from 79 indicators, **the index** should not be over-read. |
| **Having risen** five places, **the slide** claims momentum. | the slide did not rise | **Having risen** five places, **the UAE** is now 25th. |
| **Reading** the annex, **the weightings** were surprising. | the weightings did not read | **Reading** the annex, **I** found the weightings surprising. |

> **Grammar Note** — The test takes four seconds. Find the participle, find the first noun after the
> comma, and ask whether that noun did the thing. If it did not, you must either change the subject
> of the main clause or write two sentences. Writing two sentences is almost always the better
> answer.

### %%AUG c%% 2 · Clear English — saying a rank at the right level of precision

**2A  Listen to the model.** %%AUD CL5-07-09%%

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

An index position can be a matter of national pride, policy and budget, and that changes what can be
said about it in a meeting. In some countries a rise in an international innovation or competitiveness
ranking is announced by a ministry, reported in the press and used to justify spending, so a foreign
colleague who calls a five-place move statistically meaningless is contradicting a national
achievement, not making a methodological remark. In others these indices are barely noticed. Inside
companies the pattern repeats: some organizations reward the vivid comparative claim and treat
qualification as timidity, while in others any slide that over-reads a figure will be quietly
remembered. And the politeness strategies differ sharply — in some languages the way to disagree with
a number is to ask how it was built, which in English can sound like an accusation rather than a
question.

**Discuss:** Where you work, how is a national ranking treated? Could you question one in front of a
colleague from that country?

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Location note — the new engineering team
> *C. Obi · 12 October · one page*
>
> **What the index is.** The Global Innovation Index 2026 ranks **139 economies** using **79
> indicators**, published **29 September 2026**. **Built from 79 indicators, it is reliable about
> bands and loose about individual places**, and most so in the crowded middle of the table.
>
> **What I would believe from it without checking.** Switzerland first for a **16th consecutive
> year**; Sweden second, the United States third, the Republic of Korea fourth, Singapore fifth;
> Europe holding **14 of the top 25** places. **Having entered the index at 74th**, Bhutan has a
> starting band and no direction, because a first appearance cannot have one.
>
> **What the circulated slide claims, and what the figure supports.** The slide shows the **United
> Arab Emirates at 25th, up five places**, and uses it to claim an improvement of about ten percent
> in the business environment. **The rank is correct and the claim is not supported.** A five-place
> movement in that part of the table can be produced by small changes in two or three indicators, by
> a country improving its statistical reporting, or by the entry or exit of other economies. All
> three are real; none is the claim being made.
>
> **A guide to intended precision.** The published coverage is **139 economies**; one of the
> publisher's own pages gives **about 140**. **If the publisher rounds the denominator, we should not
> be arguing about five places in the numerator.**
>
> **What I am recommending.** A shortlist of three economies, all in the **top 25 band**, listed
> alphabetically so that no order is read into it. The decision between them to be made on four
> criteria of ours: engineering salary, visa processing time, our existing customer base, and flight
> connections to Ostberg. **None of the four is in the index**, which is not a criticism of it: it
> was built to compare innovation systems, not to site a team of eleven engineers.
>
> *Source: WIPO, Global Innovation Index 2026, press release 29 September 2026. Ranks, indicator
> count and coverage are the publisher's own.*

> **Writer's Tip** — When you decline to use somebody's figure, say what you would believe from the
> same source. A note that only refuses reads as obstruction; a note that refuses one claim and
> accepts three others from the same page reads as judgment, and judgment is the thing you are
> actually being paid for.

### 3 · Write

Write **140 to 180 words**: a note using a composite index to support a decision.

**Say how many indicators and economies it covers. Name what you would believe from it. Say which
claim it does not support and why. Use at least two participle clauses, with the right subjects. List
criteria of your own that the index does not contain.**

**Read your draft aloud to your partner first.** Reading a participle clause out loud is the fastest
way to catch a dangling subject. Then check your own against the list, and swap.
