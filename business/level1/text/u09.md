**1B  Underline the three *going to* forms and the one *will*.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive** | am / is / are + going to + verb | I'**m going to** call her. |
| **Negative** | am not / isn't / aren't + going to | We'**re not going to** make it. |
| **Question** | Am / Is / Are + subject + going to | **Are** you **going to** be there? |
| **Prediction from evidence** | | Look at the traffic — we'**re going to** be late. |

> **Grammar Note** — *Going to* is for a plan you **already have** or something you can **see
> coming**. *Will* is for a decision you make **as you speak**. Both are correct English; they are
> not the same sentence.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Hosting a visitor is one of the places where cultures differ most, and where goodwill is easiest
to lose by accident. In some countries a host meets the plane, carries the bag and stays until
bedtime, and leaving a visitor alone at the hotel is a failure. In others the visitor expects to
be left alone and finds constant company exhausting. Dinner may be compulsory and three hours
long, or may not happen at all. Gifts are expected in some places and awkward in others. And in
some business cultures the first evening is where the real conversation happens, and the meeting
the next morning only confirms it.

**Discuss:** When you visit another company, what do you expect your host to do? What would make
you uncomfortable?
