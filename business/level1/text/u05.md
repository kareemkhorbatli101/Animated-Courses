### 1 · *there is* and *there are*

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD CL1-05-06%%

**1B  Underline every *there is* and *there are* in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive** | There **is** a pallet. / There'**s** | There **are** four pallets. |
| **Negative** | There **isn't** a label. | There **aren't** any boxes. |
| **Question** | **Is there** a problem? | **Are there** any pallets left? |
| **Short answer** | Yes, there is. / No, there isn't. | Yes, there are. / No, there aren't. |

> **Grammar Note** — Use *there is / there are* to say that something **exists**. Use *it is /
> they are* to say something **about** a thing you have already named. *There is a pallet in A4.
> **It** is from Juno.*

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

How a workplace reacts to a missing item says a lot about it. In some workplaces the first
question is *who?* and in others it is *where?*, and people behave very differently depending on
which one they expect. In some countries admitting a mistake quickly is respected and ends the
matter; in others it is written down and remembered, so people stay quiet and the search takes
longer. Anders's sentence — *nobody's in trouble, I just need to know* — is doing real work, and
it is not equally easy to say everywhere.

**Discuss:** Where you work, what happens when somebody makes a small mistake? Does the system
find out quickly or slowly?
