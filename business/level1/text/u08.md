**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive, regular** | verb + -ed | We **checked** it. She **reported** it. |
| **Positive, irregular** | a different word | He **found** it. They **sent** it. |
| **Negative** | didn't + verb | We **didn't check** it. *(not didn't checked)* |
| **Question** | Did + subject + verb | **Did** you **check** it? |
| **Short answer** | | Yes, I did. / No, I didn't. |
| ***be*** | was / were | It **was** late. They **were** here. |

> **Grammar Note** — After *did* and *didn't*, the verb goes back to its plain form: *did you
> **find** it?*, not *did you found it*. This is the error that survives longest, and it is the
> easiest one to hear.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

What a report is **for** differs. In some organizations it exists to find the cause and change the
system, and naming a person is beside the point. In others it exists to establish responsibility,
and a report without a name is seen as incomplete or even as a cover-up. In some countries saying
"I was wrong" quickly ends the matter; in others it is recorded and used later, so people describe
events in the passive and nobody says *I*. None of this is about honesty. It is about what people
expect a written record to do.

**Discuss:** Where you work, does a report name people? What happens to the person named?
