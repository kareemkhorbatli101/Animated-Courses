%%SUB Asking for something, offering something, and the no that keeps the relationship%%

Nour gets about fifteen requests a day. She says yes to eleven, no to three, and *not this week*
to one. The three nos take longer to write than the eleven yeses, and she has never regretted the
time.

### 1 · *can*, *could*, *would you mind*

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD CL1-10-06%%

> **Anders:** Any chance you could find me cover for Thursday and Friday?
> **Nour:** Thursday yes. Friday no.
> **Anders:** That was quick.
> **Nour:** It's quick because I checked yesterday. Friday is the training day — there is nobody
> in the building who can drive a forklift.
> **Anders:** Then I'll run Friday with three.
> **Nour:** Or I can move the training. Which would you rather?

*Nour says no in three words and then offers something. Why not offer first?*

**1B  Underline the requests and the offers in 1A.**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Asking | Offering |
|---|---|---|
| **can** | **Can** you cover Thursday? | **Can** I help? |
| **could** | **Could** you cover Thursday? | **Could** I take that for you? |
| **would you mind** | **Would you mind** covering Thursday? | — |
| **shall / I'll** | — | **Shall** I call them? · **I'll** do it. |

> **Grammar Note** — *Could* is not the past of *can* here; it is the polite form. And *would you
> mind* takes *-ing*, and the answer is confusing: **"No, not at all"** means **yes, I will**.
> If you are not sure, answer with words, not with yes or no: *Of course, I'll do it now.*

**1D  Ask and answer with a partner. Three each.** Ask for something small, something medium and
something you expect to be refused.

**1E  Write five requests** you could really make this week, each at a different level of

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Saying no is one of the biggest differences between business cultures. In some places a direct
*no* is efficient and respected. In others it is almost never said, and the real answer is carried
by *that would be difficult*, *we will study it*, or a long silence — and a visitor who hears
those as a *maybe* will wait for months. In some workplaces you may refuse your manager; in
others you may not, and the way to refuse is to accept and then raise a problem. And in some
cultures a request made in front of other people cannot be refused at all, which is why careful
people ask privately.

**Discuss:** How do people say no where you work? Have you ever heard a no and thought it was a
maybe?

>
> Hi Nour,
>
> Could you find me cover on the early shift for **Thursday 19** and **Friday 20 November**?

> **Writer's Tip** — Four things make a request easy to say yes to: **how big it is**, **by when**,
> **why that date**, and **what you will do if the answer is no**. The fourth one sounds like
> giving up. It is the opposite: it tells the reader they can refuse without hurting you, and
> people answer those messages first.
