**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Countable | Uncountable |
|---|---|---|
| **Singular / plural** | one bolt, two bolts | steel *(no plural)* |
| **a / an** | **a** bolt | — |
| **Questions** | How **many** bolts? | How **much** steel? |
| **Quantity words** | many, a few, several | much, a little, a great deal of |
| **Both** | some, any, a lot of, plenty of | some, any, a lot of, plenty of |

> **Grammar Note** — Some nouns are countable in one meaning and uncountable in another: *two
> coffees* (two cups) but *how much coffee* (the substance); *three papers* (documents) but *how
> much paper* (the material). Business English is full of these, and the unit always tells you
> which one you are in.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Numbers are written differently in different places and this is a real and expensive problem. In
much of the world 1.200 means one thousand two hundred; in much of the rest it means one point
two. Dates reverse: 03/04 is 3 April in many countries and 4 March in others. Some countries use
a space — 1 200 — which is the international standard and the hardest to misread. Currency symbols
are shared: the dollar sign is used by more than twenty countries. And in some business cultures it is normal
to round a price in conversation and precise only in writing.

**Discuss:** How do you write one thousand two hundred? What would you do if a supplier wrote it
the other way?
