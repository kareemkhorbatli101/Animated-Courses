### 1 · Past simple with *because* and *so*

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD CL1-11-06%%

> **Customer:** When were you going to tell us?
> **Yara:** Today. I should have told you on the tenth.
> **Customer:** Why didn't you?
> **Yara:** Because I was waiting for a date I could promise, and I waited six days too long.
> **Customer:** … All right. That's an honest answer.
> **Yara:** It's also the mistake. The ship isn't the mistake; the six days are.

*Yara separates two things. What are they, and why does it matter?*

**1B  Underline the past simple verbs. Which sentence gives a cause?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **because + clause** | It is late **because the ship was rerouted**. |
| **so + clause** | The ship was rerouted, **so it is late**. |
| **due to + noun** | The delay is **due to a diversion**. |
| **should have + past participle** | I **should have told** you on the tenth. |

> **Grammar Note** — *Because* is followed by a **clause** (subject + verb). *Due to* and *because
> of* are followed by a **noun**. *Because of the ship was rerouted* is a very common mistake and
> an easy one to hear.

**1D  Ask and answer with a partner. Three each.**

1. Why was something late at your work recently?
2. What should somebody have told you earlier?
3. What did you do about it?

**1E  Write five sentences** about a delay. Use *because*, *so*, *due to* and one *should have*.

### 2 · Apologizing properly

**2A  Study the chart.**

| Part | Example |
|---|---|
| **Apologize once, for the right thing** | I'm sorry we didn't tell you on the tenth. |
| **Do not apologize for the weather** | *not:* "I'm sorry the sea is rough." |
| **Say the fact** | The shipment will arrive on the 28th. |
| **Say what you are doing** | We have booked immediate clearance. |
| **Offer, if you can** | We will air-freight 200 kg at our cost. |
| **Say when you will speak again** | I will call you Thursday, news or not. |

> **Grammar Note** — *I'm sorry* + *that* clause, or *I'm sorry for* + noun or *-ing*: *I'm sorry
> **for the delay***, *I'm sorry **for not telling you***. *I'm sorry for tell you* is wrong.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Apology works very differently across cultures, and this one causes real damage. In some
countries saying *I'm sorry* accepts responsibility and may have legal weight, so companies train
people never to say it. In others an apology is a social repair that costs nothing and refusing to
give one is an insult. The length matters too: in some places a short apology sounds cold, and in
others a long one sounds insincere or even manipulative. And in some business cultures bad news
is delivered by the most senior person available, while in others it comes from whoever is
closest to the work.

**Discuss:** Where you work, who gives bad news to a customer? Do people say *I'm sorry* easily?
