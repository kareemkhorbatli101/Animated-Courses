### 1 · The present simple

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD CL1-02-06%%

> **Client:** So what do you do, exactly?
> **Sofia:** We take your numbers and tell you what they mean.
> **Client:** That's it?
> **Sofia:** That's it. Most companies have the numbers already. They don't have the time.
> **Client:** Nobody has ever said it that short.

*Is a short answer always better? When is it not? Why do you think so?*

**1B  Underline the present simple verbs in 1A. Which one has *-s*?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Facts** | We **move** goods. Water **boils** at 100 degrees. |
| **Routines** | The trucks **leave** at six. She **works** on Tuesdays. |
| **Negative** | We **don't** sell to the public. He **doesn't** work here. |
| **Question** | **Do** you export? **Does** she work in finance? |
| **Short answer** | Yes, we **do**. No, she **doesn't**. |

> **Grammar Note** — The *-s* goes on **he, she, it** only, and it disappears in questions and
> negatives: *she works* → *does she work?* → *she doesn't work*. Writing *does she works* is the
> most common mistake there is at this level, and it is easy to hear.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

How much a company says about itself varies. In some business cultures a first meeting begins
with the company's history, its size and its awards, and going straight to the price is rude. In
others, the first question is the price and the history is a waste of everybody's afternoon. In
some places saying *we don't do that* is honest and respected; in others it is heard as refusing
to try, and the expected answer is *let me see what I can do*.

**Discuss:** Where you work, how long is a first meeting before business starts? What happens if
you skip it?
