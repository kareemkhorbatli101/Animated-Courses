### 1 · The present continuous

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the question below.** %%AUD CL1-07-06%%

> **Daniel:** Where are we?
> **Wei:** Nine on track, one on hold, one delayed.
> **Daniel:** What's delayed?
> **Wei:** 2210. The driver's sick. I'm re-planning it now — I'm waiting for Anders to tell me
> whether Thursday works.
> **Daniel:** And the one on hold?
> **Wei:** Customs. Ines is chasing it. That one we don't control.

*Wei says "I'm re-planning" and "we don't control". Why not "I re-plan" and "we aren't
controlling"?*

**1B  Underline the present continuous verbs in 1A. What are they all about?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive** | am / is / are + -ing | I'**m waiting**. She'**s loading**. |
| **Negative** | am not / isn't / aren't + -ing | He **isn't coming** today. |
| **Question** | Am / Is / Are + subject + -ing | **Are** you **working** on it? |
| **Right now** | | The truck **is leaving** — I can see it. |
| **Around now** | | This week I'**m working** from the yard. |

> **Grammar Note** — The present continuous is for **now** and **around now**. The present simple
> is for **always** and **usually**. *The trucks leave at six* is the rule; *the trucks are
> leaving* is this morning.

### 2 · Present simple against present continuous

**2A  Study the chart.**

| Present simple | Present continuous |
|---|---|
| The trucks **leave** at six. *(every day)* | The trucks **are leaving** now. *(this moment)* |
| Anders **works** earlies. *(always)* | Anders **is working** nights this week. *(temporary)* |
| We **don't ship** on Sundays. *(a rule)* | We **aren't shipping** today. *(today only)* |
| I **check** every order. *(my job)* | I**'m checking** this one now. *(right now)* |

> **Grammar Note** — Some verbs are almost never continuous: *know, need, want, mean, understand,
> belong, cost, have* (= own). *I'm knowing* and *it's costing fifty euros* are both wrong.

**2B  Listen and repeat.** %%AUD CL1-07-07%%

**2C  Choose the right form.**

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Saying "I don't know yet" is easy in some workplaces and dangerous in others. In some cultures a
manager who asks "is it fine?" expects reassurance first and detail later, and answering "I don't
know" sounds like a lack of control. In others, reassuring somebody before you have checked is
the serious offense. Short daily meetings are normal in many companies and feel like surveillance
in others. And in some places saying a colleague's job is stuck is heard as blaming that
colleague, even when it is only a fact.

**Discuss:** Where you work, what happens if you say "I don't know yet"? Who is allowed to say it?
