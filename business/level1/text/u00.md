# Starting Point · Your Name, and the Numbers
%%SUB Before Unit 1: saying who you are so that somebody three time zones away gets it right%%
%%GOALS
Say and spell your name, your company and your job so a stranger can write them down
Say numbers, dates and prices clearly, and ask somebody to repeat one
Use *be* to say who you are, where you work and what you do
%%

A name said quickly is a name said twice. Everyone in this book has had their name written down
wrong at least once, and all of them now spell it out the first time without being asked. That is
not politeness. It is faster.

## 2 · Numbers that people get wrong

**2A  Listen and repeat.** %%AUD CL1-00-03%%

| | |
|---|---|
| **13** / **30** | thir-**TEEN** / **THIR**-ty |
| **14** / **40** | four-**TEEN** / **FOR**-ty |
| **15** / **50** | fif-**TEEN** / **FIF**-ty |
| **16** / **60** | six-**TEEN** / **SIX**-ty |
| **17** / **70** | seven-**TEEN** / **SEV**-en-ty |
| **18** / **80** | eigh-**TEEN** / **EIGH**-ty |
| **19** / **90** | nine-**TEEN** / **NINE**-ty |

> **Clear English** — The difference is the **stress**, not the ending. *Thirteen* is strong at
> the end. *Thirty* is strong at the start. If you are not sure the other person heard you, say
> the number a second way: **"Fifteen. One five."**

**2B  Listen. Write the number you hear.** %%AUD CL1-00-04%%

## 3 · *be*: who you are, where you work, what you do

**3A  Study the chart.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **I am** / I'm | I'**m** Yara. I'**m** in operations. |
| **You are** / you're | You'**re** the new supervisor, aren't you? |
| **He / She is** | She'**s** in the warehouse. He'**s** not here today. |
| **We are** / we're | We'**re** a freight company. |
| **They are** / they're | They'**re** our supplier in Juno. |
| **Questions** | **Are** you Anders? **Is** she in today? **Where are** you from? |
| **Negative** | I'**m not** in sales. He **isn't** the manager. |

> **Grammar Note** — English needs the verb. *I engineer* is not a sentence; *I **am** an
> engineer* is. And a job takes **a** or **an**: *I am **an** operations coordinator*.

**3B  Complete with *am*, *is* or *are*.**

1. I ______ in the finance team.
2. Anders ______ the warehouse supervisor.
3. We ______ a small company — twenty people.
4. ______ you the person I spoke to yesterday?
5. The parts ______ not here yet.
6. Where ______ your office?

**3C  Ask and answer with a partner. Three each.**

1. What is your job?
2. Which company are you with?
3. Where is your office?

**Then discuss:** when somebody asks what you do, what do you say first — your job,
your company, or something else? Why?

**3D  Write five sentences about yourself.** Use *I am*, *I am not*, and one question you would
ask a new colleague.

## 4 · Putting it together

**4A  Listen and read.** %%AUD CL1-00-05%%

> **Yara:** Lindmark Freight, Yara Haddad speaking.
> **Jun-seo:** Hello. This is Jun-seo Park, from Juno Textiles.
> **Yara:** Sorry — can you spell your first name?
> **Jun-seo:** J, U, N, hyphen, S, E, O. Jun-seo.
> **Yara:** Jun-seo. Thank you. And you said Juno Textiles?
> **Jun-seo:** That's right. I'm calling about order four four one seven.
> **Yara:** Four four one seven. Let me find it.

**4B  Answer.**

1. Who answers the phone, and what does she say first?
2. What does Yara ask for, and why does she ask for it?
3. How does Jun-seo spell the hyphen?
4. What does Yara do with the order number?

**4C  Role-play with a partner.** Use your own name, your own company and a number of your own.
Your partner must write all three down and read them back. Then change roles.
