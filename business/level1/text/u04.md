**1C  Study the chart.**

| Form | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|

| **Please + verb** | softer, but still an instruction | **Please** hold. |

> **Grammar Note** — An imperative is not rude in a sign or a system message. It **is** often too
> direct to a person, especially a customer. To a person, use *can / could / would you*.

### 2 · *can* and *could* for requests

**2A  Study the chart.**

| | Asking | Offering |
|---|---|---|
| **can** | **Can** you spell that? | **Can** I take a message? |
| **could** *(softer)* | **Could** you repeat that, please? | **Could** I ask who's calling? |
| **would you mind** *(softest)* | **Would you mind** spelling that? | |
| **I'll** *(offer)* | | **I'll** put you through. |

> **Grammar Note** — After *can*, *could* and *would you mind*, there is no *to*: *could you
> **repeat** that*, not *could you to repeat*. And *would you mind* takes *-ing*: *would you mind
> **spelling** that?*

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Phone habits differ more than almost anything else at work. In some countries a call starts with
the company name and nothing else; in others you give your own name first, or your department.
Some cultures expect two or three minutes of greeting before business; others treat that as
wasting the caller's money. In some workplaces a direct call is normal and an email is slow; in
others calling without arranging it first is an interruption and almost rude. And voicemail is
used constantly in some countries and almost never in others.

**Discuss:** In your country, what do you say when you answer a work call? Do people leave
voicemail?
