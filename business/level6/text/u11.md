### 5 · Word study — proportion and ratio

**5A  Study the chart.**

%%FIG t11-f6 | A chart of proportion expressions: for every X, Y; twice as fast as; a gap of X points opened between; half as much again; the ratio of X to Y; and X per Y — each with one example sentence drawn from pay and output figures.%%

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| **for every X, Y** | **For every** 2% of extra output, a little under 1% reached pay. |
| **twice / half as fast as** | Output grew **about twice as fast as** pay. |
| **a gap of X points opened between** | **A gap of 14 points opened between** the two over 25 years. |
| **half as much again** | Output rose **half as much again** as pay. |
| **the ratio of X to Y** | **The ratio of** productivity growth **to** wage growth is about 2 to 1. |
| **X per Y** | output **per** hour; cost **per** tonne |

> **Grammar Note** — *Twice as fast as* compares rates; *twice as much as* compares quantities. A
> common error is to mix them: *output grew twice as much as pay* says something about totals, not
> speeds, and over twenty-five years the two are very different claims. If you are not sure which you
> mean, use *for every X, Y*, which forces you to say both quantities.

**1B  Underline the proportion statement. What two quantities does it tie together?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **for every X, Y** | **For every** 2% of output, under 1% reached pay. |
| **X has risen N% since YEAR** | Productivity **has risen 29% since** 1999. |
| **a gap of N points has opened** | **A gap of 14 points has opened** between them. |
| **X grew about twice as fast as Y** | Output **grew about twice as fast as** pay. |
| **the ratio is about N to 1** | **The ratio is about** 2 **to** 1. |
| **cumulatively / over the period** | **Cumulatively**, the two are 14 points apart. |

> **Grammar Note** — Use the present perfect with *since* for an accumulated divergence: *a gap has
> opened since 1999*. The past simple with *since* is wrong — *a gap opened since 1999* — because
> *since* requires a period running up to now. With a closed period use the past simple and *between*:
> *the gap widened between 1999 and 2024*.

### 2 · Percentage points and percent

**2A  Study the chart.**

| Wrong or ambiguous | Correct |
|---|---|
| We moved the increase up by one percent, from 4 to 5. | We moved it up **one percentage point**, from 4% to 5%. |
| The rate rose one percent, from 4 to 5. | The rate rose **25%** in relative terms, or **one point** in absolute terms. |
| The margin fell two percent. | The margin fell **two points**, from 9% to 7%. |
| Coverage rose ten percent, from 42.8 to 52.4. | Coverage rose **9.6 points**, which is **22%** in relative terms. |

> **Grammar Note** — A percentage point is the unit by which two percentages differ; a percent is a
> proportion of the original figure. Four to five is one point and twenty-five percent. Both
> statements are true and they describe different things: the first is what the pay table shows, the
> second is what the money costs. Papers that use the wrong one are not rounding errors away; they
> are understating costs by a quarter.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Pay is set in very different ways, and a paper like this means different things depending on how. In
countries with sectoral or national collective bargaining, the envelope may be negotiated outside the
company entirely, so a management paper describing a twenty-five-year divergence is a contribution to
somebody else's negotiation and may be read as such. Where pay is individual and confidential, the
same figures can be destabilizing, because colleagues who compare them have no way of knowing where
they stand. There are differences in what may be published internally too: in some countries pay
ranges and gender gap figures must be disclosed by law, and in others disclosing them would breach
privacy expectations. And the productivity comparison is contested everywhere, because output per
hour is measured for an economy and pay is experienced by a person.

**Discuss:** Where you work, who sets the envelope? Would a twenty-five-year comparison help or
harm?

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Pay round paper — page 1
> *N. Khalil · 14 November*
>
> **What this paper is not.** **This is not a request for a larger envelope.** I am asking for the
> **4%** agreed in September. The figures below describe twenty-five years and cannot be addressed in
> a pay round. They are here because they explain **how 4% will be heard**.
>
> **The long view.** In high-income countries, **productivity rose 29% between 1999 and 2024** and
> **real wages rose about 15%** over the same period. **A gap of 14 percentage points has opened
> since 1999.** **For every 2% of additional output per hour in that period, a little under 1%
> reached pay.** Both figures are positive; the divergence is the finding. **This is a figure for
> high-income countries as a group and is not a measurement of Lindmark**, and I have not adjusted it
> for our sector because I cannot.
>
> **The recent view, which is better, and which I am including deliberately.** Global real wages
> **fell 0.9% in 2022**, **recovered 1.8% in 2023**, and were **about 2.7% in 2024** on preliminary
> data — **the largest gain in more than fifteen years**, and **2.3% excluding China**. **Leaving
> this out would make the paper an argument rather than a briefing.**
>
> **Three figures about the distribution.** Wage inequality **has narrowed in about two thirds of
> countries since 2000**. Close to **22%** of wage workers in **low-income countries** are low-paid.
> **Men earn more than women in every country income group and across the entire wage
> distribution** — I quote that in full rather than an average gap, because **an average gap can
> close at the median while widening at the bottom**, and we have a bottom.
>
> **One arithmetic correction, to the management pack.** The pack describes a move from **4% to 5%**
> as "a one percent increase". It is **one percentage point**, and **25% more money**. The pack has
> said this the wrong way **twice in three years**, and on both occasions the committee discussed a
> cost a quarter smaller than the real one.
>
> **What I am asking for.** The 4%. **The 14-point gap printed beside it**, in the letter to staff and
> not only here. And the pack corrected before it circulates.
>
> *Source: ILO, Global Wage Report 2024–25, for all external figures.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Put the figure that weakens your case in your own paper, in the same type size.
> Nour's recent-wage figures make her long-run argument less dramatic and they make the paper
> unanswerable, because the obvious objection has already been printed and dealt with. A paper that
> only contains supporting evidence is read as advocacy, and advocacy gets discounted by whatever the
> reader guesses you left out.
