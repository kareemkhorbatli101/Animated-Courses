Hanna's note has to do two things that pull in opposite directions. The first is to tell the board

### 5 · Word study — verbs of allegation

**5A  Study the chart.**

%%FIG t04-f6 | A chart of pleading verbs arranged by what they commit the writer to: allege and claim for an assertion yet to be proved; contend and maintain for a position argued; submit for a proposition offered to a decision-maker; plead for a formal filing; and state and record for undisputed fact — each with one example.%%

| Verb | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **allege** | asserts something not yet established | **It is alleged that** the state expropriated the asset. |
| **claim** | the same, less formal, also means *demand* | The investor **claims** compensation. |
| **contend** | puts forward a position in argument | The joint venture **would contend that** no notice was given. |
| **maintain** | holds a position, often against resistance | The state **maintains** that the decree was lawful. |
| **submit** | offers a proposition to a decision-maker | **We would submit that** jurisdiction is established. |
| **plead** | formally files a case or defense | The respondent has yet to **plead**. |
| **state / record** | reports undisputed fact | The decree **was published** on 11 June. |

> **Grammar Note** — These are not interchangeable degrees of politeness. *Alleges* signals that the
> speaker has not proved the thing; *states* signals that it is not in dispute. Writing *the state
> expropriated the terminal* in your own board paper converts your case into your assertion, and if
> the document is ever disclosed that is the sentence you will be asked about.

4. The decree was published on 11 June. *(leave plain — and say why)*

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **it is alleged that** | **It is alleged that** the decree was taken without notice. |
| **X contends that** | The claimant **contends that** the treaty applies. |
| **X would submit that** | We **would submit that** jurisdiction is established. |
| **X maintains that** | The state **maintains that** the decree was lawful. |
| **it is common ground that** | **It is common ground that** the decree was published. |
| **X has yet to plead** | The respondent **has yet to plead**. |

> **Grammar Note** — *It is common ground that* is the most valuable sentence in a dispute document
> and the one lawyers reach for first, because it tells the reader exactly how much of the paper is
> safe. Everything after it can be argued; everything in it cannot. Non-lawyers almost never use it,
> and their documents are harder to defend for exactly that reason.

### 2 · The passive of process

**2A  Study the chart.**

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| **a request was registered** | The request **was registered** on 3 July. |
| **a tribunal was constituted** | The tribunal **was constituted** in November. |
| **proceedings were suspended** | Proceedings **were suspended** by agreement. |
| **an award was rendered** | An award **was rendered** in favor of the respondent. |
| **the case was discontinued** | The case **was discontinued** at the claimant's request. |
| **a decision is pending** | A decision on jurisdiction **is pending**. |

> **Grammar Note** — Here the passive is correct and the active would be wrong, because the actor is
> an institution performing a step and naming it adds nothing: *the secretariat registered the
> request* tells the reader less, not more. The test from Unit 1 still applies — *if this does not
> happen, who has failed?* — and for a procedural step the answer is nobody, which is precisely why
> the passive is right.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Investor-state arbitration is itself contested, and how it is spoken about varies enormously. In
some countries the mechanism is regarded as ordinary commercial protection and bringing a claim is
unremarkable; in others it is politically charged, treaties have been terminated, and a company that
files is understood to have ended its relationship with the host state permanently. There are
differences in what an in-house lawyer is expected to produce: in some business cultures counsel is
expected to give a probability of success and a number, and declining to do so is read as evasion,
while in others a written probability would be regarded as professionally reckless. Attitudes to
internal documents differ too — some organizations assume everything may one day be read by an
opponent, and write accordingly from the first draft.

**Discuss:** Where you work, is an internal document written as though somebody else may read it?
Should it be?

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Note to the board — the terminal concession
> *H. Lindqvist · 2 July · one page*
>
> **1 · Common ground.** The concession under which our 30% joint venture operates the bonded
> terminal **was canceled by decree on 11 June**. The decree **was published**. The joint venture
> **has been denied access** to the site since **14 June**. **None of this is in dispute.**
>
> **2 · What would be alleged.** **It would be alleged that** the cancellation constitutes an
> expropriation without compensation. **The joint venture would contend that** the state failed to
> accord fair and equitable treatment, no notice or hearing having preceded the decree. **We would
> submit that** the applicable treaty confers jurisdiction and that the state's consent to
> arbitration is established. **Nothing in this section is a statement of fact by me**; each sentence
> states a position that a tribunal, and only a tribunal, could determine.
>
> **3 · The published caseload.** **60** new registrations in the fiscal year just ended — **57**
> under the Convention and **3** under the Additional Facility Rules — against **67** the previous
> year; **1,118** cases registered since 1972; **363** cases administered during the year, **the
> highest in the institution's history**; **83** proceedings concluded, of which **37% settled or
> were otherwise discontinued and 63% were decided by a tribunal**; bilateral investment treaties the
> basis of consent in **62%** of new cases; oil and gas **25%** and mining **18%** of new cases.
>
> **4 · Why one figure appears twice.** **Several firm summaries of the same statistics report 57 new
> cases**, omitting the three Additional Facility filings. **Both figures describe the same year and
> neither is an error.** I print both so that meeting the other one changes nothing.
>
> **5 · What these statistics do not describe.** **A bonded terminal is in neither of the two largest
> sectors**, so those percentages describe other people's disputes. **No average duration and no
> average recovery is published**, and **I am not supplying either**. The closest figure the
> statistics contain to an answer on timing is that **37% of concluded proceedings ended without a
> decision on the merits** — which is not an answer to the question the board will ask.
>
> **6 · What has not happened.** No request **has been registered**. No tribunal **has been
> constituted**. The respondent **has yet to plead** anything, because there is nothing to plead to.
>
> *Source: ICSID Caseload Statistics for the fiscal year ended 30 June 2026 and the 2026 Annual
> Report. The 57 appears in secondary summaries of the same statistics.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Put the sentence *nothing in this section is a statement of fact by me* at the
> end of your pleading section, not at the start. A reader who meets it first skims the section as
> boilerplate; a reader who meets it last has already read nine carefully hedged sentences and
> understands exactly what the line is doing.

### 3 · Write

Write **150 to 190 words**: a note on a dispute, in two registers.

**Put undisputed facts in plain language with dates. Put your own case in pleading verbs — at least
three different ones. Add the line disclaiming the second section as fact. Give any published
statistics, with both versions of any conflicting figure. Say what the statistics do not describe
and what you will not supply.**

**Read your draft aloud to your partner first.** Reading a pleading sentence aloud is the fastest way
to hear whether it sounds precise or apologetic. Then check your own against the list, and swap.
