### 5 · Word study — the mandative subjunctive

**5A  Study the chart.**

%%FIG t05-f6 | A chart of mandative verbs and nouns that take the subjunctive: recommend, propose, require, insist, request, direct, and the nouns recommendation and requirement, each with one example sentence showing the bare verb form.%%

| Trigger | Example |
|---|---|
| **recommend that** | I recommend that the threshold **remain** unchanged. |
| **propose that** | I propose that the review **be** conducted individually. |
| **require that** | The policy requires that each counterparty **be** screened annually. |
| **insist that** | The committee insisted that the paragraph **be** read aloud. |
| **request that** | We request that the report **include** the method note. |
| **it is essential that** | It is essential that the assumption **be** stated. |
| **the recommendation that** | the recommendation that the threshold **remain** unchanged |

> **Grammar Note** — After these triggers English uses the bare form of the verb, with no *-s* and no
> *to*: *I recommend that the threshold **remain***, not *remains*; *I propose that it **be**
> reviewed*, not *is reviewed*. The negative is *that it **not be** changed*. This is standard in
> policies, minutes and recommendations, and the *-s* form in its place is the commonest register
> error at this level.

3. The assumption is stated in the report. *(it is essential that)*
4. The paragraph is not removed. *(I insist that … not)*

### 1 · The mandative subjunctive

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the opinion question.** %%AUD CL6-05-06%%

> **Adeel:** Your recommendation reads *that the threshold remain unchanged*. Not *remains*?
> **Oksana:** **Remain.** It is a recommendation, so the verb is in the bare form. *I recommend that
> the threshold remain unchanged.*
> **Adeel:** And if I write *remains*?
> **Oksana:** Then you have written that it does remain, which is a description. The subjunctive is
> the difference between telling the committee what is and asking it to decide.

*Oksana says one letter changes a description into a request. Is she overstating it?*

**1B  Underline the two forms. Which one asks for a decision?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **recommend / propose that + bare verb** | I propose that the review **be** individual. |
| **require / direct that + bare verb** | The policy requires that each file **contain** a screening record. |
| **insist / demand that + bare verb** | The committee insisted that it **be** read aloud. |
| **it is essential / vital that + bare verb** | It is essential that the assumption **be** stated. |
| **negative: that + not + bare verb** | I recommend that the threshold **not be** lowered. |
| **noun trigger: the recommendation that** | the recommendation that the paragraph **be** retained |

> **Grammar Note** — Note *be* and not *is* or *should be*: *I recommend that it be reviewed*. Adding
> *should* is not wrong in many varieties of English and it weakens the sentence, because *should* is
> advice and the bare form is the recommendation itself. In minutes, where what was recommended may
> matter years later, the bare form is the safer record.

### 2 · Compliance, effect, and what a score assumes

**2A  Study the chart.**

| Conflates them | Separates them |
|---|---|
| Our compliance improved. | Our **documented controls** improved; **no outcome measure moved**. |
| Risk has fallen. | The **index score** fell 0.02, described by its publisher as insignificant. |
| The jurisdiction is now compliant. | The jurisdiction is **technically compliant**; **effectiveness was not reassessed**. |
| The control works. | The control **exists**; **we have no evidence either way** on whether it works. |
| We screen counterparties. | We screen counterparties **against a score containing an assumed component**. |

> **Grammar Note** — *We have no evidence either way* is a complete and professional sentence. It is
> not the same as *it does not work*, and it is not the same as *it works*. Learners and native
> speakers alike reach for one of the other two because the honest version sounds like an admission
> of not having done something, which is often exactly what it is.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Compliance means strikingly different things from place to place. In some business cultures a
documented procedure is itself the protection, and asking whether it works is heard as questioning
colleagues' integrity; in others documentation without evidence of effect is regarded as a failure
in its own right, and internal audit exists to say so. There are differences in the authority of
external scores: some organizations will not open an account in a jurisdiction above a threshold
whatever the individual facts, and others regard any country-level score as a starting point for
inquiry rather than a decision. Where regulators themselves emphasize technical compliance,
companies rationally follow, since that is what they are examined on — which means a company can be
fully compliant, fully examined and entirely unprotected, and nobody in the chain has done anything
wrong.

**Discuss:** Where you work, would asking whether a control works be welcome? Who would you ask?

> **Two measures, one assessment system.** **Technical compliance** — whether the rules exist and are
> correctly drafted — **has improved by about 12 percentage points since 2013** among countries with
> available data. **Effectiveness** — whether the system reduces the problem — **remains at a low
> level.** Same decade, same assessing body, same published documents.
>
> **The assumption inside the score.** Evaluation data from the standard-setting body is **35%** of
> the total. **That body does not reassess the effectiveness of a jurisdiction after it leaves the
> increased-monitoring list**, so for those jurisdictions **the index assumes the graduate reached at
> least a moderate level**. **This is the publisher's own statement, and it bites hardest on exactly
> the jurisdictions where a screening tool is relied on most**, because a recent graduation is what
> makes a commercial team want to proceed.
>
> **What I can and cannot demonstrate.** I can demonstrate that we screen every counterparty and
> record it. **I have no evidence either way that our screening has prevented anything**, and I
> prefer to say so than to imply a protection I cannot show.
>
> **Recommendation.** **I recommend that the screening threshold remain unchanged**, notwithstanding
> the improvement in the global average. **I further recommend that any counterparty incorporated in
> a jurisdiction which has left the increased-monitoring list within the last three years be reviewed
> individually** rather than by score — about **11 counterparties**, costing an estimated **nine
> working days a year**. **I request that this paragraph be read aloud at the committee**, since it
> is the paragraph that will otherwise be skipped.
>
> *Source: Basel AML Index 2025, 14th public edition, for the scores, the 35% weighting and the
> statement on reassessment; the 2024 edition for the decade comparison. All figures and the quoted
> phrase are the publisher's own. The 11 counterparties and nine days are my estimates.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Write *I have no evidence either way* when it is true. It feels like an
> admission of failure and it is the sentence that makes everything else in your report believable,
> because a reader who has seen you decline one claim will accept the next. Reports that claim
> protection they cannot demonstrate are read as advocacy, and advocacy is discounted.
