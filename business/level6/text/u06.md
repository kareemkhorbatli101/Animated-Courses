### 5 · Word study — quantified uncertainty

**5A  Study the chart.**

%%FIG t06-f6 | A chart of uncertainty phrases: within the margin of error; indistinguishable on these data; the intervals overlap; not materially different; cannot be ranked on these data; and a difference of X against a margin of Y — each with one example sentence.%%

| Phrase | Example |
|---|---|
| **within the margin of error** | The four-point gap is **within the margin of error**. |
| **indistinguishable on these data** | The two countries are **indistinguishable on these data**. |
| **the intervals overlap** | **The intervals overlap** by 22 points. |
| **not materially different** | The two are **not materially different** on this measure. |
| **cannot be ranked on these data** | The candidates **cannot be ranked on these data**. |
| **a gap of X against a margin of Y** | **A gap of 4 points against a margin of 11.** |

> **Grammar Note** — *On these data* is doing essential work and is almost always left out. *The two
> cannot be distinguished* sounds like a claim about the world; *the two cannot be distinguished on
> these data* is a claim about the instrument, which is what you mean and what you can defend. Note
> that *data* takes a plural verb in formal writing — *these data are* — though *this data is* is now
> common in speech.

**5B  Rewrite each sentence with the phrase in brackets.**

3. You cannot put the candidates in order. *(cannot be ranked on these data)*
4. The gap is four and the margin is eleven. *(a gap of X against a margin of Y)*

> **Grammar Note** — The pair *I am not saying X; I am saying Y* is the most useful two-clause
> structure at this level. It pre-empts the misreading your audience is about to make, and it costs
> nine words. Keep the semicolon or a full stop between the halves: joining them with *but* turns the
> second clause into a retreat.

### 2 · Hedged comparatives

**2A  Study the chart.**

| Overclaims | Hedged, and still useful |
|---|---|
| A is better governed than B. | A's estimate is higher; **the two are not materially different**. |
| This is the best candidate. | This is **among the strongest** candidates on this measure. |
| B is worse. | B is **no better than** A, **and no worse on these data**. |
| A improved. | A's estimate rose; **the change is within the margin of error**. |
| A is in the top quartile. | A is **in the upper half**; **the top quartile is outside its interval**. |

> **Grammar Note** — *Among the highest* and *no higher than* are not weak versions of *the highest*;
> they are different and more precise claims. *Among the highest* commits you to a band, which is
> usually all a composite measure can support, and it cannot be refuted by one revision. Writers
> avoid it because it sounds less decisive, and then have to retract.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Governance indicators are politically sensitive in a way that few business measures are, because
they rank states. Some governments engage closely with the underlying assessments and publish
responses; others regard the whole exercise as an external judgement of their sovereignty, and a
foreign company whose slide ranks the country can find that slide quoted back to it in a meeting
with a ministry. There are differences inside companies too: in some organizations a percentile is
treated as a fact and the error bar as a technicality, while in others no comparative figure may be
used without its precision. And where the series itself has been recalculated, a country's recorded
past changes without anything happening in the country, which is difficult to explain to anybody who
was there at the time.

**Discuss:** Where you work, could you show a slide ranking countries? Who would see it?

> ### Hub site note — what the governance indicator can decide
> *W. Zhang · 9 May · one page*
>
> **The two estimates, with their precision.** Country A stands at the **64th percentile** for
> government effectiveness, with an approximate 90% interval of **53 to 75**. Country B stands at the
> **60th percentile**, interval **49 to 71**. **The gap is 4 points; the intervals overlap by 22.**
>
> **What the instrument is.** An aggregate of **35 cross-country data sources** — household surveys,
> firm surveys and expert assessments — produced by **more than 30 organizations**, covering **over
> 200 economies since 1996**. Every estimate is published **with a standard error**, because the
> underlying sources disagree with one another.
>
> **The publisher's guidance, in their words.** *A 90 per cent confidence interval for the governance
> estimate is given by the estimate plus or minus 1.64 times the standard error. These margins of
> error should be considered when comparing governance across countries and over time.*
>
> **What cannot be said.** **On these data, countries A and B cannot be distinguished on government
> effectiveness.** **I am not saying the indicator is unreliable; I am saying it is not precise enough
> for this comparison.** The slide circulated on 2 May has correct numbers and an incorrect arrow.
>
> **What can be said, and what it bought us.** **Both countries are in the upper half. Neither is in
> the top quartile.** Both statements survive the margins of error, and together they removed two
> countries from the long list.
>
> **A chart I am withdrawing.** The **2025 release included methodological revisions and historical
> estimates have been recalculated back to 1996**. The trend chart in the March paper was built from
> the previous release and is **not comparable**; it is withdrawn, and anybody opening the data today
> will see a different past.
>
> **The coverage count.** The data portal lists **216 economies** overall and **214** for government
> effectiveness. **A two-economy difference in the denominator is a fair guide to how finely a
> percentile is meant to be read.**
>
> **What the decision will actually rest on.** Berth availability, from the port authorities; customs
> clearance times for our own consignments over two years, which we hold for both countries; and
> whether a night shift can be staffed. **None of the three is in this indicator, and all three are
> decidable by 30 May.**
>
> *Source: World Bank, Worldwide Governance Indicators, 2025 update. Interval half-widths are
> approximate, derived from the published standard errors.*

> **Writer's Tip** — When you remove somebody's conclusion, say what their figures did buy. Wei's
> note takes the arrow off the slide and then credits the same indicator with eliminating two
> candidates. A reader who sees you use a measure well in one sentence accepts your refusal of it in
> the next; a reader who only sees the refusal concludes you dislike numbers.

### 3 · Write

Write **150 to 190 words**: a note on a comparison that cannot be made precisely.

**Give both estimates with their precision and state the overlap. Say what the instrument is. Quote
the publisher's guidance if there is any. Say what cannot be said, attaching *on these data*. Give a
band statement that survives. Say what the decision will rest on instead.**

**Read your draft aloud to your partner first.** Reading *on these data* aloud is the fastest way to
notice when you have left it out. Then check your own against the list, and swap.
