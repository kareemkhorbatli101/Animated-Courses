# Starting Point · Four Habits for Leading on Imperfect Evidence
%%SUB Before Unit 1: the sentence that lets a board trust you twice%%
%%GOALS
Review the structures this book builds on
Say what an instrument can and cannot measure
Make a decision without overstating the evidence for it
%%

Every instrument in this book is the best one available and none of them does what its name
implies. At this level the work is no longer finding a better number. It is leading on the number
you have, out loud, without pretending.

### 1 · What you already have

**1A  Work with a partner. Say one sentence of your own in each row, out loud.**
%%AUD CL6-00-01%%

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| **Cleft sentence** | **It is the March assumption that** the whole plan turns on. |
| **Concession and hold** | **Granted**, the sample is small. **Even so**, I would act on it. |
| **Passive of revision** | The figure **has since been revised** to 9.5 trillion. |
| **Quantifier clause** | Eleven countries, **none of which** is the weighted average. |
| **Like-for-like** | **On a constant sample of 50**, two of the three improved. |
| **Citation to publication** | the flagship outlook, **stated policies scenario, autumn 2026** |

**1B  Ask and answer with a partner.** One question using each structure. Then change roles.

### 2 · Habit one — name what the instrument measures

**2A  Listen and repeat.** %%AUD CL6-00-02%%

> Earlier books asked you to give a figure with its unit, date, source, scope, qualification and
> version. This book adds the question underneath all of those: **what is this instrument actually
> measuring?**
>
> *Our effective tax rate is 18.4 percent* is not a statement about money that left the company. It
> is a statement about what the tax code implies, computed forward from the law. The publisher says
> so. Almost nobody repeats it.
>
> **The rule:** before you use a measure, say in one clause what it measures. If you cannot, you do
> not yet know what you are claiming.

**2B  Say what each one measures. Out loud, with a partner.**

1. A compliance score of 72. *(compliance with what? and does it measure effect?)*
2. A governance percentile of 64. *(built from how many sources? with what margin of error?)*
3. A logistics index of 3.6. *(measured by whom, rating which countries?)*

### 3 · Habits two, three and four

**3A  Listen and read.** %%AUD CL6-00-03%%

%%FIG t00-f2 | A card taped inside the cover of a board folder, printed with four rules: name what the instrument measures; say what it cannot distinguish; decide anyway, and say what the decision is not resting on; and record the date the instrument is next republished. Beneath each rule, one example sentence in smaller type.%%

> **Two — say what the instrument cannot distinguish.** *These two countries are 4 points apart on
> an estimate with a margin of error of 11. On these data they cannot be ranked.* That is a finding.
> It sounds like a failure, and the people who can say it are the people who get asked again.
>
> **Three — decide anyway, and say what the decision is not resting on.** *I recommend the Austrian
> site. I am not resting that on the index position, which is inside its own noise. I am resting it
> on visa processing and on the two customers we already have there.*
>
> **Four — record when the instrument is next republished.** Every instrument in this book has a
> next edition, and some of them restate history when they arrive. If your paper does not say when
> the ground moves, somebody else will discover it moving.

**3B  Say it both ways. With a partner, give the weak version and then the strong one.**

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| *The data are solid.* | *The data measure __________ and cannot distinguish __________ .* |
| *This country ranks higher.* | *The gap is __________ against a margin of error of __________ .* |
| *The index supports this.* | *I am not resting this on the index. I am resting it on __________ .* |

### 4 · One instrument, three readings

**4A  Read. Then discuss the three questions with a partner.**

%%FIG t00-f3 | A chart with one bar and three labels pointing at it, each giving a different reading of the same score, and a footnote in small type at the foot of the frame quoting the publisher on what the measure is not.%%

A global index reports that a country's anti-money-laundering risk score improved from 5.30 to 5.28
on a scale where ten is worst. Three people read it.

> The first says: **risk has fallen.** The arithmetic is correct.
>
> The second reads the next sentence and says: **the publisher calls the change statistically
> insignificant.** Also correct, and also published.
>
> The third reads the method and says: **technical compliance has improved by about twelve points
> over a decade while the measured effectiveness of the system has not.** Correct, published, and
> the only one of the three that would change a decision.

1. Which of the three is being dishonest? *(Careful.)*
2. Which one would you want in a meeting about where to open an account?
3. What does the third person still have to add, so that the sentence leads somewhere?

**4B  Think about it.** *Talk with a partner.* What is the most important instrument your own
organization relies on, and what does it actually measure? How long would it take you to find out?
