# Clearline 5
%%SUB Business English · International Edition · CEFR B2+ to C1%%

## How to use this book

Clearline 4 was about persuading and deciding. This one is about **leading across borders** — and
about the particular problem of leading with instruments that cannot do what their names suggest.

**Twelve units**, sixteen pages each, the same six lessons in the same order as every book in this
series. If you have used any of them, you already know where everything is.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Sixteen words, a second set of six, and a team research task |
| **2 · Business Reading** | A real instrument, and the gap between what it says and what it can do |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures, taught as choices with consequences |
| **4 · Workplace Conversation** | Clear English, a long dialogue, a culture check and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A model of the unit's genre, then your own |
| **Team Up & Review** | A picture, a team project, a survey, and one problem with no easy answer |

**The audio.** 🔊 marks a track. 195 tracks, numbered `CL6-unit-track`.

## What is different about this book

**Every reading text is an instrument that cannot do the job its name implies.** Unit 3 reads a
measure of effective tax rates that its publisher says is not a record of tax paid. Unit 5 reads a
compliance score that rose twelve points over a decade while effectiveness stayed low. Unit 6 reads
a set of governance indicators whose own publisher tells you to consider margins of error before
ranking anything. Unit 7 reads an index whose factsheet says it is not a diagnostic tool. Unit 8
counts promises and deliveries and finds two different numbers. Unit 10 reads a dispute mechanism
with no power to compel anybody to attend.

None of these instruments is a fraud. All of them are the best available, and all of them are built
by people who published the limits in the same document. The skill this book teaches is the one
that senior work actually consists of: **using an imperfect instrument to make a real decision, and
saying in the same breath what the instrument cannot tell you.**

**The English moves up a level in register, not in difficulty of vocabulary.** Extraposition,
the mandative subjunctive, stipulative definition, the modality of soft law, quantified uncertainty,
purpose clauses, scenario conditionals. These are the structures of board papers, policies,
submissions and legal advice. Every one of them exists to make a claim precise enough to be checked.

**Two units ask you to write something you cannot enforce.** A policy, and a response to a
mediation you are not obliged to attend. Both are harder than they look, and the reason is grammar.

**Nothing assumes one jurisdiction.** Boards, taxes, disputes, pay and disclosure differ in kind and
not only in degree, and where that matters the book says so and makes finding your own the task.
