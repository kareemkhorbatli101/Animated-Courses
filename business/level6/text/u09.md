### 5 · Word study — attribution

**5A  Study the chart.**

%%FIG t09-f6 | A chart of attribution verbs arranged by strength: is attributable to, is explained by, arises from, reflects, accounts for, and is associated with — each with one example sentence and a note on how much causation is claimed.%%

| Form | Example | Causation claimed |
|---|---|---|
| **is attributable to** | The gap **is attributable to** rerouting. | strong, and defensible |
| **is explained by** | The rise **is explained by** distance. | strong |
| **arises from / stems from** | The cost **arises from** the longer route. | strong, formal |
| **reflects** | The figure **reflects** growing fragility. | moderate — a sign of, not a result of |
| **accounts for** | Rerouting **accounts for** about two thirds of the rise. | quantified share |
| **is associated with** | Longer routes **are associated with** higher cost. | weak; no causal claim |

> **Grammar Note** — *Accounts for* is the one to reach for when you can quantify: *rerouting accounts
> for about two thirds of the rise* is checkable and *the rise is due to rerouting* is not. *Reflects*
> claims only that the figure is a sign of something, which is why careful publishers use it; writers
> who want it to mean *is caused by* should use a stronger verb and expect to be asked for evidence.

2. Rerouting explains about two thirds of it. *(accounts for)*
3. The figure is a sign of fragility. *(reflects)*

**1B  Underline the quantified attribution. What two causes does it separate?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **X is attributable to Y** | The gap **is attributable to** rerouting. |
| **X accounts for N of Y** | Rerouting **accounts for about two thirds of** the rise. |
| **Y gives rise to X** | The diversion **gives rise to** 3,500 extra miles a sailing. |
| **X arises from Y** | The cost **arises from** the longer route. |
| **X reflects Y** | The tonne-mile figure **reflects** fragility. |
| **not X but Y** | **Not demand but distance.** |

> **Grammar Note** — *Attributable to* takes a noun or an *-ing* form: *attributable to rerouting*,
> never *attributable to reroute*. *Accounts for* takes a quantity and then *of*: *accounts for two
> thirds of the rise*. And *not X but Y* is the shortest way in English to replace one explanation
> with another; it works because the reader has already supplied X.

### 2 · Cause chains, and composition effects

**2A  Study the chart.**

| Device | Example |
|---|---|
| **with the result that** | The canal route closed, **with the result that** sailings lengthened. |
| **consequently** | Sailings lengthened; **consequently** tonne-miles rose. |
| **in turn** | Longer sailings raised bunker consumption, which **in turn** raised cost per tonne. |
| **whereas** | Volume rose 2.2%, **whereas** tonne-miles rose 5.9%. |
| **a composition effect** | The average rose **because the mix shifted**, not because any lane did. |
| **nothing got dearer** | **Nothing got dearer; the weight moved to the expensive end.** |

> **Grammar Note** — A composition effect is not a cause and should never be written as one. *Our
> cost per tonne rose because long lanes are expensive* is wrong twice: long lanes were always
> expensive, and the thing that changed was the share. Write *the average rose because the mix
> shifted*, and then give both shares.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Who may explain a cost movement, and to whom, varies between organizations as much as between
countries. Where finance owns the management pack, an operational explanation of a financial figure
can be received as an encroachment, however correct it is, and the person offering it may be asked
to send it through their own director first. There are also real differences in the standing of
published international data: in some organizations a figure from a United Nations body settles an
argument, and in others only internal data is admitted, on the ground that the company's own lanes
are not the world average. Both positions are defensible, and the second is often held most firmly
by people whose internal data is a single average.

**Discuss:** Where you work, who is allowed to explain a figure in somebody else's report?

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Note on the sea-freight cost per tonne
> *M. Chow · 16 August · one page*
>
> **Two measures of one trade.** Seaborne trade grew **2.2% by volume** in 2024 and **5.9% in
> tonne-miles** — close to **three times** the volume rise. For 2025 the projections are **0.5% by
> volume** and **0.3% in tonne-miles**, with about **2% a year** expected for 2026–2030.
>
> **How the publisher reads the divergence.** *The rise in tonne-miles reflects growing fragility in
> supply chains rather than robust trade expansion.*
>
> **Why that explains our own figure.** Tonne-miles are **weight multiplied by distance**. Weight
> grew about 2%; **distance grew because vessels that used the canal route are routing round the
> cape**. Canal tonnage stood at about **70% below 2023 levels** as at mid-2025. On our principal
> eastbound lane the cape routing **gives rise to** about **3,500 additional nautical miles** a
> sailing.
>
> **Our 11% rise, attributed.** **Rerouting accounts for about two thirds of the rise.** A further
> **third is a composition effect**: the share of our tonnage on long lanes rose **from 31% to 44%**,
> **with the result that** the average rose **although no individual lane's cost per tonne rose**.
> **Nothing got dearer. The weight moved to the expensive end.** Market rate movement accounts for
> about **three points**, which is approximately what nine months of renegotiation has recovered.
>
> **What each explanation would require.** Demand would require a **commercial** response —
> renegotiation, or accepted margin. Distance requires an **operational** one — routing, service
> pattern, transit promises. Composition requires an **analytical** one — restating the figure by
> lane. **Only the first has been attempted, and it was the answer to the explanation we did not
> have.**
>
> **Recommendation.** Re-time the eastbound service so the longer passage sits inside a weekly cycle;
> restate cost per tonne **by lane** in the management pack. **A single average will do this to us
> again**, and the pack it is in is not only Marek's.
>
> *Source: UNCTAD, Review of Maritime Transport 2025, for the volume, tonne-mile, projection and
> canal figures and the quoted sentence. The 11%, the 3,500 miles, the lane shares and the
> attribution shares are Lindmark's own.*

> **Writer's Tip** — When you attribute a change, give a share to every cause, including the one you
> are dismissing. *Market movement accounts for about three points* is what makes the rest of the
> attribution credible: a note that assigns a hundred per cent of a movement to the author's own
> explanation reads as advocacy, whatever the evidence behind it.
