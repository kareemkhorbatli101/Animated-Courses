> **Reader's Tip** — When two figures share a name, assume they are different measures until you have
> read both definitions. Shared names are the single most productive source of error in financial
> and policy writing, precisely because nothing looks wrong.

### 5 · Word study — stipulative definition

**5A  Study the chart.**

%%FIG t03-f6 | A chart of definition formulas: for the purposes of this paper X means; X is taken to mean; X shall be construed as; X means A and does not include B; X excludes; and save that — each with one example sentence and a note on register.%%

| Formula | Example | Register |
|---|---|---|
| **for the purposes of this paper, X means** | **For the purposes of this paper, "our ETR" means** tax accrued over profit. | board paper |
| **X is taken to mean** | "Jurisdiction" **is taken to mean** a taxing jurisdiction, not a country. | neutral formal |
| **X shall be construed as** | "Group" **shall be construed as** including branches. | contractual |
| **X means A and does not include B** | "Tax paid" **means** cash remitted **and does not include** accruals. | precise, preferred |
| **X excludes** | The figure **excludes** cash timing differences. | short, useful |
| **save that** | … all commitments, **save that** intra-group transfers are excluded. | formal exception |

> **Grammar Note** — A stipulative definition binds only the document it appears in, and that is its
> whole value: you are not claiming that the world uses the word this way, only that you will. Say
> what the term **means** and, where confusion is likely, what it **does not include**. The negative
> half does more work than the positive half, and most writers leave it out.

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **for the purposes of X, Y means** | **For the purposes of this paper, "our ETR" means** tax accrued over profit. |
| **Y is taken to mean** | "Jurisdiction" **is taken to mean** a taxing jurisdiction. |
| **Y means A and does not include B** | "Tax paid" **means** cash remitted **and does not include** accruals. |
| **Y excludes / is net of** | The figure **excludes** timing differences; it **is net of** credits. |
| **save that / except that** | all commitments, **save that** intra-group transfers are excluded |
| **it does not follow that** | **It does not follow that** we pay more tax than anybody. |

> **Grammar Note** — *It does not follow that* is the sentence that blocks the inference a table
> invites, and it belongs next to the table, not in a footnote. Writers hesitate over it because it
> sounds argumentative; in a board paper it reads as competence, since the alternative is that
> somebody draws the inference out loud in the meeting and you agree with them by accident.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Tax is the subject on which business cultures differ most sharply in what may be said out loud. In
some countries a company's tax position is treated as a legitimate matter of public interest and
senior managers expect to discuss it in those terms; in others it is private to the point that
raising it with a counterparty is a discourtesy. Reporting obligations differ in kind: some
jurisdictions require large groups to publish country-by-country information and others treat the
same data as confidential between tax authorities. Where a minimum-rate regime applies, whether a
group falls inside or outside it may depend on where its ultimate parent sits rather than on anything
it does, which means two competitors in the same market can face genuinely different rules and
neither is avoiding anything.

**Discuss:** Where you work, is the company's tax position discussable? Who decides what may be
said?

**1C  Read the model text.**

> ### Board paper — our tax position and the comparative data
> *M. Dolny with A. Rahim · 4 April · page one*
>
> **1 · Definitions, for the purposes of this paper.** **"Our ETR" means** tax accrued in the group
> accounts divided by accounting profit before tax, and **does not include** cash timing
> differences; it is **backward-looking**. **"The OECD ETR series" means** the effective tax rate
> indicators published in the OECD's corporate tax statistics for **105 jurisdictions, 2017–2025**;
> it is **forward-looking**, computed from the tax code on a standard hypothetical investment, and
> **does not mean** tax paid or accrued by any company. **"Statutory rate" means** the headline rate
> in law, before base adjustment. **"CbCR data" means** the anonymized, aggregated country-by-country
> reporting data in the same publication, covering almost **9,400 groups** headquartered in **over 60
> jurisdictions**.
>
> **2 · The figures, each typed.** Our ETR **18.4%** (backward-looking). OECD statutory average
> **21.2%** in 2025 and **28.0%** in 2000 (law, not outcome). The OECD ETR series (forward-looking).
>
> **3 · The publisher's limitation, in their words.** *The effective tax rate indicators are
> forward-looking policy measures; they are not realized tax payments and should not be read as
> actual tax paid.*
>
> **4 · What does not follow.** **It does not follow from any gap between our ETR and the OECD series
> that we pay more or less tax than comparable groups.** The two measures share a name and nothing
> else: not a denominator, not a period, not a purpose. **They may not be subtracted, ranked or set in
> one column.**
>
> **5 · The minimum-tax point.** A **January 2026 "side-by-side" arrangement exempts United
> States–parented groups from the Pillar Two minimum profits tax rules.** Our second largest customer
> group is US-parented; **we are within the rules and they are not**, and any comparison of effect
> must say so.
>
> **6 · What draft 1 did.** Draft 1 placed our ETR beside the OECD series in one table and concluded
> that we sit at the conservative end of international practice. **That conclusion was not supported
> by either measure and has been withdrawn.** Draft 1 is attached, marked superseded, because it has
> already been circulated.
>
> *Source: OECD, Corporate Tax Statistics 2026 (July 2026). The 18.4% is Lindmark's own, from the
> audited group accounts.*

> **Writer's Tip** — Attach the superseded draft and say what was wrong with it. Every withdrawn
> conclusion has already been read by somebody, and the choice is not between the reader knowing and
> not knowing: it is between learning it from you in one sentence and discovering it later as a
> contradiction. The second costs you the rest of the paper.
