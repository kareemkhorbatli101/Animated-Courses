> **Reader's Tip** — A big safety number is made of smaller numbers that are counted in different
> ways. Before you quote it, find out what is inside it: deaths from accidents and deaths from
> disease are both real and are not the same thing.

%%FIG p11-f5 | A safety induction page: a header with the site name, a numbered list of nine rules in two groups headed you must and you must not, a short box headed why the fire door matters, a diagram of a corridor with an exit route marked, and a signature block.%%

> ## Lindmark Freight · site induction, Ostberg
> *Issue 7 · page 2 of 4*
>
> **You must**
> 1. wear a high-visibility vest everywhere beyond the blue line;
> 2. wear safety boots in the racking bay and on the loading dock;
> 3. wear a hard hat in the racking bay;
> 4. report every incident **and every near miss** on the day it happens;
> 5. walk in the marked walkways when a forklift is moving.
>
> **You must not**
> 6. prop open any fire door, at any time, for any reason;
> 7. block an emergency route, even for a minute;
> 8. operate a forklift without a current licence;
> 9. move a load you cannot see over.
>
> **You need not** wear a hard hat in the office, the packing bay or the corridor, and you need not
> wear gloves for carton handling unless the carton is damaged.
>
> **Why the fire door matters.** The door holds smoke on one side of the building for about thirty
> minutes. The emergency route from the packing bay runs along the corridor on the far side. Held
> open, the door does the opposite of its job.
>
> **The scale of it.** Work kills far more people than most of us assume. The International Labour
> Organization estimates about **2.93 million work-related deaths a year**. Of those, about
> **2.6 million** are from work-related **disease** and about **330,000** from **accidents**. There
> are also roughly **395 million non-fatal injuries** a year.

> *Figures: ILO, published 2023, for the reference year 2019. Note what this means: nearly nine

**1B  Underline each modal verb. Which one is about the future, not the rule?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **must** | it is necessary | You **must** wear a vest. |
| **have to** | it is necessary (often an outside rule) | We **have to** keep the record for five years. |
| **mustn't** | it is forbidden | You **mustn't** prop the door. |
| **needn't / don't have to** | it is not necessary | You **needn't** wear gloves for these. |
| **can't** | it is not allowed / not possible | You **can't** drive it without a licence. |

> **Grammar Note** — *Mustn't* and *needn't* are opposites in a way that catches everybody out:
> *you mustn't do it* means it is forbidden; *you needn't do it* means it is your choice. Never use
> one for the other on a safety sign.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Safety culture differs enormously, and not always in the direction people expect. In some countries
stopping a senior colleague who is breaking a rule is expected of everybody, and a worker who says
nothing is considered at fault; in others correcting a senior person in front of others is close to
unthinkable, and the right route is to tell a supervisor quietly. Reporting also differs: where
near-miss reports are counted as learning, numbers go up and injuries go down; where they are
counted as blame, numbers go down and nothing else does. And a site with visitors from several
countries may have workers who have each been trained to a different standard and all believe
theirs is the normal one.

**Discuss:** At your work, may anybody stop anybody? What happens to a person who reports a near
miss?

> ### Near-miss report NM-2024-031
>
> **Date and time.** 12 March, 14:20. **Place.** Corridor C, fire door FD-3.
>
> **What happened.** FD-3 was found wedged open with a wooden block while a loaded trolley was
> taken through. The door was open for about four minutes. No injury.
>
> **What could have happened.** FD-3 holds smoke out of corridor C for approximately 30 minutes.
> Corridor C is the emergency route from the packing bay, where four people work. Held open during
> a fire in the main store, the door would have put smoke into the only exit route for those four.
>
> **Immediate action.** Wedge removed. Trolley taken through at an angle by two people.
>
> **Root cause.** Not carelessness. The doorway is 900 mm; the trolley is 920 mm. The job cannot be
> done by one person without propping the door. The dimension mismatch has existed since the
> trolleys were bought in March 2023 and has been known to the writer of this report since then.
>
> **Contributing cause.** The induction states the rule for FD-3 and does not state its purpose.
> Two inductions were delivered to this employee without the reason being given.
>
> **Actions.**
> | Action | Owner | By |
> |---|---|---|
> | Order two 850 mm trolleys (≈ €200 each) | A. Holm | 22 Mar |
> | Add the purpose of each fire door to the induction | A. Holm | 29 Mar |
> | Measure every door and trolley on site; list mismatches | Y. Haddad | 5 Apr |

> **Writer's Tip** — "Carelessness" is not a root cause; it is the end of thinking. Keep asking
> *why* until you reach something you can buy, build, measure or change. And if the cause has been
> known for a year, write that it has been known for a year. A report that protects its author
> prevents nothing.

### B · Write a team summary

> This is a warehouse. There are fifteen people. Everybody beyond the blue line must wear a vest.

Write eight sentences. Use *must*, *mustn't* and *needn't* at least twice each. One person writes,
everybody checks.

### C · Report

Read your summary to another team. They must add the reason to two of your rules.

### D · Team project — rewrite the induction

| Role | Who | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| **Manager** | | keeps to twenty minutes and decides what is cut |
| **Writer** | | writes nine rules, each with a reason |
| **Checker** | | asks "why?" until the reason is a real one |
| **Presenter** | | delivers the induction to another team in three minutes |

Write a nine-rule induction page for a workplace you know: five *must*, three *mustn't*, one
*needn't*, each with a reason in one clause. The other team must be able to repeat three reasons
afterwards.
