# Unit 3 · Chasing
%%SUB The fourth email about the same thing, and how to write it so it works%%
%%GOALS
Follow somebody up without annoying them
Use the present perfect against the past simple, with *yet*, *already* and *still*
Read a payment report and tell waiting from being late
%%

Marek's first three emails were polite. The fourth one has to be different, and the difference is
not that it is ruder. It is that it has a date in it, a consequence in it, and one question that
can be answered with a single word.

### 1 · Present perfect against past simple

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the opinion question.** %%AUD CL2-03-06%%

> **Marek:** I've written three times.
> **Customer:** You wrote on the fifth. I remember.
> **Marek:** And on the twelfth, and this morning.
> **Customer:** I didn't see the twelfth.
> **Marek:** That's possible. I'm not interested in which emails you saw. I'm interested in
> Friday.

*Marek changes the subject in the last line. Is that rude, or is it the point?*

**1B  Underline the present perfect forms and the past simple forms. What is different about the
ones with a date?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Use it for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Present perfect** | it happened, and it still matters | I'**ve written** three times. |
| | no time said, or an unfinished period | We'**ve had** no reply **this week**. |
| **Past simple** | a finished time is stated | I **wrote** on the fifth. |
| | a finished period | We **had** no reply **last week**. |

> **Grammar Note** — As soon as you say **when**, you must use the past simple: *I've written on
> Tuesday* is wrong. Say either *I wrote on Tuesday* or *I've written three times*.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Chasing is where business cultures differ most visibly. In some countries a reminder after three
days is normal and nobody minds; in others it is an accusation and damages the relationship for
years. In some places money is discussed directly and early; in others the subject is approached
through a third person, or over a meal, and a written reminder is the last step rather than the
first. Escalating to somebody's manager is a routine process in some companies and a declaration
of war in others. And in many places the real answer to "when will you pay?" is carried in how
long the person takes to reply, not in the words.

**Discuss:** Where you work, how soon would you send a first reminder? What would be too soon?
