### 1 · The passive

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the opinion question.** %%AUD CL2-05-06%%

> **Customer:** Your cartons were crushed.
> **Ravi:** Four of them were, yes. They've been replaced and they went out this morning.
> **Customer:** How did it happen?
> **Ravi:** All four were damaged on the same corner, which usually means an edge of the pallet.
> **Customer:** So it was the carrier.
> **Ravi:** It was probably in transport. I'd rather tell you what's changing than tell you whose
> fault it was.

*Ravi avoids naming the carrier. Is that honest?*

**1B  Underline the passive forms. Who did each action — and is it said?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Use the passive when | Example |
|---|---|
| **the doer is obvious** | The goods **were rejected** on arrival. |
| **the doer is unknown** | Four cartons **were damaged**. |
| **the doer does not matter** | The credit **will be applied** to your statement. |
| **you want the thing at the front** | Your pallets **are being shipped** with protection. |

> **Grammar Note** — The passive is made with **be** + past participle. *The goods were reject*
> is not a sentence; *the goods were reject**ed*** is. And if the doer matters, say it: *the
> change was agreed **with our packing supplier***.

### 2 · When **not** to use the passive

**2A  Study the chart.**

| Passive | Active | Which is better |
|---|---|---|
| Four cartons were damaged. | Somebody damaged four cartons. | **passive** — we do not know who |
| A decision was taken. | We decided. | **active** — decisions have owners |
| Mistakes were made. | I made a mistake. | **active** — always |
| Your order has been dispatched. | We have dispatched your order. | **either** |

> **Grammar Note** — Use the passive for things. Use the active for decisions and apologies. *An

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Complaining is culturally loaded. In some countries a complaint is a normal commercial act and
nobody is offended; in others it is a loss of face for the supplier and must be made indirectly,
through hints or a third party, or the relationship is damaged. In some business cultures the
supplier is expected to apologize deeply and repeatedly; in others a long apology reads as
weakness or as an admission that will be used later. And in some places a complaint that is not
made in writing does not exist, while in others putting it in writing is an escalation.

**Discuss:** Where you work, how is a complaint usually made? What would be too direct?
