### 2 · Read

> **Reader's Tip** — A procedure is written for the person who has never done it. Read it as that
> person: at every step, ask *could I do this without asking anybody?* The steps where the answer
> is no are the real procedure.

%%FIG p07-f5 | A printed standard operating procedure: a header with a document number, a revision and a date, a list of seven numbered steps written in the passive, a short equipment list, a note box, and a sign-off block with two signatures.%%

> ## Brightwell Pharma · SOP-114 rev 3
> *Gauge check and first-article inspection · issued 2 March 2024*
>
> **Equipment.** Gauge G-12 · calibration record · inspection tray · batch label sheet.
>
> **Procedure.**
> 1. The batch paperwork **is collected** from the store and **is checked** against the works
> order.
> 2. The gauge **is taken** from the cabinet and the calibration date **is confirmed**.
> 3. The first part **is measured** and the reading **is recorded** on the inspection sheet.
> 4. If the reading is in tolerance, the batch **is released** to packing.
> 5. If the reading is out of tolerance, the batch **is quarantined** and the supervisor
> **is informed**.
> 6. The inspection sheet **is signed** and **is filed** with the batch record.
> 7. The gauge **is returned** to the cabinet.
>
> **Note.** Steps must be performed in order. Any deviation **is recorded** on the batch record.
>
> **Why procedures matter.** There are about **395 million non-fatal work-related injuries a
> year** worldwide. That is roughly **one million a day**. Almost all of them happen during an
> ordinary task that somebody has done many times before.
>
> *Figure: ILO occupational safety estimates. The reference year is 2019 and the estimate was
> published in 2023; it is the most recent global figure available.*

### 1 · Sequencers with the passive

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the opinion question.** %%AUD CL2-07-06%%

*Tomas's last line is a joke and also the whole problem. What is the problem?*

**1B  Underline the passive forms. Which step has no written source?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **First + passive** | **First** the paperwork **is collected**. |
| **Then / next + passive** | **Then** the gauge **is taken** out. |
| **After that + passive** | **After that** the part **is measured**. |
| **While / meanwhile + active or passive** | **Meanwhile** the next batch **is weighed**. |
| **Finally + passive** | **Finally** the sheet **is signed and filed**. |

> **Grammar Note** — Procedures are written in the passive because the step matters and the person
> does not: *the gauge is calibrated*, not *Tomas calibrates the gauge*. But if a step needs a
> **particular** person, say so — *the supervisor is informed* names the role on purpose.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

How knowledge is handed over differs a great deal. In some workplaces the written procedure is the
authority and anything not in it should not be done; in others the written procedure is a minimum
and real competence is the unwritten part, passed from person to person. In some cultures a
younger colleague questioning an experienced one's method is normal and welcome; in others it is
disrespectful, and the way to learn is to watch for a long time without asking. And in some
companies writing down what you do privately feels like giving away the reason they need you.

**Discuss:** Where you work, how is a job handed over? Would somebody write down everything they
know?

> ### SOP-114 rev 4 — gauge check and first-article inspection
>
> *Changes from rev 3: four steps added (2a, 3a, 4a, 7a). Step 3 reworded.*
>
> 1. The batch paperwork **is collected** from the store and **is checked** against the works
> order.
> 2. The gauge **is taken** from the cabinet and the calibration date **is confirmed**.
> **2a. The gauge is warmed in the closed hand for one minute before use.** The store is held at
> 12 °C and a cold gauge reads approximately 0.02 mm small.
> 3. The **first three** parts **are measured**. **The first two are discarded** — the machine is
> still settling — and the **third** reading **is recorded** on the inspection sheet.
> 4. If the reading is in tolerance, the batch **is released** to packing.
> **4a. The inspection tray is turned so that the labels face the scanner.** Otherwise each label
> is scanned by hand, which adds about ten minutes per batch.
> 5. If the reading is out of tolerance, the batch **is quarantined** and the supervisor **is
> informed**.
> 6. The inspection sheet **is signed** and **is filed** with the batch record.
> 7. The gauge **is returned** to the cabinet.
> **7a. The machine is left running for 90 seconds after the last part.** The reason for this is
> not documented. It has been done since 2015 and the failure rate on this line is lower than on
> line 2, where it is not done.

> **Writer's Tip** — Give the **reason** beside any step a sensible person might skip. And when
> you do not know the reason — as in 7a — write that too. "The reason is not documented" is an
> honest sentence and it tells the next person what to investigate.
