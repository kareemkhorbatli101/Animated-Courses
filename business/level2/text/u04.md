### 2 · Read

> **Reader's Tip** — In a contract, find three things before you read anything else: **how long**
> you have to do things, **how long** they have, and **how either side gets out**. Everything else
> is detail until those three are clear.

> ## Supply agreement · Lindmark Freight and Nordkap Foods
> *Extract, page 3 of 4 · agreement ref SA-2026-11*
>
> **4. Charges and payment**
> 4.1 The Customer **shall** pay each invoice **within thirty (30) days**.
> 4.2 Invoices **may** be submitted monthly in arrears.
> 4.3 The Customer **shall** notify the Supplier of any disputed invoice **within ten (10) working
> days** of receipt. An invoice not disputed within that period **shall** be treated as accepted.
>
> **5. Term and termination**
> 5.1 This agreement **shall** continue until terminated.
> 5.2 Either party **may** terminate on **thirty (30) days' written notice**.
> 5.3 Either party **may** terminate immediately if the other **breaches** a material term and
> does not remedy it **within fourteen (14) days** of written notice.
>
> **6. Liability**
> 6.1 Neither party **shall** be liable for indirect loss.
> 6.2 The Supplier's total liability **shall not exceed** the charges paid in the preceding twelve

### 5 · Word study — obligation and permission

**5A  Complete the chart.**

%%FIG p04-f6 | A chart of six modal verbs arranged by strength, from must and shall at the top through have to, should and may, down to needn't and don't have to, each with a short gloss and an example clause.%%

| | Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **shall / must** | you are required to | The Customer **shall** pay within 30 days. |
| **have to** | you are required to, by somebody else's rule | We **have to** invoice monthly. |
| **should** | it is a good idea, not a rule | You **should** keep a copy. |
| **may** | you are allowed to | Invoices **may** be submitted monthly. |
| **don't have to / needn't** | it is not required | You **don't have to** dispute in writing. |
| **mustn't** | it is forbidden | You **mustn't** share the prices. |

> **Grammar Note** — *Mustn't* and *don't have to* are opposites, not variants. *You mustn't tell
> them* means it is forbidden. *You don't have to tell them* means it is your choice. In a
> contract that difference is the whole clause.

**5B  Complete with the right modal.**

2. You ______________ keep a copy, but it is sensible. *(not required)*
3. Invoices ______________ be sent monthly. *(allowed)*
4. You ______________ share the prices with a competitor. *(forbidden)*

### 1 · *must*, *have to*, *should*

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the opinion question.** %%AUD CL2-04-06%%

> **Hanna:** You have to dispute an invoice within ten working days.
> **Marek:** Says who?
> **Hanna:** Says the contract you signed in September.
> **Marek:** And if I don't?
> **Hanna:** Then it counts as accepted. Not "probably". It says *shall be treated as accepted*.
> **Marek:** … I've been disputing things in week three for four years.

*Marek has been doing it wrong for four years and nothing has happened. Does that mean it is
fine?*

**1B  Underline the obligation words. Which one is the contract's own?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Where the rule comes from | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **must** | the speaker, or a written rule | You **must** sign both copies. |
| **have to** | somebody else, or a system | We **have to** invoice monthly — it's the contract. |
| **should** | advice | You **should** keep a copy. |
| **don't have to** | no obligation | You **don't have to** reply today. |
| **mustn't** | forbidden | You **mustn't** change the price without telling them. |

> **Grammar Note** — *Must* usually comes from the person speaking; *have to* usually comes from
> outside them. "You must send it today" sounds like my decision. "You have to send it today"
> sounds like the deadline's.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

What a contract is *for* differs enormously. In some business cultures the signed document is the
relationship, and everything that matters is in it; raising a clause later is normal and
unemotional. In others the document is a formality that follows an agreement already reached
between people, and going back to the wording looks like distrust — the real negotiation happened
over dinner. In some places lawyers are involved from the first email; in others involving one is
a signal that the relationship is in trouble. And notice periods that are generous in one country
are illegal in another.

**Discuss:** Where you work, who reads the contract? What happens when somebody asks to change a
clause after signing?
