**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the opinion question.** %%AUD CL2-06-06%%

**1B  Underline the three relative clauses. What does each one identify?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **who / that** for people | the person **who** signed it |
| **which / that** for things | the reading **which** was out of range |
| **whose** for belonging | the supplier **whose** invoice is overdue |
| **where** for places | the store **where** it happened |
| **pronoun dropped** when it is the object | the box (**that**) we quarantined |

> **Grammar Note** — A defining clause tells you **which one** and takes no commas. *The reading
> which was out of range* picks one reading out of many. If you put commas round it, you are
> saying there was only one reading in the whole log, and that is a different sentence.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

What a record is *for* differs. In some organizations a record proves compliance to an outsider,
so it is written for an auditor and nobody else reads it. In others it is a working memory for the
team, and the useful column is the one nobody is required to fill in. In some workplaces writing
something down late, and saying so, is honest and normal; in others it looks like falsifying, and
people leave the box empty instead. And in some countries a signature is personally binding in a
way that makes people very careful about what they sign.

**Discuss:** Where you work, who are records written for? What happens if you write something in
late?
