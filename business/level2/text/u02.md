### 1 · The present perfect

**1A  Listen and read. Then answer the opinion question.** %%AUD CL2-02-06%%

> **Jun-seo:** Have you sent revision C?
> **Chiamaka:** I sent it on Wednesday.
> **Jun-seo:** I haven't received it.

*Chiamaka says "I sent it" and then "I have sent it". Why the change?*

**1B  Underline the four present perfect forms. What do they all have in common?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive** | have / has + past participle | I'**ve placed** the order. |
| **Negative** | haven't / hasn't + participle | They **haven't confirmed** it. |
| **Question** | Have / Has + subject + participle | **Have** you **sent** it? |
| **Just / already** | | I'**ve just sent** it. · They'**ve already started**. |
| **Not yet** | | They **haven't replied yet**. |

> **Grammar Note** — Use the present perfect for something that has happened and **still matters
> now**: the order **has been** changed, so the version on your desk is wrong. If you say **when**
> it happened, use the past simple: *I changed it on Wednesday*.

### 2 · *just*, *already*, *yet*, *still*

**2A  Study the chart.**

| Word | Where it goes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **just** | before the participle | I'**ve just** sent it. |
| **already** | before the participle | They'**ve already** started. |
| **yet** | at the end, questions and negatives | Have they replied **yet**? · Not **yet**. |
| **still** | before the negative verb | They **still haven't** replied. |

> **Grammar Note** — *Still* + negative is stronger than *yet*. *They haven't replied yet* is a
> fact. *They still haven't replied* is a complaint, and your reader will hear it as one.

### %%AUG c%% 2 · Clear English — the contracted *have*

**2A  Listen to the model.** %%AUD CL2-02-09%%

> *I have sent it* → **"I've sent it"** — one syllable, almost gone
> *They have already started* → **"they've already started"**

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

How a change is communicated varies a great deal. In some workplaces a change is not real until
it is in the system, and an email about it is only a courtesy; in others the email is the change
and the system catches up later. In some business cultures sending a correction quickly is normal
and expected; in others, correcting yourself in writing looks careless, so people wait and
telephone instead. And in some places the person who should be told is obvious, while in others
nobody is sure who is on the list — which is why the lists exist.

**Discuss:** Where you work, when does a change become real? Who has to know?
