# Starting Point · Four Tenses and a Habit
%%SUB What you can already do, and the one habit this book is going to build%%
%%GOALS
Use the present simple, present continuous, past simple and future forms accurately
Ask a question in each of those tenses
Check your own understanding out loud, instead of hoping
%%

Everybody in this book can already work in English. That is not the same as being comfortable in
it, and the gap between the two is almost always made of the same thing: not asking.

## 1 · Four tenses, one morning

**1A  Listen and read.** %%AUD CL2-00-01%%

%%FIG p00-f1 | A one-page review sheet with four boxes, each headed with a tense: present simple, present continuous, past simple, and going to. Each box holds one example sentence about a working day and a short note about when to use it.%%

> **Yara:** I **work** in operations. *(present simple — always)*
> **Yara:** This week I'**m training** the new coordinator. *(present continuous — around now)*
> **Yara:** I **started** here five years ago. *(past simple — finished)*
> **Yara:** Next month I'**m going to** hand the job over. *(going to — a plan I have)*

**1B  Complete the chart.**

| | When you use it | Your own example |
|---|---|---|
| **Present simple** | always, usually, facts | |
| **Present continuous** | now, around now, temporary | |
| **Past simple** | finished, with a time | |
| ***be going to*** | a plan you already have | |
| ***will*** | a decision made as you speak | |

**1C  Ask and answer with a partner. One question in each tense.**

1. What do you do? 2. What are you working on this week? 3. When did you start?
4. What are you going to do next month? 5. Will you be here on Friday?

**1D  Think about it.** *Talk with a partner.* Which of the five is hardest for you, and why do
you think that is?

## 2 · Questions

**2A  Listen and repeat.** %%AUD CL2-00-02%%

| Tense | Question |
|---|---|
| Present simple | **Do** you work Saturdays? · **Does** she handle that? |
| Present continuous | **Are** you working on it now? |
| Past simple | **Did** you send it? · **When did** it arrive? |
| *going to* | **Are** you going to confirm it? |
| *will* | **Will** you let me know? |

> **Grammar Note** — After *do*, *does* and *did*, the verb goes back to its plain form: *did you
> **send** it*, not *did you sent it*. This is the mistake that survives longest, and it is easy
> to hear.

**2B  Correct the mistake in each question.**

1. Did you sent it? 2. Does she handles that? 3. Are you work on it now?
4. When it arrived? 5. Will you to let me know?

**2C  Ask three people. Write their answers.**

| | Student 1 | Student 2 | Student 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| What are you working on this week? | | | |
| What did you finish last week? | | | |
| What are you going to do differently? | | | |

## 3 · The habit

**3A  Listen and read.** %%AUD CL2-00-03%%

%%FIG p00-f2 | A card taped to a monitor with six short sentences printed on it, the kind of card somebody keeps where they can see it: sorry, could you say that again; let me read that back; do you mean X or Y; can I check one thing; I'd rather ask now than guess; and can you put that in writing.%%

> **Marek:** Can I check one thing before I agree to it?
> **Hanna:** Of course.
> **Marek:** "Thirty days" — thirty days from the invoice date or thirty days from delivery?
> **Hanna:** … Good question. The contract doesn't say.
> **Marek:** Then it's not a small question.

**3B  Answer.**

1. What does Marek check, and why is it not a small question?
2. What is Hanna's answer, and what does it tell you about the contract?

**3C  Six sentences to keep.** Say each one to your partner. Then say which two you would actually
use, and why.

> Sorry, could you say that again? · Let me read that back. · Do you mean X or Y? ·
> Can I check one thing? · I'd rather ask now than guess. · Could you put that in writing?

**3D  Discuss.** In your workplace, is asking a question a sign that you understand or a sign that
you do not? What do you think about that?
