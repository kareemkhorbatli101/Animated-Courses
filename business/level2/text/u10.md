**1B  Underline the three conditional sentences. Which one contains a cost?**

**1C  Study the chart.**

| Pattern | Use |
|---|---|
| **if + present simple + will** | a real, likely result |
| **if + present simple + may / might** | a possible result |
| **if + present simple + imperative** | an instruction |
| **unless + present simple** | if … not |
| **as soon as / when + present simple** | certain, not conditional |

> **Grammar Note** — No *will* in the *if* clause: *If they will pay late* is wrong. Say *If they
> pay late, we will …* . And *when* is not *if*: *when the invoice is due* means it will be;
> *if the invoice is overdue* means it may not be.

**1D  Ask and answer with a partner. Three each.**

### 2 · Saying what will happen, politely

**2A  Study the chart.**

| Soft | Firm |
|---|---|
| If we don't receive it this week, we may have to hold the next delivery. | If we don't receive it by Friday, we will hold the next delivery. |
| It would help if we could settle February. | Unless February is settled, the account goes on stop. |
| I'm sure there's a reason — can we find it today? | I need a payment date today. |

> **Grammar Note** — *May have to* and *would* soften a threat; *will* and *unless* harden it. Both
> are useful. What matters is that you never say *will* about something you are not actually going
> to do — the second reminder that threatens nothing teaches the customer to ignore the first.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Asking for money is one of the most culturally loaded things you will do in business English. In
some countries a payment reminder on the due date is normal practice and nobody is offended; in
others it implies you think your customer is dishonest, and a relationship can be damaged by a
letter that would be routine elsewhere. Terms differ too: thirty days is standard in much of
Europe, sixty or ninety in parts of the public sector, and in some markets a long delay is simply
how credit works and is priced into the quote. And in many places the first step is never a letter
at all — it is a phone call to somebody you know, which is why who makes the call matters as much
as what it says.

**Discuss:** Where you work, when is it acceptable to chase? Who should make the call?

|---|---|---|
| **Manager** | | keeps to twenty minutes and makes the final call |
| **Writer** | | writes the policy in five numbered rules |
| **Checker** | | finds the exception everybody will want and argues for it |
| **Presenter** | | presents the policy and takes two questions |

Write a five-rule credit policy: terms, when you call, when you write, when an account goes on
stop, and who may make an exception. Every rule must use *if* or *unless*.

> The policy went in. Order numbers are checked before invoices leave, Marek calls on day
