**1C  Study the chart.**

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| **do / does / did** | **Did** we give notice? |
| **be** | **Is** this on the agenda? |
| **can / could / should** | **Should** we take seven first? |
| **wh- + auxiliary** | **When does** the notice period end? |
| **who / what as subject** | **Who owns** this? *(no auxiliary)* |
| **how + adjective** | **How long is** the notice period? |

> **Grammar Note** — *Who owns this?* has no *does*, because *who* is the subject. Compare
> *Who signed it?* with *Who did you ask?* — in the second, *who* is the object, so *did* comes
> back.

### 2 · Indirect questions

**2A  Study the chart.**

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| When does it end? | Could you tell me **when it ends**? |
| Did we give notice? | Do you know **if we gave notice**? |

| How much is it? | Would you mind telling me **how much it is**? |

> **Grammar Note** — After the opening phrase, the word order goes back to normal: *Could you tell

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

Meetings work very differently from place to place. In some workplaces anybody may interrupt the
chair and the most senior person expects to be questioned in public; in others questions go to the
chair privately beforehand and raising something new in the room would embarrass everybody. In some
companies the real decision is made before the meeting and the meeting confirms it; in others
nothing may be decided until everyone present has spoken. Silence can mean agreement, disagreement,
or simply that it is not your turn — and on a video call, where silence has no face attached, it is
almost impossible to tell which.

**Discuss:** In your workplace, who may interrupt? What does silence in a meeting mean?
