# Clearline 1
%%SUB Business English · International Edition · CEFR A2+ to B1%%

## How to use this book

Book 1 got you understood. This one gets the work done: an order placed and changed, a supplier
chased without rudeness, a contract read properly, a complaint answered, a process explained to
somebody who has never seen it.

**Twelve units**, fourteen pages each, the same six lessons in the same order as Book 1. If you
have used Clearline Foundation, you already know where everything is.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Sixteen words, a second set of six, and a team research task |
| **2 · Business Reading** | A real document, and one real number from a real report |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures, and the mistake each one causes |
| **4 · Workplace Conversation** | Clear English, a long dialogue, a culture check and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A model of the unit's genre, then your own |
| **Team Up & Review** | A picture, a team project, a survey, and one problem with no easy answer |

**The audio.** 🔊 marks a track. 195 tracks, numbered `CL2-unit-track`.

## What is different about this book

**You will be asked to say no, and to chase.** Both are harder in a second language than they are
in your first, because the polite version is longer and the words are not the obvious ones. Three
units are about little else.

**Four reports count late payment four different ways.** Days to be paid. Days past the deadline.
The share of invoices affected. Delay by country. All four are real, all four are in this book, and
the back matter sets them side by side and says plainly that they cannot be compared with each
other. Unit 10 shows you the trap: because one of them measures delay *beyond agreed terms*, a
country with short terms can look worse while being paid sooner.

**Nothing assumes one country's law.** Payment terms, notice periods and safety rules differ
everywhere. Where the law matters, this book says *this differs by country — find yours*, and
makes that the task.
