# English for Daily Life — the plan

**A general English course built on the exact architecture of *English for Furniture
Designers* (EFD Book 2).** Nothing structural changes. The topic layer is replaced.

---

## 1 · What the source book actually is

Audited from `EFD_Book_U1-U10_Illustrated_StudentBook_2.docx` — 10 units, 42,239 words,
40 figures, Upper A2 with marked bridges to Low B1.

| Layer | What is there |
|---|---|
| **Shell** | Warm Up + Parts 1–10, in that order, in every unit |
| **Tracks** | `[OP] Operator` = Warm Up + Parts 1–6 · `[PR] Practitioner` = Parts 7–9 · Part 10 = both |
| **Sub-labels** | *in-class starter · pre-class input · in-class output · in-class / homework · scripts printed inline · pre-read / discuss · pre-read / decide* |
| **Grammar spine** | One point per unit, sequenced across the ten |
| **Figures** | Exactly 4 per unit, numbered N.1–N.4, each with a caption |
| **Audio** | 5 tracks per unit: N.1 in Part 1 (Pronunciation), N.2–N.4 in Part 3, N.5 in Part 7B |
| **Cast** | Six recurring people at one named place, across all ten units |
| **Glossary** | Exactly 10 words per unit, closing Part 10 |
| **Length** | ~4,220 words per unit |

### 1.1 · The exercise grammar, section by section

Every unit uses the same twelve move types and nothing else:

1. **Matching** — Column A 1–5 (or 1–7), Column B a–f (or a–h), *one meaning is not needed*
2. **Word-bank gap-fill** — 4 words, 4 sentences
3. **MCQ** — four options, rendered `○ A)` … `○ D)`
4. **Done-for-you zero item** — `0. … — answer`, opening almost every exercise
5. **Figure + caption + short text + questions**
6. **Grammar Focus Box** — a Form / Use / Example table, then **Remember:**, then **Watch out!** with ✗ → ✓ pairs
7. **Correct the Sentence** — one error per line
8. **True / False / Not Given**
9. **Ordering task** — number the steps 1–5
10. **Role play** — Model exchange, Useful language, Card A, Card B, then ***Stretch***
11. **Writing with a word count** — 50–70, or 50–80 in Part 7, plus **Check before you finish** ☐☐☐☐
12. **Can-Do checklist** — five ☐ lines, the last marked *(Practitioner)*

### 1.5 · Three defects in the source — named, and what this course does about them

I am not going to replicate faults just because they are in the original. Each of these is a
deliberate, declared departure; everything else is a copy.

| # | What the source does | What this course does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **No answer key at all.** Its only answer mechanism is the seeded `0. … is done.` item — 135 of them. Nothing else in the book is answerable without a teacher. | Keeps all 135 seeded items **and** adds a real answer key per unit, covering every closed item. The key is a separate section so the student book still reads exactly like the source. |
| 2 | **All 70 `Model — read this first:` headers are empty.** The label is printed; no model follows. Only the `Plan (fill in, then write):` frame and the `Word bank:` line are filled in. | Every `Model — read this first:` carries a real, complete model of the right length, in the register the task asks for. |
| 3 | **Part 10's Spiral Review gives 5 word-bank items for 8 gaps.** Gaps 6–8 are unanswerable from the bank. | 8 bank words for 8 gaps, in every unit. |

One inconsistency is kept because it is harmless and visible in the original: the unit header
line reads `[PR] Parts 7–10`, while Part 10's own track label reads `[OP + PR]`. The second is
the operative one — Part 10 is for everybody. This course prints both in the same places.

## 2 · What stays, exactly

Everything in §1 — the shell, the track split, the sub-labels, the twelve move types, the
scaffolding quota in §1.2, the skills spread in §1.3 and the length budget in §1.4. Same
counts: 4 figures, 5 audio tracks, 10 glossary words, five writing tasks per unit, four
printed listening scripts, four reading texts, five Can-Do lines, 13–14 seeded `0.` items.

The only additions are the three in §1.5 — a real answer key, filled models, and a complete
word bank in the Spiral Review.

**The grammar spine stays untouched**, because it is already a general English syllabus:

| Unit | Grammar | Why it transfers without a change |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Present simple vs present continuous | routines vs now — the core A2 contrast |
| 2 | *there is/are*, *some/any*, *much/many* | describing a place and its contents |
| 3 | Imperatives and sequencers | instructions, *first / then / next* |
| 4 | Comparatives and superlatives | comparing and choosing |
| 5 | Active and passive | how things are done, agent not needed |
| 6 | Requests, offers and advice (modals) | asking, offering, advising |
| 7 | Future: *will* / *going to* / arrangements | plans at three distances |
| 8 | *must* / *have to* / *mustn't* | obligation and prohibition |
| 9 | Present perfect vs past simple | experience vs finished time |
| 10 | Reported speech | passing on what people said |

That spine is the real asset in the source book. It is kept in the same order.

## 3 · What changes — the topic layer only

Three things carry the furniture domain, and only these three are rewritten:

1. **The unit topics** (ten of them)
2. **The cast and setting** (six people at one named place)
3. **The three Practitioner parts**, which in the source are about managing a workshop

### 3.1 · The ten topics

Each topic is chosen so that the unit's existing grammar point is the natural way to talk
about it — which is what makes the drills feel like language rather than exercises.

| U | Source topic | New topic | Grammar (unchanged) | Why they fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction to Furniture and Design | **People and Routines** | present simple vs continuous | what you usually do vs what you are doing now |
| 2 | Materials and Wood | **Home and Neighbourhood** | there is/are, some/any, much/many | what is in a room, a flat, a street |
| 3 | Tools and the Workshop | **Food and Cooking** | imperatives and sequencers | a recipe *is* imperatives and sequencers |
| 4 | Measuring and Dimensions | **Shopping and Choosing** | comparatives and superlatives | cheaper, better, the biggest |
| 5 | Making a Piece — Steps and Process | **How Things Are Made** | active and passive | *the bread is baked here* |
| 6 | Upholstery and Finishing | **Health and Helping** | modals: requests, offers, advice | *could you* · *shall I* · *you should* |
| 7 | In the Showroom — Orders and Delivery | **Plans and Travel** | the future, three ways | arrangements, intentions, predictions |
| 8 | Quality, Standards and Guarantees | **Rules and Places** | must / have to / mustn't | signs, rules, what is not allowed |
| 9 | Care, Repairs and Restoration | **Experiences and Memories** | present perfect vs past simple | *have you ever* vs *last year I* |
| 10 | Style, Trends and Selling Design | **News, Stories and Messages** | reported speech | passing on what somebody said |

### 3.2 · The cast and setting

The source runs six people at Cham Furniture, Damascus, for ten units. The general version
runs six people at **14 Alder Street** — a four-floor building and the street it stands on,
in a mid-sized city that is never named, so any learner can place it.

| Person | Age | Who they are | What they bring |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Maya Oduya** | 26 | works in a bookshop, evening class on Tuesdays | routines, study, money |
| **Tomas Nilsen** | 31 | nurse, Maya's flatmate, works shifts | health, nights, helping |
| **Amina Chaudhry** | 58 | runs the corner shop downstairs | shopping, prices, the street |
| **Dani Rossi** | 19 | Amina's nephew, student, learning to cook | food, firsts, mistakes |
| **Mr Okonkwo** | 70 | retired teacher, top floor | memory, advice, the past |
| **Yuki Tanaka** | 34 | just moved in, works from home | arriving, plans, not knowing yet |

Six people, one building, one street. Enough for ten units of ordinary life, and no job
vocabulary required to follow any of it.

### 3.3 · The three Practitioner parts, re-cast

In the source these are the occupational core: managing an apprentice, a workshop case, a
Syrian case. Their **function** is general and is kept exactly; only the content changes.

| Part | Source | New | Function (identical) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **7** | Management File | **Real-World File** | a situation needing more language than Parts 1–6 give: phrase bank → dialogue → ordering → role play → writing |
| **8** | Global Case Study | **Global Story** | a story from elsewhere in the world, with Gloss, then Vocabulary in Context, then Discussion frames |
| **9** | Local Case Study — Syria in Focus | **Close to Home** | the same situation where the learner lives, then a three-option **Decision Task** |

The Part 8 / Part 9 contrast — far away, then your own street — is the best idea in the
source book. It is kept and generalised: Part 9's frames become *"Where I live…"*, so the
unit works for a learner in any country rather than only one.

### 3.4 · The tracks, renamed

The two-track split is kept exactly — one track everybody does, one optional track for
stronger or more experienced learners.

| Source | New |
|---|---|
| `[OP] Operator track` | `[CORE] Core track` |
| `[PR] Practitioner track (optional for beginners) · experienced staff` | `[PLUS] Plus track (optional) · stronger learners` |

All sub-labels are kept verbatim: *in-class starter*, *pre-class input*, *in-class output*,
*in-class / homework*, *scripts printed inline*, *pre-read / discuss*, *pre-read / decide*.

The source uses four figure jobs and repeats them across all ten units. Same four here.

| Slot | Job | Sits in | Source example | Unit 1 here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **N.1** | a labelled category set, feeding a table-completion task | Part 1 | *Common types of furniture* | Six jobs and the places people do them |

## 5 · Vocabulary

Ten words per unit, listed in the Part 10 glossary, each one met at least three times
before it appears there: once in the Warm Up or Part 1, once in a Part 3 script or the
Part 5 text, and once in a Part 10 review gap.

The test for every item: **would an ordinary adult meet this word in a conversation about
their own life, whatever their job?** Unit 1's ten are *routine · shift · flatmate ·
neighbour · colleague · commute · appointment · busy · usually · at the moment*.

---

## 6 · What this does not change

The sample unit that follows is written to be laid beside Unit 1 of the source book and
compared section by section. Every heading, every label, every exercise type, every count
matches. If a section of the sample looks unfamiliar against the source, that is a defect
in the sample, not a design decision.

## 7 · Open questions for you

1. **Level.** The source is Upper A2 bridging to Low B1. Kept. Confirm, or name the level
   you want this version pitched at.
2. **Figures.** The source's are photographic/illustrative. Rendering them is solved — the
   existing engine draws labelled sets, label-me diagrams, process strips and scenes. Say
   the word and the four per unit get drawn.
3. **The cast.** Six people at one address is a direct analogue of six people at one
   company. If you would rather the units stood alone with no recurring cast, that is a
   one-line change to the plan and a rewrite of the Part 3 and Part 7 scripts.
4. **Audio.** Five tracks per unit, scripts printed inline, exactly as the source. Confirm
   you want the scripts printed rather than hidden.
