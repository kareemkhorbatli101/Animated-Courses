**Unit 1: The Skip Outside Number 14 — Answer Key**

*Teacher's section. Open tasks carry a sample answer, not the only correct one.*

---

**Warm Up**

**W1** 1. b · 2. c · 3. d · 4. a — not needed: **e** (Buy a new phone.)

**W2** 1. skip · 2. pavement · 3. queue · 4. delivery

**W3** 1. (b) number 40 · 2. (b) took the load back

> Item 1 is the one to check. Learners who answer (a) have read *outside 14* as *ordered by 14*. The text says both in two short sentences.

**Part 1 · Vocabulary & Pronunciation**

**1A** 1. f · 2. b · 3. c · 4. d · 5. e · 6. a — not needed: **g** (landing)

**1B** STREET: kerb, pavement · SHOP: till, queue · JOURNEY: platform, shift

**1C** 1. skip / pavement · 2. queue · 3. card reader · 4. battery · 5. depot

**1D** (Track 1.1) 1. she **was** serving · 2. they **were** waiting · 3. we **were** crossing · 4. he **was** ringing · 5. you **were** looking · 6. I **was** carrying

> The point is that *was* and *were* are almost never stressed. Play line 1 twice and ask how many beats they heard. Most will say three, and *was* is not one of them.

**1E** 1. miss · 2. catch · 3. make · 4. block · 5. pay · 6. check

**1F** 1. (b) depot · 2. (a) card reader · 3. (c) shift

**1G** 1. agent · 2. shift · 3. landing · 4. stairwell · 5. crack

**1H** Open. Marking: one past continuous, one past simple, 25–40 words.

> Sample: *I was waiting for a parcel last Friday. The driver came at six, but I was out. He left a card. I collected it on Saturday morning.*

**Part 2 · Grammar**

**Notice** The van was arriving. It was in the middle of coming down the street.

**2A** 1. was waiting · 2. stopped · 3. were standing · 4. was carrying · 5. went · 6. were playing

**2B** 1. was crossing · 2. was showing · 3. cracked · 4. was speaking · 5. were waiting

**2C** LONG: she was waiting, they were playing, he was crossing · SHORT: the lorry arrived, the machine stopped, the van left

**2D** 1. While · 2. when · 3. While · 4. when · 5. While

> Rule of thumb for the board: *while* takes the long action, *when* takes the short one.

**2E** 2. Yes. Do you have an order number? · 3. No. The skip is outside the wrong house. · 4. When can you collect it? · 5. Tuesday morning. It's full, so I need the lorry.

**2F** Open. Marking: a past simple clause after *when*, a past continuous clause after *while*.

**2G** 1. …the call **stopped**. · 2. …when he **dropped** it. · 3. While we **were** waiting outside… · 4. The lorry **arrived** at half past one and left the skip.

**2H** 1. (b) was cooking · 2. (b) when

**Part 4 · Speaking**

**4A** Open pairwork. Listen for one sentence carrying both tenses.

> Expect: *I was cooking when the bell rang.* · *While I was out, the parcel came.*

**4B** Open. Marking: the driver gives a reason; the shopkeeper asks three questions.

**4C** Open. Marking: *was/were + -ing* for all six people.

**4D** Open. Marking: a reason for each opinion, not just a guess.

**4E** Open group task. Marking: one decision, and a reason that is not only the price.

---

**Part 5 · Reading**

**5A** 1. (b) two hours · 2. (b) if something blocks the kerb · 3. (c) it takes the parcel back

**5B** 1. **F** — two hours. · 2. **T** · 3. **F** — it takes the parcel back to the depot.

**5C** 1. (b) number 40 · 2. (a) it is full

**5D** 1. (b) is wrong — the notice says nothing about size. · 2. (c) is wrong — the notice says nothing about paying.

> This is the exam move: find the sentence, then cross out what the text does not say. Both wrong options here are things the text is silent on, not things it denies.

**5E** 1. kerb · 2. depot · 3. Tuesday · 4. 4471

**5F** 1. b · 2. a · 3. d · 4. c — not needed: **e** (Buy a new window.)

**5G** Open. Marking: two sentences, each with *if*, *when* or *while*.

> Sample: *When the bins are full, put the bag inside the gate.* *If a van is on the kerb, use the back lane.*

---

**Part 6 · Writing**

**6A** Open (40–50 words). Marking: what is wrong · where you were · one clear request.

> Sample (44 words): *Hello everyone. The light in the stairwell is not working. I was coming up at eight last night and I could not see the steps. Can we ask the landlord this week? I am happy to make the call. — Flat 4*

**6B** Open. Marking: three sentences with *if*.

> Sample: *If you ring the same day, there is no charge. If the skip is full, they send the lorry. If you book a week, it costs £180.*

**6C** 1. While I was cooking, the phone rang. · 2. She was serving when it stopped. · 3. They were playing all afternoon.

**6D** Open (35–45 words). Use the checklist on the page.

**Part 7 · Exam Skills**

**7A** Question 1: (a) *landing* and (c) *kerb* are places, not buildings — the text says *depot*. Question 2: (b) and (c) name things the text never mentions.

**7B** 1. **F** — write one word or a number. · 2. **T** · 3. **T** · 4. **F** — read the text first, then choose.

**7C** 2. Ask: a time, a number or a name? · 3. Listen and write one word or number. · 4. Check your spelling.

**7D** Open. Marking: two tips, each one instruction.

> Sample: *Find the sentence before you choose.* *Cross out the two options the text does not mention.*

**Part 9 · Real-World File**

**9A** 2. The skip is outside the wrong house. · 3. It is blocking my shop. · 4. When can you collect it? · 5. Can you give me a job number?

**9B** Open role-play. Marking: the address, the problem, the urgency, a date, a number.

**9C** Open (40–50 words). Marking: the day, the job number, who pays, one request.

**9D** 1. Your driver had the wrong number. · 2. Can you give me a job number? · 3. It's blocking my shop. · 4. I'll send you an email today. · 5. Thanks for your help.

> The pattern across all five: say the fact, ask for the thing, keep the person on your side. Rudeness costs you the Tuesday slot.
