**Part 1: Pronunciation** (Track 7.1)

cheap → **cheaper** · warm → **warmer** · heavy → **heavier** · good → **better** · bad → **worse** · expensive → **more expensive**

The teaching point is the one that is easy to miss: the comparative never moves the loud part. *CHEAP* becomes *CHEAP-er*, not *cheap-ER*. The *-er* is weak and short, and learners who give it weight sound like they are counting rather than speaking.

*Heavier* has three beats and *more expensive* has four. Drill them against each other so the learner hears that a long word does not take *-er* at all.

**Part 2 · Grammar — Comparatives and Superlatives**

**Part 2: Notice**

Comparing two: **cheaper** · **warmer** · **deeper**
Picking one out of a group: **the most expensive** · **the lightest** · **the best**

The signal words are the evidence. *Than* follows a comparative; *the* comes before a superlative, and *in* or *of* usually follows it.

**Part 2: Controlled Practice**

0. cheaper *(given)* · 1. warmer · 2. heavier · 3. more expensive · 4. better

Item 2 is the spelling point: *heavy* ends in a consonant plus **y**, so it becomes *heavier*. Item 3 is the length point: *expensive* has three beats, so it takes *more*, never *-er*. Item 4 is irregular and simply has to be known.

**Part 2: Comparative or Superlative?**

0. the most expensive *(given)* · 1. the cheapest · 2. older · 3. the lightest · 4. worse

Item 1 takes the superlative because *in the street* names a group; item 2 takes the comparative because *than* follows.

**Part 2: Correct the Sentence**

1. The green coat is **warmer** than the grey one. *(one comparative is enough)*
2. This is **the cheapest** shop in the street. *(cheap is short, so -est)*
3. Her telephone is older **than** mine. *(than, not that)*
4. Of all the coats, the grey one is **the lightest**. *(of all names a group)*

**Part 2: Freer Practice**

Open. One comparative and one superlative, both correctly formed.

> Sample: *My bicycle is heavier than my brother’s, and it is the oldest one in the building.*

**Part 2: Grammar Review**

1. **C)** The green coat is warmer than the grey one. · 2. **B)** It is the cheapest shop in the street.

**Part 4 · Speaking**

All four tasks are productive. Accept any answer that meets the brief; correct form, not content.

**Part 4: Comparing Things**

Marking points: ☐ one superlative in the first answer ☐ one comparative with *than* ☐ a change over time described in the second.

> Sample: *The best thing I ever bought was a second-hand sewing machine. It is heavier and uglier than the new ones and it has not broken in nine years. I used to buy the cheapest of everything. Now I buy the cheapest of most things and the best of two.*

**Part 4: Find the Difference (pairwork)**

Marking points: ☐ comparative questions, not only yes/no ones ☐ exactly three differences.

Three differences: **the battery** (a day / four hours) · **the price** (two hundred / three hundred) · **the screen** (small / bigger). The **age is the same** — two years each. Pairs who report four have guessed.

> Sample exchange: *A: How old is it? — B: Two years. — A: Mine too. Is the battery good? — B: Four hours. — A: Mine lasts a day. How much was it? — B: Three hundred. — A: Two. Is the screen bigger than mine? — B: I think so.*

**Part 4: Helping Somebody Choose**

Marking points: ☐ two things named and compared ☐ at least three comparatives ☐ a recommendation with one reason ☐ B asks at least one question before advising.

Card A must produce: two options, what is better about each, the reason for the deadlock.
Card B must produce: a question, a comparison out loud, a choice, a reason.

***Stretch:*** your friend chooses the other one. Accept *You are right, actually — you will wear it more* and nothing further. A learner who keeps arguing has failed the task.

**Part 4: Mini-Presentation**

Marking points: ☐ about one minute ☐ two things compared ☐ one superlative ☐ which they would replace first, with a reason.

> Sample (57 words): *I use two bags every day. The old one is uglier and much stronger, and it has been with me for six years. The new one is lighter and the zip is already going. The new one is the worse bag, which is annoying, because it cost more, and it is the one I will replace first.*

**Part 6 · Writing**

Each task already prints a model. These are the marking points and a second sample, so that the printed model is not the only answer a learner has seen.

**Part 6: Compare Two Things (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ at least two comparatives ☐ one superlative ☐ *than* used correctly ☐ a decision at the end ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (65 words): *The second-hand bicycle is heavier and much cheaper, and somebody has clearly looked after it. The new one is lighter, faster and three times the price. For the way I ride, which is slowly and in the rain, the old one is the better bicycle and almost certainly the best value in the shop. I would take it and spend the difference on a lock.*

**Part 6: Cheap or Good? (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ a clear position ☐ two or three reasons ☐ one exception named ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (56 words): *Good things, when the thing is used every day. A chair, a bed, shoes, a knife: these are worth the money because you touch them a thousand times. Everything else can be cheap, because you will lose it or stop wanting it. The mistake is buying a good version of something you do not really use.*

**Part 6: A Message Recommending Something (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ a clear recommendation in the first line ☐ one point for the other side ☐ a reason drawn from the friend’s life ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (57 words): *Dani, get the repair. I know a new one is tempting and I know the old one is scratched. But you drop everything, and in a month the new one would look exactly like the old one and you would be two hundred pounds poorer. Thirty pounds and two days. Then take yourself out with the rest.*

**Part 6: Reflection — The Best Thing I Own (40–60 words)**

Marking points: ☐ the thing named ☐ at least one superlative ☐ why it is the best, not simply that it is ☐ 40–60 words.

> Sample (43 words): *The best thing I own is a radio that only gets two stations. It is the oldest thing in my flat and the ugliest. It was in the kitchen of every home I have had, and it is the first thing I unpack.*

**7C: Controlled Practice — Taking Something Back**

0. Say what you bought and when — **1** *(given)*
Decide what you want — **5**
Say what went wrong — **2**
Ask what they can do — **4**
Show the receipt — **3**

**7D: Role-Play — At the Counter**

Marking points: ☐ what and when, in the first sentence ☐ the fault stated once ☐ the receipt produced without being asked twice ☐ a question that invites honest advice ☐ a decision.

> Sample: *A: I bought these three weeks ago and the sole came off. — B: That shouldn’t happen. Have you got the receipt? — A: Here. — B: Money back, or a new pair? — A: Which would you do? — B: A different pair. These are our cheapest. — A: Then I will pay the difference.*

***Stretch:*** the shop says the fault is yours. Accept *I understand, but three weeks is not long for a sole. Could somebody else look at them?* The marker is a disagreement with a next step in it.

**7E: Writing — A Message About a Fault (50–80 words)**

Marking points: ☐ what and when ☐ the fault ☐ what you are asking for ☐ a tone that leaves them room ☐ 50–80 words.

> Sample (61 words): *Good afternoon. I bought a kettle from you a month ago and it has started to switch itself off before it boils. I still have the receipt and the box. I would rather have it repaired than replaced if that is possible, but I am happy either way. Could you tell me what you need from me? Thank you. Maya Oduya*

**Part 9 · Close to Home — Mr Okonkwo’s Coat**

0. Older than Dani. *(given)*
1. She relined the coat.
2. Why he did not simply buy a new one.
3. Because some things get smaller when you say them out loud.

**Part 9: Decision Task**

No option is wrong. The task is the reasoning, and the frame must be used.

Marking points: ☐ a choice stated in the first sentence ☐ a *because* reason ☐ a second point after *Also* ☐ a comparison with one rejected option ☐ 50–70 words.

The three options, and the strongest case for each:
- **(a) mend it** — cheapest now, keeps a thing you already understand, and the repair usually outlasts the price difference. It fails when the thing was poor to begin with.
- **(b) buy the cheapest new one** — the only option available when there is no money this month, and sometimes that is the whole argument. It is also how the boots story starts.
- **(c) save up for the best** — the right answer over ten years and the wrong one over ten days. It assumes you can wait, which is exactly what the Part 5 reading says not everybody can.

> Sample (56 words): *I think I should save up and buy the best one, because this is the third cheap one I have bought and the first two are in a drawer. Also, I use it every day, which is the test. This is better than mending it, because the part that broke is the part that always breaks.*
