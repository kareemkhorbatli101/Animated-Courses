**Part 1: Pronunciation** (Track 6.1)

go → **went** · catch → **caught** · leave → **left** · forget → **forgot** · take → **took** · find → **found**

None of these takes *-ed*. The vowel in the middle changes, and there is no rule that predicts which way, so they are learned as pairs and out loud.

Two sound traps worth the time: *caught* rhymes with *thought* and the **gh** is silent, and *left* and *found* keep their final consonant clearly — learners who swallow it produce *lef* and *foun*, which an English ear hears as a different word.

**Part 2 · Grammar — Past Simple: Irregular Verbs**

**Part 2: Notice**

Regular: **missed**
Irregular: **left** · **forgot** · **went** · **caught** · **fell** · **woke** · **found**

Seven irregular to one regular, in five short sentences. That ratio is why these forty verbs are worth drilling: they are the commonest verbs in the language, and the common ones are the irregular ones.

**Part 2: Controlled Practice**

0. left *(given)* · 1. forgot · 2. went · 3. caught · 4. found

**Part 2: Regular or Irregular?**

0. took *(given)* · 1. walked · 2. slept · 3. arrived · 4. paid

Items 1 and 3 are regular and look it. Items 2 and 4 are the trap: *sleeped* and *payed* are both wrong, and both are the mistake a learner makes when the rule is winning over the ear.

**Part 2: Correct the Sentence**

1. She **went** back for her bag. *(go is irregular)*
2. Did she **catch** the next bus? *(did carries the past)*
3. Yuki **took** the train to the coast. *(take is irregular)*
4. They **did not find** the gate in time. *(the negative needs did not)*

**Part 2: Freer Practice**

Open. One regular past verb and one irregular, both correctly formed.

> Sample: *I walked to the station in the rain and then I found that the train was cancelled.*

**Part 4 · Speaking**

All four tasks are productive. Accept any answer that meets the brief; correct form, not content.

**Part 4: Talking About Journeys**

Marking points: ☐ at least three past verbs, two of them irregular ☐ one time phrase ☐ a *did you* question asked as well as answered.

> Sample: *The longest journey I took last year was eleven hours by coach. Nothing went wrong exactly, but the air conditioning broke and nobody opened a window for five hours. I always carry water now. I always forget a book.*

**Part 4: Find the Difference (pairwork)**

Marking points: ☐ questions with *did* and the plain verb ☐ exactly two differences reported.

The differences: **what they forgot** (wallet / charger) · **whether they caught it** (missed / caught). The **departure time is the same** and so is **how late they were** — which is the interesting part, because the same delay arrived by two different routes.

> Sample exchange: *A: What time did you leave? — B: Seven. — A: Me too. What did you forget? — B: My charger. — A: My wallet. Did you catch it? — B: Yes. — A: I missed it. How late were you? — B: An hour. — A: The same.*

**Part 4: Telling a Travel Story**

Marking points: ☐ four past verbs at least, two irregular ☐ a departure time ☐ a sympathetic noise from A ☐ one good thing at the end.

Card A must produce: four questions, in order, and one kind remark.
Card B must produce: a time, a thing forgotten, a second problem, a good ending.

***Stretch:*** nothing went wrong. Accept a learner who finds the interest somewhere else — the person opposite, the view, the silence — rather than one who invents a disaster.

**Part 4: Mini-Presentation**

Marking points: ☐ about one minute ☐ where ☐ what happened, in order ☐ what they do differently now.

> Sample (54 words): *Two years ago I went to visit my brother and I took the wrong train. I did not notice for forty minutes, because I was asleep. I arrived at a town I had never heard of at eleven at night. Now I take a photograph of the departure board before I get on anything.*

**Part 6 · Writing**

Each task already prints a model. These are the marking points and a second sample, so that the printed model is not the only answer a learner has seen.

**Part 6: A Journey That Went Wrong (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ at least three irregular past verbs ☐ a time phrase near the start ☐ how it ended ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (59 words): *Last March I took a coach to see my sister and I left my bag on it. I did not notice until the coach had gone. I went to the desk and a man telephoned the driver, who found it under a seat and brought it back on the evening run. I waited six hours and I was lucky.*

**Part 6: The Best Way to Travel (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ a clear opinion ☐ two or three reasons ☐ one point for the other side ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (52 words): *I would fly, and I know what people say about trains. A train is pleasant for four hours and a punishment for twelve. I would rather spend two hours being bored in an airport than a whole day being bored at a window. The view stops being a view after a while.*

**Part 6: A Message About Being Late (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ an apology ☐ what happened, briefly ☐ how late ☐ an instruction for the other person ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (53 words): *Yuki, I am so sorry. The train stopped outside the station and has not moved for twenty minutes, and nobody has said why. I think I am at least half an hour behind. Please order and start without me — I would rather you ate than waited. I will come straight there. Maya*

**Part 6: Reflection — Something I Lost (40–60 words)**

Marking points: ☐ what you lost ☐ where ☐ why it stayed with you ☐ 40–60 words.

> Sample (43 words): *I lost a scarf on a night bus when I was twenty. It was not expensive and it was not even mine. I had borrowed it from a friend who never once mentioned it afterwards, which is why I still think about it.*

**7C: Controlled Practice — Reporting Something Lost**

Leave a way to reach you — **5**

Ask what happens next — **4**
Describe it well enough to find — **3**

**7D: Role-Play — At the Lost Property Desk**

Marking points: ☐ what and when, in the first sentence ☐ where they sat ☐ a description with at least two specific details ☐ a contact ☐ a question about what happens next.

> Sample: *A: I left a bag on the ten past four yesterday. — B: Where were you sitting? — A: Last coach, by the window. — B: What does it look like? — A: Dark green, broken zip, a library card inside. — B: Leave a number. We keep things a month.*

***Stretch:*** something in the bag cannot be replaced. Accept *There is a notebook in it that I cannot replace — would you mind putting a note on it?* The marker is a specific, small request, not an appeal.

**7E: Writing — A Lost Property Message (50–80 words)**

Marking points: ☐ the train and the day ☐ where you sat ☐ a description somebody could act on ☐ how to reach you ☐ 50–80 words.

> Sample (67 words): *Good afternoon. I travelled on the seven forty from the city on Monday and I think I left a grey coat on the luggage rack in the third coach. There is a bus ticket in the left pocket and a small hole in the right one. My number is below. If it has come in, I can collect it any afternoon this week. Thank you. Dani Rossi*

**Part 9 · Close to Home — The Spare Key**

0. In a tin under the till. *(given)*
1. Because she spent a night on the stairs.
2. Only that somebody needs one.
3. Because there is a right moment for that offer, and it is not the first month.

**Part 9: Decision Task**

No option is wrong. The task is the reasoning, and the frame must be used.

Marking points: ☐ a choice stated in the first sentence ☐ a *because* reason ☐ a second point after *Also* ☐ a comparison with one rejected option ☐ 50–70 words.

The three options, and the strongest case for each:
- **(a) say yes** — the cost is almost nothing and the benefit arrives on the one night you need it; the offer is also a relationship, and accepting it is part of living somewhere.
- **(b) a key hidden outside** — no trust required and no awkwardness, but a key under a stone is a key anybody can find, and you will think about it.
- **(c) change nothing** — the only option with no new risk at all, and the one that leaves you on the stairs at midnight.

> Sample (55 words): *I think I should say no and change nothing, because I do not know my neighbours well and a key is a large thing to give. Also, I have never locked myself out. This is better than hiding a key outside, because a hidden key is the worst of both: a risk and no relationship.*
