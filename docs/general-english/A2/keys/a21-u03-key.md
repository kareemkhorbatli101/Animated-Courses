**Part 1: Pronunciation** (Track 3.1)

Stress as printed: **TRA**-ffic · **MAR**-ket · **LI**-bra-ry · **ES**-ca-la-tor · **TIME**-ta-ble · **CRO**-ssing

Every one of these takes the stress on the first syllable. That is the common pattern for English nouns of two or three syllables, and it is worth saying out loud as a pattern rather than six separate facts.

The fast forms: *library* becomes /ˈlaɪbri/ and *escalator* keeps all four syllables but the last two go very light. Teach the slow form first; learners who only hear the fast one cannot spell it.

**Part 2 · Grammar — Present Continuous and Present Simple**

**Part 2: Notice**

Always true: **opens** · **stop** · **reads**
Happening now: **is opening** · **is arriving** · **are standing** · **is reading**

The time words are the evidence: *every morning*, *every afternoon* and a plain verb one way; *right now*, *at the moment*, *today* the other. Make learners point at them.

**Part 2: Controlled Practice**

0. opens *(given)* · 1. is arriving · 2. stop · 3. are standing · 4. reads

**Part 2: Which Tense?**

0. opens *(given)* · 1. is playing · 2. leaves · 3. is working · 4. wants

Item 2 is the one to stop on: a timetable takes the present simple even though it is about the future. Item 4 is the stative verb — *want* has no -ing form.

**Part 2: Correct the Sentence**

1. The library **opens** at nine every day. *(every day is a fact)*
2. Look! The traffic **is stopping** at the bridge. *(happening now)*
3. I **want** a ticket for the six o’clock bus. *(want has no -ing)*
4. At the moment Maya **is waiting** at the crossing. *(at the moment)*

**Part 2: Freer Practice**

Open. One present simple fact and one present continuous sentence, both correctly formed.

> Sample: *The shops here close at six every day. At the moment the whole street is closing, and it is only half past five.*

**Part 2: Grammar Review**

**Part 4 · Speaking**

All four tasks are productive. Accept any answer that meets the brief; correct form, not content.

**Part 4: Talking About Your City**

Marking points: ☐ one present-simple fact ☐ one present-continuous *right now* sentence ☐ a superlative used correctly in the first answer.

> Sample: *The busiest place is the station, and it is busiest at about half past eight. I cross the city by bus, which is not the fastest way, but I can read. Right now I think my street is almost empty, because everybody is at work.*

**Part 4: Find the Difference (pairwork)**

Marking points: ☐ questions in the present continuous ☐ exactly three differences reported.

Three differences: **people waiting** (four / two) · **the man** (selling flowers / reading a newspaper) · and the third is the trap — the **market is open** in both and **it is raining** in both. Pairs who report four have guessed.

> Sample exchange: *A: What is happening at the crossing? — B: Nothing much. Is the market open? — A: Yes. — B: Mine too. How many people are waiting? — A: Four. — B: Only two here. What is the man doing? — A: Selling flowers. — B: Mine is reading a newspaper.*

**Part 4: Asking the Way**

Marking points: ☐ two directions given ☐ a time said ☐ one present-continuous sentence that changes the advice ☐ the opening question answered.

Card A must produce: a request for directions, a question about the bus, a question about opening times, thanks.
Card B must produce: two directions, a duration, something happening now, an opening time.

***Stretch:*** accept *I am sorry, I do not know that one — but the woman in the café will. Shall I ask her?* The marker is an admission plus a route to the answer.

**Part 4: Mini-Presentation**

Marking points: ☐ about one minute ☐ what happens there every day ☐ what is happening there at the moment ☐ the place named first.

> Sample (49 words): *I want to talk about the bridge in my city. Every day it is full of traffic from four until seven, and people walk across instead of waiting. At the moment, I think, nothing is happening there at all, because it is Sunday morning and the city is asleep.*

**Part 6 · Writing**

Each task already prints a model. These are the marking points and a second sample, so that the printed model is not the only answer a learner has seen.

**Part 6: Describe a Place at Two Hours (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ one present-simple fact about every day ☐ one present-continuous sentence about now ☐ two time expressions ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (54 words): *The library opens at nine and closes at seven every day except Sunday. The same three people are always waiting outside before it opens. At the moment it is almost four, and the room upstairs is filling with students. A man by the window is asleep over a newspaper, and nobody is waking him.*

**Part 6: The Best Way to Cross a City (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ a clear opinion ☐ two or three reasons ☐ one concession or problem named ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (57 words): *For me the bus is the best way, because I can read and because it costs less than everything else. It is slow at six, but at six everything is slow. People say cycling is faster and they are right, but I do not want to arrive at work wet. The bus is the comfortable answer.*

**Part 6: A Message About Meeting (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ a place and a time ☐ how to get there, with one detail ☐ one sentence about yourself ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (60 words): *Hi Dani. Shall we meet at the bench by the park entrance at two on Sunday? The 12 bus stops right there and it runs every twenty minutes on Sundays. If you walk from your side it is about half an hour, and the bridge is the quick way. I am at the market first, so message me if you are early.*

**Part 6: Reflection — My Favourite Hour (40–60 words)**

Marking points: ☐ an hour named ☐ what is happening at that hour ☐ a reason ☐ 40–60 words.

> Sample (49 words): *I like ten at night best. The traffic is gone and the shops are shut, and the only people out are walking dogs or coming off a late shift. The city is doing nothing, and it is the one hour when it belongs to whoever is awake.*

**7D: Role-Play — Asking About a Journey**

Marking points: ☐ the original plan stated ☐ an alternative asked for ☐ a duration asked about ☐ a decision said out loud ☐ nobody loses their temper.

> Sample: *A: Excuse me, is the ten past running? — B: No, there is a problem at the bridge. — A: Is there a later train? — B: Half past, platform four, but it takes forty minutes. — A: And a bus? — B: The 12, outside, now. — A: Then I am running. Thank you.*

***Stretch:*** both options are bad. Accept a staff member who says plainly which is less bad and why — *the bus is slower but it is certainly going; the train may be cancelled too* — rather than one who pretends there is a good answer.

**7E: Writing — A Message About a Delay (50–80 words)**

Marking points: ☐ an apology in the first line ☐ what happened ☐ how late ☐ what the other person should do ☐ 50–80 words.

> Sample (63 words): *Dani, I am sorry, I am going to be late. There is a problem on the line and my train is cancelled, so I am waiting for the 12 bus instead. It is here but the traffic is not moving. I think I am half an hour behind. Do not wait outside in the cold — go in and get a table. Maya*

**Part 8: Discussion**

Open. Each learner should produce at least one *similar* frame and one *different* frame, with a reason.

**Part 9 · Close to Home — The Bus That Changed Its Route**

0. At the corner of Alder Street, outside Amina’s shop. *(given)*
1. Because the corner is too small and the bus was blocking it twice an hour.
2. He stopped going into town on Tuesdays.
3. Eleven fewer a day, all between half past eight and nine.

**Part 9: Decision Task**

No option is wrong. The task is the reasoning, and the frame must be used.

Marking points: ☐ a choice stated in the first sentence ☐ a *because* reason ☐ a second point after *Also* ☐ a comparison with one rejected option ☐ 50–70 words.

The three options, and the strongest case for each:
- **(a) accept it** — the reason given is real and about safety; a narrow corner blocked twice an hour is a problem for everybody, not only the bus company.
- **(b) ask the bus company to think again** — fastest route to a change if one is possible, but an argument of one person against a stated safety reason rarely wins.
- **(c) ask the street first** — you learn what it actually costs before you argue, and eleven voices count where one does not. The cost is time, and the stop moves while you are asking.

> Sample (56 words): *I think I should accept it, because the reason is about safety and I cannot argue with a corner that is too small. Also, the bus is faster for everybody now, including me. This is better than asking the street, because we would spend a month on it and the answer would still be the same.*
