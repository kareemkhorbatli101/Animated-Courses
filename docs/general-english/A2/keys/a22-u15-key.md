**Part 1: Pronunciation** (Track 15.1)

The third time this class has met the same rule, and the first time it is worth naming as a rule.

*Have* and *has* weaken and contract in the middle of a sentence: *I’ve been*, *she’s eaten*. In a question at the front they go to a schwa: /həv juː/.
In a short answer at the end they go strong and never contract: *Yes, I have.* A learner who answers *Yes, I’ve* has made the one mistake this drill exists to prevent.

Say out loud that this is the same behaviour as *can* in Unit 8 of volume one and *will* in Unit 12 of this one. English does it to every auxiliary. A learner who sees the pattern once stops needing it taught again.

**Part 2 · Grammar — The Present Perfect**

**Part 2: Notice**

A whole life: **Have you ever slept outside?** · **She has never flown**
A small word that moves the meaning: **He has already eaten** · **They have not arrived yet**
Plain experience: **I have been to four countries** · **I have lost a passport twice**

The teaching point, and it is the only one that matters: the present perfect is for experience with no date. The moment a finished time appears — *last year*, *on Tuesday*, *in March* — English switches to the past simple. Learners from languages where the perfect carries the date will produce *I have been there last year* for months. Correct it every time and explain it once.

**Part 2: Controlled Practice**

0. have been *(given)* · 1. has / flown · 2. Have / slept · 3. has / eaten · 4. have

**Part 2: Experience or Finished Time?**

0. experience *(given)* · 1. finished time · 2. experience · 3. finished time · 4. experience

**Part 2: Correct the Sentence**

1. I **went** there last year. *(a finished date takes the past simple)*
2. Have you ever **been** to a festival? *(the third form, not the past)*
3. She has never **flown**. *(*fly* is irregular)*
4. He **has** already eaten. *(*he* takes* has*)*

**Part 4 · Speaking**

All four tasks are productive. Accept any answer that meets the brief; correct form, not content.

**Part 4: Things You Have Done**

Marking points: ☐ *Have you ever…?* at least once ☐ one *I have never* ☐ no finished dates inside a present perfect.

> Sample: *I have slept on the floor of an airport and in a car and once in a field, and the field was the best of the three. I have never ridden a horse, which surprises people who know where I grew up. The best thing I have eaten abroad was a soup I could not name and have never found again.*

**Part 4: Find the Difference (pairwork)**

Marking points: ☐ *Have you ever…?* questions ☐ exactly three differences.

Three differences: **sleeping outside** (once / many times) · **flying** (never / has flown) · and the pair must find that **been abroad** and **two festivals** match. Note that the first difference is about *how many*, not *whether*: a pair that only asks yes-or-no questions will report two and be wrong.

> Sample exchange: *A: Have you ever been abroad? — B: Yes. — A: Same. Have you slept outside? — B: Many times. — A: Once. One. Have you flown? — B: Yes. — A: Never. Two. Festivals? — B: Two. — A: Two as well. So that is two differences? — B: Three. Mine was many times, yours was once.*

**Part 4: Finding Something in Common**

Marking points: ☐ A opens with *ever* ☐ A asks again after a short answer ☐ A offers something of their own ☐ B answers in two words ☐ B says the surprising part.

Card A must produce: the *ever* question, a second ask, a question about wanting, a contribution.
Card B must produce: brevity, patience, the surprising detail, no apology.

The commonest fault is A telling their own story immediately after the first answer. The card says find something of your own — at the end, not instead of the second question.

***Stretch:*** you have done almost nothing on their list. Accept *I have done none of those, and I have never minded. I have been in the same three streets for eleven years and I know every person in them.* The marker is a positive statement and not one word of apology.

**Part 4: Mini-Presentation**

Marking points: ☐ about one minute ☐ three things done ☐ one never ☐ no finished dates inside the perfect.

> Sample (52 words): *I have lived in two countries, I have learned to swim as an adult, and I have read the same book four times and understood it differently each time. I have never learned to drive. I keep saying I will, and then another year goes past and the buses are still there.*

**Part 6 · Writing**

Each task already prints a model. These are the marking points and a second sample, so that the printed model is not the only answer a learner has seen.

**Part 6: The Best Thing You Have Done (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ two present-perfect sentences ☐ one *never* ☐ what you remember ☐ no finished date inside a perfect ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (54 words): *I have swum in the sea in February, once, on a day somebody dared me. I have done warmer and more sensible things and I remember none of them. What I have kept is the sound forty people make when one of them goes in. I have never been able to describe it properly.*

**Part 6: Is Travel the Best Way to Learn? (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ a clear opinion ☐ two or three reasons ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (53 words): *It is, and the people who say otherwise have usually travelled. Two weeks somewhere teaches you less than a book, that is true. But it teaches you one thing no book does: that the way you grew up is a choice somebody made, and that other people made it differently and are fine.*

**Part 6: Something You Have Never Done (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ what you have never done ☐ why not ☐ how you feel about it ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (53 words): *I have never been to a football match. There is no reason. My family did not go, nobody took me at the age when people get taken, and by the time it was my own decision it had somehow become a thing about me. I would go now. Nobody has ever asked me.*

**Part 6: Reflection — A Memory That Will Not Go (40–60 words)**

Marking points: ☐ what the memory is ☐ how long you have had it ☐ why it is strange ☐ 40–60 words.

> Sample (49 words): *I have carried, since I was six, the smell of the inside of a particular cupboard. Nothing happened in that cupboard. I have forgotten my first teacher and most of that house. The cupboard is still exactly there, and I have no idea what my head wanted with it.*

**7A: Phrase Bank**

Item 2 is the one to teach. *I have, actually — once* answers the question and hands the conversation back, and a learner who answers only *yes* has closed it. The *actually* is doing almost all the work.

**7D: Role-Play — Asking About a Life**

Marking points: ☐ A asks the obvious question ☐ A notices the answer is not one ☐ A asks once more ☐ B answers in three words ☐ B gives the reason rather than the answer.

> Sample: *A: You have lived abroad. — B: I have. — A: Where? — B: Everybody asks that. — A: And you never say? — B: I say the easy one. — A: Which is not the real one. — B: No. — A: All right. I will not ask again.*

***Stretch:*** they ask about the one experience you never discuss. Accept *That one I keep for about four people, and it is nothing to do with you. Ask me the one before it.* The marker is a refusal that offers something in its place.

**7E: Writing — An Experience, for Somebody Who Was Not There (50–80 words)**

Marking points: ☐ what it was, in one sentence ☐ when and where ☐ the one thing you remember ☐ 50–80 words.

> Sample (66 words): *I have been in a power cut in a block of flats, which sounds like nothing. It was a Tuesday in January and it lasted four hours. What I remember is that within about twenty minutes there were nine people on the stairs with candles, including two I had never met in six years, and that it has never happened again since the power came back.*

**Part 9 · Close to Home — What Nobody at Number 14 Has Done**

0. Three. *(given)*
1. In winter.
2. Nine years.
3. Everybody said yes and nobody picked a day.

**Part 9: Decision Task**

No option is wrong. The task is the reasoning, and the frame must be used.

Marking points: ☐ a choice stated in the first sentence ☐ a *because* reason ☐ a second point after *Also* ☐ a comparison with one rejected option ☐ 50–70 words.

The three options, and the strongest case for each:
- **(a) pick a day yourself** — the only one that produces a date, and a plan without a date is not one. It also decides for five people, and somebody will be unable to come and will not say so.
- **(b) ask once more and let it go** — honest, and it stops you being the person who organises everything for ever. It also means the thing does not happen, which is what happened the first two times.
- **(c) go alone** — the restaurant gets visited, which is more than three years of asking has managed. It is also the opposite of the point, which was the six of them.

> Sample (59 words): *I think I should go alone, because the thing I actually want is to eat there and I have been waiting for five other people to want it as much. Also, going once makes it easy to say come next Thursday, it is good. This is better than picking a day, because a date nobody chose gets quietly missed.*
