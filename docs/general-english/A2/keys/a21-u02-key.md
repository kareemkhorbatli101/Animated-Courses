**Part 1: Pronunciation** (Track 2.1)

Stress as printed: **BAL**-co-ny · **CUP**-board · up-**STAIRS** · down-**STAIRS** · re-**CY**-cling · **EN**-trance

*Upstairs* and *downstairs* take the stress on the second part, because the first part is the one that does not change. Learners who stress *UP-stairs* are hearing two words where English hears one.

The hidden letters: *cupboard* is said /ˈkʌbəd/ — the **p** is gone and the **oa** is one short sound. *Balcony* has three syllables, not two.

**Part 2 · Grammar — There Is, There Are, Some, Any, Much, Many**

**Part 2: Notice**

*there is* — a shop · no lift · not much space
*there are* — five flats · some plants · not many quiet nights

Countable: flats, plants, nights → *are*, *some*, *many*. Uncountable: space → *is*, *much*.

**Part 2: Controlled Practice**

0. There is *(given)* · 1. There are · 2. There is · 3. There are · 4. There is

**Part 2: Some, Any, Much or Many?**

0. some *(given)* · 1. many · 2. any · 3. much · 4. some

**Part 2: Correct the Sentence**

1. There **is** a garden behind the building. *(one thing)*
2. There are not **many** flats on this floor. *(you can count flats)*
3. Are there **any** lifts in the block? *(a question takes any)*
4. There is not much light in the corridor. *(light is uncountable, so* there is *not* there are*)*

**Part 2: Freer Practice**

Open. One *there is* and one *there are*, both correctly formed.

> Sample: *There is a lift in my building, and it is always slow. There are twelve flats on my floor.*

**Part 4 · Speaking**

All four tasks are productive. Accept any answer that meets the brief; correct form, not content.

**Part 4: Describing Where You Live**

Marking points: ☐ *there is* and *there are* both used ☐ at least one *much* or *many* ☐ a question asked as well as answered.

> Sample: *There are four rooms in my flat and the living room is the biggest. There is no garden, but there is a balcony where I keep two plants. In my street there is a bakery that I use every week.*

**Part 4: Find the Difference (pairwork)**

Marking points: ☐ questions with *Is there* and *How many* ☐ exactly three differences reported.

Three differences: **balcony** (small one / none) · **noise** (quiet at night / noisy on Fridays) · and the third is a trap — the **rent is the same** and so is the **number of rooms**. Pairs who report four differences have guessed.

> Sample exchange: *A: How many rooms are there? — B: Three. — A: Me too. Is there a balcony? — B: No, there isn’t. — A: There is a small one in mine. How much is the rent? — B: Six hundred. — A: The same. Is it quiet? — B: Not on Fridays.*

**Part 4: Showing Somebody the Flat**

Marking points: ☐ both partners speak ☐ *there is* / *there are* in the description ☐ one honest answer that is not all good ☐ a closing opinion from B.

Card A must produce: a welcome, the number of rooms, an honest answer, one good and one less good thing.
Card B must produce: questions about storage, noise and rent, and a verdict.

***Stretch:*** accept *The flat above is noisy on Fridays, but they stop by eleven* — a real fact plus something that makes it liveable. Do not accept *Everything is perfect.*

**Part 4: Mini-Presentation**

Marking points: ☐ about one minute ☐ what there is ☐ what there is not much of ☐ a reason for liking it or not.

> Sample (51 words): *I want to talk about my kitchen. There is a window over the sink and there are two shelves, and that is almost everything. There is not much space, so two people cannot cook there. I like it anyway, because the window looks at the garden and the light is good.*

**Part 6 · Writing**

Each task already prints a model. These are the marking points and a second sample, so that the printed model is not the only answer a learner has seen.

**Part 6: Describe a Room (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ at least one *there is* and one *there are* ☐ at least one *much* or *many* ☐ who uses it ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (56 words): *There are two chairs and a very low table in Mr Okonkwo’s living room. There are newspapers on every shelf, and there are more under the window. There is not much room to walk. He uses it every afternoon, and from his chair he can see the whole street, which is the point of the room.*

**Part 6: A Good Place to Live (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ a clear opinion in the first sentence ☐ two or three reasons, each with *because* or a linker ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (53 words): *A good place to live is not the quietest one. There are streets near me where nothing happens and nobody knows anybody. I would rather have a little noise and a shop that is open late. In my opinion the test is simple: can you borrow something from a neighbour without feeling strange?*

**Part 6: A Message About a Problem (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ polite, not accusing ☐ the problem stated once and clearly ☐ an offer of help ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (56 words): *Hello everybody. There are three bags by the front door and they have been there since Monday. There is not much room to get a buggy past them. I do not know whose they are, and I am not complaining — I am happy to move them to the cupboard if nobody minds. Maya, flat 2*

**Part 6: Reflection — My Ideal Home (40–60 words)**

Marking points: ☐ three or four things named ☐ at least one *there is not* ☐ 40–60 words.

> Sample (45 words): *In my ideal home there is one room that is only for quiet. There are no screens in it and there is not much furniture: a chair, a lamp, a shelf. Everything else can be noisy. There is a door, and the door closes properly.*

**7C: Controlled Practice — Reporting a Problem**

0. Say who you are and where you live — **1** *(given)*
Say when it started — **3**
Say what the problem is — **2**
Agree a day — **5**
Answer their questions — **4**

**7D: Role-Play — Asking for a Repair**

Marking points: ☐ the flat identified first ☐ the problem in one sentence ☐ when it started ☐ a day agreed ☐ nobody loses their temper.

> Sample: *A: Good morning, this is flat 5 at number 14. There is no hot water before eight. — B: Since when? — A: Since Monday. — B: Is the heating working? — A: Yes, only the water. — B: Could somebody come on Thursday? — A: Thursday is fine, thank you.*

***Stretch:*** accept *I am sorry, I am not at home on Thursday. Would Friday be possible?* The marker is a refusal plus an alternative, not a complaint.

**7E: Writing — A Repair Request (50–80 words)**

Marking points: ☐ the address and flat ☐ the problem and when it started ☐ why it matters, briefly ☐ a day you are free ☐ 50–80 words.

> Sample (61 words): *Good morning. I am writing from flat 5 at 14 Alder Street. There is no hot water before about eight in the morning, and it has been like this since Monday. The heating is fine, so I think it is only the water. Could somebody come and look this week? I am at home all day Thursday. Thank you. Yuki Tanaka*

**Part 9 · Close to Home — The Empty Flat**

0. On the first floor, next to Dani. *(given)*
1. Because the shutters are closed.
2. He put a box under the letter boxes so the post had somewhere to go.
3. They never agreed to do any of it — each home answers the question in its own small way.

**Part 9: Decision Task**

No option is wrong. The task is the reasoning, and the frame must be used.

Marking points: ☐ a choice stated in the first sentence ☐ a *because* reason ☐ a second point after *Also* ☐ a comparison with one rejected option ☐ 50–70 words.

The three options, and the strongest case for each:
- **(a) leave it alone** — it is not your flat and not your post; doing nothing is sometimes the respectful answer, and the pile hurts nobody.
- **(b) ask the neighbours** — cheap, friendly, and usually enough; somebody in a building of five normally knows something.
- **(c) write to the owner or the council** — the only option that can actually resolve it, but it makes a small thing official and you may not have the facts yet.

> Sample (49 words): *I think I should write to the council, because the post is not safe on an open landing and nobody else can stop it arriving. Also, it has gone on for months. This is better than asking the neighbours, because we asked each other already and nobody knows anything.*
