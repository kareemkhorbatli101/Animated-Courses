**Part 1: Pronunciation** (Track 4.1)

Stress as printed: re-**CEIPT** · **VEG**-ta-ble · **BAS**-ket · ex-**PEN**-sive · **SU**-gar · **AISLE**

*Receipt* and *expensive* take the stress on the second syllable; the other four take it on the first. Group them that way rather than teaching six separate facts.

The silent letters are the point of this drill: the **p** in *receipt*, the **s** in *aisle*, and the middle **e** in *vegetable* that four syllables on paper become three in the mouth. Learners who spell by sound will drop the letters; learners who speak by spelling will add syllables. Say both forms.

**Part 1: Six Things and How We Count Them**

0. Milk — a bottle of milk — the fridge at the back *(given)*

Accept any sensible pair. The expected answers:

| Thing | We say | Where in the shop |
|---|---|---|
| Bread | a loaf of bread, a slice of bread | by the door, where it is fresh |
| Rice | a bag of rice, a kilo of rice | the dry aisle, with the pasta |
| Tea | a packet of tea, a cup of tea | next to the coffee |

The teaching point: none of these four is countable on its own. We count the container, the piece or the weight.

**Part 2 · Grammar — Countable and Uncountable, A, An, The**

**Part 2: Notice**

Countable: **onions** · **tins** · **eggs**
Uncountable: **bread** · **milk** · **meat** · **cheese**

With the uncountable ones we count the container or the piece instead: *a basket of onions*, *a slice of bread*, *a bottle of milk*.

**Part 2: Controlled Practice**

0. some *(given)* · 1. an · 2. some · 3. a · 4. the

Item 1 takes *an* because *onion* starts with a vowel sound. Item 4 takes *the* because it is the receipt we already know about.

**Part 2: A Few or A Little?**

0. a few *(given)* · 1. a few · 2. a little · 3. a little · 4. a few

Item 3 is the one to stop on: *money* is uncountable in English even though you can count coins.

**Part 2: Correct the Sentence**

1. I would like **some** bread, please. *(bread is uncountable)*
2. There are **a few** onions in the basket. *(onions are countable)*
3. Amina sells **a little** cheese and a lot of bread. *(cheese is uncountable)*
4. Yuki buys **a bottle of** water every morning. *(water is uncountable; the bottle is not)*

**Part 2: Freer Practice**

Open. One countable food and one uncountable one, each with the right quantifier.

> Sample: *There are always a few eggs in my fridge, and usually not many. There is never much milk, because I drink it too fast.*

**Part 4 · Speaking**

All four tasks are productive. Accept any answer that meets the brief; correct form, not content.

**Part 4: Talking About Food**

Marking points: ☐ a frequency expression in the first answer ☐ one *a few* or *a little* ☐ one negative — something they never buy.

> Sample: *I shop twice a week, usually at the big shop and sometimes at the market. There is always rice and there are always eggs, and I never buy biscuits, because I eat the whole packet. The one thing I cannot find here is the cheese we have at home.*

**Part 4: Find the Difference (pairwork)**

Marking points: ☐ *How many* with countable, *How much* with uncountable ☐ exactly three differences.

Three differences: **bread** (fresh / none) · **milk** (a little / a lot) · **lemons** (three / one). The **eggs are the same** in both, six and six. Pairs who report four have guessed.

> Sample exchange: *A: How many eggs are there? — B: Six. — A: Me too. Is there any bread? — B: No, none. — A: I have fresh bread. How much milk have you got? — B: A lot. — A: Only a little here. And lemons? — B: One. — A: Three.*

**Part 4: At the Market**

Marking points: ☐ a price asked and given ☐ *How much* or *How many* used correctly ☐ an amount, not just a name ☐ the money and the change both handled.

Card A must produce: a price question, an amount, one more item, payment.
Card B must produce: a price, a how much/how many question, a total, change and receipt.

***Stretch:*** accept *I am sorry, I have not got enough — could you take the lemons off?* The marker is a clean, unembarrassed withdrawal, not an apology that goes on.

**Part 4: Mini-Presentation**

Marking points: ☐ about one minute ☐ one *a lot of* ☐ one *a little* or *a few* ☐ who eats it.

> Sample (48 words): *I cook a soup with lentils about once a week. I need a lot of lentils, a few carrots and a little oil, and the rest is water and time. It is cheap and it makes enough for three days, which is good, because I cook for one.*

**Part 6 · Writing**

Each task already prints a model. These are the marking points and a second sample, so that the printed model is not the only answer a learner has seen.

**Part 6: Describe a Meal You Cook (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ one countable food with *a few* ☐ one uncountable food with *a little* or *a lot of* ☐ who eats it ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (58 words): *Maya makes the same pasta every Sunday. She needs a lot of tomatoes, a little salt and three or four cloves of garlic, and nothing else at all. It takes twenty minutes and it is better the next day. She cooks enough for two, because Tomas comes home at some point and he is always hungry.*

**Part 6: Small Shop or Big Shop? (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ a clear opinion ☐ two or three reasons ☐ one point for the other side ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (52 words): *The small shop is better, even though it costs more. You can go at seven in the morning, you can go in slippers, and somebody notices if you have not been in for a week. The big shop has everything and knows nobody. I pay a little extra for the second thing.*

**Part 6: A Shopping Message (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ three things with amounts ☐ one instruction for if something is missing ☐ polite ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (55 words): *Dani, are you going past the shop? Could you get a kilo of rice, a little butter and some cheese — the big bag if there is one. If there is no butter do not go anywhere else, we can manage. I think there is money in the jar by the door. Thank you. Yuki*

**Part 6: Reflection — A Food I Miss (40–60 words)**

Marking points: ☐ the food named ☐ why it is hard to find ☐ what you did with it ☐ 40–60 words.

> Sample (44 words): *I miss the small sour plums we ate in summer. There are plums here, but they are big and sweet and they are not the same thing. We used to eat them with salt, standing by the tree, and nobody ever brought a bowl.*

**7B: A Problem at the Till** (Track 4.5)

0. The change. *(given)*
1. A twenty.
2. He gave change from a ten instead of from a twenty.
3. The tomatoes went through twice.

**7C: Controlled Practice — When Something Is Wrong**

0. Say politely that you think there is a problem — **1** *(given)*
Thank them — **5**
Say what you gave and what it cost — **2**
Wait while they check — **3**
Take the right change — **4**

**7D: Role-Play — Sorting It Out**

Marking points: ☐ the problem raised without blame ☐ the facts given plainly ☐ the apology accepted lightly ☐ thanks at the end.

> Sample: *A: Sorry, I think the change is wrong. — B: Let me look. What did you give me? — A: A twenty. It was eleven forty. — B: Ah, I gave you change from a ten. Sorry about that. — A: It is easy to do. — B: Here is the rest. — A: Thank you.*

***Stretch:*** the assistant is sure there is no mistake. Accept *Would you mind checking the receipt with me? I may have it wrong.* The marker is a second ask that gives the other person a way out.

**7E: Writing — A Message About a Receipt (50–80 words)**

Marking points: ☐ when you shopped ☐ the mistake, specifically ☐ the amount ☐ a tone that is not angry ☐ 50–80 words.

> Sample (62 words): *Good morning. I was at your shop yesterday at about four. Looking at the receipt at home, I see the milk is on it twice. It is only one pound ten and I am not worried, but I thought you would want to know, in case the till is doing it to everybody. I am usually in on Saturdays. Thank you. Maya Oduya*

**Part 8 · Global Story — The Shop With No Prices**

0. In Fez, in Morocco. *(given)*
1. A label — a price written on the thing.

3. Because a shop with four hundred products and six assistants cannot haggle four hundred times a day.

**Part 8: Discussion**

Open. Each learner should produce at least one *similar* frame and one *different* frame, with a reason.

> Sample: *This is similar to where I live because at our market you still ask the price and nobody writes it down. This is different from where I live because in the shops everything has a label and nobody would dream of arguing. I think the most important thing is that the talking is part of it, because once there is a label there is nothing to say.*

**Part 9 · Close to Home — The Book Under the Till**

0. Under Amina’s till. *(given)*
1. Names and small numbers — what people owe.
2. Somebody moved away without paying, and Amina closed the page and said nothing.
3. He wants her to stop; he thinks the book is a bad idea.

**Part 9: Decision Task**

No option is wrong. The task is the reasoning, and the frame must be used.

Marking points: ☐ a choice stated in the first sentence ☐ a *because* reason ☐ a second point after *Also* ☐ a comparison with one rejected option ☐ 50–70 words.

The three options, and the strongest case for each:
- **(a) keep the book** — it is cheap, it is why the street uses the shop at all, and two bad debts in eleven years is a very small cost.
- **(b) stop it** — a small shop has almost no margin; the son is right that it is money the business cannot really spare, and it is simpler for whoever runs the shop next.

> Sample (54 words): *I think I should keep it only for people I have known a long time, because that is where the risk is smallest. Also, I can still say yes to almost everybody. This is better than keeping it for anybody, because one bad month would cost me more than the goodwill is worth.*
- **(c) keep it for people you have known a long time** — most of the benefit with most of the risk removed, but it creates two kinds of customer in a street of four hundred people, and everybody will know which kind they are.
