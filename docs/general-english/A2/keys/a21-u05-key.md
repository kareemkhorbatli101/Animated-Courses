**Part 1: Pronunciation** (Track 5.1)

This drill is the one that decides whether a learner sounds fluent in the past simple, so give it real time.

/t/ after a voiceless sound — walk**ed** · cook**ed** · wash**ed** · stopp**ed** · watch**ed**
/d/ after a voiced sound or a vowel — stay**ed** · open**ed** · call**ed** · play**ed** · arriv**ed**
/ɪd/ after /t/ or /d/ only — rest**ed** · wait**ed** · want**ed** · decid**ed** · start**ed**

Only the third group adds a beat. *Walked* is one beat and *rested* is two. Learners who say *walk-ed* are reading the spelling; learners who say *rest-d* have over-corrected. Drill the pairs, not the rule.

**Part 2 · Grammar — Past Simple: Was, Were and Regular Verbs**

**Part 2: Notice**

*was / were* — **was** warm · **were** noisy · **was** an ordinary day
*-ed* verbs — **opened** · **carried** · **stayed** · **cooked** · **arrived**

*Was* and *were* describe. The *-ed* verbs tell you what somebody did. Make learners sort the list into two columns before they do anything else.

**Part 2: Controlled Practice**

0. opened *(given)* · 1. carried · 2. were · 3. cooked · 4. was

Item 1 is the spelling point: *carry* ends in a consonant plus **y**, so it becomes *carried*, not *carryed*.

**Part 2: Was, Were or -ed?**

0. was *(given)* · 1. were · 2. walked · 3. was · 4. arrived

Items 2 and 4 are the trap: *was walk* and *was arrive* put the past in twice. One past per verb.

**Part 2: Correct the Sentence**

1. I **walked** to the beach on Saturday. *(one past, not two)*
2. Did you **walk** to the lake? *(did already carries the past)*
3. The children **were** noisy all afternoon. *(children is plural)*
4. Dani **did not cook** on Sunday. *(the negative needs did not)*

**Part 2: Freer Practice**

Open. One *was* or *were* sentence and one *-ed* verb, both correctly formed.

> Sample: *Last Saturday was long and quiet. I cleaned the whole flat and then I watched three films.*

**Part 2: Grammar Review**

1. **B)** Did you walk to the lake? · 2. **A)** Sunday was warm.

**Part 4 · Speaking**

All four tasks are productive. Accept any answer that meets the brief; correct form, not content.

**Part 4: Talking About Last Weekend**

Marking points: ☐ at least three past verbs ☐ one *was* or *were* ☐ a *did you* question asked, not only answered.

> Sample: *Last weekend was not normal, because my sister arrived on Friday. We walked round the city on Saturday and it rained the whole time. I wanted to show her the park and we never got there.*

**Part 4: Find the Difference (pairwork)**

Marking points: ☐ questions with *did* and the plain verb ☐ exactly three differences.

Three differences: **the weather** (warm / cold) · **who with** (a friend / alone) · and the third is the trap — **the place is the same** and so is the **time they came back**. Pairs who report four have guessed. Only two of the four facts differ, so the honest answer is two, and the pair who says so has listened properly.

> Sample exchange: *A: Where did you go? — B: The lake. — A: Me too. What was the weather like? — B: Windy and cold. — A: Windy but warm here. Who did you go with? — B: Nobody. — A: I was with a friend. What time did you come back? — B: Six. — A: The same.*

**Part 4: Telling the Story**

Marking points: ☐ four past verbs at least ☐ the weather named ☐ a *did you* question from A ☐ one thing that went wrong.

Card A must produce: four questions, in order.
Card B must produce: a place, the weather, a companion, and a problem.

***Stretch:*** the weekend was dull. Accept a learner who makes two minutes out of nothing — the queue, the rain, what they almost did — rather than one who invents a holiday.

**Part 4: Mini-Presentation**

Marking points: ☐ about one minute ☐ where ☐ the weather ☐ one thing that did not go to plan.

> Sample (50 words): *Two summers ago we went to a beach three hours away. The weather was perfect and the water was warm. Then we walked back to the car and it was not there, because we parked on the wrong road, and we spent an hour looking for it in the dark.*

**Part 6 · Writing**

Each task already prints a model. These are the marking points and a second sample, so that the printed model is not the only answer a learner has seen.

**Part 6: Describe Last Weekend (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ one *was* or *were* ☐ at least three *-ed* verbs ☐ the weather ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (55 words): *Last Saturday was the first warm day of the year. I opened every window in the flat and then I decided not to clean anything at all. I walked to the market and carried home more than I needed. On Sunday it rained all day and I was quite pleased, because I wanted an excuse.*

**Part 6: The Best Kind of Weekend (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ a clear opinion ☐ two or three reasons ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (54 words): *The best weekend has other people in it on Saturday and nobody in it on Sunday. I like company, but I need one day where nothing is arranged and nobody is waiting. A weekend that is full of plans is a short week. I would rather be a little bored than a little late.*

**Part 6: A Message After a Visit (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ a thank you ☐ one specific detail ☐ one apology or joke ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (56 words): *Dani, thank you for Saturday. There was far too much rice and you knew it, and it was still the best meal I have eaten this month. I am sorry I arrived so late — the bus simply did not come. Next time I will bring something, and I will not promise to be on time.*

**Part 6: Reflection — A Weekend I Remember (40–60 words)**

Marking points: ☐ when ☐ what happened ☐ why it stayed with you ☐ 40–60 words.

> Sample (43 words): *I remember the weekend we moved house, which sounds like a strange thing to remember well. It rained, nothing fitted through the door, and we ate on the floor at midnight. We were exhausted and we laughed more than we had all year.*

**7C: Controlled Practice — When a Plan Falls Through**

0. Say what happened to you — **1** *(given)*
Accept it and move on — **5**
Listen to the reason — **2**
Say calmly what it cost you — **3**
Agree the new arrangement — **4**

**7D: Role-Play — Sorting Out a Lost Morning**

Marking points: ☐ the facts stated without shouting ☐ the cost named once ☐ a real offer from B ☐ the offer accepted or refused clearly.

> Sample: *A: I waited an hour and nobody came. — B: I am very sorry, we cancelled it at seven. — A: Nobody told me. — B: That was our mistake. — A: It was a cold hour. — B: I know. Shall I move you to Saturday and take off the cost? — A: Yes. Thank you.*

***Stretch:*** the company offers nothing. Accept a learner who asks once, plainly — *Is there anything you can do?* — and then states a decision rather than arguing. Do not accept a monologue.

**7E: Writing — A Message About a Lost Morning (50–80 words)**

Marking points: ☐ what was booked ☐ what happened, with times ☐ what you want ☐ a calm tone ☐ 50–80 words.

> Sample (59 words): *Good morning. I booked two places on Saturday’s trip. We were at the stop from a quarter to eight until nine, and nothing came. I have since read that it did not run. I understand that the weather was bad; I would like to have been told. Could you move the booking to next Saturday? Thank you. Maya Oduya*

**Part 8: Discussion**

Open. Each learner should produce at least one *similar* frame and one *different* frame, with a reason.

I think the most important thing is that it was a choice all along, because once you see that, you can argue about it.*

**Part 9 · Close to Home — Tomas and the Saturday He Missed**

0. Eleven. *(given)*
1. Because he chose the hours and the pay is better.
2. A message and a photograph of the cake he had left in the kitchen.
3. That in forty years of teaching he never once worked a Saturday, and he does not know how anybody manages it.

**Part 9: Decision Task**

No option is wrong. The task is the reasoning, and the frame must be used.

Marking points: ☐ a choice stated in the first sentence ☐ a *because* reason ☐ a second point after *Also* ☐ a comparison with one rejected option ☐ 50–70 words.

The three options, and the strongest case for each:
- **(a) take the hours and the pay** — the money is real and so is the quiet; people who work nights often prefer them, and Tomas is one of them.
- **(b) move to weekdays** — the only option that actually solves the problem rather than managing it, at the cost of pay and of work the person may like better.
- **(c) protect one weekend a month** — cheap to ask for, hard to refuse, and it keeps both things. It does not fix anything, which is sometimes the right answer and sometimes a way of not deciding.

> Sample (55 words): *I think I should ask to change to weekdays, because eleven Saturdays out of twelve is not a job, it is a wall. Also, the extra pay never quite covers what you miss. This is better than protecting one weekend a month, because one weekend a month is still eleven you are not there for.*
