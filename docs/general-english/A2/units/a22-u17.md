> **Listen to the two forms. Say each pair aloud.**

> Somebody makes it — the verb carries the weight: /meɪks/

**Part 2: Grammar Focus Box**

*Figure 17.12 · The thing moves to the front, and the maker becomes optional.*

| | **Form** | **Use** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **active** | makes, made, produces | the maker is the point | Maya made this book. |
| **passive** | is made, was made, are produced | the thing is the point | This book was made by hand. |
| **be + 3rd form** | is / was / are / were + made | the form never changes | The buttons were changed. |
| **by + maker** | was made by Maya | only when the maker is news | This one was made by Maya. |

**Remember:** the passive is *be* plus the third form. The third form never changes: *made*, *broken*, *put*, *sold*.

> **Watch out!** ✗ *This book was make by hand.* → ✓ *This book was made by hand.* (the third form, not the first). ✗ *The bowl made in a factory.* → ✓ *The bowl was made in a factory.* (*be* is not optional).

**Part 5: Why Nobody Knows Who Made Anything**

*Figure 17.23 · One cup, in five stages.*

Before you read: can you name one person who made one thing you own?

> **Gloss:** machine = a thing with moving parts that does work · stage = one step in a long job · hand-made = made by a person and not by a machine
>
> Count the things in your room. Now name the maker of one of them. Most people cannot name one, and this is new. Two hundred years ago almost everything was made within a short walk of where it was used. The maker was somebody you could complain to. Now a cup is made in stages, in two countries, and no single person has held all of it. This is why the passive became so useful. English needed a way to talk about a thing when there is nobody to put in front of the verb. The grammar followed the machines. And it works the other way too: a thing sold as hand-made is sold on the maker, not on the cup.

**Part 5: Three Things That Are Made Twice**

*Figure 17.25 · Three materials, and what each one becomes next.*

Before you read: what do you throw away that could be used again?

> **Gloss:** melt = to turn a solid into a liquid with heat · pulp = wet paper broken up to be made into paper again · ore = the rock metal is taken out of · aluminium = a light grey metal, used for cans and pans · fibre = one of the tiny threads a material is made of
>
> Three materials are made twice, and each one loses something the second time. Glass is melted and made again, and it can be done for ever, because melting takes nothing out of it. Paper is turned into pulp and made again. The fibres are shortened each time, so after six or seven turns it is only good for boxes. Metal is the strange one. It is melted like glass and loses almost nothing. The saving is enormous: recycled aluminium takes about a twentieth of the power needed to make it from ore. So the material it is most worth recycling is the one people throw away without looking.

1. Why can glass be made again for ever?

2. What happens to paper each time it is made again?

3. How much power does recycled aluminium take?

**Part 8 · Global Story — The City That Drinks Its Own Water Twice**

***[PLUS] Plus track · pre-read / discuss***

*Figure 17.35 · Water leaving a city and the same water coming back.*

Before you read: would you drink water that had been used before?

> **Gloss:** sewage = the dirty water that leaves a city through pipes · membrane = a very thin sheet with holes too small to see · reservoir = a lake kept for a city’s water

> Singapore has almost no land and no river worth the name, and for years most of its water was bought from Malaysia. So the used water is now taken back. Sewage is carried to a plant, pushed through membranes with holes a bacterium cannot pass, and cleaned again with light. What comes out is cleaner than the water in the reservoir. It is called NEWater, and about two-fifths of the island’s water is now produced this way. Most of it is sold to factories, which need water cleaner than people do. The rest is put into the reservoir first, because a thing is easier to drink after it has been rained on.
