**Part 2 · Grammar — There Is, There Are, Some, Any, Much, Many**

***[CORE] Core track · pre-class input***

**Part 2: Notice**

> **Read these lines about number 14. Underline *there is* and *there are*. Which things can you count?**
>
> There is a shop on the ground floor. There are five flats above it. There is no lift. There are some plants on Yuki’s balcony. There is not much space in Amina’s kitchen. There are not many quiet nights in the flat above Dani.
>
> You can count flats, plants and nights: they take *are*, *some* and *many*. You cannot count space: it takes *is* and *much*.

**Part 2: Grammar Focus Box**

*Figure 2.5 · Countable and uncountable: what changes, and what does not.*

| | **Form** | **Use** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **There is** | there is + one thing | one thing, or a thing you cannot count | There is a shop downstairs. |
| **There are** | there are + things | more than one thing you can count | There are five flats. |
| **some / any** | some in +, any in − and ? | things you do not count exactly | There are some plants. Are there any lifts? |
| **much / many** | much + uncountable, many + countable | asking or saying how many | not much space · not many nights |

**Remember:** *some* when you say yes; *any* when you say no or ask.

> **Watch out!** ✗ *There are a shop downstairs.* → ✓ *There is a shop downstairs.* (one thing takes *is*). ✗ *There are not much flats.* → ✓ *There are not many flats.* (you can count flats).

**Part 8 · Global Story — The Street That Built Its Own Square**

> In a small city in Brazil there was a street with no shade and nowhere to sit. There were cars on both sides and there was not much room for anybody else. Before you read: what happens to a building when one flat stays empty?

> There is an empty flat on the first floor of number 14, next to Dani. It was empty in the spring, and it is empty now. There is no furniture in it and there is not much light, because the shutters are closed. For a while nobody thought about it. Then the post began to arrive for somebody who was not there. It sat on the landing in a pile, and the pile grew bigger every week. Mr Okonkwo put a box under the letter boxes so the post had somewhere to go. Dani started to clean that part of the landing. Amina asked twice at the shop whether anybody knew the name. Nobody did. There are five flats at number 14, and one of them is a question. Each of the five homes answers it in its own small way. Nobody ever agreed to.
