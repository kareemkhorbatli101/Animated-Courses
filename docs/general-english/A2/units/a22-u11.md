**Part 1: Pronunciation**

**🔊 Audio Track 11.1**

> **Listen to *going to*. Say each one aloud, then say it to a partner.**
>
> I’m going to ask them — in speech this is one quick sound: /ˈɡənə/
>
> He’s going to cook — the same again: *he’s gonna cook*
>
> We’re going to the station — here it is slow and clear, because *to* is a place
>
> Are you going to come? — quick again, because *come* is a verb
>
> The rule is simple. When *going to* carries a verb, English runs it together. When it carries a place, it stays apart. Say all three words every time and you are understood, and you always sound like a book.

**Part 2: Grammar Focus Box**

*Figure 11.5 · Arranged, and only decided: two futures, two jobs.*

| | **Form** | **Use** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **present continuous** | am/is/are + -ing | an arrangement, with a time | I am meeting him at nine. |
| **going to** | am/is/are going to + verb | a decision, not yet arranged | I am going to cook for eight. |
| **going to** (evidence) | the same form | what you can already see | Look at that sky — it is going to rain. |
| **negative** | am/is/are not | both forms, the same way | I am not working on Saturday. |

**Remember:** an arrangement needs another person or a time: *I am meeting him at nine*, not *I am meeting one day*.

> **Watch out!** ✗ *I going to cook.* → ✓ *I am going to cook.* (*going to* always needs *am*, *is* or *are*). ✗ *I will meet her at nine, it is arranged.* → ✓ *I am meeting her at nine.* (English uses the continuous for arrangements).

**Part 5: How Far Ahead Do People Plan?**

*Figure 11.9 · One invite, from the idea to the evening itself.*

Before you read: is a plan made a month ahead a kinder plan or a colder one?

> **Gloss:** spontaneous = done without planning · notice = how much warning somebody gets · calendar = where people keep their dates · compliment = something kind said about you

> Ask somebody in one country to dinner next Tuesday and they say yes. Ask somebody in another country the same thing and they look in a calendar and offer you a date five weeks away. **Part 5: Three Ways of Keeping a Week**

*Figure 11.10 · Three ways people keep track of what they are doing.*

Before you read: where do you keep what you are doing next week?

> **Gloss:** paper diary = a book you write in by hand · shared = used by more than one person · reminder = a note that comes back to you later
>
> People keep a week in very different ways, and the way shapes the week. A paper diary holds about as much as a person can really do, because the page runs out. A shared calendar at work holds far more, and anybody can put something in it. That is why a Thursday fills up with other people’s ideas. A phone reminder is the kindest of the three to a forgetful person. It is the worst for anybody who then stops remembering at all. None of them is the right one. The question is only which kind of forgetting you can afford.

**Part 8 · Global Story — The Huts That Nobody Books**

***[PLUS] Plus track · pre-read / discuss***

*Figure 11.13 · A booked bed, and a bed nobody booked.*

Before you read: can a thing work better with no booking at all?

> **Gloss:** shelter = a small building that keeps the weather off · key = the thing that opens a lock · trust = believing that people do what they should

> In the mountains of Norway there is a chain of small shelters, a day’s walk apart. For most of them there is no booking at all. You buy one key, once, and it opens every door in the chain. You walk in, you take a bed, you write your name in the book, and you pay later. If the shelter is full when you arrive, nobody turns you away. The people already there move up, and the last one in sleeps on the floor. The rule everybody knows is the one about weather. If somebody arrives wet and late, the bed goes to them. It works because the plan is the walk and not the bed. Somebody taking the last bed one week carries a stove for a stranger the next.
