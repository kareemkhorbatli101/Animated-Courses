# Sound and Symbol Table

English does not spell one sound one way. This table gives the twenty sounds
the course asks you to say, each with a plain spelling you can read on sight,
the symbol a dictionary uses, and words from the book.

The plain spelling in the middle column is how the sound is written in
Unit 1. Use it until the symbols are easy.

vowel turns into: *teach-**er***, *ba-**na**-na*. You never say it loudly.

## The three endings the course drills

**Plural s** and **he/she/it s** are the same three sounds (Units 3 and 6):

| After the sound | You say | Words |
|---|---|---|
| p, t, k, f | **s** | books, starts, works, shops |
| most other sounds | **z** | keys, lives, opens, sisters |
| s, z, sh, ch, x | **iz** | watches, finishes, buses, oranges |

**Past ed** is three sounds too, never *ed* as a full syllable except after t
or d (Unit 11):

| After the sound | You say | Words |
|---|---|---|
| p, k, f, s, sh, ch | **t** | walked, watched, washed, stopped |
| most other sounds | **d** | played, opened, lived, carried |
| t or d | **id** | visited, wanted, needed, started |

## Two things that change in fast speech

**going to** becomes one run of sound, *gonna*, in speech. Write **going to**
(Unit 14).

**can** is weak in a sentence and strong on its own. *I can **swim*** has a
quiet can; *Yes, I **can*** has a loud one. **can't** is always loud, and that
is how you hear the difference (Unit 9).
