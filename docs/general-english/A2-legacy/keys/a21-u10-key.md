**Part 1: Pronunciation** (Track 10.1)

This is the easiest drill of the ten and the one learners most often get wrong in real use, because the rule is so simple that nobody teaches it.

An English instruction puts its verb first and gives the verb the beat: **MIX** it well. **POUR** it slowly.
The words around the verb go small and fast: *it*, *the*, *too*, *now*.
A negative keeps the beat on the verb, not on the *not*: Do not **PRESS** too hard.

The failure to listen for: a learner who stresses every word equally. The sentence stops sounding like an instruction and starts sounding like a list, and a partner trying to follow with their hands has nothing to follow. Drill it with the class doing the action as they say it; the beat and the hand land together or the rhythm is wrong.

**Part 1: Four Things People Make**

0. A cake — a bowl, a spoon, an oven — mix the dry things first *(given)*

Accept any sensible set. The expected answers:

| What you make | What you need | The first step |
|---|---|---|
| A small book | paper, thread, a needle, glue | cut the paper to one size |
| A shelf | wood, screws, a drill, a level | measure the wall, not the wood |
| A pot of soup | a pan, water, whatever is left | cut everything the same size |

The shelf row is the one to talk about: measuring the wall before the wood is exactly the step an expert leaves out, which is the Part 5 reading in one line.

**Part 2 · Grammar — Imperatives, Sequencers and Adverbs**

**Part 2: Notice**

Order: **Mix** it well · **Do not press** too hard · **warm** the teapot · **pour** the water on · **wait** · **brush** the edges · **Work** slowly
When: **First** · **Then** · **After that** · **Finally**
How: **carefully** · **slowly**

The teaching point: the three jobs sit in three different places. The order word comes first, the verb comes next with the beat on it, and the *-ly* word comes last. Learners who put the *-ly* word in the middle are understood, but they sound translated.

**Part 2: Controlled Practice**

0. First *(given)* · 1. Then · 2. After that · 3. Do not · 4. Finally

**Part 2: Order, When or How?**

0. order *(given)* · 1. when · 2. how · 3. order · 4. when

**Part 2: Correct the Sentence**

1. **Mix** it well before you start. *(an instruction has no* you*)*
2. **Do not press** the leaves. *(no* to *after* do not*)*
3. Pour the water **carefully**. *(an adverb after the verb, not an adjective)*
4. After that, **leave it to dry**. *(*leave it to dry*, not* leave it dry*)*

**Part 2: Freer Practice**

Open. One positive instruction, one negative.

> Sample: *Put the pan on the small ring and turn it down as soon as it starts. Do not lift the lid for the first ten minutes, however much you want to.*

**Part 2: Grammar Review**

**Part 4 · Speaking**

All four tasks are productive. Accept any answer that meets the brief; correct form, not content.

**Part 4: How You Make Things**

Marking points: ☐ at least two order words ☐ one *-ly* word ☐ a real answer to the third question.

> Sample: *The last thing I mended was a chair leg, and it took me a weekend because I did the glue twice. I work slowly, and it shows in the sense that the chair is still standing. The step people always leave out is waiting. Everybody moves it too early.*

**Part 4: Find the Difference (pairwork)**

Marking points: ☐ *What is step one?* and *What comes after that?* questions ☐ exactly three differences.

Three differences: **step three** (glue / press) · **step four** (press / glue) · **the last step** (leave it to dry / brush the edges). **Measure and cut are the same** on both. Pairs who report four have guessed.

> Sample exchange: *A: What is step one? — B: Measure. — A: Same. Then? — B: Cut. — A: Same. After that? — B: Press. — A: Mine says glue. One. — B: Then glue. — A: Mine says press. Two. — B: Finally brush the edges. — A: Mine says leave it to dry. Three.*

**Part 4: Teaching Somebody a Job**

Marking points: ☐ A gives one step at a time ☐ A says how, not only what ☐ A names the common mistake ☐ B says what they see or feel ☐ B does not run ahead.

Card A must produce: one step, a manner, a warning, a pause.
Card B must produce: an action, an observation, a question, patience.

The commonest fault is A giving the whole job in one breath. Stop the pair and make A say one step and then stop talking. The silence is the teaching.

***Stretch:*** your partner has done the step wrong. Accept *Leave it there — look, you are pushing with your arm. Try it again with just your hand.* The marker is a correction that keeps the work in the learner’s hands.

**Part 4: Mini-Presentation**

Marking points: ☐ about one minute ☐ four steps in order ☐ at least two *-ly* words ☐ one *do not*.

> Sample (52 words): *First, warm the teapot and pour the water away; cold china kills the tea. Then put in one spoon for each person. Pour the boiling water on slowly, right onto the leaves. Wait four minutes and do not press them, however late you are. Finally, pour it out and drink it hot.*

**Part 6 · Writing**

Each task already prints a model. These are the marking points and a second sample, so that the printed model is not the only answer a learner has seen.

**Part 6: How to Make Something (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ four steps in order ☐ two *-ly* words ☐ one *do not* ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (61 words): *First, cut the bread thickly, because thin bread falls apart under the butter. Then butter it right to the edges, not just the middle. After that, lay the cheese on evenly and close it carefully. Do not press it flat, whatever you have seen in a shop. Finally, cut it corner to corner, which tastes better and I cannot explain why.*

**Part 6: Quickly or Slowly? (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ a clear opinion ☐ two or three reasons ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (53 words): *Quickly, and I know how that sounds. A slow job waits for a good afternoon, and the good afternoon does not come. A fast job is finished and wrong, and a wrong finished thing can be mended on a Sunday. The only jobs worth doing slowly are the ones you cannot do twice.*

**Part 6: Instructions That Went Wrong (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ what the instructions said ☐ what you did ☐ what happened ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (51 words): *The box said soak the beans overnight, so I soaked them for two hours and told myself it was nearly the same. Then I cooked them for the time it gave. We ate at ten, out of a pan of small stones in water, and nobody said anything, which was worse.*

**Part 6: Reflection — Something I Can Make (40–60 words)**

Marking points: ☐ what you can make ☐ who taught you ☐ how you work ☐ 40–60 words.

> Sample (49 words): *I can make bread, and it is the only thing I make the same way every time. My neighbour taught me across a kitchen table in one afternoon and wrote nothing down. I work slowly and I weigh everything, because the one time I guessed, it came out flat.*

**7C: Controlled Practice — Teaching a Job Well**

0. Show the whole job once, quickly — **1** *(given)*
Watch their hands, not their face — **4**

Break it into steps and name each one — **2**

**7D: Role-Play — Teaching a Job**

Marking points: ☐ A shows before telling ☐ A corrects the how, not the person ☐ A asks for it again ☐ B copies rather than guesses ☐ B says what they feel.

> Sample: *A: Hold it there. — B: Like this? — A: Nearly. Lower. — B: Now? — A: Yes. Press it, but not hard. — B: It feels like nothing. — A: That is what it should feel like. Do it again while I watch.*

***Stretch:*** the learner does it a different way and it works. Accept *That is not how I was taught, and it works, so keep it — but do it my way once so you know both.* The marker is a teacher who can be wrong without losing the room.

**7E: Writing — Instructions for a Complete Beginner (50–80 words)**

Marking points: ☐ the steps in order ☐ at least one *-ly* word about how ☐ one warning ☐ 50–80 words.

> Sample (64 words): *Lay the cloth flat and smooth it with your hand until there is no fold left anywhere. Thread the needle and tie one knot, no more; two knots pull through. Sew slowly from right to left, and keep the stitches the same size rather than small. Do not pull the thread tight after each stitch. Finally, tie off on the back where nobody looks.*

**Part 9 · Close to Home — The Sign That Is Still Upside Down**

0. The 14 Dani tied to the gate. *(given)*
1. He meant to do it on the Wednesday, and by the Thursday it was a thing he walked past.
2. He was not sure Dani had finished.
3. That it is on purpose.

**Part 9: Decision Task**

No option is wrong. The task is the reasoning, and the frame must be used.

Marking points: ☐ a choice stated in the first sentence ☐ a *because* reason ☐ a second point after *Also* ☐ a comparison with one rejected option ☐ 50–70 words.

The three options, and the strongest case for each:
- **(a) do it yourself** — two minutes, no conversation, and nobody has to be told they did something badly. It also quietly takes the job off the person whose job it was.
- **(b) tell the person who did it** — treats them as somebody who can fix their own work, which is what they are. It costs them a small embarrassment they did not ask for.
- **(c) leave it** — the sign works upside down, everybody can read a 14 either way up, and the street has survived worse. Leaving things is also how a street ends up with nothing working.

> Sample (55 words): *I think I should tell Dani, because it is his sign and he is the only one who can turn it without it becoming a comment. Also, he tied it on in the dark and has no idea. This is better than doing it myself, because then nobody ever learns the gate needs looking at.*
