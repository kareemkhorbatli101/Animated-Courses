**Part 1: Pronunciation** (Track 11.1)

This is the first drill of the second book and the one that does most for how a learner sounds.

*Going to* + a verb collapses to /ˈɡənə/ in ordinary speech: *I’m gonna ask them.* Nobody says the three words separately, and a learner who does is immediately heard as reading.
*Going to* + a place does not collapse: *We’re going to the station.* Here *to* is a preposition with a noun after it, and it keeps its own beat.

The test to give the class: if you can put a verb after it, it collapses. If you can put *the* after it, it does not.

The failure to listen for: learners who have been taught that *gonna* is bad English. It is not; it is what the form sounds like at speed. They should be able to produce it and recognise it, and write *going to* every time.

**Part 1: Four Plans, Four Kinds**

0. A train at nine, already paid — fixed, with a ticket — *I am catching the nine o’clock* *(given)*

Accept any sensible pair. The expected answers:

| The plan | How sure is it? | How you say it |
|---|---|---|
| Dinner with a friend on Friday | arranged, with a person | *I am having dinner with Sam on Friday.* |
| Learning to drive this year | decided, nothing booked | *I am going to learn to drive.* |
| Moving to another city one day | a wish, no date | *I would like to move one day.* |

The point of the table is the ladder, not the vocabulary: a ticket, a person, a decision, a wish. Learners who put *going to* on the first row and the continuous on the third have the ladder upside down.

**Part 2 · Grammar — Going To and the Present Continuous**

**Part 2: Notice**

Arranged, with a time or a person: **Maya is meeting her sister at nine** · **Yuki is asking them all today** · **Tomas is working on Saturday**
Only decided: **Dani is going to cook** · **Amina is going to close early** · **I am going to learn to drive**

The teaching point: both forms are present tense doing a future job, and the difference is not the time but the commitment. An arrangement has somebody else in it. A decision has only you.

**Part 2: Controlled Practice**

0. is meeting *(given)* · 1. is going to · 2. is working · 3. is going to · 4. is closing

**Part 2: Arranged or Decided?**

0. arranged *(given)* · 1. decided · 2. arranged · 3. decided · 4. arranged

**Part 2: Correct the Sentence**

1. I **am** going to cook for eight people. *(*going to* needs* am, is *or* are*)*
2. She is going to **meet** her sister at nine. *(plain verb after* going to*)*
3. We are **having** dinner at eight on Friday. *(the continuous needs* -ing*)*
4. He is going to **work** on Saturday. *(no* -s *after* going to*)*

**Part 2: Freer Practice**

Open. One arrangement with a time, one decision with no date.

> Sample: *I am seeing my brother at the airport at six on Sunday, and I am already dreading the traffic. I am going to start running again, and I have said that twice this year.*

**Part 4 · Speaking**

All four tasks are productive. Accept any answer that meets the brief; correct form, not content.

**Part 4: Your Week**

Marking points: ☐ at least two present-continuous arrangements with times ☐ one *going to* decision ☐ a real answer to the third question.

> Sample: *I am working on Saturday and seeing my mother on Sunday, which is most weekends. I am going to learn to cook three things properly, and I have been going to do that since March. My sister arranges everything in our family, about six weeks ahead, and she is never wrong about who is free.*

**Part 4: Find the Difference (pairwork)**

Marking points: ☐ *What are you doing on…?* questions ☐ exactly three differences.

Three differences: **Wednesday** (free / hospital) · **Friday** (dinner at eight / free) · and the pair should notice that **Monday and Saturday are the same**. Pairs reporting four have guessed; pairs reporting two have stopped at Wednesday.

> Sample exchange: *A: What are you doing on Monday? — B: A meeting at ten. — A: Same. Wednesday? — B: Hospital. — A: I’m free. One. Friday? — B: Nothing. — A: Dinner at eight. Two. Saturday? — B: Nothing. — A: Nothing. So three? — B: Two. Monday and Saturday match.*

**Part 4: Arranging Something Together**

Marking points: ☐ A asks before explaining ☐ A gives a day and a time ☐ A says who else is coming ☐ B says what they are doing ☐ B offers one thing.

Card A must produce: a question, a day, a time, acceptance of help.
Card B must produce: a plain answer, one question, a yes or no, an offer.

The commonest fault is A explaining the whole evening before asking whether B is free. Stop them and make the first line a question.

***Stretch:*** you cannot come and the reason is awkward. Accept *I can’t do Saturday, and I’d rather not go into why — nothing to do with you. Can I come to the next one?* The marker is a refusal that neither lies nor over-explains.

**Part 4: Mini-Presentation**

Marking points: ☐ about one minute ☐ three arrangements with times ☐ one *going to* decision that is not booked ☐ the two forms used correctly.

> Sample (55 words): *On Tuesday I am taking my car in at eight, which I arranged a month ago. On Thursday I am meeting an old colleague for lunch. On Saturday my brother is coming and staying two nights. And at some point this year I am going to repaint the back room, which I have said before.*

**Part 6 · Writing**

Each task already prints a model. These are the marking points and a second sample, so that the printed model is not the only answer a learner has seen.

**Part 6: An Invitation (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ who you are ☐ the day and the time ☐ what you are doing, in *going to* or the continuous ☐ a way out for the reader ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (57 words): *This is Dani in flat 3. I am cooking far too much rice on Sunday at six, which happens to me about once a month. Anybody who is in is welcome, and anybody who is not is not in trouble. Bring nothing. If six is too early, come at seven and the rice will still be there.*

**Part 6: Plan Ahead or Decide Late? (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ a clear opinion ☐ two or three reasons ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (54 words): *Decide late, because a diary four weeks deep belongs to the person I was last month. I agree to things in April that I resent by May. A plan made on the morning is made by somebody who knows what the week actually did to them. The cost is that I see fewer people.*

**Part 6: Changing an Arrangement (50–70 words)**

Marking points: ☐ the apology first ☐ the real reason ☐ a new day offered ☐ 50–70 words.

> Sample (52 words): *I am very sorry about Thursday. My mother is coming in on the early train and I only heard this morning, and I am not going to pretend otherwise. Can we do the same time next week? I am free every evening except Tuesday, and I am going to keep Thursday clear.*

**Part 6: Reflection — Something I Keep Not Doing (40–60 words)**

Marking points: ☐ what you are going to do ☐ how long you have said it ☐ what stops you ☐ 40–60 words.

> Sample (46 words): *I am going to write to my aunt. I decided this at a funeral four years ago and I meant it completely. Her address is on the shelf in front of me. What stops me is that the first line is now about why I waited.*

**7D: Role-Play — Changing an Arrangement**

Marking points: ☐ A apologises before explaining ☐ A gives the real reason ☐ A offers a new day ☐ B asks once and no more ☐ B ends it warmly.

> Sample: *A: I’m sorry, I can’t do Friday. — B: What’s happened? — A: My shift changed and I found out this morning. — B: These things happen. — A: Can we say the Friday after? — B: That works. — A: I’m going to keep it free this time.*

***Stretch:*** it is the third time you have changed it. Accept *This is the third time, and I know it. If I cancel again, stop asking me and I won’t blame you.* The marker is naming the pattern yourself before the other person has to.

**7E: Writing — Arranging Something with Four People (50–80 words)**

Marking points: ☐ the day, the date and the time ☐ what people have to do and by when ☐ one line addressed to one person ☐ 50–80 words.

> Sample (65 words): *Sunday the ninth, one o’clock, at the long table in the back room. I am booking for however many of us answer by Friday, so answer by Friday. Maya, I know Sundays are hard with the exam, so come for an hour if that is all you have. There is no present and no occasion. Say yes or no to all of us at once.*

**Part 9 · Close to Home — The Saturday Nobody Had Arranged**

0. On the Tuesday. *(given)*
1. What he could cook.
2. Her sister, to the Sunday.
3. Because he was on nights and did not read his phone.

**Part 9: Decision Task**

No option is wrong. The task is the reasoning, and the frame must be used.

Marking points: ☐ a choice stated in the first sentence ☐ a *because* reason ☐ a second point after *Also* ☐ a comparison with one rejected option ☐ 50–70 words.

The three options, and the strongest case for each:
- **(a) ask everybody at once** — nobody is an afterthought and nobody is wondering who else was asked. It also gives the two strangers no private way to say no.
- **(b) ask the ones you know first** — you find out whether it is happening at all before you risk anything on a doorstep. The two left over can usually tell they were second.
- **(c) a note through every door** — the kindest to a person who would hate being asked to their face, and the easiest of the three to ignore completely.

> Sample (55 words): *I think I should put a note through every door, because the two who have never spoken to me can read it alone and decide alone. Also, a note gives a date without putting anybody on the spot. This is better than asking everybody at once, because asking in front of others is a trap.*
