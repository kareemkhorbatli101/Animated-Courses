# 06 · The Task Taxonomy

> Six rubric families carry 420 exercises across the five source books, with the same redundant
> distractor — *"a mistake in the code"* — used **29 times** verbatim in an auto book, a furniture
> book and a trade book (`00-diagnosis.md` §3.3).

English Animated specifies **48 task types** in nine families, each with a declared cognitive level,
a level band, and a rule for use. A writer selects from the taxonomy; a writer may not invent a
seventh rubric and call it a unit.

**Cognitive levels** (revised Bloom): **RE** remember · **UN** understand · **AP** apply ·
**AN** analyse · **EV** evaluate · **CR** create.

---

## Family L · Vocabulary (11 types)

| # | Type | Cog | Band | What the learner does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | **Image harvest** | RE | A1–B1 | Find and name 10–20 items in an Establishing Scene; write them into a labelled frame |
| L2 | **Definition match** | UN | A1–A2 | The classic match. **Capped at once per unit, A-level only, and the redundant option must be plausible and unit-specific.** |
| L3 | **Cline ordering** | AN | A2–C2 | Place 6–8 items on a scale: *furious → irritated*, *certain → possible*, *suggest → demand* |
| L4 | **Odd one out, with reason** | AN | A2–C2 | Four items, one doesn't fit — **and the learner states the criterion.** Often more than one defensible answer |
| L5 | **Collocation grid** | AP | A2–C2 | Tick which verbs go with which nouns; then find the one native speakers never say |
| L6 | **Chunk completion** | AP | A2–C2 | Restore the missing word in a fixed expression, from memory, then from a corpus line |
| L7 | **Semantic map** | AN | B1–C2 | Build a labelled network: superordinate, co-hyponym, opposite, collocate, register sibling |
| L8 | **Register sort** | EV | B1–C2 | Sort 12 ways of saying one thing across formality × distance × channel |
| L9 | **Word-building derivation** | AP | A2–C2 | Generate the full family; mark the stress shift; identify the one that doesn't exist |
| L10 | **Connotation audit** | EV | B2–C2 | Two near-synonyms in real sentences: what does each commit the writer to? (*slim / skinny*, *firm / stubborn*) |
| L11 | **Corpus Note task** | AN | B1–C2 | Read real frequency or collocation data, predict, then check and explain the surprise |

**Rules.** Items are never taught as bare lists. Every set is met *in use* before it is sorted.
L2 is capped. At least three L-types per unit, at least one at AN or above.

## Family G · Grammar (9 types)

| # | Type | Cog | Band | What the learner does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | **Guided discovery** | AN | all | Return to the Part 2 text; answer 4–6 questions whose answers *are* the rule. The rule is never given first |
| G2 | **Meaning match** | UN | all | Two correct forms, two situations: which goes where, and what changes? |
| G3 | **Controlled production** | AP | all | Gapped or transformational, always inside a continuous text, never on disconnected sentences |
| G4 | **Form-to-meaning diagram** | UN | all | Map utterances onto a timeline, a probability line, or a scope bracket (`07` V9) |
| G5 | **Error autopsy** | AN | A2–C2 | Not "correct the mistake" but: *what did the writer think the rule was?* The misconception is named |
| G6 | **Minimal-difference pair** | AN | B1–C2 | Two sentences differing in one feature; describe the difference in meaning, not in form |
| G7 | **Grammar in the wild** | AN | B1–C2 | A real text; find every instance of the target and explain each writer's choice |
| G8 | **Rewrite for effect** | CR | B1–C2 | Rewrite a paragraph so the emphasis, formality or stance changes. Grammar as a dial |
| G9 | **Contrast Clinic** | AP | all | 6 items pitting the unit's two grammar targets against each other. Compulsory, Part 6 |

**Rules.** Guided discovery (G1) precedes every rule statement, in every unit, at every level. No
grammar exercise is longer than 8 items. No grammar is delivered by a character in a dialogue.

## Family R · Reading (8 types)

| # | Type | Cog | Band | What the learner does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Prediction from paratext** | UN | all | Headline, image, byline, first line only — predict content, then check |
| R2 | **Skim for shape** | UN | all | 60 seconds: what kind of text, what is it doing, where does it turn? |
| R3 | **Scan race** | AP | all | Timed location of specific information; competitive, measured |
| R4 | **Ordering & reconstruction** | AN | A2–C2 | Restore paragraph order or a removed sentence — **and justify from cohesion, not content** |
| R5 | **Inference ladder** | AN | B1–C2 | Three questions: stated · implied · assumed-but-never-said |
| R6 | **Vocabulary in context** | AP | all | Infer meaning from co-text before any gloss, then rate confidence |
| R7 | **Writer analysis** | EV | B1–C2 | Who wrote this, for whom, to do what, and what did they leave out? |
| R8 | **Text-vs-text** | EV | B1–C2 | Set 5A against 5B: where do they conflict, and which do you believe? |

**Rules.** No text without R1. From B1, R7 or R8 is compulsory in every Reading part. Comprehension
questions never outnumber analytical ones at B1+.

## Family S · Listening (7 types)

| # | Type | Cog | Band | What the learner does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **Gist under pressure** | UN | all | One question, one listen, no transcript, no second chance |
| S2 | **Detail capture** | AP | all | Structured note-taking into a frame — never a tick-box |
| S3 | **Decoding clinic** | AN | all | 30–60 s of fast speech replayed four times: count the words · catch the numbers · where did two words join · what was swallowed |
| S4 | **Attitude read** | AN | A2–C2 | How does the speaker feel, and *which sound* told you? Learners must cite the evidence |
| S5 | **Inference from silence** | AN | B1–C2 | What was deliberately not said, and why? |
| S6 | **Live note-and-relay** | CR | B1–C2 | Take notes, then tell a partner who did not hear it. Feeds Part 10 |
| S7 | **Accent comparison** | AN | B2–C2 | The same content from three speakers; what changed, what was harder, what strategy worked |

**Rules.** **Transcripts live in the back of the book, never on the page.** S3 appears in every
unit. From A2, at least one listening task requires note-taking rather than selection.

## Family P · Speaking (6 types)

| # | Type | Cog | Band | What the learner does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Fluency sprint (4/3/2)** | AP | all | Tell it in 4 minutes, then 3, then 2. Same content, rising fluency. Measured |
| P2 | **Information gap** | AP | all | Each learner holds half; the task cannot be done alone |
| P3 | **Opinion gap** | EV | A2–C2 | No correct answer; the group must reach a position and defend it |
| P4 | **Role play with hidden brief** | CR | A2–C2 | Each card carries a goal the other does not know. Produces real negotiation |
| P5 | **Structured presentation** | CR | A2–C2 | Prepared turn with a declared structure, timed, with a question afterwards that was not anticipated |
| P6 | **Chaired discussion** | CR | B1–C2 | One learner chairs: manages turns, summarises, closes. Rotates |

**Rules.** Every Speaking part has an observer role with a named behaviour to watch for. Every
Speaking part ends with self-assessment on **two** criteria — never a full rubric.

## Family W · Writing (6 types)

| # | Type | Cog | Band | What the learner does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| W1 | **Model deconstruction** | AN | all | Annotate a model for the writer's *choices*, not for its rules |
| W2 | **Anti-model repair** | EV | A2–C2 | A grammatically clean text that fails communicatively. Diagnose and fix. One per book minimum |
| W3 | **Guided draft** | AP | all | Plan frame → draft, with the frame withdrawn progressively across the book |
| W4 | **Genre transformation** | CR | B1–C2 | The same content as an email, a post, a report, a complaint. What must change, and why |
| W5 | **Peer review on two criteria** | EV | all | Named, specific, limited. Reviewer and writer both sign |
| W6 | **Publish** | CR | all | The text reaches a real reader: a partner, the platform wall, the portfolio, or next unit's Part 10 |

**Rules.** Every writing task has a reader who is not the teacher. Every task is drafted at least
twice from A2.1. No writing task is assessed on grammar alone.

## Family M · Mediation & Interaction (6 types) — new to this series

| # | Type | Cog | Band | What the learner does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Relay** | AP | A1–C2 | Pass on information accurately to someone who did not receive it |
| M2 | **Simplify for an audience** | CR | A2–C2 | Take a dense text and make it usable by a named, specific reader |
| M3 | **Summarise and compress** | AN | A2–C2 | 300 words → 60, losing nothing that matters. Then defend every cut |
| M4 | **Bridge a misunderstanding** | EV | B1–C2 | Two parties have misread each other. Reconstruct both, then repair |

| M6 | **Cross-language mediation** | CR | A2–C2 | Relay between English and the learner's own language — the plurilingual competence the CEFR CV names and no coursebook teaches |

## Family T · Thinking & Decision (5 types)

| # | Type | Cog | Band | What the learner does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | **The Decision** | EV | all | Competing goods, three evidence formats, an answer frame, a committed choice |
| T2 | **The Reveal** | EV | all | What actually happened. Compare with your decision. Was your *reasoning* sound even if the outcome wasn't? |

## Family V · Visual (6 types)

Every one of these exists because **no figure may appear without a task** (`02` §7, rule 6).

| # | Type | Cog | Band | What the learner does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | **Label and locate** | RE | A1–B1 | Complete a cutaway or a floor plan from a word bank |
| V2 | **Read the document** | AP | all | Extract specified information from annotated realia: an invoice, a lease, a lab report, a thread |
| V3 | **Narrate the sequence** | CR | all | Turn a Process Strip into connected prose, with the linkers supplied and then withdrawn |
| V4 | **Spot and describe the difference** | AN | all | A Comparison Pair with 6–10 planted differences; describe, don't just find |
| V5 | **Interrogate the data** | EV | B1–C2 | What does this chart claim, what does it actually show, and what has been chosen for you? |
| V6 | **Caption it** | CR | A2–C2 | Write the caption, the alt text, and the headline. Three registers, one image |

## The seven rules of use

1. **No task type appears twice in the same unit in the same form.**
2. **Every unit uses at least six families.**
3. **Every unit meets its evaluate/create minimum** (2 at A, 3 at B1, 4 at B2, 5 at C1, 6 at C2 —
   `03-grading-spine.md` §7).
4. **Every figure is cited by a task** (Family V, or by name inside another family's rubric).
5. **Rubrics are rewritten for every instance.** A shared instruction is a shared instruction, not
   a shared sentence. Rubric reuse is flagged automatically at QA.
6. **Redundant distractors are unit-specific, plausible, and never reused across units or books.**
   The corpus's 29 repetitions of one distractor are the exact failure this rule exists to prevent.
7. **Example items (`0. is done`) are retained from the corpus** — they genuinely help — but the
   worked example must model the *thinking*, not merely show the answer format.

## Difficulty controls

Two tasks of the same type at different levels differ by *parameters*, not by vocabulary alone.
The writer's dials:

| Dial | Easier ← → Harder |
|---|---|
| **Support** | Word bank → first letter → nothing → distractors present |
| **Input length** | Per `03-grading-spine.md` |
| **Processing depth** | Locate → match → infer → evaluate → produce |
| **Simultaneity** | One operation → two → listen-and-write → listen-and-respond-live |
| **Preparation** | Scripted → notes → 30 seconds → none |
| **Answer openness** | One right answer → several defensible → the justification *is* the answer |
| **Interlocutor** | Cooperative → neutral → briefed to disagree → briefed to interrupt |
| **Stakes** | Private → partner → group → recorded → published |

A single task type therefore spans A1 to C2 without redesign. This is how 48 types cover 140 units
without the learner ever meeting the same exercise twice.
