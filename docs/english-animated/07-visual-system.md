# 07 · The Visual System

> The brief: *"sophisticated illustrations with even more realistic details and more pedagogically
> useful visuals."*

---

## 1 · What is wrong with the 635 figures in the corpus

Measured, not asserted:

| Observation | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Three genres account for nearly all figures | avatar bust + role label · avatar bust + speech bubble · icon strip in rounded cards |
| Letterbox strips with ~12% content coverage | AlHasan figures at 1180 × 280–360 px |
| Large canvases with 40% dead space | EAT figures at 3000 × 1680 px with empty lower third |
| Speech text clipped mid-sentence | `"— And she? — That we guessed."` cut by the bubble edge |
| Labels colliding with rules | `Tarek / apprentice` overlapping the footer line |
| **No figure anywhere is referenced by a task** | 0 of 635 |
| Content duplicated from the text below | speech bubbles repeat printed dialogue verbatim |
| No cutaway, no process diagram, no annotated document, no chart, no map, no floor plan | 0 of 635 |

The underlying failure is one sentence: **the pictures show who is in the room; they never show how
anything works.** Nothing in them is harder to extract from the image than from the paragraph
beside it, so a learner has no reason to look.

---

## 2 · The four laws

**Law 1 — No figure without a task.**
Every illustration is the object of at least one exercise (`06-task-taxonomy.md`, Family V), and the
exercise must be *impossible to do from the text alone*. A figure that can be ignored will be.

**Law 2 — The figure carries information the text does not.**
If the caption and the paragraph beside it already say it, the figure is deleted. Redundancy is the
default failure mode of coursebook art and it is banned at brief stage.

**Law 3 — Realistic specificity.**
Objects are drawn from reference, in correct proportion, with the details a practitioner would
notice: the right number of valves, the right paperwork on the clipboard, the right wear on the
tool. Learners trust images that are *right*, and a correct image teaches lexis the generic icon
cannot. A generic flat glyph of "delivery" teaches nothing; a drawn pallet jack under a half-loaded
pallet with a strapped corner teaches *pallet · strap · load · jack · corner protector*.

**Law 4 — Authored once, published twice.**
Every figure is built in named layers with a declared reveal order (§6). The still is frame 1;
the animation is the sequence. The engine already in this repository renders the second.

## 3 · The twelve figure types

Each type carries a declared pedagogical job, a typical task, and a level band.

---

### V1 · The Establishing Scene
**Job:** vocabulary harvesting, prediction, orientation, a world worth re-entering.
**Spec:** a wide, deep, realistically detailed environment with **20–30 findable, nameable,
non-obvious elements**. Human figures drawn at consistent scale, in real postures doing real work.
Depth: foreground detail, mid-ground action, background context. Something happening in at least
three places. One detail that only rewards a second look.
**Appears:** Part 0 of every unit, full bleed across the opening spread.
**Tasks:** V1 image harvest · L1 · R1 prediction · P2 information gap (two learners get different
crops).
**Against the corpus:** this replaces the "six avatars and their job titles" opener. Same function,
twenty times the content.

### V2 · The Cutaway
**Job:** make internal structure visible; teach technical lexis with its referent; motivate the
passive, relative clauses and sequence.
**Spec:** a machine, a building, a body system, a plant, a vehicle or an organisation, drawn in
section. Leader lines with **≤8 words per callout**. A cut edge that reads as a cut edge. Materials
distinguished by hatch or tone, not colour alone.
**Tasks:** V1 label-and-locate · V3 narrate the sequence · G7 grammar in the wild.

### V3 · The Process Strip
**Job:** sequence, causality, narrative tense, discourse marking.
**Spec:** 4–8 numbered panels on an explicit time axis. The *same* viewpoint across panels so
change is legible. One thing changes per panel. State shown, not described.
**Tasks:** V3 narrate · R4 ordering · G3 controlled production in continuous text.

### V4 · The Comparison Pair
**Job:** comparatives, change over time, evaluation, noticing.
**Spec:** two states, identical framing, **6–10 deliberately planted differences** at three levels
of obviousness. Before/after, then/now, option A/option B, draft/redraft.
**Tasks:** V4 spot-and-describe · L3 cline · T1 decision evidence.

### V5 · Annotated Realia
**Job:** genre literacy — the single largest gap in the corpus.
**Spec:** a **photorealistic reproduction of a real document**, fully legible, with callouts.
Across the series: boarding pass · tenancy agreement · payslip · invoice · lab report · nutrition
label · prescription · job ad · CV · chat thread · dashboard · terms of service · utility bill ·
delivery note · minutes · grant application · court listing · consent form · peer review ·
style guide.
**Spec detail:** real layout, real typographic hierarchy, plausible reference numbers, the small
print *actually present and readable*. Learners will read the small print; that is the point.
**Tasks:** V2 read-the-document · R3 scan race · M2 simplify for an audience.

### V6 · The Data Visual
**Job:** describing trend and quantity; critical numeracy.
**Spec:** honest, well-made charts with a story in them. **From B2, at least one deliberately
misleading chart per book** — truncated axis, cherry-picked window, ratio shown as area — presented
without warning and critiqued in the task.
**Tasks:** V5 interrogate the data · T4 criteria design · W4 genre transformation (chart → prose).

### V7 · The Dialogue Stage
**Job:** pragmatics, register, implicature, the gap between what is said and what is meant.
**Spec:** characters in a real, specific setting, drawn at full figure so **body language is
legible**: posture, orientation, distance, what the hands are doing, who is looking away. Speech
balloons *and* thought balloons — the pragmatic gap is the content. Never more than 12 words per
balloon, never text that repeats the page.
**Tasks:** S4 attitude read · L8 register sort · M4 bridge a misunderstanding.
**Against the corpus:** this replaces the floating bust with a speech bubble quoting the paragraph
below it.

### V8 · The Map or Network
**Job:** place, relationship, system; prepositions, relative clauses, systems lexis.
**Spec:** geographic maps, floor plans, org charts, supply chains, concept networks. Scale bar and
orientation where geographic. Relationships drawn as typed edges, not undifferentiated lines.
**Tasks:** V1 · P2 information gap · M1 relay.

### V9 · The Grammar Visual
**Job:** make grammatical *meaning* spatial before form is tabulated. The centrepiece of the
Grammar Lab's **See** stage.
**Spec — a defined vocabulary of grammar shapes used consistently across all 14 books:**

| Shape | Encodes |
|---|---|
| Timeline with NOW marker | tense |
| Lens / bracket over a timeline span | aspect — the "zoom" of the continuous |
| Dotted span with a boundary | perfect — a period reaching to now |
| Vertical probability ladder | modality: certainty from *will* down to *might not* |
| Two-track branch | conditionals: real and unreal paths |
| Scope bracket over a sentence | negation, quantifier and adverb scope |
| Weight bar under a sentence | end-weight, fronting, information structure |
| Arrow of attention | theme/rheme, given/new |
| Side-by-side mirror | active/passive as the same event, differently framed |

Consistency matters more than invention: a learner who learns the probability ladder at A2 reads it
instantly at C1.
**Tasks:** G4 form-to-meaning · G6 minimal pair · G1 guided discovery.

### V10 · The Phonetics Visual
**Job:** pronunciation made visible.
**Spec:** mid-sagittal mouth cross-sections for phoneme contrasts, with tongue position and airflow.
Pitch contours drawn over a waveform for intonation. Stress grids with weighted dots for rhythm.
Linking and elision shown as the written form physically joining.
**Tasks:** the Pronunciation Lab's Hear and Say stages.

### V11 · The Error Autopsy
**Job:** noticing, through a visual mis-fit.
**Spec:** a learner-plausible error rendered as something physically wrong — an arrow pointing at
the wrong side of NOW, a bracket covering the wrong span, two gears that do not mesh. Correct and
incorrect shown together, with the **misconception named**: *"the writer thought the perfect was
about the past; it is about now."*
**Tasks:** G5 error autopsy.

### V12 · The Synthesis Infographic
**Job:** consolidation; the source text for mediation.
**Spec:** one page, the whole unit's content as a structured visual — the system, the argument, the
timeline, the chain. Dense enough to be worth summarising.
**Appears:** Part 10, as the mediation source; reprinted small in Part 12.
**Tasks:** M2 simplify · M3 compress · T5 concept spotlight.

## 5 · Production standards

| | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Source format** | Layered vector (SVG / native), never flattened. Raster only for photographic realia textures. |
| **Print resolution** | Vector at any size; raster ≥ 600 dpi at placed size. |
| **Legibility test** | Every figure must be fully readable **printed at 50% of its placed size, in greyscale**. This single test kills most of the corpus's defects. |
| **Text in figures** | On a separate, named text layer. Never baked into artwork. Enables translation, alt text, and the animated reveal. |
| **Callout length** | ≤ 8 words. Longer belongs in the caption. |
| **Caption** | One sentence, and it must say something the figure cannot: the *so what*. |
| **Overflow** | Zero tolerance. Automated bounding-box check at QA. |
| **Colour** | Functional, never decorative-only. Any distinction carried by colour is carried redundantly by shape, position or label — colour-blind safe by construction. |
| **Contrast** | WCAG 2.2 AA minimum for all text and meaningful graphics. |
| **Alt text** | Written by the author, not the designer, at brief stage. Full descriptive alt + a short label. Required for every figure. |
| **Themes** | Light and dark variants exported from the same source. |
| **Representation** | Age, body type, visible disability, assistive technology, head covering, skin tone and gender distributed across the series and *never* correlated with role status. Audited per book. |
| **No stock** | The corpus's generic icon library is retired. Every asset is drawn to brief. |

## 6 · The animation layer

This is where the series name becomes a technical specification. The repository already contains an
XML→video engine with 3D sets, rigged actors, viseme-driven mouths and explicit camera cuts
(`courses/eam-b1/*/scene.json`, `schema: scene/2`).

### 6.1 The eight animation states

Every figure declares which of these it supports, in its brief:

| State | Behaviour | Natural fit |
|---|---|---|
| **REVEAL** | Layers appear in a declared order, timed to narration | V2, V9, V12 |
| **TRAVERSE** | Camera moves along a path through the artwork | V1, V2, V8 |
| **MORPH** | State A dissolves to state B | V4, V11 |
| **STEP** | Panels advance, each held, under learner control | V3 |
| **HOTSPOT** | Learner taps a region; label, gloss and audio appear | V1, V2, V5, V8 |
| **BUILD** | The learner assembles the figure; it is a task, not a display | V9, V1 |
| **SYNC** | A visual tracks live audio — pitch contour draws as speech plays | V10 |
| **INTERROGATE** | Underlying data is switchable: re-scale the axis, change the window, see the claim change | V6 |

### 6.2 Layer-naming contract

```
fig_<book>_<unit>_<part>_<type>/
  00_ground        static background, never animated
  10_context       mid-ground, revealed first
  20_subject       the thing being taught
  30_detail        the second-look reward
  40_callouts      leader lines + labels   (text layer, localisable)
  50_annotation    arrows, highlights, brackets, added by narration
  60_overlay       task furniture: numbered hotspots, blanks, answer frames
  _states.json     reveal order, timing, hotspot regions, morph pairs
```

`_states.json` is the handshake between the book and the engine. A designer delivers one file; the
print pipeline reads layers 00–40, the platform reads everything.

### 6.3 The twenty videos per book

Rendered by the existing engine, in the existing `course/1` catalogue schema:

- **10 Dialogue Stage videos** — the Part 2 listening, staged in 3D with the resident cast, in the
  book's setting, with viseme-synced mouths and explicit camera cuts. Subtitles optional and
  switchable; the audio is the same track that ships with the book.
- **10 Documentary shorts** — 90–120 seconds, built from the unit's V2/V3/V6/V12 figures animated
  under narration. The unit's content, delivered visually, with the target language in use.

New 3D sets required for English Animated, beyond the repository's existing `workshop`,
`classroom`, `horseshoe`, `oilfield`, `restaurant` and `furniture_office`: **street · train
carriage · market · newsroom · hospital ward · port/quayside · lecture theatre · open-plan office ·
kitchen · research station · hearing room · studio.** Twelve sets cover all 14 books; each is
authored once, named per the existing object-index convention, and reused across the shelf.

> **Note for the engine backlog.** `RESIDUALS.md` §J.2 records that `furniture_office` has no
> builder and therefore cannot be named, so object-level questions degrade for `efd/unit01`. Every
> new English Animated set must ship with a registered builder **and** a named object manifest from
> day one — the hotspot and BUILD animation states depend on named objects, and a set that cannot be
> named cannot carry a task.

## 7 · Brief template

Every figure is commissioned on this form. Incomplete forms are not sent to the studio.

```
FIGURE BRIEF
ID              fig_b11_u03_p05_v05
Book / Unit     B1.1 · Unit 3 · Part 5 (Reading)
Type            V5 Annotated Realia
Line            A (World)

PEDAGOGICAL JOB   (one sentence; what the learner can do after looking)
TASK IT SERVES    (task id and number — a figure without this is rejected)
INFORMATION THE TEXT DOES NOT CARRY   (if blank, the figure is cut)

CONTENT
  Must show            (enumerated, specific)
  Must be accurate to  (reference source)
  Callouts             (≤8 words each, exact wording)
  Deliberate detail    (the second-look reward)
  Planted difficulty   (what is hard to find, and why that is useful)

CAPTION           (one sentence; must add, not repeat)
ALT TEXT          (full descriptive + short label)

ANIMATION
  States           REVEAL, HOTSPOT
  Reveal order     00 → 10 → 20 → 40 → 30
  Hotspots         7 regions, listed
  Narration        38 s, script attached

CONSTRAINTS
  Placed size      180 × 110 mm
  Legible at       50% greyscale  ✔ required
  Colour-blind safe ✔ required
  Themes           light + dark
```
