## A1.1 · Unit 1 · The Yellow Building

**0B** · Guess it
The man with the bag still on his shoulder and a piece of paper in his hand. Everybody else has put something down. He has not, because he does not yet know where anything goes.

**1C** · Sort — people or places?
> **PLACES** — country · city · school
> **PEOPLE** — woman · class · friend · teacher · student
> **NEITHER** — name · address
**The two that are neither:** *name* and *address*. They are not people and they are not places — they are the words we use to **find** a person or a place. Accept *class* and *school* in two columns if the learner can say why: a class is a group of people and a room, and a school is a building and everybody in it.

**2A** · Monday morning, nine o'clock 🔊 Track 1.1
**1** T · **2** T · **3** F · **4** F
**Listen once** — room 3.

**Table** — First name: Yusuf · Family name: HASSAN · Country: Egypt · Room: 3.

**Notice** — the word is *be*. Four forms: **am** (I) · **is** (she, he, it) · **are** (you, we, they) — and the fourth is the one most people miss: there is no fourth. Three forms do all six persons, which is why the verb feels so strange.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 is · 2 is · 3 are · 4 is · 5 is · 6 am · 7 are · 8 are · 9 are · 10 are

**3E** · Use — guided
1 She is not a teacher. → Is she a teacher?  
2 They are not in the café. → Are they in the café?  
3 I am not late. → Am I late?  
4 You are not Brazilian. → Are you Brazilian?

The negative puts *not* straight after the verb. The question swaps the first two words and moves nothing else.

**5C** · Answer
1 Yellow.  
2 Sixteen.  
3 She is at the desk — reception.  
4 Because she reads the list on Sunday, before anybody arrives.  
5 Two.  
6 Who put the tag on the cat. The text does not answer it, and that is the point: a name tells you somebody decided, and it does not tell you who.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**steep** — the street goes up sharply · **floor** — a level of a building, not the thing you walk on here · **tag** — a small label tied to something · **nobody** — not one person

**5E** · The counter-text
1 Five — Marco at 07.10, Ana at 08.30, Teresa at 08.45, Lin at 08.52 and Chika at 08.55. (Emre signed in **at** nine, not before it.)  
2 Marco, at 07.10, and he works at the café next door.  
3 None of the three. The sixth line has no capitals, no family name first and no room number.  
4 Open answers. The useful ones ask him rather than guess: a name written wrongly on day one is written wrongly on every list for a year.

**6C** · Chunk
1 phone number · 2 How old · 3 room number · 4 how … spell

**6D** · Contrast Clinic
> **1** I **am** twenty.
> **2** They **are** fifteen and seventeen.
> **3** **Are** you **forty**? *(FOR-ty, not four-TEEN)*

**12A** · Recycle — twelve items
1 is · 2 are · 3 am · 4 Are · 5 Brazilian · 6 family · 7 spell · 8 *(whichever the teacher says — the test is the stress, not the number)* · 9 am · 10 This · 11 are · 12 desk

## A1.1 · Unit 2 · The Six O'Clock Class

**0B** · Guess it
Marco. The cafe lights are on, three people are already at his counter and the cafe clock says twenty to seven — so he was here before any of them. The school is still dark.

**1C** · Sort — a thing you do, or a way you feel?
> **BOTH** — sleep
> **YOU DO IT** — drink · wash · work · study · eat
> **YOU FEEL IT** — happy · tired · hungry · thirsty
**The one in both:** *sleep*. You do it — *I sleep eight hours* — and you need it, which is a feeling: *I need sleep*. It is the only word on the list that works as an action and as a state.

**2A** · Twenty to seven 🔊 Track 2.1
**1** T · **2** F · **3** T · **4** F
**Listen once** — six o'clock.

**Table** — Marco: gets up 6.00 · starts work 6.30 · goes to bed 22.30. Ana: gets up 7.00 · starts work 8.30 · goes to bed 23.00. The cleaner: gets up 4.00 · starts work 5.00 · sleeps in the afternoon, 1 to 4.

**4 is false** because half past ten is early, not late — the text says so.

**Noticing** — after *I*, *we*, *they* and *you* the verb is the plain form, with nothing on the end. It does not change at all. (In Unit 3 it changes once, for *he*, *she* and *it*.)

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 gets up · 2 opens · 3 work · 4 don't work · 5 don't work · 6 Do

**3E** · Use — guided
1 They don't open at six. → Do they open at six?  
2 I don't eat breakfast at home. → Do I eat breakfast at home?  
3 We don't study on Tuesday. → Do we study on Tuesday?  
4 You don't work at night. → Do you work at night?  
5 They don't go to bed early. → Do they go to bed early?

In every one the verb itself does not move and does not change. Only *do* arrives.

**3G** · Check — six items, mark your own
1 get up · 2 don't open · 3 Do … work · 4 study · 5 *I get up at six.* — there is no *am* with an action verb · 6 *Do you work on Saturday?* — once *do* is there, the verb loses its ending

**5C** · Answer
1 At half past six.  
2 At work — he makes it there and eats it standing up.  
3 At five.  
4 She sleeps, from one to four.  
5 Because it is the only half hour of her day that nobody else is in. The radio is company she chose.  
6 Because none of them is ever in the street at the same time as the others. Each of them sees an empty street and calls it quiet, and four empty streets at four different hours add up to a busy one.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**standing up** — not sitting down to it · **finishes** — stops work · **another building** — a second one, not the same · **with the radio on** — while it is playing · **he is not sorry about it** — he does not apologise for it

**5E** · The counter-text
1 Half past eight.  
2 Half past nine — an hour later than the weekdays, and yes, it is on the sign.  
3 Sunday.  
4 "The cleaner comes in at 5 by the back door. Do not ring the bell before 8.30 — nobody at the desk." It is for the delivery drivers, and it is written in pen because it was not true when the sign was printed.

**5F** · Two texts
No. The sign says when the school is **open to the public**. Dona Filipa is not the public; she is the person who makes it possible to be open at half past eight. Opening hours and working hours are two different things, and no sign ever shows the second one.

**6C** · Chunk
1 half · 2 seven o'clock *(or any hour)* · 3 every · 4 morning · 5 night · 6 on

**6D** · Contrast Clinic
> **1** **at** night *(not* in *the night)*
> **2** **on** Monday
> **3** **on** Saturday afternoon *(a named day wins over* in the afternoon*)*
> **4** **at** six o'clock
> **5** **in** the morning
> **6** **at** half past eight

**12A** · Recycle — twelve items
1 get up · 2 don't open · 3 Do … work · 4 at · 5 on · 6 in · 7 at · 8 past · 9 breakfast · 10 to · 11 tired · 12 is

## A1.1 · Unit 3 · Eleven Screens

**0B** · Guess it
The one with the map open is working out how to get somewhere; the one whose phone is face down has decided not to be interrupted. Neither of those is "not working". The three looking at the same thing at the same moment are the ones who are somewhere else.

**1C** · Sort — where does it live?
> **NEITHER** — internet · online
> **IT IS ON THE SCREEN** — message · photo · notification · map · app · video
> **YOU CAN TOUCH IT** — camera · screen
**The two that are neither:** *internet* and *online*. The internet is not in the phone and you cannot touch it; *online* is not a thing at all, it is a place you are. Everything in the middle column exists only while the screen is on.

**2A** · Eleven o'clock, at the back 🔊 Track 3.1
**1** F · **2** T · **3** F · **4** T
**Listen once** — about ten.

**Table** — Yusuf: thinks 10 · the phone says 96 · first app: messages. Lin: thinks 90 · the phone says 96 · first app: the map.

**Noticing** — questions 1 and 4 are about *you* and *they*, so they take **do**; questions 2 and 3 are about *your sister* and *it*, one person or thing, so they take **does** — and the verb after *does* has no **s** on it.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 checks · 2 does · 3 Do · 4 doesn't · 5 puts · 6 don't

**3E** · Use — guided
1 Which app does she open first?  
2 How often does he check his phone?  
3 When do they charge the battery?  
4 Where does Teresa put her phone?  
5 Why does Lin use a map every morning?

In every one: question word, then *do* or *does*, then the person, then the plain verb.

**3G** · Check — six items, mark your own
1 checks · 2 Does … turn off · 3 don't use · 4 does … take · 5 *Does she check her phone?* — *does* already has the s · 6 *How often does he check it?* — a question word still needs *does* after it

**5C** · Answer
1 In a drawer, from nine until one.  
2 About forty times a day.  
3 Not for the way — she knows the way. For the time: it tells her whether she is late.  
4 Nothing. She takes a photo of her grandson every week and sends it to nobody; the photos stay on the phone.  
5 Because the number arrives once a week and he never does anything with it. Surprise without a decision after it is just a feeling, and feelings reset.  
6 The person. All four phones can do all four things. What differs is what each person has decided the phone is for — and three of the four have not decided anything.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**she does not miss it** — she does not feel the absence · **the real number** — the measured one, not the remembered one · **anyway** — even though there is no need · **a long battery** — one that lasts · **they stay on the phone** — they go nowhere else

**5E** · The counter-text
1 Forty-six hours and fifty-four minutes.  
2 Saturday, at eight hours twenty-six — and it is the weekend, not a work day.  
3 Ninety-six times.  
4 It is the first pick-up of the day: twelve minutes past six. It is the most interesting number because it is the only one that happens before the day starts — before any decision has been made, before the eyes are properly open.

**5F** · Two texts
Neither is wrong. Yusuf is counting the times he **decided** to look, and that is about ten. The phone is counting every time it was picked up, decided or not. The gap between the two numbers is the whole of this unit.

**6C** · Chunk
1 How · 2 How · 3 How · 4 say · 5 mean · 6 understand

**6D** · Contrast Clinic
> **1** **does** — one person
> **2** **does** — one person
> **3** **does** — one person
> **4** **Do** — you
> **5** **Does** — one person, and *take*, not *takes*
> **6** **Do** — more than one

**12A** · Recycle — twelve items
1 checks · 2 Does … turn off · 3 many · 4 much · 5 Which · 6 Where · 7 Why · 8 log · 9 take · 10 charge · 11 get · 12 are

## A1.1 · Unit 4 · Eleven Signs, One Building

**0B** · Guess it
The handwritten one. A printed sign screwed to the wall was ordered, paid for and fixed; a handwritten one taped up was written by somebody who needed it said today. The newest sign in any building is always the one in pen.

**1C** · Sort — a thing, or a rule about a thing?
> **A PLACE OR A THING** — toilet · sign · exit · stairs · lift
> **A RULE** — quiet · forbidden · allowed
> **BOTH** — open · closed
**The two in both:** *open* and *closed*. A door is open — that is a fact about a thing. The school is closed — that is a rule about people. The same two words do both jobs, which is why a sign saying CLOSED is never only a description.

**2A** · Beside the lift 🔊 Track 4.1
**1** F · **2** T · **3** F · **4** T
**Listen once** — yes. She is staff, and the notice says staff only.

**Table** — use the lift: she **can**, and she did not know it. Use the back exit: she **can**. Leave the trolley in the corridor: **doesn't know** — nobody has ever said.

**3 is false:** the line about students with a teacher was added in blue pen by somebody with no authority to add it.

**Noticing** — 1 and 2 are about a rule (permission and prohibition); 3 is a request for permission; 4 is about a body — four floors with a trolley is not something she is forbidden to do, it is something she cannot do.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 can · 2 can't · 3 Can I · 4 Could you · 5 can't · 6 can

**3E** · Use — guided
1 Can I open the window? → Could you open the window, please?  
2 Can I leave early? → Is it OK if I leave early?  
3 Can I use your charger? → Could I use your charger for a moment?  
4 Can you say that again? → Could you say that again, please?  
5 Can I sit here? → Is it OK if I sit here?

*Can I…?* asks about me. *Could you…?* asks somebody else to act. *Is it OK if…?* asks about the rule rather than about the person.

**3G** · Check — six items, mark your own
1 can't use · 2 Can · 3 can carry · 4 Could · 5 *She can use the lift.* — *can* never takes an s · 6 *Can you help me?* — no *to* after *can*

**5C** · Answer
1 Eleven.  
2 The city.  
3 Out of order.  
4 Four.  
5 Because the people who made seven of them have left, and nobody now in the building chose them or could change them. A rule with no author is a rule nobody can argue with, which is not the same as a good rule.  
6 Open answers. The strongest ones notice that a rule nobody owns is also a rule nobody defends — so it survives by being unopposed, not by being right.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**screwed to the wall** — fixed with screws, meant to stay · **taped** — stuck up with tape, meant to be temporary · **argues with** — questions, disputes · **since March** — from March until now · **too narrow** — not wide enough

**5E** · The counter-text
1 STAFF ONLY.  
2 "and students with a teacher" — it widens the rule, so it does not agree with the printed line; it contradicts it politely.  
3 Eleven September — with no year on it.  
4 Only the first. The printed line is the school's rule. The blue line changed the rule without authority, and the pencil line did not change the rule at all — it named a person to ask, which is the only one of the three that is actually useful.

**5F** · Two texts
The notice is the rule, and it is wrong. A rule that is out of date is still the rule until somebody takes it down, which is exactly why taking it down is the whole of Part 11.

**6C** · Chunk
1 Excuse · 2 I · 3 you · 4 OK · 5 you · 6 lot

**6D** · Contrast Clinic
> **1** **sorry** — the answer is no
> **2** **excuse me** — before you speak
> **3** **excuse me** — before you move
> **4** **sorry** — it has already happened
> **5** **excuse me** — before you speak
> **6** **sorry** — it has already happened

**12A** · Recycle — twelve items
1 can't use · 2 Can · 3 Could · 4 OK · 5 Excuse · 6 sorry · 7 are · 8 moment · 9 order · 10 put · 11 often · 12 open

## A1.1 · Unit 5 · One Dictionary, Eleven People

**0B** · Guess it
Eleven can sit — there are eleven desks. Ten can sit down comfortably, because there are only ten chairs. And one can look a word up, because there is one dictionary. The number depends entirely on what you think the room is for.

**1C** · Sort — one, or more than one?
> **YOU CAN COUNT IT** — page · pen · word · notebook · desk · chair · exercise · answer
> **YOU CANNOT COUNT IT** — homework
> **BOTH** — paper
**The one in both:** *paper*. As a material it cannot be counted — *some paper*. As a sheet or a document it can — *two papers*. Note that *homework* never can, however much of it there is: it is one of the commonest uncountable traps in English.

**2A** · In front of the cupboard 🔊 Track 5.1
**1** F · **2** T · **3** F · **4** T
**Listen once** — two hundred euros.

**Table** — desks: eleven, enough. notebooks: four, not enough. dictionaries: one, not enough.

**1 is false:** eleven desks and ten chairs. **3 is false:** there are twelve on the list and eleven in the room.

**Noticing** — what decides is the word **after** the verb, not the word *there*. One dictionary, so *is*; eleven desks, pencils and notebooks, so *are*.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 there are · 2 a · 3 There is · 4 There aren't · 5 any · 6 an

**3E** · Use — guided
1 There aren't any chairs in the corridor. → Are there any chairs in the corridor?  
2 There isn't a clock on the wall. → Is there a clock on the wall?  
3 There aren't eleven students in this class. → Are there eleven students in this class?  
4 There isn't any paper in the cupboard. → Is there any paper in the cupboard?  
5 There aren't any questions on page nine. → Are there any questions on page nine?

Notice what happens to *some*: it becomes **any** in both the negative and the question.

**3G** · Check — six items, mark your own
1 There are · 2 There is · 3 any · 4 an · 5 *There are eleven desks.* — the verb agrees with the desks · 6 *Are there any pens?* — a question needs the verb first, and *some* becomes *any*

**5C** · Answer
1 One.  
2 Open, face down, on a desk at the back, since Tuesday.  
3 She met the same word in a different sentence and did not know it.  
4 Seven.  
5 Because one meeting never produces a word you can use, in anybody. What she did on Tuesday was normal and complete; what was missing was meetings two to seven.  
6 Anything that makes the same word arrive again, far apart and in different sentences: a card, a reader, the word written where she will see it. Somebody has to arrange it, and the person who meets the word is the only one who can.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**face down** — turned over, screen or page to the table · **there is no way round it** — it cannot be avoided · **left to chance** — nobody arranged it · **a word you have** — one you can use, not just recognise · **over three weeks** — spread across three weeks

**5E** · The counter-text
1 Eleven.  
2 Ten — and no, it does not match. One person stands every lesson, and nobody has written that down anywhere except here.  
3 A second dictionary. Two are listed; one is in the room. (Twenty pencils are also listed and there are none at all.)  
4 NOTEBOOKS: written 7, crossed out, written again as 4. It tells you the room was counted twice, three days apart, and that three notebooks left the room between the two counts — which is a bigger finding than either number.

**5F** · Two texts
The handwritten column is right and the printed one is a wish. The inventory records what the school believes it owns; the text records what is on the shelf. Four rows disagree, and nobody has signed the sheet, so nobody has to explain any of them.

**6C** · Chunk
1 boxes · 2 countries · 3 knives · 4 shelves · 5 children · 6 lot

**6D** · Contrast Clinic
> **1** **is** — one
> **2** **are** — eleven
> **3** **are** — three
> **4** **is** — water cannot be counted
> **5** **is** — paper cannot be counted, however much of it there is
> **6** **are** — two

**12A** · Recycle — twelve items
1 There are · 2 There is · 3 any · 4 boxes · 5 cities · 6 knives · 7 children · 8 women · 9 look … up · 10 do · 11 Can · 12 many

## A1.1 · Unit 6 · Two Streets from the Café

**0B** · Guess it
Because one of them has a small handwritten sign on it and the other has nothing. The tomatoes and the price are identical, so the sign is the only difference — and at seven in the morning a sign is the only thing a stranger has to go on.

**1C** · Sort — can you count it?
> **BOTH** — chicken · fish
> **YOU CANNOT COUNT IT** — water · cheese · milk · rice · bread
> **ONE, TWO, THREE** — apple · tomato · egg
**The two in both:** *chicken* and *fish*. Alive in a field or a river they are countable — *two chickens*, *three fish*. On a plate they are a substance — *some chicken*, *a bit of fish*. English counts the animal and weighs the meat, and the word does not change.

**2A** · Seven o'clock at the stall 🔊 Track 6.1
**1** T · **2** T · **3** F · **4** T
**Listen once** — two euros twenty.

**Table** — tomatoes: two kilos, 2.20 a kilo. eggs: six — but there aren't any left. bread: two loaves, 1.10 each.

**3 is false:** he pays in cash. The market does not take cards.

**Noticing** — *many* goes with things you can count (eggs, photos, bottles); *much* goes with things you can only weigh or measure (rice, time, water). The word after it decides, and nothing else does.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 How many · 2 How much · 3 any · 4 a lot of · 5 a bit of

**3E** · Use — guided
1 How many apples does he want?  
2 How much water does she need?  
3 How much rice do they buy every week?  
4 How many eggs are there in the box?  
5 How much bread does the café use a day?

In 3 and 5 the container makes it countable if you want it to: *how many kilos*, *how many loaves*.

**3G** · Check — six items, mark your own
1 How much · 2 How many · 3 any · 4 a · 5 *How much rice do you need?* — rice cannot be counted · 6 *I bought two loaves of bread.* — the number lands on the container

**5C** · Answer
1 Two euros twenty.  
2 Nine.  
3 The name of the farm and the name of the village.  
4 Nineteen years.  
5 Because they are old now and they do not need to be anywhere at seven. The stall did not lose its customers; the customers changed their hour.  
6 The first one, and no. By the end of the day both stalls sell about the same amount. What the sign bought was not more customers but earlier ones — and the first stall has mistaken the time of day for a victory.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**side by side** — next to each other · **a handwritten sign** — written by hand, not printed · **there are not many of those left** — few remain · **by the end of the day** — when the day finishes · **thinks it is winning** — believes it is doing better

**5E** · The counter-text
1 Five euros thirty-four.  
2 Six litres — the line says 6 x 1 L, so the amount is in the line, not only the price.  
3 Nineteen euros thirty-six.  
4 Ten per cent off the next shop, Tuesdays only, before nine. It wants him to come back on the one morning of the week he goes to the market, at the one hour he would be there.

**5F** · Two texts
Yes — about forty per cent more on the tomatoes. But the receipt does not tell you that the shop is open at seven in the evening, takes a card, and is forty seconds from his door, and the market is none of those things. A price is only half a comparison.

**6C** · Chunk
1 loaf · 2 bottle · 3 kilo · 4 slice · 5 tin · 6 Half

**6D** · Contrast Clinic
> **1** **many** — you can count eggs
> **2** **much** — bread is a substance
> **3** **many** — you can count apples
> **4** **much** — this one is about money, and money is always *much*
> **5** **many** — the container is countable even when its contents are not
> **6** **much** — water is a substance

**12A** · Recycle — twelve items
1 much · 2 many · 3 any · 4 bit · 5 loaf · 6 bottle · 7 kilo · 8 slice · 9 run out · 10 all · 11 are · 12 Can

## A1.1 · Unit 7 · Friday at Six

**0B** · Guess it
The one watching the game. Three of the four are looking at a phone, which means the park is where they are and not what they are doing. The fourth has chosen to be there, and that is the whole difference between watching and sitting.

**1C** · Sort — alone, or with people?
> **ALONE** — a book · drawing · a film · running · swimming
> **WITH PEOPLE** — football · a game · dancing
> **BOTH** — cooking · music
**The two in both:** *cooking* and *music*. Both work completely alone and completely in company, and they are the only two on the list that change their nature when you add people rather than just becoming louder. Accept *a film* in either column with a reason — the point is that most film-watching is now solitary and did not used to be.

**2A** · On the bench 🔊 Track 7.1
**1** F · **2** F · **3** T · **4** F
**Listen once** — most evenings, about five days a week.

**Table** — Emre: loves playing the guitar · likes cycling · hates swimming. Lin: loves cycling · likes watching films · hates running.

**1 is false:** most evenings is not every day. **4 is false:** she hardly ever does — about once a month.

**Noticing** — in sentence 2 the frequency word is **before** the verb. In sentence 1 there is no frequency word at all; what follows *love* is a verb with **-ing** on it, which is a different question entirely.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 playing · 2 usually · 3 hardly ever · 4 never · 5 always

**3E** · Use — guided
1 I sometimes play football at the weekend.  
2 She is always late for the Friday class.  
3 They hardly ever go to a concert.  
4 We often watch a film in English.  
5 He is usually tired on Monday morning.

In 2 and 5 the word goes **after** *is*. Everywhere else it goes **before** the verb.

**3G** · Check — six items, mark your own
1 cooking · 2 hardly ever · 3 always · 4 running · 5 *I never play football.* — *never* is already negative · 6 *I like playing the guitar.* — *like* needs **-ing** after it

**5C** · Answer
1 Every Saturday at four.  
2 Eleven.  
3 That she is not very good, which is true, and that she is too old, which is not.  
4 Forty minutes.  
5 Because the game had already started. Joining something that is running feels like interrupting it, and sitting down costs nothing in the first ten seconds.  
6 Because separating them is the point. One of her reasons is a fact and the other is a story, and only the story is stopping her. If she only ever said the true one, nobody could help.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**nobody organises it** — there is no person in charge · **if there are enough of them** — only when the number is sufficient · **out loud** — so that others can hear · **had already started** — it began before they arrived · **nobody would have minded** — no one would have objected

**5E** · The counter-text
1 Six o'clock.  
2 The film club, with eleven.  
3 The book group — nought, and there is no line drawn under it either.  
4 Somebody who was not organising the club and did not want to sign a line — the four ticks beside it say four other people felt the same. It is in a different pen because it was added after the poster was finished, which is exactly what makes it honest.

**5F** · Two texts
The football game. It has run for nine years with no organiser, no poster and no names, which means nothing has to keep happening for it to continue. The club needs a room, a teacher and a list, and any one of the three can fail.

**6C** · Chunk
1 once · 2 twice · 3 weekend · 4 free · 5 days · 6 hardly

**6D** · Contrast Clinic
> **1** **after** — *She is always late.* (after *is*)
> **2** **before** — *They never go swimming.*
> **3** **before** — *I sometimes play football.*
> **4** **before** — *He hardly ever watches films in English.*
> **5** **after** — *We are usually tired on Friday.* (after *are*)
> **6** **after** — *You are often right.* (after *are*)

**12A** · Recycle — twelve items
1 cooking · 2 hardly ever · 3 always · 4 once · 5 twice · 6 weekend · 7 free · 8 Most · 9 listen · 10 thing · 11 many · 12 are

## A1.1 · Unit 8 · What Dona Filipa Actually Does

**0B** · Guess it
The engineer, who is not wearing one. The four uniforms tell you what those people are for, which makes the work visible and easy to judge. The hardest job on the page has nothing to look at — and that is also why nobody thanks the person doing it.

**1C** · Sort — a person, or a place?
> **A PLACE** — hospital · factory · school
> **BOTH** — kitchen · office
> **A PERSON** — cook · nurse · cleaner · manager · driver
**The two in both:** *office* and *kitchen*. Both are rooms, and both are also the people in them — *ask the office*, *the kitchen is busy tonight*. English does this with almost every workplace, which is why *the school decided* makes sense even though a building cannot decide anything.

## A1.1 · Unit 9 · Five Minutes from the Metro

**0B** · Guess it
Open answers. The useful observation is that nothing in the square tells them: three of the four ways out look the same, and the one piece of information that would decide it — which street goes up — is the one the phone map flattens.

**1C** · Sort — a place, or a direction?
> **A PLACE** — hill · station · square · church
> **A DIRECTION** — left · right · behind · between
> **BOTH** — opposite · corner
**The two in both:** *corner* and *opposite*. You can stand on a corner — a place — and you can go round the corner — a direction. *Opposite* tells you where something is, and *the shop opposite* is itself a place. Accept *left* and *right* in both columns if the learner offers *on your left*.

**2A** · In the square 🔊 Track 9.1
**1** T · **2** F · **3** F · **4** T
**Listen once** — about ten minutes.

**Table** — first: straight on, past the church. second: left, at the traffic lights. third: no turn — keep going, past three streets on the left.

**2 is false:** you turn left there. **3 is false:** the pharmacy is in the square, at the bottom.

**Noticing** — they are instructions. An English instruction uses the plain verb with no subject at all, and *don't* in front of it for the negative. It is not rude: in directions, recipes and signs it is the normal form.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 opposite · 2 between · 3 on · 4 in · 5 at · 6 next to

**3E** · Use — guided
1 Turn left at the traffic lights. → Don't turn left at the traffic lights.  
2 Go straight on. → Don't go straight on.  
3 Take the second street. → Don't take the second street.  
4 Get off at the square. → Don't get off at the square.  
5 Walk up the hill. → Don't walk up the hill.

The subject disappears and the verb does not change. *Don't* is the only thing added.

2 Four.  
3 Twenty-eight, of thirty-seven booked.  
4 Six of the seven.  
5 Because five minutes is the time for four hundred flat metres, and these four hundred go up a one-in-eight hill. The sentence is true and useless, which is the worst kind of sentence to put on a website.  
6 Because it depends on Ana being free. It fixed September and January and nothing else, and the next time she is busy the problem comes back exactly as it was.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**flat ground** — level, not sloping · **a gradient of one in eight** — it rises one metre in every eight · **look the same** — are hard to tell apart · **one at a time** — individually, not together · **nobody has changed it** — it is still as it was

## A1.1 · Unit 10 · Thirty-Nine Euros

**0B** · Guess it
The one whose sign gives only the new price. Without the old price there is nothing to compare the new one with, so twenty looks like a decision somebody else has already made for you. The three honest signs invite arithmetic; the fourth does not.

**1C** · Sort — the thing, or the money?
> **BOTH** — change · receipt
> **THE MONEY** — cost · cash · price
> **THE THING** — colour · shirt · size · shoes · jacket
**The two in both:** *change* and *receipt*. Change is money, and it is also a handful of coins you can drop. A receipt is a piece of paper you can hold, and it is also the only record that the money moved. Both of them are objects whose whole purpose is financial.

**2A** · The second of December 🔊 Track 10.1
**1** T · **2** T · **3** F · **4** F
**Listen once** — thirty-nine euros.

**Table** — the jacket: before the sale, eighty-nine; in the sale, thirty-nine; what she gets, a credit note for thirty-nine.

**3 is false:** she tried it on at home, not in the shop. **4 is false:** a credit note, not cash.

**Noticing** — the same thing that decides *is* and *are*. **Was** goes with I, he, she and it; **were** goes with you, we and they. One jacket was; all of them were.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 was · 2 were · 3 tried · 4 didn't · 5 Did · 6 asked

**3E** · Use — guided
1 I paid by card.  
2 She tried it on.  
3 They were half price.  
4 He asked for a receipt.  
5 It was too expensive.

Sentences 3 and 5 use *was* and *were*; the other three take **-ed**. Note *paid* is irregular — it is the one word here that does not follow the rule, and the next book is full of them.

**3G** · Check — six items, mark your own

**5C** · Answer
1 Eighty-nine euros.  
2 Eleven.  
3 Thirty-nine euros.  
4 Twenty-two euros.  
5 Because the shop paid twenty-two, and eighty-nine, thirty-nine and nineteen are all more than twenty-two. Every price in the story was profitable; the sale was never a rescue.  
6 Because the eleven wanted the jacket. Lin bought it in four minutes without trying it on, which tells the shop nothing about the jacket and everything about the word SALE. The eleven are the only real information in the story.
