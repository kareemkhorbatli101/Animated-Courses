## A2.1 · Unit 1 · Three Years at the Stall

**0B** · Guess it
**Thirty years:** the woman behind the scales. Her table has a worn patch where her hand rests, her apron pocket is shaped by the cash box, and she is not looking at the scales. **Last week:** the young man at the end. His apron is new and still folded at the hem, he is watching the dial, and his crates are stacked where the trolley has to go round them.

**1B** · Sort — a good day or a bad day?
**A good day:** I got better at it · I explained it to somebody · I understood it. **A bad day:** I had to repeat it · I made a mistake · I forgot it.  
**The one that goes in both:** *I made a mistake.* It is a bad day if nothing follows it and a good day if you learned from it — which is the sentence Siti uses about Hafiz.

**2A** · How long have you been doing this? 🔊 Track 1.1
**1** T · **2** F · **3** F · **4** T
**Table** — Siti: started 1995; learned from her aunt; finds nothing hard now. Hafiz: started three years ago; learned from Siti; still finds the weighing slow.

**Noticing** — **Finished:** 1 (*I started in 1995*) and 3 (*I worked at the fish stall for two years* — that job is over). **Up to now:** 2 (*I've been here since 1995*) and 4 (*I've worked here for three years*).

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 started · 2 has been / since · 3 worked · 4 has worked / for · 5 has not learned · 6 is getting

**3E** · Use — guided
1 since Monday · 2 for six months · 3 since 2019 · 4 for a long time · 5 since I was a child · 6 since breakfast · 7 for two hours · 8 since last week

**5C** · Answer
1 Four in the morning.  
2 Since 1995 — thirty years.  
3 Her aunt, who had the table before her. She watched for two years before she was allowed to take the money.  
4 He is quick with the customers and slow with the scales.  
5 Because the scales are easy and people are not. Being good at the hard thing and slow at the easy one is the way round that can be fixed.  
6 Eleven of the forty-one.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**trader** — somebody who sells in a market · **take the money** — handle the cash, which is the part you are trusted with last · **quick with** — good and fast at dealing with · **the right way round** — the better of two ways to be wrong · **almost nobody** — very few, and the sentence is about how unusual thirty years is

**5E** · The counter-text
1 340.  
2 Three continuous years of monthly trading.  
3 The continuous period ends and the count begins again.  
4 The one pinned below: "Table 19 free Tuesdays only. Ask Mei Ling. Not the fish end." It is the only notice with a person's name on it.

**6A** · Sort — point, or length?
> **a POINT (since)** — Monday · breakfast · I was ten · 2019
> **a LENGTH (for)** — ages · three years · a long time · six months

**6B** · Chunk completion
1 for · 2 since · 3 for · 4 for · 5 since

**6C** · The other time words
1 yet · 2 already · 3 ago · 4 still · 5 recently · 6 lately

**6D** · Contrast Clinic — finished or not
> **1** has lived — *in her life*, and the life is not over.
> **2** Have … tried — *ever* with no finished time.
> **3** Did … try — *yesterday* is a finished time.
> **4** have worked — *since* runs up to now.
> **5** worked — *in 2019* is finished.
> **6** lived — the period is closed at both ends.

**7C** · Make it mean something
The second — *I've been here since '95* — sounds like a person: the contraction and the shortened year are what ordinary speech does. You would use the first, fully pronounced, when somebody has to write it down: a form, a recorded line, or a conversation in which you are not sure you are being understood.

**12A** · Recycle — ten items
1 have lived / have been living · 2 worked · 3 for · 4 since · 5 yet · 6 better · 7 makes · 8 up · 9 have … been · 10 Still

## A2.1 · Unit 2 · The Van at Half Past Four

**0B** · Guess it
The rain, the broth on Mei Ling's stove, and the queue at the fish stall. The rain: the puddles are deep and the gutter is already running. The broth: there is a skin on it and the pot is half down. The queue: the crates beside it are empty and stacked, so they have been selling for an hour before the van arrived.

**1B** · Sort — what the plant needs, what the trader needs
> **what the trader needs** — a van · ice · a licence · a price card · scales
> **what the plant needs** — rain · seed · soil · sun
> **both** — water
**The one in both:** *water*. The plant needs it to grow; the trader needs it to keep the herbs and the leaves from wilting on the table. It is the same word doing two jobs, which is why it is the useful one to argue about.

**2A** · The van was late again 🔊 Track 2.1
**1** b) 03:40 · **2** a) when he left · **3** c) forty · **4** a) still cooking
**Table** — Zul: at the farm, loading the last crates; the road closed. Ravi: at his stall, waiting with an empty ice tray; the fish had nowhere cold to go. Mei Ling: at her stove, cooking broth; nothing went wrong for her, which is why she is the calm one.

**Noticing** — **Background:** 2 (*It was raining when I left*) and 4 (*While I was loading…*). **Event:** 1 (*It started raining at three*) and 3 (*I loaded the van*).

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 was raining · 2 was loading · 3 closed · 4 waited · 5 was waiting · 6 phoned · 7 did not answer · 8 was serving

**3E** · Use — guided
1 While I was walking to the market, it started to rain. *(or* It started to rain when I was walking…*)*  
2 While she was cooking, the power went off.  
3 We were talking about him when he walked in.  
4 He was loading the van when the phone rang.  
5 They were closing the stall when Nurul arrived.

**5C** · Answer
1 On Tuesday — they were picked at three in the afternoon.  
2 Because wet leaves hide the ripe fruit and the sun makes the skin split.  
3 Twelve degrees. Below ten, tomatoes stop ripening and never start again.  
4 Two hours, for fifty-eight kilometres.  
5 Because she has been buying from the same farm since 2011 and knew what was coming.  
6 Because the market opens on Sunday with Tuesday's stock — so it is cheapest, and it is the worst day to buy tomatoes.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**the highlands** — the cool hill country where the vegetables are grown · **split** — burst open, so the fruit cannot be sold · **ripen** — become ready to eat, which cold can stop permanently · **unload** — take the crates off the van · **the chain** — every step from the field to the table, each one able to spoil it

**5E** · The counter-text
1 Forty crates of tomatoes loaded; thirty-eight received. *(Beans and herbs match.)*  
2 Loaded Wednesday 00:55; signed for at the bay Wednesday 04:40.  
3 Zul — the initial is Z and it is in blue. "Door was open when I got there at midnight. Not mine." It accuses whoever closed the cold room on Tuesday night of leaving the door open — and by implication explains the nine-degree reading.  
4 **The cold room temperature:** the explainer says 12°C, the note records 9°C at loading. **The journey:** the explainer says the road takes two hours when dry, and the note shows 01:20 to 04:40 — three hours twenty.

**6A** · Sort — before, during, or after?
> **DURING** — at the same time · while · meanwhile · just as
> **AFTER** — afterwards · later · then
> **BEFORE** — earlier

**6B** · Chunk completion
1 at · 2 Meanwhile · 3 just · 4 all · 5 the · 6 At

**6C** · The sudden word
**Sentence 2.** The pallet going over is a single sudden event inside an action that was already running, which is exactly what *suddenly* is for. In 1 and 3 it attaches to a state or a timetable, which cannot be sudden; in 4 it attaches to tiredness, which arrives gradually.

**6D** · Contrast Clinic — long or short
> **1** was driving — at six, in the middle of it.
> **2** were unloading / started — a long action interrupted by a short one.
> **3** arrived — a point, not a stretch.
> **4** were … doing / went — background and event.
> **5** carried on / stopped — two completed actions set against each other.
> **6** rained — the whole morning is the event, and there is nothing to interrupt it.

**7C** · Make it mean something
A trader uses the loud *WAS* when somebody has just blamed them: the buyer says the fish was left out, and *it WAS raining* answers the accusation. The quiet version is for the same fact when nobody is arguing — a reason, not a defence.

**12A** · Recycle — ten items
1 was raining / arrived · 2 While · 3 in · 4 go · 5 straight · 6 for · 7 since · 8 better · 9 are · 10 opens

## A2.1 · Unit 3 · Forty Minutes of Broth

**0B** · Guess it
The post the menu is pinned to, the shutter on the building behind, and the drain cover in the middle of the lane. Each is in the same position in both pictures: the post has the same lean, the shutter the same two bent slats, and the drain the same cracked corner.

**1B** · Sort — a thing, a person, or a feeling?
> **a person** — neighbour · cousin
> **a feeling** — holiday · childhood · memory · game
> **a thing** — song · photo · playground · toy
**The one in two columns:** *song*. It is a thing you can play and the feeling it brings back, and the whole unit is about that doubling. *(Accept* holiday *— a period and a feeling — if the learner argues it.)*

**2A** · The broth takes forty minutes 🔊 Track 3.1
**1** T · **2** T · **3** T · **4** F
**Table** — How long the broth takes: forty minutes then, forty minutes now. Bowls an hour: a hundred and ten on a Tuesday then, forty now. Who waits: everybody then, almost nobody now.

**Noticing** — **A habit that stopped:** 1 (*used to start at two*) and 2 (*she started at two every day for thirty years*). **Now:** 3 (*People don't wait anymore*) and 4 (*I still make it the same way*).

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 used to start · 2 used · 3 used to wait · 4 anymore · 5 still make · 6 do not sell

**3E** · Use — guided
1 I used to play in that street every evening.  
2 I never used to like coriander. *(or* I didn't use to like coriander.*)*  
3 I still go there every Friday.  
4 We used to have a television with four channels.  
5 She didn't use to be shy.

**5C** · Answer
1 Eight. She did it every summer until she was fifteen.  
2 People stood in the lane and talked while they waited. The waiting was part of it.  
3 He stood in the queue for six minutes, looked at his phone twice, and left for the stall at the end.  
4 Because her grandmother never used one, and she has never needed one either.  
5 A hundred and ten bowls on a Tuesday then; forty now.  
6 The cousin says nobody comes back for the forty minutes, they come back for the taste, and the taste is in the bones and not in the time. Her answer is three words: *He has never made it.*

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**the lane** — the narrow street outside the stall, where people waited · **out of a packet** — made from a ready-made mix, in ninety seconds · **the bones** — what the long broth is made from, and what the cousin says the taste is really in · **come back for** — return because of · **he may be right** — a concession, made just before it is taken away

**5E** · The counter-text
1 Nine in 1998 (five printed plus three vegetable dishes and an egg added by hand); four now.  
2 2.20 then; 9.50 now.  
3 The beef broth — *house* then, *house speciality* now. The price has gone from 2.20 to 9.50 and **the time has not changed**: forty minutes on both menus.  
4 "The beef one takes forty minutes. It has always taken forty minutes. Please do not ask."

**6A** · Sort — then, now, or how often?
> **THEN** — in those days · back then
> **HOW OFTEN** — ever · most of the time · never · rarely · hardly ever
> **NOW** — nowadays · these days

**6B** · Chunk completion
1 anymore · 2 Nowadays · 3 as · 4 no · 5 as · 6 most

**6C** · The strong words and the quiet ones
1 **hardly ever** — twice a year is nearly never, and *never* would be false. *Rarely* would be correct and would sound written.  
2 **never** — absolute, and the only one of the three that is. The other two leave the door open.  
3 **rarely** — this sentence is itself about formal writing, which is where *rarely* lives. *Hardly ever* would be too spoken for the claim it is making about itself.

**6D** · Contrast Clinic — stopped or not
> **1** used to
> **2** didn't use *(or* used*)* — *used to be* if she was and is not; *didn't use to be* if she was not and now is.
> **3** still
> **4** anymore
> **5** still
> **6** anymore

**7C** · Make it mean something
The first — stress on **used** — answers *Do you still?* It puts the weight on the change, and the answer is no. The second puts the weight on the activity and answers a different question: not whether, but how you got there.

**12A** · Recycle — ten items
1 used · 2 anymore · 3 still · 4 In · 5 every · 6 While · 7 rained · 8 for · 9 taught · 10 were

## A2.1 · Unit 4 · The Corner Pitch

**0B** · Guess it
The drain. It is the one line in the handbook's responsibility table with a dash in both columns. In the picture there is a damp dark patch spreading from the cover, flies above it, and the two nearest traders have moved their crates a metre clear — which is what happens: by Thursday it smells, and everybody works round it.

**1B** · Sort — a person, a piece of work, or a time?
> **a person** — staff · team · manager · boss · colleague
> **a time** — shift
> **a piece of work** — role · job · task
**The two that move:** *team* is a group of people and a unit of work (*the night team*, *the team that does the deliveries*). *job* is a piece of work and a position — *that is my job* means both, and in this unit it means both at once.

**2A** · Your first morning 🔊 Track 4.1
**1** b) a trader · **2** b) without warning · **3** a) the gate · **4** a) the drain
**Table** — Mr Tan: manager, writes the rota; go to him about pitches and the cold store. Nurul: inspector, food safety; she comes to you, without warning. Zul: drives the van, brings the ice; go to him if the ice is short.

**Noticing** — **The place:** sentence 3 (*The office is the place where you report a broken light*). The other three describe people, including sentence 4, which describes a van by naming its owner.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 who / that · 2 where · 3 who / that · 4 that / which · 5 that / which · 6 which

**3E** · Use — guided
1 That's the trader who opens the gate at four.  
2 This is the room where we keep the scales.  
3 He's the colleague who I trust with the money. *(or* …the colleague I trust…*)*  
4 That's the van that broke down on Tuesday.  
5 She's the person whose name is on the rota every Sunday.

**5C** · Answer
1 Nine years.  
2 Not the rota — the rota takes an hour on a Sunday. The job is the corner.  
3 About forty per cent more than the trader six metres further in.  
4 The trader who has been there longest and has never asked him for anything. The trader who pays the most and is the most reliable person in the market. The woman who started eleven months ago, is on her own with two children, and is six metres further in.  
5 Longest serving. It is good because it cannot be argued with and bad because it rewards nothing except staying.  
6 That it says *the manager decides* — which tells you it was written by somebody who has never had to.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**a pitch** — the space a trader rents and sells from · **six metres further in** — the measured distance that costs forty per cent of the takings · **my predecessor** — the manager before him · **rewards nothing except** — recognises only one thing, and that thing is time · **if I am honest** — a marker that the next clause costs the speaker something

**5E** · The counter-text
1 Section 4.  
2 Clause 4.2: he must consult the traders committee — three traders elected each January — and the committee must meet before any pitch changes hands. The extract in 5B quotes only 4.1, *the manager decides*, which is why he thinks he is alone.  
3 Fourteen days, with a seven-day appeal window.  
4 "Where two traders are equally suitable, the pitch goes to the longest serving." It was crossed out and initialled KT — so the predecessor's rule was deliberately removed, and the current manager does not know it ever existed.

**6A** · Sort — would you want it?
**Open — be honest.** The two that are in both columns for most people are **skilled** and **seasonal**: skilled work is satisfying and exposing, and seasonal work is freedom for some people and insecurity for others. *(Accept* difficult *for the same reason if the learner argues it.)*

**6B** · Chunk completion
1 person · 2 numbers · 3 feet · 4 starts · 5 on · 6 suits

**6C** · Two words that look the same
1 useful · 2 helpful · 3 useful · 4 helpful

**6D** · Contrast Clinic — who, which, where or that
> **1** where
> **2** where
> **3** that / which — **and it can be dropped**
> **4** that / which — subject, so it cannot be dropped
> **5** who / that — subject, so it cannot be dropped
> **6** who / that — subject, so it cannot be dropped

**7C** · Make it mean something
The second adds commas, and the commas make the clause non-defining: there is only one trader, and by the way they pay the most. The first identifies which trader is meant out of several. In this market the first is true and the second would be a different and much more awkward claim.

**12A** · Recycle — ten items
1 who / that · 2 where · 3 that / which · 4 charge · 5 takes · 6 used · 7 anymore · 8 While · 9 works · 10 are
