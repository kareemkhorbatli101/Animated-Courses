## B2.1 · Unit 9 · Three Readers, One Style Guide

**0B** · Guess it
The app preview, held up. The app had three hundred and forty thousand readers last month against a print run of a hundred and twelve thousand. The style guide on the table — two hundred and fourteen pages — has no entry for *app*, *newsletter* or *push alert*, so the channel with the most readers is the one with no written rules at all.

**1B** · Sort — people, a time or a shape, or what carries it?
> **a time or a shape** — turnaround · brief · format · slot · deadline
> **people** — desk · subscriber · conference
> **what carries it** — circulation · channel · house style · style guide
**The piece of furniture that is a group of people:** *desk*. *The desk decided* never means the table decided, and *conference* behaves the same way — both are rooms, objects and committees, and in this building almost always the committee.

*Brief* and *format* are worth arguing over: a brief is a document before it is a shape, and a format is a shape before it is anything. Accept either in a column of its own if the learner can say which sense they mean.

**1D** · Collocation Lab
1 The running order · 2 A late change · 3 a house voice · 4 a final version · 5 filed on time  
**A measure of a person:** *filed on time* — every day for nine years. The other four are properties of the thing being made, not of whoever made it.

**1E** · The phrasal verbs, and the three fixed expressions
***We are going with it*** and ***that can wait*** decide what happens today; ***it will not make the cut*** decides what happens never.  
The third is the one that needs authority, because the first two are reversible by anybody who is still awake at six and the third is not reversible at all. It is also the only one of the three that will be quoted back at the person who said it.

**2A** · The style guide meeting 🔊 Track 9.1
**1** T · **2** F · **3** F · **4** T
**Table** — *the paper*: nine markers per thousand words; the reader is somebody holding a page and able to see how long the article is; the desk says more markers cost precision. *the newsletter*: thirty-one; the reader is on a phone between two other things; the writer says fewer markers cost the reader their place. *the app*: fourteen; the reader is the same as the newsletter's; nobody has decided anything, because the app version is made by cutting the print version.

**Noticing** — Sentences 2 and 4, *that said* and *having said that*, concede: both grant the previous point before continuing. Sentence 5, *by contrast*, announces a comparison. Sentence 6, *in any case*, says the argument no longer matters — it sets the previous point aside rather than answering it.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 However · 2 Accordingly · 3 To begin with · 4 That said · 5 In any case

**3E** · Use — guided
1 *The figures, however, do not support that* — quieter, more confident. *The figures do not support that, however* — an afterthought. · 2 *The newsletter is, nevertheless, read to the end* / *… read to the end, nevertheless.* · 3 *The guide will, accordingly, be rewritten* / *… be rewritten accordingly.* *(at the end,* accordingly *stops being a marker and becomes a manner adverb)* · 4 *The app version, meanwhile, went out unchanged* / *… went out unchanged meanwhile.* *(the end position is barely possible, which tells you something about* meanwhile*)* · 5 *The print version, by contrast, carries nine markers* / *… carries nine markers, by contrast.* Force falls from front to middle to end in every case.

**3H** · Check — eight items
1 However · 2 Accordingly · 3 To begin with · 4 That said · 5 In any case · 6 *The guide was revised in 1998, whereby the newsletter entry was added* — or simply *The guide was revised in 1998.* *Whereby* cannot open a sentence; it joins a clause to a noun phrase before it. · 7 Front: *However, the figures do not support that* — most force, a formal announcement. Middle: *The figures, however, do not support that* — less force, more confidence. End: *The figures do not support that, however* — least force, an afterthought. · 8 Any sentence where the second clause does not hold in spite of the first — e.g. *The guide is long; nevertheless, it is detailed.* Length and detail do not oppose each other, so *nevertheless* promises a contrast that never arrives.

**5C** · Comprehension — eight questions
1 Two hundred and fourteen pages; last fully revised in 1998.  
2 Four lines, saying that discourse markers should be used sparingly.  
3 Nine per thousand words in print, thirty-one in the newsletter, fourteen in the app.  
4 Because the app version is produced by cutting the print version, so its density is whatever survived the cut.  
5 Three quarters of sixty-one thousand — about forty-six thousand.  
6 Because a number that looks like a decision and is not is the most misleading thing on the page. Fourteen reads as a compromise between nine and thirty-one; it is an accident, and the paper has been citing it as evidence of a middle way.  
7 One document with one voice cannot hold two true statements that contradict each other about different readers. The guide is not wrong; it is a single-valued answer to a question that has three values.  
8 That the guide should state one rule and let each channel set its own figure under it — which is option three, proposed by the structure of the sentence rather than by the writer.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**used sparingly** — rarely, with no figure attached, which is why the entry has lasted · **not by policy but because** — the result of a process, not a decision · **reading on a phone between two other things** — interrupted, with no sense of how much is left · **does not come back** — a reader who loses the thread abandons the piece permanently · **has one voice** — the guide describes a single register, for a single reader

**5E** · The counter-text
1 Print: none. Newsletter: five — *so*, *here is the thing*, *however*, *and the guide?*, *which brings me to*. App: one — *however*.  
2 *Which brings me to the awkward bit*, which promises a next item and is followed by nothing. The paragraph ends there.  
3 It kept whichever markers happened to survive the cut, which is why *however* is doing work in the app version that it was not doing in print: it now joins two facts that were separate sentences.  
4 Open. Most readers choose the newsletter version on a phone and the print version on paper — which is the finding, because they are not the same version and the guide says they should be.

**6A** · Sort — contrast, result, time, or formal only?
> **contrast** — however · nevertheless
> **time** — meanwhile · thereafter
> **result** — thereby · accordingly · hence
> **formal only** — whereby
*Thereby* is the one to argue about: it marks a result and it is also formal-only, so accept it in either column with the argument. *Hence* is formal but not formal-only — it survives in speech, where *whereby* does not.

**6B** · Chunk completion
1 In the first place · 2 That said · 3 As it happens · 4 For one thing · 5 On the other hand · 6 By contrast · 7 In any case · 8 To begin with

**6C** · Three that are not interchangeable
*however* simply contrasts: the next clause pushes back. *nevertheless* contrasts in spite of something: it concedes the first clause and holds the second anyway. *that said* grants the other person a point first, which is why it is the one that keeps a negotiation open.

**6D** · The phrasal verbs and the fixed expressions
***The point being.*** It is left unfinished on purpose: the speaker states the relationship and lets the listener supply the clause, which keeps the floor without committing to a form of words. Finish it and you have made a claim somebody can answer; leave it and you have made one they have to complete for you.

**6E** · Contrast Clinic
> **1** You may dispute the count. **In any case**, the survey is separate.
> **2** The guide was revised in 1998; **accordingly**, it says nothing about newsletters. *(or* hence*)*
> **3** The rule was agreed, **thereby** requiring every channel to state its own density.
> **4** The print desk objected; **meanwhile**, the newsletter went out unchanged.
> **5** The guide has one voice, **whereas** the paper has three readers. *(formal written contrast; accept* by contrast *at the front of the second clause)*
> **6** The newsletter is informal. **That said**, it is the most read thing the paper makes.
> **7** **To begin with**, the markers were counted; **thereafter**, the readers were surveyed.
> **8** The app has more readers; **however**, it has no style guide.
