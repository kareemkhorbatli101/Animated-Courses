## A1.2 · Unit 1 · Nine Days In

**0B** · Guess it
Because the shift has not ended. The train is four hours late, so everything he should have done between seven and eleven is still waiting to be done, and the only quiet place to do it is a dark dining car at two in the morning.

**1C** · Sort — the job, or how it felt?
> **HOW IT FELT** — awful · calm · lucky
> **PART OF THE JOB** — crew · ticket · report · shift · mistake
> **BOTH** — busy · delay
**The two in both:** *busy* and *delay*. A delay is a measured number of minutes and also a feeling in a corridor; *busy* is a description of a shift and of a person. Accept *mistake* in two columns if the learner argues that it is both an event and a judgement.

**2A** · Two in the morning 🔊 Track 1.1
**1** F · **2** F · **3** T · **4** T
**Listen once** — eleven at night, four hours late.

**Order** — 1 the freight train broke down · 2 the dining car closed at nine · 3 Dev woke Ruth and they made the sandwiches · 4 Nadia made the announcement at half past eleven.

**2 is false:** Nadia made it, not Dev.

**Noticing** — nothing was added to the end. The change is inside the word: *leave → left*, *go → went*, *come → came*, *say → said*. These are irregular verbs and the past is made by changing the word, not by adding to it.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 left · 2 went · 3 was · 4 came · 5 said · 6 began · 7 became

**3E** · Use — guided
1 The train left at seven.  
2 I went to the kitchen.  
3 She came back with Ruth.  
4 We began the shift at six.  
5 Nobody said anything.

The form is the same after *I*, *she*, *we* and *nobody*. The past simple has no person endings at all.

**3G** · Check — six items, mark your own
1 left · 2 went · 3 came · 4 said · 5 *We left at eleven.* — *leave* is irregular · 6 *He went to the kitchen.* — *go* is irregular, and *goed* is not a word

**5C** · Answer
1 Four hours.  
2 A freight train broke down eighty kilometres ahead.  
3 Nine days.  
4 Two hundred, in ninety minutes.  
5 Because people who are told nothing assume the worst, and because the delay was already four hours old. Saying *I do not know much* is information; saying nothing is not.  
6 It is true, and it is missing everything that happened. NO INCIDENTS means nothing went wrong, and the two hundred sandwiches, the announcement and the three letters are all things that went right — for which the form has no box.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**broke down** — stopped working · **stationary** — not moving · **she was not pleased** — she was annoyed · **between them** — the two of them together · **said it anyway** — said it despite having little to say

**5E** · The counter-text
1 It left at 23.04 and it was scheduled for 19.00.  
2 F3, meaning a freight movement. It records the cause and not the fault — the company has twenty-two codes and none of them names anybody.  
3 NONE.  
4 Nowhere. There is no field on the form for anything a passenger wrote, and the three letters went to a different department entirely.

**5F** · Two texts
The log. It is in a database and it will be there in ten years; the sandwiches exist only in the memory of about a hundred and ninety-two people, and in nothing the company owns.

**6C** · Chunk
1 After · 2 first · 3 end · 4 While · 5 next · 6 What happened

**6D** · Contrast Clinic
> **1** **While** — two long things at the same time
> **2** **While** — two long things at the same time
> **3** **While** — two long things at the same time
> **4** **When** — one short event
> **5** **When** — one short event
> **6** **When** — one short event

**12A** · Recycle — twelve items
1 left · 2 went · 3 came · 4 said · 5 began · 6 After · 7 At · 8 end · 9 While · 10 next · 11 sort out · 12 What happened

## A1.2 · Unit 2 · Forty Seconds at Mirror Lake

**0B** · Guess it
The one with the smallest bag. A return journey is packed for a known number of nights; a single is packed for everything you own that you can carry. The heavy bags are the ones that are not coming back.

**1C** · Sort — the place, or the thing you carry?
> **PART OF THE STATION OR TRAIN** — aisle · shelter · platform · window · seat
> **SOMETHING YOU CARRY** — suitcase · luggage · backpack
> **BOTH** — timetable · ticket
**The two in both:** *timetable* and *ticket*. Both exist on the station wall and in your pocket. They are the only two objects in this unit that have to be in two places at once for the journey to work.

**2A** · The ticket window 🔊 Track 2.1
**1** F · **2** T · **3** F · **4** T
**Listen once** — about seven months. She last travelled in March.

**Table** — last journey: to Winnipeg, a return, seven months ago. This journey: to Vancouver, a single, today.

**1 is false:** a single. **3 is false:** she travelled in March, not last summer.

**Noticing** — the past has moved onto *did* and *didn't*, so the verb goes back to its plain form. One past marker to a sentence, and when *did* is present it takes it.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 bought · 2 didn't · 3 Did · 4 didn't · 5 came · 6 did

**3E** · Use — guided
1 She didn't book a return ticket. → Did she book a return ticket?  
2 They didn't set off at six. → Did they set off at six?  
3 He didn't miss the train. → Did he miss the train?  
4 We didn't change at Winnipeg. → Did we change at Winnipeg?  
5 The guard didn't wait. → Did the guard wait?

In every one the main verb loses its past form completely, including the irregular ones.

**3G** · Check — six items, mark your own
1 didn't · 2 Did · 3 did · 4 didn't · 5 *Did you buy it?* — *did* already carries the past · 6 *I didn't go.* — same rule, and *go* goes back to its plain form

**5C** · Answer
1 One thousand one hundred.  
2 In 2009.  
3 Thirty-one years.  
4 Forty seconds.  
5 Because nothing was announced and nothing was closed on any particular day. People left one at a time, each for a reason that was not about the town, and the sum of those reasons looks like a decision only afterwards.  
6 Fifteen more people got on than got off, which means the town is still exporting people — and it is the only measurement of the town that anybody actually takes.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**a grain elevator** — the tall building where grain is stored · **for good** — permanently · **in a place like this** — in a town of this kind · **put down** — let passengers off · **the same thing** — indistinguishable

**5E** · The counter-text
1 Two — Monday and Thursday.  
2 Four. Two of the four westbound times are crossed out in pen, so the service has halved.  
3 The fourth of November, with no year.  
4 It was decided before the timetable was printed. A printed line is part of the design; a crossing-out in pen is a change made afterwards. So the winter request rule is older than the cuts.

**5F** · Two texts
Several were. The four crossings-out are four decisions and the printed winter line is a fifth; what nobody ever made was a decision about the town, which is a different thing and would have had a name on it.

**6C** · Chunk
1 ago · 2 last · 3 last · 4 before · 5 ago · 6 ago

**6D** · Contrast Clinic
> **1** **ago** — counting back from now, after the number
> **2** **last** — naming a season that has finished
> **3** **last** — naming a time
> **4** **ago** — counting back, and refusing to say how far
> **5** **ago** — counting back
> **6** **last** — naming a week

**12A** · Recycle — twelve items
1 didn't · 2 Did · 3 did · 4 ago · 5 last · 6 last · 7 before · 8 ago · 9 change trains · 10 set off · 11 left · 12 After

## A1.2 · Unit 3 · One Thousand Four Hundred Seats

**0B** · Guess it
The row that is not wrapped. Wear is the giveaway: the old row has arms rubbed pale where hands go and the new row is identical seat to seat. A thing that has been used stops being identical to the thing next to it.

**1C** · Sort — the thing, or what you think of it?
> **WHAT IT IS MADE OF** — metal · plastic · cloth · wood
> **WHAT YOU THINK OF IT** — weak · quality · new · strong
> **BOTH** — cheap · price
**The two in both:** *cheap* and *price*. A price is a fact and *cheap* is a judgement about the same number — and a cheap material is also, in ordinary speech, a kind of material. The word does both jobs and the listener has to decide which you meant.

**2A** · In the shed 🔊 Track 3.1
**1** T · **2** F · **3** F · **4** T
**Listen once** — nineteen dollars.

**Table** — cloth: $41, about seven years, repairable three or four times. Synthetic: $19, about three years, not repairable.

**2 is false:** cloth is the dearer of the two. **3 is false:** when it tears it is finished.

**Noticing** — the length of the adjective. One syllable takes **-er**; three or more take **more** in front. Nothing about the meaning decides it.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 cheaper · 2 stronger · 3 as · 4 as · 5 more · 6 than

**3E** · Use — guided
1 Cloth is more expensive than synthetic, but it lasts more than twice as long.  
2 Metal is heavier than plastic and it lasts nearly four times as long.  
3 The old seat has been repaired four times and the new one is already torn.  
4 The train is much slower than the plane — four nights against five hours.  
5 A return is dearer than a single, and it is not twice the price.

Accept any sentence with one comparative and *than*, as long as the two things are named.

**3G** · Check — six items, mark your own
1 cheaper · 2 stronger · 3 as … as · 4 more · 5 *It is cheaper than cloth.* — not both *more* and *-er* · 6 *It is cheaper than cloth.* — the word is *than*, never *that*

**5C** · Answer
1 Forty-one dollars.  
2 About three years.  
3 Fourteen hundred.  
4 Eighty-six thousand for cloth and eighty-nine for synthetic.  
5 Because three thousand dollars on a decision worth around ninety thousand is about three per cent, which is inside the error of every assumption in the calculation.  
6 It means the decision will be made by whoever is accountable for this year, not by whoever will pay in year four — so the money is not deciding it, the calendar is.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**it can be repaired** — it is possible to mend it · **once and a half** — one and a half times · **leaves in one year** — is all spent in a single year · **in four pieces** — in four separate payments · **signs for it** — authorises the spending

**5E** · The counter-text
1 Forty-one dollars for cloth, nineteen for synthetic.  
2 Six of the eight favour synthetic: price, weight, cleaning time, stain resistance, colours and delivery.  
3 SERVICE LIFE and REPAIRABLE.  
4 Three years, and no. They are missing because they are the only two rows on which synthetic loses, and because a blank is not a lie.

**5F** · Two texts
Yes. Thirty thousand is the saving on this order and three thousand is the extra cost over ten years, and the sheet simply stops counting at the moment synthetic is ahead.

**6C** · Chunk
1 bit · 2 much · 3 as · 4 as · 5 than · 6 difference

**6D** · Contrast Clinic
> **1** **heavier** — two syllables ending in y, so -ier
> **2** **more useful** — two syllables, no y
> **3** **more difficult** — three syllables
> **4** **more expensive** — three syllables
> **5** **cheaper** — one syllable
> **6** **stronger** — one syllable

**12A** · Recycle — twelve items
1 cheaper · 2 stronger · 3 as · 4 more · 5 bit · 6 much · 7 as · 8 last longer · 9 break down · 10 difference · 11 Did · 12 went

## A1.2 · Unit 4 · Two Adults and Three Children

**0B** · Guess it
Open answers. The strongest ones work from behaviour rather than faces: who has put a coat over whom, who is holding whose bag, who has not spoken since Winnipeg. Two people who look alike may be strangers; two people who share luggage are not.

**1C** · Sort — the person, or the connection?
> **A PERSON** — cousin · mother · husband · brother · aunt · daughter
> **A SITUATION** — alone · married · together
> **BOTH** — family
**Family** is both: a set of people and a situation you are in. Accept *married* in two columns as well if the learner points out that *a married couple* describes people. The useful observation is that six of these ten words are positions in a diagram and the other four are states.

**2A** · Coach 6, half past eight 🔊 Track 4.1
**1** F · **2** T · **3** T · **4** F
**Listen once** — his sister's.

**Table** — the woman in seat 14: a stranger, who lent him a coat. The man with the coat: nobody, and the coat is hers. The person he is going to: his aunt, who brought him up.

**1 is false:** it is his sister's. **4 is false:** he is travelling alone.

**Noticing** — *my* has to hold a noun and *mine* refuses one. *It's not my* stops where the noun should be, and the listener waits for it.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 mine · 2 's · 3 's · 4 his · 5 theirs · 6 ours

**3E** · Use — guided
1 Sam's bag.  
2 Mine.  
3 Ours.  
4 The guard's keys.  
5 My sister's children.

The apostrophe always attaches to the owner, never to the thing. In 2 and 3 there is no noun at all, so the standing-alone form is the only option.

**3G** · Check — six items, mark your own
1 mine · 2 's · 3 hers · 4 Whose · 5 *It's not mine.* — *my* cannot end a sentence · 6 *That is Sam's bag.* — people take ’s, not *of*

**5C** · Answer
1 Two adults and up to three children travelling together, for the price of two adults.  
2 Eleven.  
3 Because the fare covers five people and there were six.  
4 Seventeen.  
5 Because she is one adult, not two. The fare is written for a shape of family that she is not in, and the children are not the problem — the missing second adult is.  
6 It is paying for a number of bodies in a particular arrangement, and not for care. Three of the four bookings were doing care work and paid full fare; the one that got the discount had the most adults in it.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**travelling together** — making the journey as one group · **between them** — the two of them combined · **they split it** — they shared the cost · **cannot travel alone** — needs somebody with her · **looking after** — caring for

**5E** · The counter-text
1 Two adults and up to three children.  
2 Two to fifteen inclusive, because under-twos travel free and sixteen counts as an adult. The two ages are the two places the price changes.  
3 "Proof of relationship may be requested."  
4 Because defining it would mean choosing, in writing, whose household counts — and that choice is then the company's. Leaving it undefined moves the decision to whoever is at the counter, where it is made ninety times a day and never recorded.

**5F** · Two texts
All four are families and only one fits the sentence, which tells you the sentence is a description of a price and not of a family.

**6C** · Chunk
1 Whose · 2 mine · 3 ours · 4 belongs · 5 of mine · 6 's

**6D** · Contrast Clinic
> **1** **mine** — standing alone
> **2** **mine** — standing alone
> **3** **My** — in front of a noun
> **4** **My** — in front of a noun
> **5** **mine** — standing alone
> **6** **My** — in front of a noun

**12A** · Recycle — twelve items
1 mine · 2 's · 3 hers · 4 Whose · 5 belongs · 6 of · 7 got married · 8 brought up · 9 live with · 10 grew up · 11 as · 12 Did

## A1.2 · Unit 5 · Is There a Doctor on Board?

**0B** · Guess it
Years. A rubber mat wears in months; the floor under it wears in decades, and it is worn in the shape of two feet because the galley is two metres wide and there is only one place to stand. Twenty-three years, and the floor is the only record of it.

**1C** · Sort — the body, or the advice?
> **SOMETHING YOU DO ABOUT IT** — exercise · medicine · water
> **SOMETHING THE BODY HAS** — cold · sick · pain · headache · cough
> **BOTH** — rest · stress
**The two in both:** *rest* and *stress*. Rest is a thing the body needs and a thing you do; stress is a state the body is in and, in a working life, a thing that is done to it. Both are also nouns and verbs with no change of form.

**2A** · Quarter past five 🔊 Track 5.1
**1** F · **2** T · **3** F · **4** T
**Listen once** — her back.

**Table** — the pain: she says it is nothing; Nadia says sit down. The hours: she says she has four hundred breakfasts; Nadia says do not lift that. The doctor: she has not seen one; Nadia suggests Winnipeg.

**1 is false:** she has not. **3 is false:** there is nobody on board.

**Noticing** — 1, 2 and 3 are advice; 4 is a promise. The hardest to say is 3, because a question invites a refusal and the other two do not.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 should · 2 shouldn't · 3 Why don't you · 4 shouldn't work · 5 should · 6 should rest

**3E** · Use — guided
1 You should take something for it. / Why don't you take something for it?  
2 You should see a doctor. / Why don't you see a doctor?  
3 You should keep a bottle on the counter. / Why don't you keep a bottle on the counter?  
4 You shouldn't carry those alone. / Why don't you ask somebody to help?  
5 You should take a day. / Why don't you take Thursday?

The *Why don't you* version is gentler every time, and in 4 and 5 it is also more specific, which is what makes it easier to act on.

**3G** · Check — six items, mark your own
1 should · 2 shouldn't · 3 Why don't you · 4 should · 5 *You should sit down.* — nothing comes between a helper and its verb · 6 *She should rest.* — *should* never takes an s

**5C** · Answer
1 Four nights.  
2 Eleven hours.  
3 Bandages, a blanket and a list of telephone numbers.  
4 Thirty times out of forty-one.  
5 Because the eleven are the rare case and the announcement happens every time. The part that is hard forty-one times a year is not the medicine; it is speaking to a hundred and ninety-two frightened people.  
6 Making an announcement that does not frighten people, and dealing with a medical event. The first is harder, because it happens every time and because nobody thinks of it as a skill.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**at its furthest point** — where it is most distant · **a padlock** — a lock you put on and take off · **answered** — came forward in response · **the worst part** — the hardest element · **in a voice that** — speaking in a way that

**5E** · The counter-text
1 Nine.  
2 The ice pack and the glucose gel. The ice pack is for an injury; the glucose gel is the one item on the list for a medical emergency rather than a cut.  
3 Nineteen months ago.  
4 Advice. It gets you a person in Toronto telling you what to do, which is worth something — and the train is still eleven hours from anybody who can do it.

**5F** · Two texts
The announcement. The telephone number is the system on paper and the announcement is the system that runs forty-one times a year, and nobody has ever been trained in the second one.

**6C** · Chunk
1 should · 2 shouldn't · 3 Why don't · 4 feel better · 5 Take · 6 hurts

**6D** · Contrast Clinic
> **1** **shouldn't** — advice against
> **2** **should** — advice for
> **3** **should** — advice for
> **4** **should** — advice for
> **5** **shouldn't** — advice against
> **6** **shouldn't** — advice against

**12A** · Recycle — twelve items
1 should · 2 shouldn't · 3 Why don't · 4 feel better · 5 Take · 6 hurts · 7 lie down · 8 give up · 9 have a cold · 10 see a doctor · 11 mine · 12 as … as

## A1.2 · Unit 6 · The Dining Car in 2035

**0B** · Guess it
The one where nobody is looking at a phone. Four strangers who arrived separately and are still at the table an hour later have been talking longest, because nothing else is keeping them there.

**1C** · Sort — the thing, or what happens to it?
> **A THING** — machine · car · robot · city · energy
> **SOMETHING THAT HAPPENS** — disappear · replace · grow · improve
> **BOTH** — change
**Change** is both, with no change of form at all: *a change* and *to change*. Accept *energy* in two columns if the learner notices that it is a thing you buy and a property something has. English is unusually willing to let a noun become a verb untouched, and this is why.

**2A** · After the second sitting 🔊 Track 6.1
**1** T · **2** F · **3** T · **4** F
**Listen once** — 2035.

**Table** — the engine: electric, 96 per cent. The dining car: replaced by a machine, 70 per cent. The crew: six to four, 74 per cent.

**2 is false:** he says probably, about seventy per cent. **4 is false:** they do not.

**Noticing** — 1 is a prediction, 2 is an offer, 3 is a decision made at that second, and 4 is a prediction with a doubt in it. The word *will* is identical in all four and only the situation distinguishes them.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 will · 2 will probably · 3 'll · 4 Maybe · 5 Will · 6 won't

**3E** · Use — guided
1 The dining car will probably close.  
2 The crew will certainly get smaller — the plan is costed.  
3 Trains will definitely get faster.  
4 Maybe the route will stay the same.  
5 I don't think people will stop booking.

Accept any certainty word in the right position: *probably* and *definitely* after *will*, *maybe* and *perhaps* at the front.

**3G** · Check — six items, mark your own
1 will · 2 'll · 3 won't · 4 probably · 5 *The engine will be electric.* — nothing between a helper and its verb · 6 *She will come tomorrow.* — *will* never takes an s

**5C** · Answer
1 About four hundred thousand dollars a year.  
2 Why people chose this train instead of flying.  
3 Sixty-one per cent.  
4 A machine that sells sandwiches.  
5 Because the sandwiches really will be fresher, always available, and nobody will wait forty minutes for a table. The plan is not wrong about what it measures.  
6 Because the thing being removed is not a food service. The finance argument is about a cost centre and table nine is the product, and the two have never been in the same document.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**head office** — the company's central administration · **surveys passengers** — asks them questions systematically · **a different document** — not the one being quoted · **always be available** — obtainable at any time · **exchange addresses** — give each other contact details

**5E** · The counter-text
1 Five.  
2 The dining car replacement, at seventy per cent.  
3 One point seven million dollars a year.  
4 "Reputational." Open answers for the replacement: the strongest ones name the sixty-one per cent and say what would happen to bookings. One word is not enough because a risk without a number cannot be weighed against savings that have four.

**5F** · Two texts
Yes, and that is the problem: the service will improve on every measure the plan uses, and the reason people book will not be among them.

**6C** · Chunk
1 probably · 2 Maybe · 3 think · 4 don't think · 5 We'll see · 6 Who knows

**6D** · Contrast Clinic
> **1** **probably** — after *will*
> **2** **Maybe** — at the front
> **3** **Maybe** — at the front
> **4** **Maybe** — at the front
> **5** **probably** — after *will*
> **6** **probably** — after *will*

## A1.2 · Unit 7 · Twenty-Two Red Handles

**0B** · Guess it
The one about the law. It is the only one that was not the company's to put up or take down, so nobody has ever had the authority to replace it — which is also why it is the most faded and the highest on the wall.

**1C** · Sort — the thing, or the rule about it?
> **A RULE OR A STATUS** — illegal · safety · legal · allowed
> **BOTH** — warning · speed
> **A THING YOU CAN SEE** — exit · seatbelt · helmet · licence
**The two in both:** *speed* and *warning*. A speed is a number you can read off a dial and also a limit somebody set; a warning is a sign you can see and the act of warning. Both are the thing and the rule about the thing at once.

**2A** · In the vestibule 🔊 Track 7.1
**1** F · **2** T · **3** T · **4** F
**Listen once** — breakable covers.

**Table** — the crew: must check them every trip; mustn't lock the box; don't have to sign the form. The passengers: must not touch it except in an emergency; mustn't block it; don't have to report a near miss.

**1 is false:** they may, in an emergency — that is what it is for. **4 is false:** it is a proposal.

**Noticing** — sentence 3 says it is not necessary. It gives permission not to do something, which is the opposite of forbidding it.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 mustn't · 2 have to · 3 don't have to · 4 has to · 5 must

**3E** · Use — guided
1 Compulsory — *You have to wear a seatbelt.* (the law, in most countries)  
2 Forbidden — *You mustn't touch the emergency handle*, except in an emergency.  
3 Not necessary — *You don't have to sign the visitor book.*  
4 Compulsory — *You have to carry a licence when you drive.*  
5 Not necessary — *You don't have to arrive an hour early.*

Note that 1 and 4 both come from outside the speaker, which is why *have to* fits them better than *must*.

**3G** · Check — six items, mark your own
1 mustn't · 2 have to · 3 don't have to · 4 has to · 5 *You mustn't touch it.* — *don't must* does not exist · 6 *She must wear a helmet.* — no *to* after *must*

**5C** · Answer
1 Twenty-two.  
2 Four.  
3 About fifty minutes.  
4 A man in car 7 had a seizure and a passenger pulled the handle; the crew came and he is alive.  
5 Because *probably* is the honest word and the writer will not round it up. Eight seconds did not matter that night as far as anybody can tell, and nobody can tell for certain.  
6 Open answers. The strongest ones notice that the three mistakes are cheap and countable and the fourth is neither, and that a decision made only on countable things will always choose the covers.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**leaned on one** — rested their weight against it · **the length of it** — from one end to the other · **the nearest crew member** — the closest one · **breakable covers** — covers designed to break · **would not have mattered** — would have made no difference

**5E** · The counter-text
1 Four.  
2 A fine of up to five hundred dollars, in twenty-four point — the largest type on the notice.  
3 2011.  
4 It was written for the company, not for the passenger. The largest line protects the company from improper use and the smallest line is the one a frightened person needs in the two seconds they have.

**5F** · Two texts
No. Nobody reads four numbered instructions and a penalty line while somebody is having a seizure — which is exactly why the one line that matters should not be the smallest one on the page.

**6C** · Chunk
1 mustn't · 2 have to · 3 don't have to · 4 allowed · 5 illegal · 6 responsible

**6D** · Contrast Clinic
> **1** **don't have to** — it is not necessary
> **2** **mustn't** — forbidden, and it is the law
> **3** **mustn't** — forbidden
> **4** **don't have to** — it is optional
> **5** **mustn't** — forbidden, except in an emergency
> **6** **don't have to** — and the sentence says you can

**12A** · Recycle — twelve items
1 mustn't · 2 have to · 3 don't have to · 4 has to · 5 allowed · 6 illegal · 7 responsible · 8 follow · 9 pay a fine · 10 fill in · 11 probably · 12 shouldn't

## A1.2 · Unit 8 · Twenty-Three Years and Two

**0B** · Guess it
The one with two years. A small number in a box headed YEARS OF EXPERIENCE looks like an answer that is going to lose, so the pen stops while the person works out whether there is anywhere else on the page to put the rest.

**1C** · Sort — the thing, or the doing of it?
> **BOTH** — cook · win
> **A THING YOU HAVE** — adventure · experience · language · moment · skill
> **A THING YOU DO** — climb · drive · swim
**The two in both:** *win* and *cook*. A cook is a person and to cook is an action; a win is a result and to win is a verb. Note that *experience* is the only word on this list that is uncountable in one sense and countable in another: *she has experience* and *an experience*.

## A1.2 · Unit 9 · Forty Below

**0B** · Guess it
The one with no coat. Nobody stands outside at minus thirty-eight without a coat on purpose; they came out for something that was going to take forty seconds, and the forty seconds became nine minutes because nobody else knows how long this will take either.

**1C** · Sort — the weather, or what it does to you?
> **BOTH** — wet · ice
> **SOMETHING YOU FEEL** — degrees · cool · dry
> **SOMETHING IN THE SKY** — sky · cloud · snow · storm · wind
**The two in both:** *ice* and *wet*. Ice falls out of the sky and forms on a rail; wet describes the weather and the state of your coat. Both are conditions outside you and sensations on you, and English uses one word for each pair.

**2A** · On the platform 🔊 Track 9.1
**1** F · **2** T · **3** F · **4** T
**Listen once** — minus forty-four.

**Table** — tonight: leave at 23.00, changed to not at all. Tomorrow morning: nothing planned, changed to leaving at six. The weekend: unchanged, and it depends on the weather.

**1 is false:** they are putting it off, not cancelling it. **3 is false:** she says *not yet*.

**Noticing** — in 1 the evidence is in front of him: the sky and the forecast. In 4 the decision was made this morning. Both take *going to* and the reason is different: evidence in one, a decision already made in the other.

**3E** · Use — guided

3 I'll take that for you — decided as you speak.  
4 I'm going to take a course in the spring — decided last week.  
5 The train leaves at 06.10 — a timetable, so the present simple, which is the fourth future and the one this unit does not teach.

## A1.2 · Unit 10 · Four Thousand One Hundred Cards

**0B** · Guess it
The one with no year on it, because nobody writes the year on a box that is still being filled. It is also the fullest, which means the cards have not stopped coming — they have only stopped being counted.

**1C** · Sort — the thing, or your opinion of it?
> **PART OF THE STORY** — ending · memory · story
> **BOTH** — favourite · true
> **WHAT YOU THINK OF IT** — funny · boring · longest · interesting · amazing
**The two in both:** *true* and *favourite*. Truth is a property of the story and a claim you are making about it; a favourite is a thing and a judgement at the same time. *Longest* looks factual and is almost always an opinion, which is worth arguing about.

**2A** · In the storeroom 🔊 Track 10.1
**1** F · **2** T · **3** F · **4** T
**Listen once** — nine.

**Table** — complaints: expected most of them, actually 210. Stories: expected almost none, actually 1,100. Nothing at all: expected none, actually 990.

**1 is false:** 210 of 4,100. **3 is false:** there is not.

**Noticing** — *too* means more than you want: too long to read. *Not enough* means less than you need: not enough time. They describe the same mismatch from opposite sides, and the thing that is wrong is different in each.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 the strangest · 2 the best · 3 enough · 4 too · 5 the most interesting

**3E** · Use — guided
Accept any sentence with a correct superlative and a reason using *too* or *enough*. For example: *The longest journey I have made was four nights, and by the third night it was too long.* · *The hardest thing about English is listening, because people do not leave enough space between words.*

**3G** · Check — six items, mark your own

**5C** · Answer
1 "The best thing about your journey?"  
2 Four thousand one hundred.  
3 Two hundred and ten.  
4 About four hundred.  
5 Because every one of them arrived by another route as well — a letter, an email, a complaints process. The card was never the channel for a complaint; it only looked like one.  
6 That it is selling time in a room with strangers. The seat and the journey are what is priced; what people write about afterwards is each other.

**four lines** — the space provided · **came back** — were returned · **by another route** — through a different channel · **by description** — identified by what they looked like · **for eleven years** — throughout that period

2 Four. The writer used all four, then the right margin, then the back.  
3 2016.  
4 That they were not writing to the company at all. Writing over the address makes the card unpostable, which means it was never meant to be sent — it was written because the writer wanted to write it down.
