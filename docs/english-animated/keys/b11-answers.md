## B1.1 · Unit 1 · Ground Floor

**0B** · Guess it
**Open.** Better reasons use evidence in the drawing: the planning notice on the hoarding (which names demolition of the single-storey structures), the two-metre frontage with no lease visible, the scaffold already up next door. Worse reasons are predictions about the kind of street it is, with nothing in the picture behind them. Mark the reason, not the choice.

**1B** · Sort — how much is destroyed?
**Least to most destroyed, as most groups order them:** do up · repaint · refit · convert · extend · redevelop · demolish.  
*Do up* and *repaint* are regularly swapped, and the swap is the useful disagreement: *repaint* changes less of the fabric, *do up* changes more but is cheaper and shorter. *Extend* destroys almost nothing and takes longest. The question to put back to the class is **what are you measuring** — fabric, cost, or how long people are out.

**2A** · Three people on the same street 🔊 Track 1.1
**1** A · **2** C · **3** C · **4** A · **5** B · **6** B
**Noticing** — the six sentences are keyed above, by letter.

**Gist** — Tom. He is the only one with somewhere to go and the only one who talks about the street as a view rather than a living.

**Detail** — Grace: was workshops and people she knew; is scaffolding and a lift that breaks; will be flats she cannot afford to clean in. Mr Kamau: was quiet and poor; is busy and expensive; will be finished, and he says *it's progress*. Tom: was nothing to look at; is noisy; will be worth more than he paid.

**Inference** — "I've been here twenty-two years and nobody has written my name down" and "they put the notice up in English." Neither sentence contains a worry; both report a position she has no standing in.

**Attitude** — the vowel in *progress*: lengthened and falling, which carries resignation. The word itself is approving; the sound is not.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 opened · 2 was · 3 were repairing · 4 rented · 5 died · 6 went · 7 had … raised · 8 worked *(or* was working*)* · 9 blocked *(or* was blocking*)* · 10 did not complain · 11 bought

**3E** · Use — guided
**Order:** Mwangi rented the frontage from Otieno → Otieno died → the first tower was finished → Mwangi was working by lamp for eleven days → the second tower took the open ground → the drivers moved three streets away.  
**Model connecting text:** *Mwangi had rented the frontage from Otieno for fifteen years before Otieno died in 2009. By the time the first tower was finished in 2014, the rent had already risen twice. For eleven days afterwards he was working by lamp, because the new building blocked the sun until ten. The second tower then took the open ground, and the drivers moved three streets away.*

**5C** · Close reading
1 Open ground, where matatu drivers parked and argued.  
2 Because the first tower blocked the sun until after ten in the morning for eleven days after it was finished.  
3 Before, he repaired work clothes; now he takes in suits. And his customers are young people who have just started earning and bought the wrong size online — a different trade, not a smaller one.  
4 That he raised the rent twice and then forgot about it. The detail does two things: it explains why Mwangi is still there at all, and it makes the displacement of the other thirteen businesses a matter of attention rather than malice.  
5 Their rent went up, they moved somewhere cheaper, and then that place got expensive too. The sentence refuses the word *failure* and describes a process instead.  
6 That the new buildings brought him the customers he has now — and that the lane had been dangerous after dark for years before anyone built anything.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**frontage** — the width of street a stall or shop occupies · **stretch** — a length of one street, treated as a unit · **sentimental** — attached to the past in a way that gets in the way of judgement · **trading** — still open and doing business · **take in** — alter a garment to make it narrower · **the lane had been dangerous** — past perfect: dangerous before the period the sentence is about, and the point is that it was dangerous *first*

**5E** · The counter-text
1 Demolition of the existing single-storey structures and erection of a nine-storey building: commercial floorspace at ground and first floor, residential above, with access, servicing and landscaping.  
2 25 February 2026 — twenty-one days from publication on 4 February.  
3 Development Control, PO Box 30075, in writing.  
4 One: the applicant, Westrand Holdings (Nominee) Ltd — and *Nominee* means that is not a person either. No individual is named anywhere on the notice.  
5 Nothing, through this route: representations received after the closing date will not be taken into account. They can inspect the application at the public counter, 09:00–15:00 Monday to Thursday — which is when a person with a job is at work.

**6A** · Register sort
> **both** — by the time · at first · in the meantime
> **speech** — back then · ever since · up to that point · from then on · before long · later on
> **the planning notice** — thereafter · subsequently · previously
The three in both columns are the ones with no register marking at all: *by the time*, *at first*, *in the meantime*. The notice column is Latinate and the speech column is Germanic, which is the whole pattern and is worth saying out loud.

**6B** · Chunk completion
1 By · 2 Until · 3 ever · 4 before · 5 At / Later

**6C** · Semantic map
**EARLIER:** previously · up to that point · back then · at first · before long *(relative to a later point)*  
**AT THE SAME TIME:** in the meantime · ever since *(a span running up to now)*  
**LATER:** thereafter · subsequently · from then on · later on · before long  
**A BOUNDARY:** by the time · from then on · up to that point · at first  
*Three sit in two zones:* **from then on** *(later, and a boundary),* **up to that point** *(earlier, and a boundary),* **before long** *(earlier or later, depending on the anchor). Accept any placing the learner can justify with a sentence.*

**6D** · Contrast Clinic — past simple against past perfect
> **1** closed — a dated event. *(Pairs with 2.)*
> **2** had already closed — before the notice. **The difference:** in the first the period ran its course; in the second the notice went up after it was over.
> **3** had left — before that year. *(Pairs with 4.)*
> **4** left — during that year. **The difference:** four by 2016 is a running total; four in 2016 is one year's worth, and the two can describe completely different streets.
> **5** had expired — before she read it. *(Pairs with 6.)*
> **6** expired — the same day. **The difference:** in the first she was a day too late; in the second she still had hours.

**7C** · Make it mean something
The elided version — /aɪ dəʊnəʊ wɒt ðeɪ juːstə duː/ — sounds like a person. The fully articulated one sounds like a document read aloud. You would want the second when you are being quoted, when you are speaking to somebody who is straining to follow, or when the precise words matter more than the relationship — a dictation, a legal statement, a number read back.

**12A** · Recycle — twelve items
1 had closed · 2 was working · 3 was · 4 priced · 5 use · 6 Until · 7 had not received · 8 By · 9 None · 10 a · 11 which / that · 12 goes

## B1.1 · Unit 2 · What It Costs To Make

**0B** · Guess it
**Open — keep the number.** The cloth is 1,302 of a 4,800 jacket, which is about 27 per cent. Most classes guess much higher, usually between a half and two thirds. The point in Part 8 is that the guess is wrong in a predictable direction: people price the material they can see and not the hours they cannot.

**1B** · Sort — who carries the risk?
**Paid first to paid last:** the cloth merchant *(cash on collection)* · the trim supplier *(30 days)* · the machinists *(weekly)* · the workshop's landlord *(monthly)* · Mariam *(on invoice, 60 days)* · the buyer's own company *(on sale)*.  
**Who carries the risk:** Mariam. She has paid for cloth, trims and labour before anybody has bought a jacket, and her invoice is settled after the buyer has sold them. If nobody buys, the buyer records no loss and she has forty jackets and an empty account.

**2A** · Forty jackets 🔊 Track 2.1
**1** A · **2** B · **3** A · **4** B · **5** A · **6** B
**Noticing** — the six sentences are keyed above.

**Gist** — No. He leaves without placing it; what he places is a sample order.

**Detail** — Price: he says *we're working to a number*; she hears that the number is already fixed and is not hers. Quantity: he says *forty to start*; she hears that forty is the whole order. Timing: he says *fairly soon*; she hears a date she will be held to and he will not.

**Inference** — *We've been looking at a few workshops* is the continuous, which points at the activity rather than a result. If he had been to three workshops he would say *we've spoken to three* — a countable result. The continuous is doing the work of a number he does not have.

**Attitude** — the second *I can do that* has a falling-rising tone on *can* and a longer vowel. The first is an offer; the second is a concession with a cost in it.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 has been working · 2 has made · 3 has trained · 4 has not been sleeping · 5 has gone *(or* has been going*)* · 6 have been doing · 7 have never had · 8 have been hearing

**3E** · Use — guided
1 I've been writing emails to them. **Changes:** the three disappear and the effort appears — it now sounds like a complaint rather than a report.  
2 We've looked at four suppliers. **Changes:** a countable result replaces an activity, and the speaker becomes answerable for the number.  
3 She's cut the cloth for forty. **Changes:** the morning disappears and the work is finished.  
4 They've been paying in instalments. **Changes:** two becomes a pattern, and the sentence stops saying how much is left.

**5C** · Close reading
1 The price on the invoice, and nothing else.  
2 Four days of rework when the first batch comes back with the seams wrong; two weeks of delay while a replacement is cut; the senior fitter's time on a problem the cheaper workshop was never equipped to avoid.  
3 The buyer records no loss at all. The cost shows up as a sourcing problem eighteen months later, and nobody connects the two.  
4 A single figure with a name attached — the invoice — as against a figure that has to be built from four departments, three quarters and one person's weekend. The first always wins an argument, whatever it leaves out.  
5 That the workshops which survive are the ones that learned to put the assembled number on the page before anybody asked for it.  
6 Because the obvious reading is that buyers are being mean, and the piece is arguing that they are hitting the targets they were given. Naming that early stops the reader dismissing the argument as a complaint about people.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**procurement** — the function that buys, measured on price · **rework** — doing again what was done wrong, and the cost falls on the other side · **underpricing** — quoting below the real cost, usually without knowing it · **deferred** — put off, and still owed — here maintenance · **assembled** (the assembled number) — built from parts held by different people · **subsidising** — paying part of somebody else's cost out of your own margin

**5E** · The counter-text
1 Cloth 1,302 + lining 118 + trims 186 + labels 124 = **1,730**.  
2 Eleven hours — 1.5 cutting, 8.0 making, 1.5 finishing — at 150 an hour, the workshop rate rather than the machinist rate. **1,650** in all.  
3 Sampling: eleven hours across three rounds, costed at zero. It is on the sheet because the work was really done; it is at zero because it is carried by the workshop and charged to nobody. Putting it on the page at zero is the one honest move available.  
4 1,080 on 4,800 — **22.5 per cent**.  
5 The sampling line. Eleven hours at 150 would be 1,650, which is more than the margin on the whole batch of forty — and it is the assembled number the article says nobody puts on the page.

**6A** · Sort — which group?
> **this points at a result** — finalised · outstanding · incomplete · twice already · in one go · provisional · cumulative · so far
> **this points at an activity** — steadily · repeatedly · keep at · ongoing · all morning · gradually · for the last six months · intermittent · up to now · bit by bit · I've been meaning to… · get through · throughout
**The three that go in both:** *so far* (a running total, and a period still open), *up to now* (the same), and *cumulative* (a result built out of an activity). *get through* is arguable as a fourth — *I got through it* is a result, *I've been getting through it* an activity.

**6B** · Chunk completion
1 bit / bit · 2 in / go · 3 outstanding · 4 final / finalised · 5 been meaning

**6C** · Collocation grid
**ongoing:** costs ✓ · work ✓ · progress ✓ — *ongoing payment* ✗  
**outstanding:** costs ✓ · payment ✓ · work ✓ — *outstanding progress* ✓ *(but it means "excellent", not "unfinished", which is the trap)*  
**cumulative:** costs ✓ · progress ✓ — *cumulative payment* ✓ · *cumulative work* ✗  
**provisional:** costs ✓ · payment ✓ · progress ✓ — *provisional work* ✗  
**The one nobody says:** *ongoing payment*. *Ongoing costs* and *outstanding payment* are both ordinary; the two words simply do not combine.

**6D** · Contrast Clinic — simple against continuous
> **1** has been cutting — *since seven* fixes a span, so the activity is the point.
> **2** has been chasing — *for a month* is a span, and the sentence is a complaint about effort.
> **3** has cut — forty is a countable result.
> **4** have been speaking — *all week* is a span, and no number is offered.
> **5** have spoken — three is a result the speaker can be held to.
> **6** has sent — *twice* counts completed actions.

**7C** · Make it mean something
The natural, elided version sounds like somebody defending their work: the reductions carry fluency, and fluency reads as confidence. The fully pronounced version sounds like a witness statement — every syllable separable, because somebody may need to write it down.

**12A** · Recycle — twelve items
1 has been making · 2 has made · 3 outstanding · 4 breaks · 5 unpaid · 6 been meaning · 7 had closed · 8 priced · 9 Until · 10 use · 11 had not received · 12 None

## B1.1 · Unit 3 · The Name On The Door

**0B** · Guess it
The plate for the first-floor office. The lettering is newer than the others and the screw holes beside it are the old ones, re-used — so the plate was swapped without moving the fixing. The business it names left two years ago, which the hoarding behind it dates.

**1B** · Sort — cline of permanence
**Easiest to change → hardest, as most groups order them:** nickname · spelling on an email · the name on a lanyard · the name on a payslip · the name on a passport · citizenship.  
**Where it stops being a choice:** most classes mark it between the lanyard and the payslip, because the payslip is the first one that needs somebody else's agreement. A defensible alternative is between the payslip and the passport, because a payslip can be changed by asking once and a passport cannot. The disagreement is the task.

**2A** · Three ways to say what happens next 🔊 Track 3.1
**1** C · **2** D · **3** B · **4** A
**Noticing** — the four sentences are keyed above.

**Gist** — the Thursday meeting. It is the only one of the four that is in a diary and has another person in it.

**Detail** — *I'm seeing him on Thursday*: decided, by her and him together. *I'm going to tell him*: decided, by her alone, before now. *I'll call you back*: decided at that moment, for the listener. *The lift goes out of service at six*: decided by nobody in the conversation — it is a timetable.

**Inference** — *I'm calling you back at four*. The present continuous reports an arrangement that exists; *I'll call you back* is produced on the spot to end the call, and both speakers know it.

**Attitude** — the vowel in *have*: lengthened, with a fall-rise. The words are identical both times and only the second one is reluctant.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 is meeting · 2 is going to tell · 3 will take · 4 runs out · 5 am not doing · 6 am going to sit *(or* am sitting*)*

**3E** · Use — guided
1 **More:** *I'm looking at it this afternoon.* **Less:** *I might look at it.* **Socially:** the continuous puts it in a diary and makes it checkable; *might* hands the listener the knowledge that it may not happen, which is more honest and less reassuring.  
2 **More:** *We're reviewing the contract on Monday.* **Less:** *We're thinking of reviewing the contract.* **Socially:** *going to* is a decision already taken; the continuous adds a date and therefore an obligation; *thinking of* withdraws the decision.  
3 **More:** *I'm due to meet them on the fourteenth.* **Less:** *I'm hoping to meet them next week.* **Socially:** *be due to* makes the meeting institutional — somebody else is also expecting it — while *hoping to* makes the speaker the only person who would notice if it did not happen.

**5C** · Close reading
1 Because the registrar was watching, and the mother put something longer on the certificate.  
2 Having two names in two rooms — something that changed depending on which room you were in, with no secrecy and no decision in it.  
3 That one of the names was going to have to win. The mother decided, in about half a second, not to correct the teacher.  
4 The gap is between choosing and letting the paperwork choose. The long name was already on the register, the library card, the inoculation record and a bank account; choosing the short one would have meant nineteen arguments with institutions, each demanding a document in the name being left behind. So the choice was made by what already existed.  
5 The school register, the library, the clinic (inoculation record), the bank, and the registry itself. All five hold a record in the long name, and each one requires another to verify it — which is what makes them collectively immovable.  
6 Nothing in the documents. What changed is the narrator: having thought for eleven years that the short name was the one that did not count, they have started to mind that when their mother dies nobody alive will use it.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**registrar** — the official who records a birth and whose presence changed the name · **inoculation record** — a clinic's vaccination card, here one of the institutions holding the long name · **carry** (carrying the name) — bear it as the recorded form, so the document can be used as proof of it · **mind** (I have started to mind) — be troubled by, in the understated British sense · **count** (the one that did not count) — be treated as real by institutions

**5E** · The counter-text
1 Six.  
2 Two persons who have known the applicant for not less than five years and who are not related to the applicant by blood or marriage.  
3 It does not say. The small print says applications are processed in the order received and that the registry does not give an estimated processing time — the only honest sentence on the form, and it is in the smallest type.  
4 It remains on record; gazette publication, where required, is arranged separately. The form does not say the old name is removed anywhere, which is the point of 5C question 4.  
5 **Four.** The form fee (1,000), the certified copies of the identity card and passport (400 + 400), the sworn statutory declaration (2,500) and the photographs (600) — 4,900 in all, and no two of them are in the same place on the page. *(Accept five if gazette publication is counted, since it is "arranged separately" and therefore charged separately.)*  
**5C on the two texts:** the form **supports** the extract. Six documents, four fee points, two witnesses of five years' standing, and no processing time is a description of nineteen arguments compressed into one page. **The more honest document is the form** — it at least admits it will not tell you how long it takes.

**6A** · Cline — how bound are you?
**Fully committed → free to walk away:**  
commit · promise · arrange · be due to · be set to · be about to · imminently · shortly · intend · aim · look set to · be likely to · expect · anticipate · as things stand · provisionally · be thinking of · hesitate · put off · I was going to… · back out of.  
*The useful arguments: **be about to** and **imminently** are about time, not commitment, and learners place them high because imminence feels binding. **I was going to…** is the most bound-sounding phrase on the list and is the least binding thing anybody says.*

**6B** · Chunk completion
1 due · 2 about · 3 thinking · 4 As / stand · 5 backed

**6C** · Register sort
**Contract only:** be due to · provisionally · as things stand · imminently. **Speech only:** be thinking of · I was going to… · back out of · put off. **Both:** intend · arrange · expect · commit. *(Accept* anticipate *in both.)* The pattern: the contract column takes phrases that can be dated or disclaimed, and the speech column takes phrases that attribute a state of mind.

**6D** · Contrast Clinic — the four futures
> **1** is starting — present continuous: a fixed arrangement with another party.
> **2** are announcing — an arrangement already in place. *(are going to announce* is acceptable and slightly weaker.)*
> **3** closes — present simple: a timetable, decided by nobody present.
> **4** 'll sort — decided at this moment, for the listener.
> **5** am not going to sign — an intention formed before now, which the week makes explicit.
> **6** 'll go — decided on the spot.

**7C** · Make it mean something
**I** didn't say she stole it *(somebody else did)* · I **didn't** say it *(I deny saying it at all)* · I didn't **say** it *(I implied it)* · I didn't say **she** stole it *(somebody else stole it)* · I didn't say she **stole** it *(she borrowed it)* · I didn't say she stole **the** money *(she stole different money)* · I didn't say she stole the **money** *(she stole something else)*.

**12A** · Recycle — twelve items
1 am seeing · 2 expires · 3 'll · 4 backed · 5 goes · 6 proof · 7 has been making · 8 breaks · 9 had closed · 10 priced · 11 which · 12 was working

## B1.1 · Unit 4 · Nobody's Job

**0B** · Guess it
The propped second-floor fire door, the unsigned first-aid box, and the absence of an accessible toilet. **How to find out:** read the 2019 inspection report, which has an *action by* column — and note that items 1 and 2 say *occupier* while item 3 is blank. Then read the lease, which the report itself says is where responsibility is allocated.

**1B** · Sort — cline of obligation
**Absolute → optional:** a duty of care · a right · standard practice · a recommendation · a courtesy · a preference.  
**Where it stops being enforceable:** between *a right* and *standard practice*. A duty of care and a right are both actionable; standard practice is evidence of what a reasonable person would do, which is weaker and is still used in court. The argument worth having is whether standard practice belongs above or below the line — it is the only item that changes status depending on who is suing whom.

**2A** · It's not our building 🔊 Track 4.1
**1** C · **2** C · **3** A · **4** A · **5** B · **6** B
**Noticing** — the six sentences are keyed above. **Now:** 1, 3, 5. **Then:** 2, 4, 6.

**Gist** — No. One person agrees to find out who owns the common parts, which is not an action on the problem.

**Detail** — Wanjiru cannot authorise work on the common parts; she says the landlord should. Mr Kamau cannot spend on a building he leases; he says the tenants' association should. Priya cannot do anything at all as a sub-tenant; she says somebody should write it down.

**Inference** — *Required by whom* is unsaid because naming the requirement would name the document, and the document is the lease, which is between him and the landlord. The passive with no agent is doing exactly the work Unit 6 will name.

**Attitude** — the second *fine* is level-then-falling with a long vowel and a glottal stop at the end. The first rises. Same word, and the second closes the subject rather than accepting anything.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 had to maintain · 2 did not have to provide · 3 must not block · 4 had to submit · 5 did not have to include · 6 has to include *(or* must include*)*

**3E** · Use — guided
1 *We mustn't tell the tenants.* **Changed:** from an absence of obligation to a prohibition — the thing moves from optional to forbidden.  
2 *You don't have to use that door.* **Changed:** from a prohibition to a release — the door becomes available but unnecessary.  
3 *He didn't have to sign it.* **Changed:** the signing may still have happened; only the necessity is removed.  
4 *They didn't need to park there.* **Changed:** from prohibition to absence of need — and note that *needn't have parked* would mean they did and it was pointless.

**5C** · Close reading
1 19 September 2019.  
2 That the premises met the regulations **then in force** sufficiently that nothing was a contravention on that date. It does **not** mean full compliance, and nobody anywhere defines the gap between substantial and full.  
3 The propped second-floor fire door; first-aid provision not signed; no accessible sanitary facility.  
4 Item 3. From the document alone: it is the only one of the three with no entry in the *action by* column, so no person or role was ever attached to it.  
5 That where a material change of use is undertaken the premises will be assessed against the regulations in force at that date, and the noted items may become requirements in due course.  
6 E. Wanyama, in the capacity of **Inspecting Officer (reporting only)** — which is the capacity that cannot direct anybody to do anything.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**premises** — the building and its land, as a legal unit · **in force** — applying at that date, and the three words the whole argument rests on · **substantial** — enough, but not full, and the shortfall is undefined · **for attention** — noted without being required · **in due course** — at an unstated future point · **capacity** — the role somebody acts in, which here limits what the signature can do

**5E** · The counter-text
1 Nothing. *Noted* acknowledges receipt and commits to no action, which is why it is the most-used word in institutional email.  
2 It does not say. The report says the premises were assessed against the regulations then in force and that responsibility between owner and occupier is a matter for the lease — so *required* could mean the regulations, the report or the lease, and the sentence works because it means none of them.  
3 A material change of use — which the report names as the trigger. The email makes that sound like an external event; in fact it is something the owner himself would initiate.  
4 **The email.** Forty-one words with a reason in them travels; nine hundred words with a blank cell in a table does not. That is the lesson of the unit.

**6A** · Sort — strength
**Binding → entirely optional:**  
mandatory · compulsory · binding · obliged · required · banned · have no choice but to · be supposed to · be meant to · should have · needn't have · permitted · be free to · exempt · let off · didn't have to · get out of · discretionary · voluntary · optional · it's up to you.  
*The arguments worth having: **banned** is absolutely binding and is a prohibition rather than an obligation, so some classes put it at the far end instead. **be supposed to** and **be meant to** sound binding and are the two weakest things on the list that are not explicitly optional.*

**6B** · Chunk completion
1 voluntary / optional · 2 exempt · 3 no choice but to · 4 needn't · 5 got out

**6C** · Register sort
**Contract only:** mandatory · binding · exempt · discretionary · compulsory · permitted. **Speech only:** let off · get out of · it's up to you · be meant to · didn't have to. **Both:** obliged · required · voluntary · optional · should have. *(Accept* banned *in both.)*

**6D** · Contrast Clinic — *mustn't* against *don't have to*
> **1** mustn't — a prohibition. The wrong way round (*don't have to*) would make a fire door optional.
> **2** mustn't — a prohibition. *Don't have to* would turn a boundary into a convenience.
> **3** don't have to — a release. *Mustn't* would forbid somebody from doing unpaid work, which is an odd thing to forbid and a very different sentence.
> **4** don't have to — no obligation. *Mustn't* would mean booking is not allowed, which some venues do mean, and that is the trap.
> **5** needn't — it happened and was unnecessary. *Didn't have to* would suggest it may not have happened at all.
> **6** mustn't — a prohibition with a reason attached. *Don't have to* would be an invitation.

**7C** · Make it mean something
Said at speed, with no stress on the modal, it is kind: *you didn't have to come in* releases the listener. With *DIDN'T* stressed it is an accusation — it says the coming in was unnecessary and somebody has made a point of it. The words are identical and only the stress decides which conversation you are having.

**12A** · Recycle — twelve items
1 mustn't · 2 don't have to · 3 didn't have to · 4 care · 5 waiver · 6 obligation · 7 am seeing · 8 has been making · 9 had closed · 10 breaks · 11 had · 12 None

## B1.1 · Unit 5 · The Rota

**0B** · Guess it
**Three differences:** the Sunday night name, the Thursday early that is blank on the screen and filled in marker on the board, and the two names against the same Saturday on the board where the screen has one. **People follow the whiteboard**, because it is the one that can be changed in ten seconds by the person standing in front of it.

**1B** · Sort — what can be automated?
**Easiest → hardest to automate:** when the bins go out · who covers a sick shift · who gets the last desk · who is told first · who gets a reference · who is let go.  
**Where to stop:** most people stop after *who gets the last desk*, because *who is told first* is the first decision whose output is a relationship rather than an allocation. The useful disagreement is with somebody who stops after the bins — ask them what makes a sick shift different, and the answer is the favour economy in Part 5.

**2A** · It did what we told it 🔊 Track 5.1
**1** A · **2** C · **3** B · **4** A · **5** C · **6** B
**Noticing** — the six sentences are keyed above.

**Gist** — it stopped people swapping shifts. Not by forbidding it: by leaving nothing to swap.

**Detail** — Fairness rule (no more than two nights consecutively): produced a rota nobody could plan around, because it moved people every week. Availability rule (weight by declared availability): produced an incentive to declare less availability. Seniority rule (break ties by length of service): produced the same four people on every Sunday.

**Inference** — the favour economy. The system removed the thing that was holding the place together, and nobody noticed because it was never written down anywhere and so never appeared in any report of what changed.

**Attitude** — the stress and the lengthened vowel on *actually*, with a fall-rise. *Actually* concedes an objection, and the objection is one he has already had with himself.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 goes · 2 reallocates · 3 asks · 4 will have to · 5 had been · 6 would look

**3E** · Use — guided
1 **Further:** *If we switched it off, we would lose the audit trail.* **Closer:** *If we switch it off, we lose the audit trail* → *When we switch it off, we lose the audit trail.* **Changes:** the second conditional makes it a thought experiment; *when* makes it a plan.  
2 **Further:** *If she had complained, they would have listened.* **Closer:** *If she complains, they will listen.* **Changes:** the third conditional closes the possibility entirely and turns it into a reproach.  
3 **Further:** *If you pressed override, it would log who did it.* **Closer:** *If you press override, it logs who did it* is already zero — the closest move is *Pressing override logs who did it*, which removes the condition and states a property.

**5C** · Close reading
1 No person works more than two nights consecutively; weight allocation by declared availability; break remaining ties by length of service.  
2 *Working correctly* implies it is doing the right thing. *Working as specified* says only that it matches the document — and the document can be wrong. People hear the first and stop asking questions.  
3 Swapping: before the system, somebody who could not do a Sunday found somebody who could, and owed them one. It was not in the spec because it is not a rule — it is a favour economy, and nobody writes those down.  
4 That it had got harder to change a shift.  
5 Because he answered a different question from the one she was asking, and did it confidently. Being right about the question he answered is what let him stop listening to hers.  
6 The override first and the allocator second — and he would make the override cost thirty seconds instead of an email. If the cheapest thing to do is the thing the system wanted, it is a rule rather than a tool.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**specified** — written into the requirements document, as against merely intended · **audit** (audit it against the spec) — check line by line for conformance · **weight** — give more or less influence to a factor · **break ties** — decide between candidates the rules rank equally · **override** *(noun)* — the manual route round the automatic decision · **allocator** — the component that assigns shifts

**5E** · The counter-text
1 Three: 1.1, 1.2, 1.3.  
2 Length of service — rule 1.3.  
3 Clause 2. **Three:** written authorisation from the space manager; submitted not less than 48 hours in advance; stating the reason. And a fourth consequence — allocation is re-run for the affected week.  
4 Clause 3 puts shift trading between individuals explicitly **out of scope**, along with preference ranking. The spec knows the thing exists and declines to carry it.  
5 The space manager and the system owner, T. Fraser. **Neither had worked a Sunday** — which is the sentence in the interview that the document silently confirms.

**6A** · Sort — how direct is the link?
**A direct cause → a loose association:**  
result in · thereby · hence · consequently · as a result · which means that · consequence · the upshot is · outcome · in which case · end up · repercussion · knock-on · trade-off · worst case · otherwise · provided that · as long as · conversely · inadvertently · one thing leads to another.  
*The arguments: **provided that** and **as long as** are conditions rather than causes and some classes take them off the line altogether — accept that if they say why. **inadvertently** describes how a cause operated, not how strong it was, and is the one item most often misplaced at the strong end.*

**6B** · Chunk completion
1 upshot · 2 thereby · 3 as long / provided · 4 otherwise · 5 in which case

**6C** · Register sort
**Report only:** thereby · hence · consequently · repercussion · conversely · inadvertently. **Speech only:** one thing leads to another · end up · worst case · knock-on · the upshot is. **Both:** as a result · consequence · outcome · trade-off. *(Accept* in which case *in both.)*

**6D** · Contrast Clinic — zero, first, second
> **1** press / logs — **zero conditional**: a property of the system, true every time.
> **2** were / would fix — **second**: not real, and the speaker is not the landlord.
> **3** switch / will lose — **first**: a real possibility today.
> **4** had asked / would have happened — **third**: the past, closed.
> **5** doesn't come / will cover — **first**: real, and tomorrow.
> **6** gets / fails — **zero**: always true, a physical fact.

**7C** · Make it mean something
**A genuine offer:** rising tone on *want*, the whole phrase light and short. **A withdrawal of interest:** falling tone, flat, with a slight pause before it — it hands the decision over because the speaker has stopped caring. **An ultimatum:** level stress on all three words with a hard final consonant and no rise — it means *and live with it*.

**12A** · Recycle — twelve items
1 press / logs · 2 were / would change · 3 edge · 4 oversight · 5 thereby · 6 as long / provided · 7 mustn't · 8 am seeing · 9 has been making · 10 priced · 11 who / that · 12 will wait

## B1.1 · Unit 6 · The Rule Nobody Follows

**0B** · Guess it
The no-cooking notice (a kettle and a microwave are on the shelf beneath it), the no-storage notice (boxes are stacked against it), and the bicycle notice (a bike is chained to the first-floor rail behind it). In each case the evidence is the thing the notice forbids, placed in front of the notice and visibly settled — dust, a cable tie gone grey, a box with a date on it.

**1B** · Sort — cline of authority
**Hardest → easiest to change:** a statute · a regulation · a lease clause · a published policy · a written procedure · a notice on a door · standard practice.  
**Where it stops being a rule:** most classes mark it between the written procedure and the notice on the door, because the notice is the first one nobody has to agree to. A good counter-argument puts the line lower — standard practice is cited in court, and a notice on a door is not. Defend the mark, not the order.

**2A** · It's on the door 🔊 Track 6.1
**1** A · **2** B · **3** C · **4** B · **5** A · **6** B
**Noticing** — the six sentences are keyed above.

**Gist** — No. Nobody says so; the conversation ends with the wedge still in place.

**Detail** — Fire-door rule: 2019, by the managing agent, after a fire; last applied at the 2019 inspection. Key-return rule: 2019, by the managing agent; applied continuously, because it is the only one with a charge attached. No-cooking rule: 2014, by nobody anybody can name; never applied.

**Inference** — *It was brought in after something* is the most important sentence because it is the whole unit in seven words: an agentless passive, a vague noun, and a rule that everybody obeys the shape of and nobody can account for. The *something* was a fire, and the fact that nobody says so is why the rule has no force.

**Attitude** — the falling-rising tone on *know* and the slight pause before it. He knew. A genuine *I didn't know* is flat and quick.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 The fire door was propped open again. *(agent dropped — nobody will say who)*  
2 The rule was brought in by the managing agent after a fire in 2019. *(agent kept — it is the point)*  
3 The policy has not been reviewed since 2022. *(agent dropped — there is no doer)*  
4 The wedge will be removed before the inspection. *(agent dropped — and this is the sentence the unit is suspicious of)*  
5 Keys must be returned to the box by six. *(agent dropped — a rule addressed to everybody)*  
6 The door was wedged with an extinguisher by a tenant. *(instrument and agent both kept)*

**3E** · Use — guided
1 *It was decided not to enforce it.* **Gains:** it stops being one person's judgement and becomes institutional. **Loses:** the person who can be asked why.  
2 *The policy was written in 2019.* **Gains:** the focus moves to the document and its date. **Loses:** the fact that the author was the landlord's agent, which is the reason it reads as it does.  
3 *The rule has been ignored for a year.* **Gains:** it becomes a property of the rule rather than an accusation. **Loses:** the two tenants, and with them any possibility of enforcement.

**5C** · Close reading
1 That a rule does not apply any more. It is never produced in writing because there is nothing to produce.  
2 Making a rule takes a decision somebody can point at afterwards; unmaking one takes the same decision with none of the credit and all of the exposure. So rules only ever accumulate.  
3 Paragraph three. The writer used to think the accumulation was simply untidy and that the answer was a periodic cull.  
4 It holds a space — it can be produced at a moment of crisis, and the decade of being ignored will not help you then. And it marks a boundary: everybody knows the door should not be propped, and they know it from the notice, not from enforcement.  
5 A live rule that has quietly become dead without anybody noticing — because then nobody is shrugging. The organisation believes it is operating and the people believe it was never serious; neither side is lying, and the gap is where the incident comes from.  
6 That the honest version of *which rules do we believe are working?* can only be answered by somebody who does not already know the answer. It matters because every person inside the organisation does already know, which is what makes the audit useless.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**repealed** — formally cancelled, by the same kind of act that created it · **accumulate** — build up without anything removing them · **asymmetry** — the imbalance between the cost of making and unmaking · **cull** — a deliberate clearing-out · **produced** (at a moment of crisis) — brought out and relied on · **operating** (the rule is operating) — in force and having an effect, as against merely existing

**5E** · The counter-text
1 Nine.  
2 Issued March 2019, by "The Management" — which names nobody.  
3 **Rule 2** (keys returned by 18:00) is the only one with a consequence: a replacement is charged. The other eight have a dash in the *if breached* column.  
4 Rule 1: fire doors must not be propped open, since 2019 — and the *if breached* column is empty. The notice says nothing at all will happen.  
5 The small print: "The Management reserves the right to vary, waive or not enforce any of these conditions at its discretion." A rule the issuer may decline to enforce at will is not a rule; it is a notice about a preference.

**6A** · Sort — how hidden is the doer?
**Completely hidden → almost named:**  
mistakes were made · steps have been taken · it was decided · it is understood · be brought about · be done away with · notionally · duly · hereby · retrospectively · is thought to be · is said to · is widely regarded as · it emerged that · it turned out that · supposedly · purportedly · ostensibly · reportedly · allegedly · apparently · unilaterally · nobody will say who.  
*The two to argue about: **nobody will say who** is at the named end because it concedes that a doer exists and is being withheld — which is more informative than any passive. **unilaterally** names no one and tells you there was exactly one of them.*

**6B** · Chunk completion
1 It was understood · 2 was done away · 3 Steps have been taken · 4 emerged · 5 unilaterally

**6C** · Register sort
**Official notice:** hereby · duly · retrospectively · notionally · be brought about. **Newspaper:** allegedly · reportedly · purportedly · ostensibly · is said to · is widely regarded as. **Speech only:** nobody will say who · it turned out that · be done away with. **In two:** it is understood · supposedly · apparently · is thought to be · it emerged that · mistakes were made *(notice and newspaper)*.

**6D** · Contrast Clinic — *by* against *with*, agent against instrument
> **1** with — an instrument.
> **2** by — an agent.
> **3** by — an agent.
> **4** by … using *(or* from*)* — the agent takes *by*, the template is a means and prefers *using* or *from*; *with* is possible and reads oddly.
> **5** by — an agent, in name only, which is the joke.
> **6** with … by — instrument first, agent second, which is the normal order when both appear.

**7C** · Make it mean something
The fast version is for a meeting: it is how the sentence is actually said, and slowing it down would sound like a correction. The fully articulated version is for a recorded line, a dictation, or any moment when somebody has to write the words down — because the lost /t/ in *enforced twice* is exactly where a transcript goes wrong.

## B1.1 · Unit 7 · Show Me Once

**0B** · Guess it
The one at the desk, with the leaver reading from a screen and the arriver holding nothing. In the other two handovers the arriver has the tool, the keyboard or the cloth in their hands. The one that will fail is the one where the knowledge is being described rather than done — which is the forty-page document in Part 5.

**1B** · Sort — cline of control
**Most teacher-controlled → most learner-controlled:** instruct · demonstrate · model · cue · prompt · stand back.  
**Where teaching stops:** most marks fall between *model* and *cue*, because a cue is the first move that requires the learner to produce something. A defensible alternative is between *cue* and *prompt* — a prompt supplies part of the answer, a cue only points at it. Compare marks; the argument is the task.

**2A** · Four days to hand over 🔊 Track 7.1
**1** A · **2** A · **3** C · **4** B · **5** C · **6** B
**Noticing** — the six sentences are keyed above.

**Gist** — the tension. She decides not to explain it, and lets the apprentice get it wrong once instead.

**Detail** — First time: she says *hold it like this* and does it herself, slowly, with the apprentice watching. Second time: she says almost nothing and puts her hands over the apprentice's. Third time: she says *go on then* and stands back, and does not touch the cloth at all.

**Inference** — to herself. Nobody else is in the room at that point, and the sentence is the instruction she is giving herself not to intervene.

**Attitude** — the second *got it*, which is shorter, lower and has no rise at the end. The first rises, which is the sound of somebody hoping they have got it.

**3D** · Use — controlled
1 to hold · 2 do · 3 use · 4 cutting · 5 to notice · 6 explaining

**3E** · Use — guided
1 She allowed me to use the machine. *(allow takes* to*; let takes the bare infinitive)*  
2 He forced us to stay late. *(force takes* to*; make takes the bare infinitive)*  
3 I watched her cutting it. *(the* -ing *makes it part of the action rather than all of it)*  
4 They wanted me to come back. *(want takes* to*, like tell — but tell can take a that clause and want cannot)*

**5C** · Close reading
1 Forty pages, and most of it is screenshots.  
2 That p.19 says "run the usual check" without saying what the usual check is; and that p.31 carries a note saying "ask Felix about the Tuesday thing".  
3 It was a note Felix wrote to himself. The Tuesday thing is that the figures come in late on Tuesdays, so the reconciliation is run on Wednesday.  
4 It has made the handover describe the parts he still notices and omit the parts he does not — which are exactly the parts six years of practice have made automatic, and therefore the parts a new person needs.  
5 That a gap of more than two per cent can be caused by a payment landing between the two pulls, in which case you wait and run it again on Monday. It was not in the document because Felix did not know it — he had been doing it without noticing.  
6 On the grounds that the forty pages were the only reason Ruth knew p.19 was where to put the missing paragraph. The document could not contain the knowledge, and it could carry the structure the knowledge belongs in.

**5D** · Vocabulary in context
**reconciliation** — checking two records of the same thing against each other · **pull** (between the two pulls) — one extraction of data from a system · **land** (a payment lands) — arrive and register, at a moment nobody controls · **the usual check** — the phrase that marks knowledge the writer has stopped seeing · **screenshots** — pictures of a screen, which carry steps and not reasons · **source file** — the original record the two totals are reconciled against

**5E** · The counter-text
1 "Run the usual check." Nothing else.  
2 What the usual check is (compare the two totals); the threshold (more than about two per cent); and what to do when the source file is fine (a payment landed between the two pulls — wait and run it again on Monday).  
3 Felix could have written the first two: he knows them and simply did not notice he knew. He could **not** have written the third, because he did not know it — Ruth got it from Priya.  
4 The two per cent threshold.  
5 "Run the usual check." It is an instruction only to somebody who already knows what it means, which is the definition of a handover that cannot hand anything over.

**6A** · Sort — which pattern?
> **+ to** — want someone to · teach someone to · enable someone to · advise someone to · remind someone to · persuade someone to · expect someone to · allow someone to · invite someone to · help someone to · ask someone to · encourage someone to · force someone to · tell someone to · urge someone to
> **+ of / on / for -ing** — accuse someone of · congratulate someone on · warn someone about · blame someone for · warn someone against
> **+ from -ing** — stop someone from · prevent someone from
*help someone to* also works without the *to* (*help her carry it*), which is the one verb on this list that takes both. *warn* appears twice because it takes three patterns, which is what 6C is about.

**6B** · Chunk completion
1 warned … against · 2 blamed … for · 3 congratulated … on · 4 prevents / stops … from · 5 enabled … to

**6C** · The three warnings
**warn someone to:** *She warned me to keep my hand clear of the needle.* — an instruction. **warn someone against:** *She warned me against using the big scissors on the first day.* — do not do it. **warn someone about:** *She warned me about the tension on that machine.* — information, with no instruction attached, and the listener decides what to do with it.

**6D** · Contrast Clinic — *to*, bare, and *-ing*
> **1** fit — bare infinitive: the whole action, start to finish.
> **2** sign — *make* takes the bare infinitive.
> **3** fitting — *-ing*: part of the action, caught in progress.
> **4** to sign — *force* takes *to*.
> **5** to go — *allow* takes *to*.
> **6** go — *let* takes the bare infinitive.
**1 / 3:** the bare infinitive says he saw all of it; the *-ing* says he saw some of it on his way past. **5 / 6:** the grammar differs and the register does too — *allow* is institutional and implies a rule, *let* is personal and implies a decision by one person.

**7C** · Make it mean something
Stress on **go** means *do it again* — the stress falls on the verb of repetition. Stress on **over** means *check it* — the particle carries the meaning, as it does in most phrasal verbs. Two jobs, one phrase, and in a handover the difference is an afternoon.
