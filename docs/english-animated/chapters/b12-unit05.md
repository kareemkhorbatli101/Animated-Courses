### Finished by then, or still going then

### 3A · Notice

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_b12_u05_p03_v09` · V9 Grammar Visual · timeline**

### 3C · Know

| | Shape | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **future continuous** | *will be* + *-ing* | what will be **going on** at a future point |
| **future perfect** | *will have* + past participle | what will be **finished** by a future point |
| **future perfect continuous** | *will have been* + *-ing* | how **long** something will have been going by then |

> **The deadline word is *by*.** *by March*, *by the end of the year*, *by then*.
> *By* means at or before. *Until* means up to and not after, and they are not interchangeable.
> ✓ *We'll have finished by March.* ✗ *We'll have finished until March.*

> ***by the time* takes a present tense**, like all time clauses.
> ✓ *By the time the ward **opens**, the list will have doubled.*
> ✗ *By the time the ward will open…*

> **The future perfect is the tense of plans and audits.** It is how you describe a state of the
> world at a date, which is exactly what a projection is.

> **Watch out.** State verbs still resist the continuous.
> ✓ *By then we will know.* ✗ *By then we will be knowing.*

## Part 5 · Reading ◆ **A · THE WORLD**

### 5A · Predict

An opinion column. The title is
**"A plan that is refused is not a failure. A plan that is approved and wrong is."**
**What do you expect the argument to be?** Two sentences.

### 5B · Read — two minutes

---

**A plan that is refused is not a failure. A plan that is approved and wrong is**

Every public body I have worked in has had the same quiet arrangement, and nobody has ever
written it down because writing it down would end it.

A department produces a three-year plan. The plan contains an assumption about demand. The
assumption is chosen not because anybody believes it but because it is the number that produces a
plan which can be approved. If you put the real number in, the plan asks for money that does not
exist, and a plan that asks for money that does not exist is returned for revision, which costs
four months.

So the department puts in four per cent. Demand rises nine. In year two somebody notices, and
what they notice is not that the assumption was wrong — everybody knew the assumption was wrong —
but that the department has now been running on an approved plan for two years and has no
document anywhere that says what it actually needs.

That is the real cost, and it is not financial. It is that at the end of three years there will
be no record of the gap. There will be a plan that was met, a service that is failing, and no
piece of paper connecting the two.

I am not arguing that departments should submit plans that will be refused. I am arguing that
they should submit the four per cent plan **and** a one-page annex saying what the nine per cent
world looks like, and that they should do this every year whether anybody reads it or not,
because in four years somebody will ask when this started and somebody ought to be able to
answer.

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# Plan honestly, or plan approvably

The three-year plan is due in six weeks. The model assumes four per cent. Demand has risen nine
per cent for two years.

- **Submit at four per cent.** It will be approved. The department will be running on a plan
  that nobody in it believes, for the third year.
- **Submit at nine per cent.** It will be returned for revision, which costs four months, and
  the department will spend those four months on the current plan anyway.
- **Submit at four with a nine per cent annex.** It will be approved and the annex will probably
  not be read, and it will exist.

### 11A · The evidence

> **FIGURE `fig_b12_u05_p11_v06` · V6 Data Visual**

1. **The model and the actuals** from Part 5E — and the footnote on the growth cell.
2. **The chart** — modelled and actual demand, and the point at which they separate.
3. **The voice.** The finance lead, thirty seconds: *"I have submitted the four per cent plan for
   six years. I have never believed it and I have never been criticised for it. If I submit nine
   I will be told to revise it, and when I resubmit at four, which I will, the only thing that
   will have changed is that it will be August."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In fours. Everybody speaks twice. Use the frame.

> *By year three we will have ______ , which assumes ______ . If instead ______ , then ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *projection · forecast · target · assumption · baseline · variance ·
capacity · demand · shortfall · on current trends · best case · worst case*

### 11C · Decide

Write **three sentences**. Choose, say why, and name what it costs.

> I think they should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .
> What this costs is ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> Submit at four with the annex, and ask for the annex to be minuted rather than attached.
> An attachment can be lost and a minute cannot. The column is right that the real damage is the
> absence of a record, and the cheapest possible record is one sentence in the minutes of a
> meeting that is going to happen anyway. It costs nothing, it will change nothing this year, and
> in four years it will be the only evidence that anybody knew.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** The plan went in at four per cent with a one-page annex. The finance lead
> asked for the annex to be minuted and it was, in eleven words.
>
> The plan was approved in April. Demand in that year rose 8.4 per cent. The annex was not
> discussed at any meeting.
>
> In the third year the regional office began a review of why four hospitals had missed the same
> target. One of its first requests was for any documentation showing that departments had
> identified the risk in advance. Three hospitals had nothing. This one had eleven words in a
> minute, and the review's final report quotes them.

**Nobody read the annex. The eleven words in the minute did the whole job, three years later.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say what the other three hospitals should do now.
