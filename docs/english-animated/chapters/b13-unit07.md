### Reporting without taking responsibility for it

### 3A · Notice

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_b13_u07_p03_v09` · V9 Grammar Visual · mirror**

### 3C · Know

| Shape | Example | Where the claimer went |
|---|---|---|
| ***It* + passive + *that*** | *It is said that he left.* | deleted, and the claim is foregrounded |
| **subject + passive + *to*** | *He is said to have left.* | deleted, and the person is foregrounded |
| **verbs that do this** | *say · think · believe · understand · allege · report · expect* | |
| **present** | *is thought to be* | now |
| **past** | *is thought to have been* | then — and the reporting is still now |

> **Two shapes, one meaning, different first words.** *It is thought that he left* and *He is
> thought to have left* report the same claim. The second one puts the person in the subject
> slot, which is why newspapers prefer it and why it does more damage.

> ***to have* + past participle moves the claim backwards, not the reporting.**
> *is said to be* = he is, now. *is said to have been* = he was, then. The *is said* stays in
> the present in both, because the saying is happening now.

> **The claimer is deleted, and that is the point.** These structures exist so that a claim can
> be printed when nobody will attach their name to it. **Reporting responsibly means putting the
> name back when you have one.**

> **Why it matters.** This is the grammar of every sentence you will ever read about something
> nobody has proved. Learn to see it and you can read a newspaper twice.

## Part 5 · Reading ◆ **A · THE WORLD**

### 5A · Predict

A review, titled **"Four Weeks, at the Malthouse."**
**What do you expect the reviewer to say about the ending?** Two sentences.

### 5B · Read — two minutes and a quarter

---

**FOUR WEEKS**
*Malthouse Theatre · a review*

*Four Weeks* is built entirely from documents. There is no invented dialogue in it, which the
programme tells you twice, and which you stop noticing about ten minutes in, and then notice
again very sharply at the end.

The subject is a school teacher in a regional town, about whom something began to circulate in
2017. The play never tells us what. This is its one structural decision and everything else
follows from it: we hear eleven people describe a claim none of them can state, in the form
*it is said that*, *apparently*, *I was told*, and, nine times across ninety minutes, *there is
no smoke without fire*.

What the documents establish is this. No complaint was ever made. The teacher was moved to a
non-teaching role for what the letter calls *a period of review*, which lasted four weeks and
was then extended twice. He resigned in the eleventh week. The claim was never put to him in
writing, because there was nothing to put, and the play's most uncomfortable scene is a
nineteen-minute reconstruction of a meeting in which three senior people try to find a form of
words for a thing none of them has heard first-hand.

The second act is the town. It is also the weakest part, because the writer cannot resist the
chorus, and a chorus implies a collective that in a rumour does not exist — what exists is two
hundred separate conversations, each of which thought it was the only one.

The ending is a projection of the complaints register for that year. It is four lines long. It
is on screen for ninety seconds, which is far too long and exactly right, and the man's name is
not on it.

Go. Then notice, on the way out, how much of what you now believe about him came from a play
that was careful to tell you nothing.

### 6C · Three that are not neutral at all

**supposedly · ostensibly · purportedly**

> All three signal that the writer does not believe it. *Ostensibly* adds that the stated
> reason is a cover for another one. **Using any of them is taking a position**, which is
> exactly what the other six do not do.

**Write three sentences about the play in Part 5, one with each.**

### 6D · Two verbs for what a claim does

> **p** **put about** · **p** **stand up**

> *A story was put about that he had been asked to leave.*
> *None of it stood up.*

**Put about** names a deliberate act with no named actor. **Stand up** is what a claim does
under checking, and it is almost always negative: *did not stand up*.

**Rewrite two sentences from Part 5B using them.**

> **f** **None of which has been established.**

## Part 8 · Writing ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

### A report of an unverified claim · 190 words

> **FIGURE `fig_b13_u07_p08_v02` · V2 Cutaway**

### 8A · Read the model

> It is said in the department that the appointment was rushed, and that the person appointed
> was asked to leave a previous post. `[the claim, reported, with no owner — because there is no
> owner]`
>
> None of which has been established. No complaint has been made, no date has been given, and
> no person has yet repeated it to me who heard it first-hand. `[the status, in four flat
> clauses, before anything else]`
>
> What is a matter of record is that the appointment panel had three members rather than the
> usual four, and that the fourth withdrew eight days before, in writing, citing workload.
> `[the checkable fact, which is smaller and duller than the rumour and is the only solid thing
> here]`
>
> Three weeks of this have cost one person their teaching allocation for the semester and have
> cost the department nothing, which is the asymmetry that makes rumours efficient.
> `[the harm, named, with the asymmetry stated]`

**Questions about the writer's choices:**

1. The status comes before the evidence. **Why not after?**
2. The checkable fact is "smaller and duller". **Why include it at all?**
3. The last paragraph names an asymmetry. **What would the report be without it?**

### 8B · The anti-model

> There have been some concerns raised about the recent appointment and a number of people have
> expressed disquiet. While nothing has been proven, it is felt that the matter should be looked
> into, and it is understood that steps are being taken. Further information will be provided
> in due course.

Correct English, and it repeats the claim while appearing not to. **Find the four structures
that have deleted a person**, and say what *it is felt* achieves that *I think* would not.

### 8C · Plan

> the claim, reported → the status, flat and early → what is a matter of record →
> the harm, and who carries it

### 8D · Write — 190 words
### 8E · Peer review — three criteria

> ☐ Is the status of the claim given before any detail of it?
> ☐ Is every checkable fact separated from every reported one?
> ☐ Is the harm named, with the person who carries it?

### 8F · Write it again · 8G · Publish

**Do not publish this one on the wall.** Hand it to one person and ask them one question: would
you be comfortable if the subject read it?

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# Three weeks, three versions, no complaint

Something has been going round for three weeks. No complaint has been made. The person has
already lost a teaching allocation because a timetable was quietly rearranged. The head of
department has to do something or decide not to.

- **Investigate.** Open a Stage 1 concern, interview the people who have repeated it, and make
  it a matter of record. It becomes real, with a file, and a file does not close.
- **Do nothing formal.** Rumours die down. This one has no source and no complainant, and
  opening a process creates a document where there is currently only talk.
- **Go to the person.** Tell them exactly what is being said, in writing, today — before any
  process, so they are not the last to know and can decide for themselves what happens next.

### 11A · The evidence

> **FIGURE `fig_b13_u07_p11_v06` · V6 Data Visual**

1. **The three versions and the record** from Part 5E — and the detail that is demonstrably
   false.
2. **The chart** — how the three versions spread, week by week, and how many people heard each
   one first-hand.
3. **The voice.** The person concerned, thirty seconds: *"I found out in week three from a
   student. Not a colleague — a student, who was embarrassed. I would have preferred almost any
   process to that, including one that found against me, because at least I would have been in
   the room."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In fours. Everybody speaks twice. Use the frame.

> *It is ______ that ______ , none of which ______ . What is a matter of record is ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *rumour · hearsay · allegation · corroboration · anonymity · due
process · harm · unverified · credible · substantiate*

### 11C · Decide

Write **three sentences**. Choose, say why, and name what it costs.

> I think the head of department should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .
> What this costs is ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> Go to the person, today, in writing, before anything else. Every version of this problem that
> begins with an investigation ends with the subject learning about it from somebody who is
> enjoying it. Telling them first costs the department its control of the timing and gives the
> person the one thing nobody in a rumour ever has, which is the ability to ask a question of
> somebody who has to answer. The investigation may still follow. It should not come first.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** She went to him on the Thursday with a one-page note listing the three
> versions in the words she had heard them. He read it, asked for an hour, and came back with a
> request: open the Stage 1 himself, as the person concerned, so that the file would say he had
> asked for it.
>
> The process took six weeks and found nothing, because there was nothing. One of the three
> versions was traced to a confusion with a different person of the same surname at a different
> university, which had been corrected in an email in week one — an email that nine people
> received and none of them forwarded.
>
> He stayed two more years and then left, for a job he had applied for before any of this.
> Asked afterwards what had helped, he named the note, and said the thing that still bothered
> him was the nine people, none of whom had done anything wrong, and all of whom had read the
> correction.

**Nobody lied, nobody acted maliciously, and a correction that nine people read reached nobody.
The whole thing cost one person a semester and the department a sentence in a file.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say what should have happened in week one.
