### Saying how many, exactly

### 3A · Notice

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_a22_u09_p03_v09` · V9 Grammar Visual · ladder**

Now look at the second picture. **One bar, cut six ways.
Which cut is *most*, and which is *several*?**

> **FIGURE `fig_a22_u09_p03_v09b` · V9 Grammar Visual · scope**

### 3C · Know

| | How many | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **all** | 100 % | *all **the** claims* · *all **of** them* |
| **most** | more than half | *most **of** them* |
| **half / several / a couple of** | in between | *half **of** it* · *several **of** them* |
| **both** | exactly 2 | *both **of** them* — only two |
| **either** | one of 2 | *either **of** them*, singular verb |
| **neither** | 0 of 2 | *neither **of** them* **is** — singular verb |
| **none** | 0 of any number | *none **of** it* / *none **of** them* |

> **The *of* rule.** Before a pronoun, *of* is compulsory: *all **of** them*, never *all them*.
> Before *the* or *my*, *of* is optional: *all (of) the claims*.
> Before a bare plural, there is no *of*: *all claims are honest* — and that is a general
> statement about every claim in the world.

> **Countable or not.** *none of them* (the claims) · *none of it* (the money).
> *few* and *several* go with countables. *little* and *much* go with uncountables.

> **Watch out.** *both*, *either* and *neither* are only ever about **two**.
> ✗ *both of the four claims* ✓ *all four claims*

## Part 5 · Reading ◆ **A · THE WORLD**

### 5A · Predict

An opinion column. The title is **"Everybody wants a fair share and nobody means the same thing."**
**What three meanings of *fair* can you think of before you read?**

### 5B · Read — one hundred and ten seconds

---

**Everybody wants a fair share and nobody means the same thing**

There is €180,000. There are four claims, totalling €246,000. All four are honest. That is the
whole situation and it has no solution, only decisions.

When people in that room say *fair*, they mean one of four things, and the argument is not about
the money. It is about which of the four they are using, and almost nobody says which.

**Equal.** Forty-five thousand each. Clean, defensible, and it leaves two of them with less than
half of what they need, which means neither of those two can start work at all. Equal shares of
an insufficient sum can be the least useful way of spending it.

**Proportional.** Each gets 73 per cent of what they asked for. Slightly worse than equal for the
small claims and slightly better for the large ones, and it rewards whoever estimated most
optimistically in March.

**By need.** The two with no insurance get everything. This is the one most people privately
believe in and the one nobody proposes out loud, because it requires saying that the other two
matter less.

**First come.** It has already partly happened. Two of the four submitted in March and have been
quietly assumed into the budget ever since.

Pick one and defend it. Picking none of them and splitting the difference is also a choice, and
it is the only one that nobody will later be able to explain.

---

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# One hundred and eighty thousand

Four claims. €238,500 asked for. €180,000 available. All four honest.

- **Equal.** €45,000 each. Two of the four cannot start work with that, so two of the four
  projects do not happen and the money sits.
- **By need.** The two with no insurance get everything they asked for; the two who are insured
  get nothing and wait for their insurers.
- **Proportional.** Everybody gets about 75 per cent. Everybody can start; nobody can finish.

### 11A · The evidence

1. **The allocation sheet** from Part 5E — and the claim that was quietly reduced.
2. **The chart** — what each claim buys at 45, at 75 and at 100 per cent.
3. **The voice.** One of the insured claimants, twenty seconds: *"I'd take nothing. I want that
   on the record because I will be saying it in the room and I do not want anybody saying
   afterwards that I was pushed. But I'd want it written down that I took nothing, because my
   insurer is going to ask me in March whether I was compensated."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In threes. Everybody says one. Use the frame.

> *I think the money should go ______ , because ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *fair · equal · priority · need · shortage · limited · a fair share · go round*

### 11C · Decide

Write **two sentences**. Choose, and say why.

> I think the money should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> By need, and the committee should say the word *need* out loud and vote on it by name. Equal
> shares of an insufficient sum produce four stalled projects, and proportional shares produce
> four half-finished ones; need produces two finished things and two angry people, and angry
> people with insurance have somewhere else to go. The important part is not the choice. It is
> that whoever is refused is refused in a sentence that can be repeated.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** They went by need. The vote was 6–3 and took two hours, most of which was
> spent on whether *need* could be defined without insulting anybody.
>
> The insured claimant who offered to take nothing did take nothing, and asked for the letter.
> His insurer settled in April, and the letter is the reason: it proved he had not been
> compensated twice.
>
> €4,100 was left over because one of the two funded claims came in under estimate. It was not
> carried over. It went back to the county in December and the committee has been asked about it
> at every meeting since.

**Two things were finished. One man got his insurance because he had asked for a letter. And
four thousand pounds went back.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say what should have been done with the €4,100.
