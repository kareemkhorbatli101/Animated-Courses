### 3C · Know

| First verb | Then | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **decide, plan, hope, offer, promise, refuse, manage, afford, learn, agree** | **to** + verb | *I've **decided to ask** them.* |
| **enjoy, avoid, mind, suggest, finish, keep, give up, practise** | verb + **-ing** | *I **don't mind asking** them.* |
| **like, love, hate, start, begin, continue** | **either**, with almost no change | *I **like cooking** / I **like to cook**.* |

> **The useful half-rule.** Verbs about a **future decision** usually take **to**:
> *decide to, plan to, hope to, promise to, refuse to*.
> Verbs about an **experience you are already having** usually take **-ing**:
> *enjoy -ing, avoid -ing, mind -ing, keep -ing*.
> It is a half-rule. It covers most of the list and you will still have to learn the rest.

> **After a preposition, always -ing.** *think **about buying*** · *good **at counting*** ·
> *instead **of waiting***

> **Watch out.** ✗ *I enjoy to cook.* ✓ *I enjoy cooking.*
> ✗ *We can't afford doing both.* ✓ *We can't afford to do both.*

## Part 5 · Reading ◆ **A · THE WORLD**

### 5A · Predict

An opinion column. The title is **"The cheapest thing in the shop is never the cheapest."**
**What do you expect the argument to be?** One sentence.

### 5B · Read — one hundred seconds

---

**The cheapest thing in the shop is never the cheapest**

I bought a kettle for nineteen ringgit. It lasted five months. Then I bought one for ninety and I
have had it eleven years, which means the expensive kettle has cost me about eight ringgit a year
and the cheap one cost me forty-five.

Everybody knows this argument. Almost nobody acts on it, and I think I know why. The expensive
kettle asks you for ninety ringgit **today**. The cheap one asks for nineteen today and the rest
later, in instalments you do not notice because they arrive as inconvenience rather than as bills.

So the real question is not *what does it cost* but *what does it cost per year, and who pays the
difference*. If you have ninety ringgit, the expensive kettle is obviously right. If you have
thirty, the argument is not just wrong, it is insulting — you cannot buy the cheap-in-the-long-run
option with money you do not have.

Which is why I distrust this advice when it is given by people who have never had to choose.
The arithmetic is correct. It is also a description of how much easier everything is when you can
pay in advance.

---

### 5C · Answer

1. What did each kettle cost, and how long did each last?
2. What is the cost per year of each one?
3. **Why does the writer say almost nobody acts on the argument?**
4. What does the writer mean by *"instalments you do not notice"*?
5. In what situation does the writer say the argument is insulting?
6. What is the writer's final point about who gives this advice?

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# One machine, or one system

The stall has 2,600 saved. There is enough for one of these.

- **The chiller.** 2,400 new, 900 second-hand. Siti says they throw away about 180 a week in the
  hot months.
- **The phone-order system.** 2,350 for the first year. Hafiz says four nearby stalls have one
  and take orders the night before.
- **Neither, yet.** Wait six months. Measure the waste properly. Decide in January.

### 11A · The evidence

1. **The two quotations** from Part 5E — and the line the seller added in pen.
2. **The chart** — produce thrown away each week, over fourteen months.
3. **The voice.** Siti, twenty seconds: *"I've been throwing vegetables away for thirty-one
   years. I don't need a chart to tell me what that costs. I would like to know why I never
   counted it."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In threes. Everybody says one. Use the frame.

> *I think they should spend it on ______ , because ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *waste · worth · afford · save · value for money · second-hand · bargain*

### 11C · Decide

Write **two sentences**. Choose, and say why.

> I think they should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> They should buy the second-hand chiller for 900 and spend four months counting what they throw
> away, properly, on paper. That leaves 1,700 and an actual number. If the waste turns out to be
> 180 a week, the chiller pays for itself before Christmas and they can argue about the phone
> system with something other than opinions.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** They bought the second-hand chiller. The waste fell from about 180 a week
> to 40, which is more than anybody expected, and the machine paid for itself in eleven weeks.
>
> In March the compressor failed. The repair was 740 — nearly as much again — and the warranty
> covered the motor but not the compressor, which is the line the seller had added in pen and
> nobody had read.
>
> Hafiz set up phone orders himself, on a free messaging group, badly, and it now brings in about
> a tenth of what the paid system promised and costs nothing.

**The chiller worked, and then it broke. The cheap version of the other idea worked too.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say what they should do about the compressor.
