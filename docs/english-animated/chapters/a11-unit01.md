## Part 3 · Grammar Lab 1 ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### *am · is · are* — and who they belong to

### 3A · Notice

Look at the four sentences in Part 2 again.

1. Which word goes with **I**?
2. Which word goes with **she**, **he** and **it**?
3. Which word goes with **you**, **we** and **they**?
4. One of these three groups has **three** words in it. Which?

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_a11_u01_p03_v09a` · V9 Grammar Visual**
> Three columns, colour-coded and shaped. **One figure** stands alone over *am*. **One figure**
> stands alone over *is*, with a chair and a cat beside it (he · she · it). **Groups of figures**
> stand over *are* (you · we · they). The shapes carry the rule before the table does: alone and
> speaking = *am*; alone and spoken about = *is*; more than one, or the person in front of you =
> *are*. *Animation:* BUILD — the learner drags each pronoun to a column before the answer shows.

### 3C · Know

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | **am** | I am a student. |
| **he · she · it** | **is** | She is a teacher. |
| **you · we · they** | **are** | They are in room 3. |

**Short forms — what people actually say.**

> I'm · you're · he's · she's · it's · we're · they're

> **Watch out.** *You* is one person **and** more than one person. It is always **are**.
> *You are my teacher.* *You are my class.*

> **Why it matters.** This is the most common verb in English. You will say it a hundred times
> today.

> **FIGURE `fig_a11_u01_p03_v09b` · V9 Grammar Visual**
> A second, smaller visual for **negatives and questions**: the same three columns with *not*
> dropping in after the verb, and the verb and the pronoun physically swapping places to make a
> question. The swap is animated. *Animation:* MORPH.

| | |
|---|---|
| **–** | I **am not** / I'm not · she **is not** / she isn't · they **are not** / they aren't |
| **?** | **Am** I…? · **Is** she…? · **Are** you…? |

## Part 4 · Speaking ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

### 4A · Fluency starter — the circle

Stand in a circle. Say **three things** about yourself. Ten seconds.
Move one place to the left. Say the **same** three things to a new person. Eight seconds.

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# Yusuf, or Joseph?

Yusuf is from Cairo. His name is **Yusuf**.

At the café next door, Marco calls him **Joseph**. In the first week, Yusuf corrected him twice.
Marco said sorry twice. Now it is week three and Marco still says **Joseph**, and he says it in a
friendly way, every morning, in front of other people.

Yusuf has two choices. Neither one is free.

### 11A · The evidence

> **FIGURE `fig_a11_u01_p11_v04b` · V4 Comparison Pair**
> Two mornings at the café counter, drawn identically. **Left:** Yusuf says nothing, takes the
> coffee, Marco is smiling, Yusuf is not quite. **Right:** Yusuf corrects him, Marco's face has
> changed, there are two people behind in the queue and one of them is looking. **Eight planted
> differences**, including what Yusuf is doing with his hands.

🔊 **Track 1.6.** Lin, 25 seconds: *"I changed mine in my first month here. It was easier. Now
nobody at work knows my real name and I don't know how to start."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In threes. Use the frame. Everybody says one.

> *I think Yusuf should ______ , because ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *easy · difficult · polite · his name · every day · in front of people ·
friendly · tired*

### 11C · Decide

Write **two sentences**. Choose, and say why.

> I think Yusuf should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> I think Yusuf should say it one more time, but differently. Two corrections did not work, and
> Marco is not unkind — he is friendly, and that makes it harder. So: say it when nobody is
> waiting, and write it down. People remember a name they have seen.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** Yusuf wrote his name on a napkin and gave it to Marco on a quiet afternoon.
> Marco kept it. He now says **Yusuf**, and he says it slightly too carefully, which Yusuf finds
> funny.
>
> Lin never went back to her name. She says she does not mind. **She also says she minds.**

**One question. You do not have to answer it out loud.**

> Yusuf's reasoning was good **and** it worked. Would it still have been good reasoning if Marco
> had not kept the napkin?
