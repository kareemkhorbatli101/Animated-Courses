### If it happens, this happens

### 3A · Notice

> **FIGURE `fig_a21_u05_p03_v09b` · V9 Grammar Visual · branch**

### 3C · Know

| | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **first conditional** | *if* + present , *will* + verb | *If the van **comes** at six, I **'ll open** at half past.* |
| **unless** | = *if … not* | *I **won't open unless** the fish is here by five.* |
| **time clauses** | *as soon as / until / by the time / whenever* + **present** | *I'll start **as soon as** it **arrives**.* |

> **The rule under all three.** After *if*, *unless*, *when*, *as soon as*, *until* and
> *by the time*, English uses the **present**, even though you are talking about the future.

> **Watch out.** ✗ *If it will rain…* ✓ *If it rains…*
> **unless** is already negative. ✗ *unless it doesn't rain* for *if it rains*.

> **Why it matters.** A plan with no *if* in it is not a plan. It is a hope.

## Part 5 · Reading ◆ **A · THE WORLD**

### 5A · Predict

A review of a travel app. The title is **"It told me I would make it."**
**What do you expect the reviewer to be angry about?** One sentence.

### 5B · Read — one hundred seconds

---

**It told me I would make it ★★☆☆☆**

The app does one thing well. If you put in where you are and where you want to be, it gives you a
route, a fare and a time, and the time is usually right.

The problem is what it does not do. It will not tell you what happens if any part of that route
fails. I used it for a 05.40 connection at Sentral. It said I would arrive at 05.22. It did not
say that the 05.22 is the only bus that makes that connection, and that if it runs four minutes
late — which it did, because of roadworks on Jalan Ipoh that have been there since February — the
next option is 07.10.

Eighteen minutes of margin, and no warning that all eighteen were in one place.

A better app would say: *you will make this unless the bus is more than three minutes down*. That
is one sentence. It is also the only sentence I needed.

Two stars, and one of them is for the fare calculator, which is honest about what a refund is
worth and how long it takes.

---

5. What one sentence does the writer say the app should have given her?
6. What is the second star for?

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# Two hours, every day

From the first of next month the market's delivery slot moves from four o'clock to six. The
restaurant buyers come between five and seven and they will not wait.

- **Accept it.** Start two hours later. Lose the restaurant trade, keep the costs where they are.
- **Pay for a private van.** Keep the four o'clock arrival. It costs 1,100 a month, shared between
  however many traders sign up.
- **Fight it.** The committee can object within fourteen days. It has never won an objection.

### 11A · The evidence

1. **The timetable and the notice** from Part 5E — and the footnote.
2. **The chart** — what the market takes hour by hour, and who is buying.
3. **The voice.** Mr Tan, twenty seconds: *"If six traders sign, it's a hundred and eighty each
   and it is obviously worth it. If three sign, it's three hundred and sixty and it obviously
   isn't. I can't tell you which it is until people decide."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In threes. Everybody says one. Use the frame.

> *If the market ______ , then ______ . So I think they should ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *slot · restaurants · cost · share · refund · delay · in time · otherwise*

### 11C · Decide

Write **two sentences**. Choose, and say why.

> I think the market should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> They should take the private van for three months and make the committee object at the same
> time. The objection will probably fail, but it is free, and it is the only way of finding out
> whether the slot was ever negotiable. Paying for the van answers the question the objection
> cannot: whether the four o'clock trade is worth 1,100 a month, and nobody in that market
> actually knows.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** Nine traders signed, so it was 122 each. The van kept the four o'clock
> arrival and the restaurant trade held up.
>
> The objection failed in eleven days, as everybody expected, but the reply named a second slot
> at 05.15 that nobody had known existed, and four traders moved to it in the second month and
> stopped paying for the van.
>
> By month four, five traders were paying 220 each and two of them were thinking about stopping.

**The van worked. The objection, which failed, found the better answer.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say what the five who stayed should do now.
