### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_b13_u05_p03_v09` · V9 Grammar Visual · scope**

### 3C · Know

| | Shape | Spotlights |
|---|---|---|
| ***it*-cleft** | *it was X who / that…* | a noun — a person, a thing, a time |
| ***wh*-cleft** | *What I did was read them.* | an action, or a whole clause |
| **the one / the thing** | *The thing that worries me is…* | a noun, more conversationally |
| **the reason** | *The reason why he said nothing is…* | a cause |
| **all-cleft** | *All I did was read eleven essays.* | and it means *only that, nothing more* |

> **A cleft takes one sentence and makes it two clauses, so that one part can be made
> prominent.** *He failed the criterion* becomes *It was the criterion that he failed* — and
> now the sentence is about the criterion.

> **The *all*-cleft always carries a defence or a minimisation.** *All I did was ask* means
> *and look what happened*. Native speakers hear the implication even when it was not meant.

> **The cleft needs both halves.**
> ✗ *It was failed the criterion.* ✓ *It was the criterion that failed.*

> **Why it matters.** Word order in English is fixed; emphasis in speech is carried by stress.
> In writing, a cleft is how you make somebody read the stress you meant.

**THE QUIET ROOM**
*from a collection of short fiction*

For eleven weeks Vinh sat in the same chair, third from the window, and did not speak. He had a
reason, and the reason was not nerves.

He had worked out in the second week that the gap between a question and an answer in that room
was about four seconds. He had timed it, once, with the stopwatch on his phone, under the table.
Four seconds, and then Marguerite would be speaking. He needed eleven. He had measured that too,
writing his answer down and then reading it aloud at the speed people talked, in a stairwell,
twice.

So he wrote. He wrote the answers to every question that was asked and most of the ones that
were not, and at the end of each session he put the sheet in his bag and went to the library,
where the gap between a question and an answer was as long as he wanted it to be.

In week nine the tutor asked a question nobody answered. The silence went on. Vinh counted it:
six seconds, seven, nine. At eleven he opened his mouth, and at eleven and a half Marguerite,
who had been looking at her phone, said something that was almost but not quite what he had been
going to say.

He wrote that down too.

At the end of term the tutor returned the essays. His had a comment on the last page that said:
*This is the best piece of writing I have had from this group in four years. Why have I never
heard any of this?*

He read it twice. Then he took out the eleven sheets of paper from eleven weeks, squared the
edges, and put them on her desk on his way out, without saying anything, which was, he realised
on the stairs, exactly the problem she had just asked him about.

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# A mark that is due on Friday

Participation is fifteen per cent. The criterion says *contributes regularly to seminar
discussion*. Thanh has spoken twice in eleven weeks and has the highest essay mark in the group.

- **Apply the rule.** 4 out of 15. It is defensible at audit, it is what the handbook says, and
  it drops his unit mark by seven points.
- **Override it.** 13 out of 15, with a written justification. The mark stands unless the audit
  pulls it, and an overridden mark is flagged for three years.
- **Mark the eleven sheets.** He wrote an answer to every question asked and never submitted
  them. Nothing in the criterion says contribution has to be spoken. Nothing says it can be
  written either.

### 11A · The evidence

> **FIGURE `fig_b13_u05_p11_v06` · V6 Data Visual**

1. **The mark sheet and the handbook page** from Part 5E — and the row with two marks in it.
2. **The chart** — essay mark against participation mark, for every student in the group.
3. **The voice.** Thanh, thirty seconds: *"I am not asking her to change the rule. I know she
   cannot. What I want is for somebody to write down that the rule and the thing it is supposed
   to measure came apart, because if nobody writes it down, next year there is another one of
   me and she starts from nothing."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In fours. Everybody speaks twice. Use the frame.

> *It was ______ that ______ . What ______ is ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *reveal · emphasis · focus · detail · withhold · pacing · payoff ·
the point is · what matters is · it turns out*

### 11C · Decide

Write **three sentences**. Choose, say why, and name what it costs.

> I think Priya should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .
> What this costs is ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> Mark the sheets, and write down why. The third option is the only one that does not require
> pretending: it does not pretend the rule is right and it does not pretend she has the power to
> rewrite it on a Friday. It also produces a document. An overridden mark disappears into an
> audit file; a marked set of eleven written contributions is a precedent with a date on it,
> and the next tutor in this position has something to point at.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** Priya marked the eleven sheets and gave 12 out of 15, with a note that the
> contributions were written because the format made spoken ones impossible for this student at
> this speed. The mark was not audited. Nobody queried it.
>
> In March the following year a different tutor, in a different department, cited it. She had
> heard about it from Priya at a teaching lunch, which is the only route by which anything in a
> university travels in under four years.
>
> The handbook sentence was finally changed two years later. The new version says *contributes
> to seminar discussion in a form agreed with the tutor*, and the person who drafted it has
> never met Thanh and does not know the sentence exists because of him.

**One mark, one note, and a sentence in a handbook that two thousand students will read and
nobody will trace back. It turns out the thing nobody saw was the eleven sheets of paper in
his bag, every week, for a whole semester.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say what Priya should have done in week three.
