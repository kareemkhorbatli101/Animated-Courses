## Part 3 · Grammar Lab 1 ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### Three ways of saying everybody

### 3A · Notice

Look at the four sentences in Part 2.

1. In sentence 1, how many patients?
2. In sentence 3, is there a particular patient?
3. **Sentences 1 and 3 both make a general claim. What is the difference in feel?**
4. Sentence 4 uses *the* with a singular. **Can that ever be generic? Try: *The patient is the
   last person consulted.***
5. Try *"The patients wait too long."* **Who exactly is waiting?**

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_b12_u08_p03_v09` · V9 Grammar Visual · scope**

### 3C · Know

| | Shape | Means |
|---|---|---|
| **bare plural** | *Patients wait too long.* | all of them, everywhere, as a class |
| **a / an + singular** | *A patient waits 41 days.* | any one, as a representative type |
| **the + singular** | *The patient is the last to be consulted.* | the category as an institution — formal |
| **the + plural** | *The patients waited 41 days.* | **a specific group**, already identified |

> **The trap is the fourth row.** *The patients* is never generic in ordinary English. It always
> means *those patients*, and if the reader does not already know which ones, the sentence is
> broken.

> **Determiners that narrow further:** *certain patients* (some, unspecified) ·
> *given patients* (specified, in a formula) · *such patients* (of the kind just described) ·
> *the respective departments* (each its own) · *the former / the latter* (of two just named).

> **The phrases that carry a definition:** *by definition · for the purposes of · in principle ·
> in practice · as a whole · in the main*.

> **Why it matters.** A report that says *the patients* without having said which ones is a
> report that four departments will read four ways, and all four will be reading in good faith.

### 3D · Use — controlled

> (1) __________ patients wait too long. That is a general claim and nobody disputes it.
> (2) __________ patients in this report waited 41 days on average — but
> (3) __________ report does not say which patients, and (4) __________ four departments
> reading it each have (5) __________ working definition of their own. For
> (6) __________ purposes of finance, a day case is an admission. In clinical coding it is not.
> (7) __________ latter definition produces a figure four hundred lower, and
> (8) __________ ambiguity has never been raised because nobody owns the report.

### 3E · Use — guided

Rewrite each one so it says what the writer means.

1. *The patients are waiting longer.* *(meaning: all patients, everywhere)*
2. *Patients waited 41 days.* *(meaning: the 1,340 in this report)*
3. *A patient is entitled to an interpreter.* *(meaning: every patient, as a rule)*
4. *The patient is not consulted.* *(meaning: the category, in a formal sentence)*
5. *Such patients are excluded.* *(meaning: the ones described in the previous sentence)*

### 8A · Read the model

> An admission, in this report, means any patient given a bed number, whether or not they stayed
> overnight. `[the definition, in the first sentence, and it names the test: a bed number]`
>
> That includes day cases, which most people would not call an admission, and about four hundred
> of them a month fall into this figure. It excludes patients seen and discharged from the
> emergency department without a bed number, of whom there are about nine hundred.
> `[one surprising inclusion and one surprising exclusion, both with numbers]`
>
> Clinical coding uses the national standard, which requires an overnight stay. Their figure for
> the same month is 1,340 lower. Neither definition is wrong.
> `[the other definition, the size of the gap, and a refusal to adjudicate]`
>
> If somebody quotes an admission figure at you, the only useful question is whether a day case
> is in it. `[what the reader should do, in one sentence]`

**Questions about the writer's choices:**

1. The definition names a physical test — a bed number. **Why is that better than a principle?**
2. Both the inclusion and the exclusion have numbers. **What would be lost without them?**
3. The last sentence is an instruction. **Who is it for?**

and say what *differing methodologies* is standing in for.

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# One figure, four readings

A report says *the patients waited an average of 41 days*. Four departments each read it as their
own population. Their figures range from 31 to 58.

- **Publish a definition.** One sentence at the top of the report. It would make the figure
  comparable and it would make this year's figure look worse than last year's, which was
  produced the other way.
- **Publish all four.** Honest, unusable, and the regional office will pick one.
- **Leave it.** Everybody carries on reading their own number, as they have since 2011, and the
  report continues to be quoted in four meetings a month.

### 11A · The evidence

> **FIGURE `fig_b12_u08_p11_v06` · V6 Data Visual**

1. **The report page and the four figures** from Part 5E — and the sticky note.
2. **The chart** — the four populations, their overlaps, and the four hundred people in dispute.
3. **The voice.** The information manager, thirty seconds: *"I was asked to write that note in
   2011. I did write it. I could not find anybody who would own it, because owning it would mean
   choosing, and the two people who would have had to agree had both correctly been told that
   their definition was the right one."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In fours. Everybody speaks twice. Use the frame.

> *By definition ______ . In practice ______ . So the figure means ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *category · definition · criterion · boundary · overlap · ambiguity ·
classification · in principle · for the purposes of · fall under*

### 11C · Decide

Write **three sentences**. Choose, say why, and name what it costs.

> I think the hospital should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .
> What this costs is ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> Publish the definition and publish last year's figure recalculated on the same basis, in the
> same table. The reason this has survived thirteen years is that fixing it makes somebody's
> number worse, and the only way to remove that cost is to restate the comparison at the same
> time. One sentence and one recalculated column turn an embarrassment into a correction, and a
> correction is a thing an organisation can survive.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** The definition went in, with the recalculated prior year beside it. The
> figure for this year rose from 41 to 47 days and last year's rose from 39 to 46, so the
> direction of travel was unchanged and the headline was *waiting times up by one day*.
>
> Three departments adopted the published definition within a quarter. Clinical coding did not,
> and could not, because the national standard binds them.
>
> The report is still quoted in four meetings a month. In two of them somebody now asks which
> definition, which the information manager describes as the entire return on thirteen years.

**One sentence and one column. Three of four departments. And somebody now asks the question in
half the meetings.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say what should be done about clinical coding.
