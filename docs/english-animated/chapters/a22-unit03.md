## Part 3 · Grammar Lab 1 ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### The past before the past

### 3A · Notice

Look at the four sentences in Part 2.

1. Sentence 1 and sentence 2 are about the same forecast. **What does sentence 2 add?**
2. In sentence 3, which happened first — arriving, or starting?
3. In sentence 4, could you use a plain past? **Would anything be lost?**
4. **When do you actually need the past perfect?** Try: *First he moved the car. Then it flooded.*
5. Try *"By the time we got there, it already started."* **What is missing?**

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_a22_u03_p03_v09` · V9 Grammar Visual**

Now look at the second picture. **Two events, one line. Which one gets the *had*?**

> **FIGURE `fig_a22_u03_p03_v09b` · V9 Grammar Visual · mirror**

### 3C · Know

| | Shape | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **past simple** | *moved, changed, decided* | events in the order they happened |
| **past perfect** | *had* + past participle | an event **before** the one you are already talking about |

> **You do not need it when the order is already clear.**
> ✓ *He moved the car. Then it flooded.* — the order is the sentence order.
> **You do need it when you go backwards.**
> ✓ *It flooded. He **had moved** the car two days earlier.*

> **The words that invite it:** *by the time · already · before · until · once · previously ·
> the night before · up to that point*

> **Watch out.** *had* is the auxiliary for everybody. ✗ *he hadded* ✓ *he had moved*
> And in speech it is almost never a full word: *it'd already started*.

> **Why it matters.** An inquiry is not asking what happened. It is asking **what had already
> happened when each person decided.**

### 3D · Use — controlled

> By the time the hall opened, the water (1) __________ (already / reach) the doors. Donal
> (2) __________ (move) his car on the Thursday, two days before, because the forecast
> (3) __________ (change) that morning. Eimear (4) __________ (decide) against the sandbags
> after he (5) __________ (move) it, and she says now that she
> (6) __________ (not know) the forecast (7) __________ (change) at all.

### 3E · Use — guided

Join each pair, putting the **earlier** event in the past perfect.

1. The forecast changed. / He moved the car.
2. The drain reversed. / Anybody noticed the tide.
3. She put the sandbags in the shed in 2019. / The water came in January.
4. The hall opened. / The first family arrived.
5. He warned four people. / Nobody acted.

### 3F · Use — open

**SUBMISSION 14 — D. Hegarty, 9 Strand Road**

I moved my car to the top road at about four on the Thursday afternoon. I did it because the
forecast had changed that morning — the surge had gone from 1.1 metres to 1.6 — and because the
yard drain had backed up twice in November.

I told four people. I am not going to say which four. Two of them laughed, and I want to be clear
that they laughed kindly and that I would have laughed too.

I am not submitting this to blame anybody. I am submitting it because the inquiry has asked
whether anybody was warned, and the honest answer is that four people were warned by me and at
least eleven were not warned by anybody, and the reason is that I did not want to be the man
knocking on doors about a forecast.

---

**SUBMISSION 15 — E. Nolan, 14 Strand Road**

I was one of the four.

I did not laugh. I thought about the sandbags and decided against them, and I decided against
them partly because of how the other two had reacted.

I want to record that Donal Hegarty was right and was treated as though he were being silly, and
that I joined in by saying nothing. That is the part I would like in the record, and I understand
that it is not evidence of anything.

I would also like to say that if he had knocked on eleven doors, I do not think it would have
made any difference, and I am saying that because I do not want this to become a story about one
man who should have done more.

### 6C · Two that look the same

**before** and **by the time** both put one thing earlier. They are not interchangeable.

> *He moved it **before** the forecast changed.* — one thing, then the other.
> ***By the time** the forecast changed, he had moved it.* — the deadline, and it was already met.

**Rewrite these three with *by the time*.**

1. He had already left when she phoned.
2. The hall was full when the last family arrived.

### 8A · Read the model

> At about ten the drain in my yard started running the wrong way. By then the tide had been
> rising for two hours and the forecast had been revised twice, though I did not know that until
> March. `[the event, then straight backwards — and one thing marked as learned afterwards]`
>
> I had kept sandbags in the shed since 2019. I did not put them out. Donal had moved his car two
> days earlier and two people had laughed, and I had said nothing.
> `[three past perfects in a row, and they are doing the real work of the paragraph]`
>
> The water reached the floor at about half past eleven. Everything after that is in the record.
> `[the plain past returns for the main line, and the writer stops]`

**Questions about the writer's choices:**

1. The second paragraph is entirely past perfect. **What is it for?**
2. The last line hands the rest over to the record. **Why end there?**

### 8B · The anti-model

> It was a terrible night. The water came in very quickly and everybody was shocked. We did what
> we could but there was nothing anybody could have done. Nobody saw it coming.

Correct English. **Name two things it does not tell an inquiry** — and one thing it quietly
claims.

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# Saying it in that room

The inquiry asks: was anybody warned? Donal warned four people. Two of them laughed. The inquiry
sits in the community hall, and those two will be in it.

- **Say it all.** Who he told, and who laughed. It is true and it is what was asked.
- **Say he told people.** No names, no laughing. The answer is still honest.
- **Say nothing.** Let the official record say nobody was warned. He has to live here.

### 11A · The evidence

1. **The inquiry timeline** from Part 5E — and the two entries marked "no record".
2. **The chart** — what was known, by whom, hour by hour.
3. **The voice.** Eimear, twenty seconds: *"If he names them, this becomes a story about two
   people who laughed, and it is not that story. If he names nobody, it becomes a story about
   nobody knowing, and it is not that either."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In threes. Everybody says one. Use the frame.

> *I think Donal should ______ , because ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *evidence · witness · blame · fault · responsible · record · official · in hindsight*

### 11C · Decide

Write **two sentences**. Choose, and say why.

> I think Donal should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> He should say that four people were told and that he will not name them, and say out loud, in
> the room, that he is not naming them because the question the inquiry is really asking is not
> about four people. Refusing in public is not the same as hiding. It puts the warning in the
> record and keeps the inquiry pointed at the drain.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** He said four people were told and declined to name them. The chair accepted
> it without comment and moved on in under a minute.
>
> One of the two who had laughed stood up at the end and named herself, which nobody had asked
> for and which Donal says he wishes she had not done, because it made the hall go quiet and
> because it turned out to be the only part of the day anybody remembered.
>
> The final report found that the warning chain had failed at the council, not in the town. It
> does not mention Donal. The eleven-page section on the storm drain does not mention anybody.

**He kept the names out. Somebody put one back in. And the report was about a drain.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say what you think of what she did.
