## Part 3 · Grammar Lab 1 ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### Three things that can follow an object

### 3A · Notice

Return to the six sentences.

1. Which verbs take **to + verb** after the object?
2. Which two take the **bare** verb — no *to*?
3. Sentence 4 has **-ing**. What does it tell you about *when* she saw it?
4. Compare: *I saw her do it* and *I saw her doing it.* **Which one did you watch all of?**
5. Try *I want you hold it.* **What is wrong — and what kind of wrong is it?**

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_b11_u07_p03_v09` · V9 Grammar Visual**

### 3C · Know

| Pattern | Verbs | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **object + to + verb** | want · ask · tell · advise · remind · expect · allow · teach · persuade · encourage · force · invite · enable | *I want you **to hold** it.* |
| **object + bare verb** | let · make · have · (and *help*, either way) | *Let her **try**.* · *She made me **do** it again.* |
| **object + -ing** | see · hear · watch · notice · find · catch | *I saw her **doing** it wrong.* |
| **object + bare verb**, with the senses | see · hear · watch | *I saw her **do** it.* — the whole thing, start to finish |

> **Watch out.** *Let* and *make* take the bare verb. *Allow* and *force* mean almost the same and
> take *to*. The meaning is not the guide; the verb is.

> **Why it matters.** *I saw her doing it* describes a moment. *I saw her do it* describes the
> whole act — which is what a witness statement needs.

### 3D · Use — controlled

> Mariam wanted the apprentice (1) __________ (hold) the cloth differently, so she made her
> (2) __________ (do) it three times. She did not let her (3) __________ (use) the big scissors on
> the first day. On the second day she saw her (4) __________ (cut) against the grain and said
> nothing, because she expected her (5) __________ (notice) it herself. She did. On the fourth day
> Mariam heard her (6) __________ (explain) the grain to somebody else, and that is when she knew.

### 8A · Read the model

> **Running the Wednesday reconciliation — for whoever does this next**
>
> Run it on Wednesday, not Tuesday. The figures come in late on Tuesday and you will reconcile

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# Four days, or forty pages

Felix leaves on the 30th. His replacement starts on the 27th. That is four days of overlap, and
Felix has eleven days of work left that only he can do.

- **Spend the four days teaching.** His eleven days become somebody else's problem, and two of
  them cannot be done by anybody else before March.
- **Spend the four days finishing, and write the forty pages in the evenings.** He has done this
  before. The pages are good and the gaps are the things he has stopped noticing.
- **Ask for the start date to move.** It costs the business three weeks of an empty seat, and he
  has no standing to ask.

### 11A · The evidence

1. **Page 19** from Part 5, before and after.
2. **The chart**: what survives one handover, two handovers, and six months.
3. **The voice.** Ruth, twenty-five seconds: *"The forty pages were the only reason I knew where
   to put the thing I found out."*

### 11B · Steelman

Argue the option you like least. Two minutes.

### 11C · Deliberate

> *I think he should ______, because ______. The strongest argument against me is ______, and my
> answer to it is ______.*

### 11D · Decide — eighty words, two reasons

### 11E · Model decision

> He should write the pages and finish the eleven days, and I think the four days of teaching is
> the romantic answer.
>
> Teaching in person transmits more per hour than any document — that is not in dispute, and if
> he had four weeks I would say spend them. He has four days, and four days of sitting beside
> somebody produces a person who can do the normal case and has met none of the exceptions,
> because the exceptions do not happen to order. In March she will hit the 6% case and he will be
> gone either way.
>
> What the forty pages do is different. They are not a teacher; they are a place to put things.
> Ruth's own sentence is the argument: the pages were why she knew where p.19 was. A document that
> is wrong in a findable place beats a conversation that was right and is now unrecoverable.
>
> He should spend one of the four days on the exceptions only, and let the normal case go in the
> document.

### 11F · The Reveal

> **What happened.** He wrote the pages and worked the eleven days. He gave Ruth one afternoon, not
> four days, and spent all of it on the things that go wrong.
>
> Eighteen months later the document is on version 4 and about a third of it is Ruth's. The
> section Felix was proudest of — a long explanation of why the system was designed that way — was
> deleted in version 2 by Ruth, who said nobody needed it.
>
> He found out, and minded, and said nothing.

**The handover worked. The part he most wanted to pass on was the part that did not survive.**

> Was the reasoning sound? And: was Ruth right to cut it?
