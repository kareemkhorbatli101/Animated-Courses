## Part 3 · Grammar Lab 1 ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### Adding a clause without narrowing the noun

### 3A · Notice

Look at the four sentences in Part 2.

1. In sentence 1, how many students are being described? In sentence 2?
2. In sentence 3, what does *most of whom* refer to?
3. In sentence 4, what is the antecedent of *which*? **It is not a noun.**
4. **Try putting *that* into sentence 2.** What happens?
5. Try *"Forty-one students, most of them work, have done better."* **What is wrong?**

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_b13_u01_p03_v09` · V9 Grammar Visual · scope**

### 3C · Know

| | Shape | |
|---|---|---|
| **defining** | no commas, *that* allowed | *The students **who work** have done better.* — only those ones |
| **non-defining** | commas, never *that* | *The students, **who work**, have done better.* — all of them, and by the way they work |
| **quantifier + *of which / of whom*** | *most of whom*, *all of which* | *41 students, **most of whom** work* |
| **sentential *which*** | refers to the whole clause | *Attendance has halved, **which** nobody disputes.* |

> **Of whom and of which take a verb of their own.**
> ✗ *41 students, most of them work* — that is two sentences joined by a comma.
> ✓ *41 students, most of whom work* · ✓ *41 students, and most of them work*

> **Sentential *which* is the only relative that can point at a whole clause.**
> It cannot be *what*. ✗ *Attendance has halved, what nobody disputes.*

> **In writing, a non-defining clause needs both commas.** One is an error that changes the
> meaning of everything after it.

> **Why it matters.** An audit sentence with the commas in the wrong place reports a different
> population from the one that was studied, and nobody will notice.

**namely · respectively · incidentally**

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# Keep recording, or stop

Attendance has halved since 2019. The pass rate overall is unchanged. The pass rate for students
in paid employment has risen eleven points — but the university has never collected employment
data, so that figure comes from a student survey with 41 responses.

- **Keep recording.** The evidence that it helps working students is weak and is the only
  evidence anybody has. The evidence that it harms anybody is weaker.
- **Stop recording.** Attendance returns, the room looks like a university, and the committee
  can stop having this argument. Students in work lose the only accommodation they have.
- **Keep recording and finally collect the employment data.** Two years and a question on
  enrolment. It would settle it, and it would mean admitting that six years of data were the
  wrong data.

### 11A · The evidence

> **FIGURE `fig_b13_u01_p11_v06` · V6 Data Visual**

1. **The 2019 minutes and the current pack** from Part 5 — and the footnote.
2. **The chart** — attendance, attainment and the four unrecorded lectures.
3. **The voice.** Thanh, thirty seconds: *"I work Tuesday and Thursday nights, which are the two
   nights this unit is taught, and which I did not choose. I have never been to a lecture in this
   subject. I got a seventy-eight. I do not know what that proves and I would like somebody to
   stop telling me it proves I should have come."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In fours. Everybody speaks twice. Use the frame.

> *The students, most of whom ______ , have ______ , which ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *engagement · retention · metric · by default · design · attention ·
opt out · dwell*

### 11C · Decide

Write **three sentences**. Choose, say why, and name what it costs.

> I think the university should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .
> What this costs is ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> Keep recording and collect the employment data, and say in the minutes that six years of
> attendance figures were collected because they were easy and the figure that mattered was not
> collected because it was difficult. The committee's 2019 chair was right that it would be
> difficult. It was also the only question. An institution that can count a room every week for
> six years can ask one question on an enrolment form.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** The question went on the enrolment form in the following year: *do you
> undertake paid work during term? If so, how many hours?* It is optional and about seventy per
> cent answer it.

> In the first full year the data showed that students working more than fifteen hours a week had
> a pass rate four points **below** the cohort, not eleven points above. The eleven-point figure
> had come from a survey answered mostly by students who were doing well, which is what surveys
> do.
>
> Recording was kept. The argument for it changed completely: it is now defended as a reasonable
> adjustment for a group that is doing worse, rather than as something that was working.

**They got the number. It said the opposite of what everybody had been arguing. And the
recordings stayed.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say what the first eleven-point figure was
> actually measuring.
