## Part 3 · Grammar Lab 1 ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### Putting the negative first, and paying for it

### 3A · Notice

Look at the four sentences in Part 2.

1. In sentence 1, where would *I have* be in ordinary order?
2. **In sentence 2, what is the auxiliary, and where did it come from?**
3. **Rewrite sentence 3 in ordinary order.** How many words change position?

5. Try *"Never I have been so clear."* **What is missing?**

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_b13_u10_p03_v09` · V9 Grammar Visual · mirror**

### 3C · Know

| Fronted element | Example | What moves |
|---|---|---|
| ***never · rarely · seldom*** | *Never have I been so sure.* | auxiliary before subject |
| ***hardly · scarcely · no sooner*** | *No sooner had it been signed than…* | and *than*, not *when* |
| ***not until · at no point*** | *Not until Friday did anybody ask.* | *do / did* appears if there is no auxiliary |
| ***little*** | *Little did we know.* | fixed, and almost always ironic |
| ***only then · only when*** | *Only then did she answer.* | the inversion comes after the whole clause |
| ***nor · neither*** | *Nor was there any warning.* | joins to a previous negative |

> **The operation is the question operation.** *I have never been* → *Have I been?* → *Never
> have I been*. If there is no auxiliary, English supplies *do*, exactly as in a question:
> *Nobody asked* → *Not until Friday did anybody ask*.

> ***no sooner* takes *than*, not *when*.** *No sooner had it been signed than my name was on
> it.* This is the single most common error with this structure.

> **Inversion is formal and it is emphatic, and it costs something.** Used twice in a speech it
> is powerful. Used six times it is a parody of a speech, and the audience will hear the
> structure instead of the content.

> **Why it matters.** This is the grammar of occasions: toasts, tributes, resignations,
> verdicts. You will not need it often and when you need it nothing else will do.

### 3D · Use — controlled

> (1) __________ have I been so clear about what I want and so unable to say it.
> (2) __________ until the email went out __________ anybody ask me. (3) __________ sooner had

### 5B · Read — two minutes and a quarter

**NINE YEARS OF NIGHTS**
*interview transcript, lightly edited*

**Q: You have been offered a day role with a promotion. You have not accepted it.**

I have not turned it down either. I want to be careful about that, because the version going
round is that I am being difficult, and I am not being difficult. I have asked two questions and
neither has been answered.

**Q: What are the two questions?**

What happens to the building between eleven at night and six in the morning, and who does it.
That is one question really. The other is why nobody asked me before my name went on a list.

**Q: Tell me about the nights.**

There are nine of us across the week and I have done four of them for nine years. In that time
I have let three hundred and sixty people into buildings they were locked out of, most of them
at a point in the evening where they had decided the whole degree was a mistake. You do not
read about that in a restructure document. It is not a duty. It is what happens when the person
who has the keys is also the only person awake.

**Q: What will the day role involve?**

Supervision. A team of four. More money, which I am not going to pretend does not matter, and a
title. It is a step up. Everybody has said *a step up* to me eleven times in a fortnight and
nobody has said what is at the top of the step.

**Q: Would you have said yes if they had asked first?**

Probably. That is the part people find hard to believe. If somebody had come to me in March and
said we need you on days, here is why, here is what happens to the nights — I would almost
certainly have said yes, and I would have helped them work out the handover.

Not until the email went out did anybody ask me. By then I was in a list, and once you are in a
list you are either grateful or you are difficult. There is no third thing you are allowed to be.

**Q: Is there anything you want to say to the people organising the event?**

Move the chairs. Forty chairs in rows is a thing you do to an audience. We are nine people and a
kettle. Nobody who works nights wants to be looked at.

### 8A · Read the model

>

> `[the acknowledgement, with a number — and the first inversion]`
>

>

**Questions about the writer's choices:**

1. The opening does "one small true thing". **Why does an address need one?**
2. There are exactly two inversions. **Where would a third have gone, and what would it cost?**

the content.

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# A promotion nobody asked about

Josie has worked four nights a week for nine years. She has been named in a restructure paper as
moving to a day supervisory role, with more money and a title. The paper is signed. The event is
booked for Friday. She has asked two questions and neither has been answered.

- **Take it.** More money, a team, and the night rota will be covered by somebody. Nine years is
  a long time on nights and nobody stays for ever.
- **Turn it down.** Stay on nights, decline the title, and accept that she will be the person
  who said no to a promotion, which is a sentence that follows people.
- **Answer the two questions first.** Refuse to decide until somebody puts in writing what
  happens to the building between eleven and six, and who does it.

### 11A · The evidence

> **FIGURE `fig_b13_u10_p11_v06` · V6 Data Visual**

1. **The restructure paper and the night log** from Part 5E — and the line that appears in both.
2. **The chart** — what the night log actually records, by category, across one week.
3. **The voice.** Josie, thirty seconds: *"Three hundred and sixty people. I am not saying that
   to be thanked for it. I am saying it because it is going to stop happening in March and
   nobody has written it down anywhere, and in two years somebody will ask why the complaints
   went up and there will be no document to find."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In fours. Everybody speaks twice. Use the frame.

> *Never ______ . Not until ______ did ______ . Only then ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *address · audience · occasion · tribute · acknowledgement · promotion ·
gravity · a step up · take the floor · in all my years*

### 11C · Decide

Write **three sentences**. Choose, say why, and name what it costs.

> I think Josie should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .
> What this costs is ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> Answer the two questions first, in writing, and then take it. The questions are not a
> negotiating position and they are not reluctance; they are the only part of nine years that
> cannot be handed over in a conversation, and once she has moved to days nobody will ever ask
> them again. The cost is a fortnight of being called difficult by people who have not read the
> log, and a fortnight is cheap for a document that will still exist in two years.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** She asked for the two answers in writing. It took eleven days. The written
> answer said night cover would be provided by a contracted service from March, which was true,
> and did not mention lockouts, because lockouts are not in the contract.
>
> She took the job. In her four minutes on the Friday she read out the number — three hundred
> and sixty — and said that from March there would be a telephone number instead, and that the
> telephone number was fine, and that somebody should write down what it did not do. The room
> was quiet and then clapped for slightly too long.
>
> Lockout callouts went from an average of seven a week to nineteen in the first semester under
> the contract, because a contracted callout has to be logged and a person with keys does not.
> The nineteen is the first number anybody has ever had. It is in a report now, and the report
> exists because of four minutes in a room with forty chairs in it.

**She was right that it would stop happening and wrong about why it mattered. The thing that
survived was not the service. It was the number.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say what should be in the contract.
