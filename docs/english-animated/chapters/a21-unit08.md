### Six ways of saying you must

### 3A · Notice

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_a21_u08_p03_v09` · V9 Grammar Visual**

Now look at the second picture. **The ladder runs from forbidden to free. Where does
*supposed to* sit, and why is it the most interesting rung?**

> **FIGURE `fig_a21_u08_p03_v09b` · V9 Grammar Visual · ladder**

### 3C · Know

| | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **have to / must** | no choice | *You **have to** move it.* |
| **be not allowed to / mustn't** | forbidden | *You**'re not allowed to** serve.* |
| **be supposed to / be meant to** | the rule says so, and people often don't | *You**'re supposed to** wear gloves.* |
| **should / ought to / you'd better** | advice, with weight | *You **should** report it.* |
| **don't have to / no need to** | free to choose | *You **don't have to** close today.* |
| **it's up to you** | nobody is deciding for you | *The mask? **It's up to you**.* |

> **The one everybody gets wrong.**
> *mustn't* = it is forbidden. *don't have to* = you may if you want.
> ✗ *You don't have to smoke here.* (if you mean it is banned)

> **Why it matters.** *You're supposed to* is the most honest phrase in this list. It tells a
> newcomer both what the rule is **and** that people break it, which is exactly what they need to
> know on their first morning.

### 3D · Use — controlled

## Part 5 · Reading ◆ **A · THE WORLD**

### 5A · Predict

An interview with a food safety inspector. The title is
**"I am not trying to catch anybody."**
**Do you believe that?** One sentence, before you read.

### 5B · Read — one hundred seconds

---

**"I am not trying to catch anybody"**
*An interview with a market food safety inspector*

**You arrive without warning. Why?**
Because a stall that is clean on Tuesday because I told them I was coming on Tuesday tells me
nothing. I am not trying to catch anybody. I am trying to see the ordinary day.

**What do you find most often?**
Handwash in the wrong place. The rule is one metre. It is almost always two or three, because
somebody moved it to make room for a crate in 2019 and nobody moved it back.

**Does that actually matter?**
Yes, and I will tell you exactly why, because people assume it is paperwork. At one metre you
wash your hands between the money and the food, because the basin is in the way. At three metres
you have to decide to wash them. Nobody decides forty times an hour.

**What do you do when you find it?**
I write it down and I give them until the end of the month. I close a stall perhaps twice a year,
and both times it is something I could smell from the aisle.

**Do traders lie to you?**
Almost never. They tell me the truth and then they tell me why the rule is stupid, and about a
third of the time they are right. I write that down too. Nobody reads it.

---

2. What does she find most often, and why is it usually there?

4. How long does she usually give traders?
5. How often does she close a stall, and for what?
6. What does she do when a trader says a rule is stupid?

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# One metre, or two

The handwash basin is two metres from the stall. The rule says one. It has been two metres since
2019, when somebody moved it to make room for a crate.

- **Close and fix it now.** One day's trade lost — about 480 — and the plumber can come Thursday.
- **Fix it at month end.** The deadline. Keep trading. If Nurul comes back before then, it is a
  400 fine and a second entry on the record.
- **Argue it.** The basin is two metres from the counter but one metre from where she actually
  stands. Measure it from the person, not the furniture.

### 11A · The evidence

1. **The inspection report** from Part 5E — and the box that was ticked and crossed out.
2. **The chart** — handwashing frequency measured at one, two and three metres.
3. **The voice.** Nurul, twenty seconds: *"I measure from the food. Not from the counter and not
   from where you happen to be standing. If I measured from the person, every basin in this
   market would pass and half of them would still not be used."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In threes. Everybody says one. Use the frame.

> *I think Siti should ______ , because ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *safety · hygiene · fine · careful · clean · rule · warning · inspection*

### 11C · Decide

Write **two sentences**. Choose, and say why.

> I think Siti should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> She should move it now and not close. A basin is not a wall; two of them could carry it in
> twenty minutes on a Tuesday afternoon when the row is empty, and the only reason this has been
> open for six years is that everybody has treated it as a plumbing job. The argument about
> measuring from the person is a good argument and it should be made at the committee, next year,
> when nothing is at stake.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** Hafiz and Zul moved it on the Tuesday in about half an hour, without a
> plumber, and the stall did not close.
>
> Siti measured her own handwashing for a week afterwards, out of curiosity, and found she was
> doing it roughly three times as often. She had been sure the distance made no difference.
>
> The argument about measuring from the person was made at the committee in January. It lost.
> Nurul had already written it into her own report, four years earlier, under the heading
> *trader comments*, where nobody had read it.

**Half an hour's work. Three times the handwashing. And a good argument that had already lost
once in writing.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say what you think of the trader comments box.
