## Part 3 · Grammar Lab 1 ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### Reporting at a distance you choose

### 3A · Notice

1. In sentence 1, how much does the reporter commit to? In sentence 2, how much?
2. Sentence 4 has two layers of reporting. **Name both, and say who the second source is.**

4. **Which of the six could be printed if the speaker denied all of it?**

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_b22_u08_p03_v09` · V9 Grammar Visual**

### 3C · Know

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **neutral reporting** | *said · told · stated* | the reporter takes no position |
| **casting doubt** | *claimed · alleged* | the reporter signals they do not endorse it |
| **giving ground** | *conceded · acknowledged · admitted* | the speaker lost something by saying it |
| **holding ground** | *maintained · insisted · asserted · denied* | the speaker was under pressure and held |
| **answering** | *countered · responded · replied* | there was something to answer |
| **reporting an absence** | *stopped short of · would not be drawn · declined to comment* | nothing was said, and that is the news |

> **Every attribution verb is a claim by the reporter, not by the speaker.** *He claimed* and
> *he said* report the same words; the first adds the reporter's doubt and is actionable if the
> doubt is unfounded. Choosing the verb is choosing how much of somebody else's sentence you are
> prepared to carry.

> **The passive reporting frame removes the reporter entirely.** *He is said to have told the
> crew* has no subject for *said*: somebody, unnamed, is the source, and the reporter has placed
> two layers between themselves and the claim. It is the most distancing construction available
> and the easiest to abuse.

| | |
|---|---|
| **the double-layer frames** | *is said to have · is understood to have · was quoted as saying* |
| **the absence frames** | *declined to comment · would not be drawn · could not confirm · stopped short of* |
| **the continuation frame** | *went on to say* |
| **the honesty markers** | *according to the transcript · in his own words · that is a paraphrase* |

> **Why it matters.** The difference between what a person said and what a document says they said
> is one word, often, and it is the word on which an inquiry turns. Reporting at the right
> distance is not a style question; it is the difference between a quotation and an allegation.

## Part 5 · Reading ◆ **A · THE WORLD**

### 5A · Predict

The title is **"Assumed and knew."**
**What is the difference, in an inquiry, between a witness who assumed something and one who knew
it?**

### 5B · Read — two minutes

---

**Assumed and knew**

The inquiry sat for three days and heard fourteen witnesses. The transcript runs to a hundred and
eighteen pages. The draft report quotes thirty-one passages, each in quotation marks, each
attributed.

Four of the thirty-one are not quotations. They are paraphrases, accurate in substance, set in
quotation marks because the drafter understood the marks as a courtesy to the reader rather than
as a claim about the words.

One of the four is on page sixty-one. The witness, a shift supervisor, said: *I assumed the gantry
was clear.* The report quotes him as saying: *I knew the gantry was clear.*

Both sentences are about the same moment and the same belief. One of them describes a man who
checked nothing and one describes a man who checked. The finding against him rests on the second.

The supervisor read the draft, did not recognise the sentence, and asked for the transcript page.
He was sent it. He has not disputed the substance of the finding, which he accepts. He has
disputed one word, which the inquiry has agreed to correct, and he has asked that the correction
be recorded rather than made silently.

The inquiry has not yet decided whether to do that.

### Quotation marks you can hear

### 7A · Hear 🔊 Track 8.3

> *He said — ⏸ I assumed the gantry was clear ⏸ — and then stopped.*
> the pauses are the quotation marks, and the quoted part is said faster and flatter
> *He said he assumed the gantry was clear.* — no pauses, no shift, and the words are now yours

### 7B · Say

Say both. Then say this, making the quotation audible without the word *quote*:

> *The report says he knew. The transcript says — I assumed.*

### 7C · Make it mean something

Say these two:

> *He said he knew.* — flat: a report, and you are not commenting
> *He said he KNEW.* — the stress on the quoted word: and the transcript says otherwise

**Stressing a word inside a report is a comment on the word, not on the speaker,** which is why a
reporter who stresses a quoted verb has stopped reporting and started arguing.

### 7D · Fluency 30 ⏺

Thirty seconds: **one sentence reported four times — flat, with audible quotation marks, with a
stressed quoted word, and at two removes.** Three times, faster.

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# How the port's inquiries quote

**Option one:** paraphrase freely. Quotation marks in an internal report are a readability device,
and every drafter in the building has used them that way for twenty years.

**Option two:** verbatim only, with a transcript page reference for every passage in quotation
marks, and no paraphrase inside marks ever.

**Option three:** option two, plus a marked style for deliberate paraphrase, so that a reader can
see at a glance which sentences are the witness's and which are the inquiry's.

### 11A · The evidence

> **FIGURE `fig_b22_u08_p11_v06` · V6 Data Visual · full page**

🔊 **Track 8.6.** The inquiry chair, 30 seconds: *"My worry is the obvious one. Give witnesses a
page number and they will come back, and fourteen witnesses coming back is three more days I do
not have. I have been chairing these for eleven years and I will tell you what I have never once
had: a witness who came back to correct a sentence they actually said."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In threes. Use the frame, and attribute every claim you borrow.

> *I would ______ , because ______ . He said — in his own words — ______ .*

### 11C · Decide

Write **three sentences**: the decision, the evidence, and the rule as you would write it into the
inquiry's handbook.

### 11D · Model decision

> Option three. The page reference does the whole of the work and the marked style costs nothing:
> a reader who can see which sentences are the inquiry's own stops attributing the inquiry's
> reasoning to the witness, which is the second failure in this report and nobody has mentioned it.
> The chair's objection is correct about the cost and wrong about the direction: witnesses who come
> back come back about sentences they did not say, and every one of those is an error the inquiry
> would otherwise publish.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** The port adopted the rule. Witnesses were sent their own transcript pages with
> the draft. Requests to correct rose from two per cent of witnesses to twenty-four.
>
> Then something nobody predicted: of the corrections requested in the first year, four fifths
> were to sentences the inquiry had paraphrased, and almost none to sentences that had been quoted
> exactly. The chair's three extra days turned out to be three days of fixing the inquiry's own
> words, which the inquiry had been publishing under other people's names for eleven years.

**One question. You do not have to answer it out loud.**

> The witnesses were correcting their own evidence. Whose words were they correcting?
