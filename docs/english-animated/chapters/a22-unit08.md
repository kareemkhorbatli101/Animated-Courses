## Part 3 · Grammar Lab 1 ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### Adding something, rather than narrowing it

### 3A · Notice

Look at the four sentences in Part 2.

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_a22_u08_p03_v09` · V9 Grammar Visual · scope**

Now look at the second picture. **One sentence, two readings, and the only difference is two
marks.**

> **FIGURE `fig_a22_u08_p03_v09b` · V9 Grammar Visual · mirror`**

### 3C · Know

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **defining** | no commas · tells you **which one** | *The hall **that was built in 1884*** *(so there is another hall)* |
| **non-defining** | commas · **adds** information | *The hall, **which was built in 1884**,* *(there is one hall)* |

> **Three rules for non-defining clauses.**
> 1. Always commas — one before and one after, unless the sentence ends.
> 2. Never ***that***. Use *which*, *who*, *whose*, *where*, *when*.
> 3. You can always delete the whole clause and the sentence survives.

> **With a proper name it is almost always non-defining**, because a name already identifies.
> ✓ *Sinéad, who has run it for nine years, …* ✗ *Sinéad who has run it for nine years …*

> **Quantity phrases take *of which* and *of whom*.**
> *eleven buildings, **most of which** are below the line* ·
> *forty-one traders, **all of whom** were asked*

> **Why it matters.** The comma is the difference between describing a place and
> distinguishing it from another one. On a plaque, that is the whole job.

## Part 5 · Reading ◆ **A · THE WORLD**

### 5A · Predict

A review of the town's new heritage trail. The title is
**"Eleven plaques and one missing sentence."**
**What do you expect the reviewer to object to?** One sentence.

### 5B · Read — one hundred and ten seconds

---

**Eleven plaques and one missing sentence ★★★☆☆**

Ballinmore's heritage trail, which opened in June, is an hour long, properly signposted, and
better than most things of its kind. The plaques are brass, which will outlast the funding, and
the text on them was written by somebody who has read the parish records rather than the
tourist board's previous leaflet.

It is also, in one respect, a lie by omission, and I want to be careful about how I say that,
because the omission is not the trail's fault.

Plaque 4 is on the hall, which was built in 1884 as a corn store and became a hall in 1931. The
plaque gives both dates and the name of the merchant who paid for it. What it does not say — and
what every person in this town knows — is that the hall is the only building in the lower town
above the 1.9 metre line, that two hundred people were in it on the night of the storm, and that
there is a proposal to sell it.

A heritage plaque is not a news bulletin and nobody expects it to be. But the trail is the only
written account of this place that a visitor will ever read, and it stops in 1931.

Three stars, and the third one is for the brass.

---

### 5C · Answer

1. How long is the trail, and when did it open?
2. **What two things does the reviewer praise about the plaques?**
3. What does plaque 4 say about the hall, and what does it leave out?
4. How many people were in the hall on the night?
5. **Why does the reviewer say the omission is not the trail's fault?**
6. What is the third star for?

### 5D · Vocabulary in context

> signposted · a lie by omission · the parish records · a news bulletin · it stops in 1931

### 5E · The counter-text

> **FIGURE `fig_a22_u08_p05_v05` · V5 Annotated Realia**

1. What two dates does the plaque give, and whose name is on it?
2. **What is the building valued at, and on what basis?**

## Part 8 · Writing ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

### A place, with what the sign leaves out · 100 words

> **FIGURE `fig_a22_u08_p08_v06` · V6 Data Visual**

### 8A · Read the model

> The hall, which was built as a corn store in 1884, stands eleven metres back from the sea and
> four metres above it. `[the added clause, and then the two numbers that turn out to be the
> point]`
>
> It became a hall in 1931, when the pier was rebuilt and the town briefly had money. The plaque
> gives both dates and the merchant's name, which was Dolan. `[a second clause, and one fact
> that the plaque does have]`
>
> It is also the only building in the lower town that the water did not reach, which is why two
> hundred people were in it in January, and which is not on the plaque. `[the defining clause is
> deliberate here — there is only one such building, and the sentence is about which one]`

**Questions about the writer's choices:**

1. The first paragraph gives two measurements before any history. **Why that order?**
2. The last sentence uses *that*, not *which*, and no comma. **Is that a mistake?**

### 8B · The anti-model

> The hall is a lovely old building with a great deal of history. It has been at the centre of the
> community for many years and holds a special place in the hearts of local people. It is well
> worth a visit.

Correct English, and there is no building in it. **Name three things it does not contain.**

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# The hall, or the wall

The hall is the only building in the lower town above the 1.9 metre line. It is valued at
410,000. The sea wall needs 340,000 that nobody has.

- **Keep it.** It is where two hundred people went in January and the only place they could have
  gone.
- **Sell it.** The wall gets built, and the hall committee gets a room in the new school, which
  is further away and below the line.
- **Sell half.** Split the building: the upper floor stays community, the ground floor is sold
  as two units. It raises about 180,000 and complicates everything.

### 11A · The evidence

1. **Plaque 4 and the valuation** from Part 5E — and the line that would change the plaque.
2. **The chart** — height above sea level of every building in the lower town.
3. **The voice.** Sinéad, twenty seconds: *"I opened it at ten on the Sunday because I had the
   key and it was dry. Nobody asked me to and nobody has thanked me, which is right, because
   anybody with the key would have done it. What I want to know is who will have the key."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In threes. Everybody says one. Use the frame.

> *I think they should ______ , because ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *landmark · heritage · restored · destroyed · visitor · view · community · worth seeing*

### 11C · Decide

Write **two sentences**. Choose, and say why.

> I think they should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> They should keep it and find the 340,000 somewhere else, and they should say in public why:
> that the hall is not being kept because it is old but because it is four metres up. The
> heritage argument is the weaker one and it is the one everybody is making, and if the hall is
> ever sold it will be sold by somebody who has only heard the heritage argument and has
> correctly decided that it is sentimental.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** They kept it. The decision went 9–2 and took forty minutes, which Sinéad
> says is the fastest the committee has ever done anything.
>
> The argument that was made in the room was about 1884, the corn store and the Dolan family.
> Nobody mentioned the four metres until the vote was over, when Tomás said it on the way out and
> four people stopped walking.
>
> Plaque 4 now has a second brass panel underneath it, added in September. It is nineteen words
> long and it is about the height.

**They made the right decision for the wrong reason, and then somebody fixed the reason.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say what the nineteen words should be.
