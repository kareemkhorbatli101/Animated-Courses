## Part 3 · Grammar Lab 1 ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### When the two halves are in different times

### 3A · Notice

Look at the four sentences in Part 2.

1. In sentence 1, which half is about the past and which about now?
2. In sentence 2, which half is about now and which about the past?
3. **Sentences 1 and 2 are mirror images.** Write out which time each half is in.
4. Sentence 3 does the same thing. **What is the real situation behind it?**
5. Try *"If we had advertised in March, we would have had somebody by now."*
   **Is that wrong? What changes?**

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_b12_u03_p03_v09` · V9 Grammar Visual · branch**

### 3C · Know

| | *if* half | result half | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **second** | past simple | *would* + verb | unreal now → unreal now |
| **third** | past perfect | *would have* + pp | unreal past → unreal past |
| **mixed A** | **past perfect** | ***would* + verb** | unreal **past** → unreal **present** |
| **mixed B** | **past simple** | ***would have* + pp** | unreal **present state** → unreal **past** |

> **Mixed A** — the past did not happen, and that is why **now** is like this.
> *If we **had advertised** in March, we **would have** somebody by now.*

> **Mixed B** — a permanent condition about **now**, and its effect on the **past**.
> *If I **were** permanent, I **would have said** this in March.*

> **How to choose.** Ask what time each half is really in. English has no special form for a
> mixed conditional; it takes the *if* half from one type and the result half from the other.

> **Watch out.** *by now*, *today*, *at the moment* in the result half are almost always
> signals for mixed A.
> ✗ *If we had advertised in March, we would have had somebody by now* is possible, but it
> means the having happened and finished in the past, which is not what anybody means here.

## Part 5 · Reading ◆ **A · THE WORLD**

### 5A · Predict

A personal essay by a doctor. The title is
**"I am in emergency medicine because of a train."**
**What do you expect the train to be?** Two sentences.

### 5B · Read — two minutes

---

**I am in emergency medicine because of a train**

In 2007 I was in my final year and I wanted paediatrics. I had wanted paediatrics since I was
nineteen and I had arranged my entire fifth year around it, including an elective I paid for
myself.

The interviews for the paediatric rotations were in Temuco on a Tuesday. There was one train. It
was cancelled, and the replacement bus — which existed, and which I could have taken — would have
got me there forty minutes late, and I decided, standing on a platform at six in the morning,
that arriving forty minutes late to an interview was worse than not arriving.

I now think that was wrong. I think they would have seen me. I was twenty-four and I had never in
my life been late to anything and I did not know that lateness is survivable.

So I took the emergency rotation that was still open, because it was the only one still open, and
I have been in emergency medicine for eighteen years and I am good at it and I would not change
it.

Here is the part that bothers me. If I had taken the bus, I would be a paediatrician now. I would
be a different person: emergency changes what you are like, it genuinely does, and I have watched
it happen to eleven other people. The me that exists is a product of a cancelled train.

And here is the part that bothers me more. Every year I interview fifth-years. Last year a
candidate arrived thirty-five minutes late, apologised once, and was excellent, and two of the
three people on the panel wanted to mark her down for it.

I told them about the train. We appointed her. She starts in March and I have not told her any
of this.

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# Friday

Dr Benítez's locum contract ends on Friday. The permanent post has been vacant for eleven months
and has been advertised four times.

- **Renew the locum.** Costs about 2.0 times the permanent salary. The department is covered on
  Saturday. It is the fourth renewal.
- **Recruit properly.** Three months to appoint and start. The department runs three doctors
  short through the summer, which is the busiest quarter.
- **Offer him the permanent post.** He has never been asked. He is not on the shortlist because
  he has not applied, and he has not applied because nobody has suggested it.

### 11A · The evidence

> **FIGURE `fig_b12_u03_p11_v06` · V6 Data Visual**

1. **The vacancy notice and the invoice** from Part 5E — and the surcharge line.
2. **The chart** — cost, cover and risk for each of the three options over twelve months.
3. **The voice.** Dr Benítez, thirty seconds: *"Nobody has asked me. I am aware that I could
   have said something, and I have thought about why I have not, and the honest answer is that
   if I were permanent I would have said it in week two, and I am not, so I have spent three
   months being careful instead."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In fours. Everybody speaks twice. Use the frame.

> *If they ______ , then ______ . Which would mean ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *post · vacancy · appointment · shortlist · the right fit · apply for ·
take up a post · knock-on · as a result*

### 11C · Decide

Write **three sentences**. Choose, say why, and name what it costs.

> I think the hospital should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .
> What this costs is ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> Ask him, today, before Friday. The entire reason he is not a candidate is that nobody has
> spoken a sentence out loud, and the department is about to spend eleven months and forty
> million pesos solving a problem that one conversation might close. If he says no, nothing is
> lost and the recruitment runs anyway. The thing to notice is that the obvious option has been
> available every day for three months and has not been taken, and that is a fact about the
> department rather than about him.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** Dr Herrera asked him on the Wednesday. He said yes within about ninety
> seconds and then said that he had been waiting for somebody to ask since October.
>
> Finance refused the appointment in its first form because the post had been advertised four
> times and the rules require a competitive process. It was re-advertised for ten working days.
> He applied. Two other people applied. He was appointed in January.
>
> The ten days cost one more locum month, at 2.0, which is about four million pesos, and
> everybody involved describes this as the cheapest part of the whole year.

**One sentence, three months late, and then ten days of process because the rules exist for a
reason.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say whether the competitive process was worth
> four million.
