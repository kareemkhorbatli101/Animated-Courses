## Part 3 · Grammar Lab 1 ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### One letter, and the question word in front

### 3A · Notice

Look at the four questions in Part 2 again.

1. Which two have *do*, and who are they about?
2. Which two have *does*, and who are they about?
3. After *does*, does the verb have an **s** on it?
4. Where is the question word — at the front, or in the middle?

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_a11_u03_p03_v09a` · V9 Grammar Visual**
> Five columns — *I · you · we · they · he/she/it* — with the verb under each. The first four are
> identical and in one colour. The fifth has one extra letter, in a second colour, and the letter
> is larger than the word it is attached to.

### 3C · Know

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **I / you / we / they** | **check** | They check their phones at night. |
| **he / she / it** | **checks** | She checks her phone before she gets up. |

> **One letter, one person.** The **s** goes on for *he*, *she* and *it* and nowhere else. It is
> the only ending the present simple has, and it is the one everybody forgets.

> **FIGURE `fig_a11_u03_p03_v09b` · V9 Grammar Visual**
> Questions: *do* and *does* arriving at the front of the sentence, and the **s** coming off the
> verb as *does* takes it. The two cannot both have it. A small arrow moves the s from the verb
> to the helper.

| | |
|---|---|
| **?** | **Do** you check your phone at night? · **Does** she check her phone at night? |
| **–** | I **don't** check it. · He **doesn't** check it. |
| **? + word** | **Where** do you put it? · **How often** does he look at it? |

> **The s is only in one place at a time.** *Does she checks?* is wrong because the **s** has
> already gone onto *does*. One **s** per question, and the helper gets it.

> **Why it matters.** Every question you will ever ask about somebody else's life has this shape.
> It is the whole of asking about habits.

### 3D · Use — controlled

Complete with **do**, **does**, **don't**, **doesn't**, **check** or **checks**.

> Lin (1) ______ her phone before she gets up. Yusuf (2) ______ not know how often he looks.
> (3) ______ you turn off your phone at night? Teresa (4) ______ . She (5) ______ it in a drawer.
> Her students (6) ______ believe her.

### 3E · Use — guided

Make a question with the word in brackets.

1. She opens the camera first. *(which app)*
2. He checks his phone forty times a day. *(how often)*
3. They charge the battery at night. *(when)*

5. Lin uses a map every morning. *(why)*

### 3F · Use — open

**In pairs.** Ask your partner five questions about one other person — a brother, a friend, a
neighbour. Every question must have a question word in front.

### 3G · Check — six items, mark your own

## Part 4 · Speaking ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

### 4A · Fluency starter — the chain question

One person asks a question with a question word. The next person answers **and** asks the next
one. **No question word may be used twice.** Seven words, so the chain is seven long.

### 4B · The functional core — asking about habits

> **FIGURE `fig_a11_u03_p04_v04a` · V4 Comparison Pair**
> The same interview, written down twice by two learners. The left one has questions with a
> question word on every line. The right one has only yes/no questions. **Six planted
> differences.** Only one of the two produced an answer longer than one word.

**Language Bank**

| | |
|---|---|
| **the seven words** | *what · where · when · who · why · how · which* |
| **how + a word** | *how many · how much · how often* |
| **asking again** | *How do you say…? · What does it mean? · I don't understand.* |
| **soft openers** | *Can I ask you something? · And you?* |

**Information gap.** Learner A has Lin's week. Learner B has Yusuf's week.
**Find out which of the two spends more time online, without saying a number.**

### 4C · The performance — the interview ⏺

Interview one classmate about **one habit**. **Two minutes.** You must ask:

> one *how often* question · one *what* question · one *why* question · one question you did not
> plan

Then tell the class in three sentences, using *he* or *she*. **Record it.**

### 4D · Observer

One person listens for **one thing**: did every *he* and *she* sentence have the **s**?

### 4E · Two criteria

> ☐ Did I ask four questions and not read them?
> ☐ Did my voice go down at the end of the question-word questions?

### 5A · Predict

**Whose drawer do you think it is?**

### 5B · Read — sixty seconds

---

**The phone in the drawer**

**Teresa** teaches in room 3. She puts her phone in a drawer at nine o'clock and she takes it out
at one. She says she does not miss it. Her students do not believe her.

**Yusuf** checks his phone about forty times a day. He thinks it is about ten. The phone knows the

## Part 7 · Pronunciation & Fluency Lab ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### Two tunes, and the question decides

> **FIGURE `fig_a11_u03_p07_v10` · V10 Phonetics Visual**
> Two pitch lines over the same four words. The first ends going up and the second ends going
> down. Under each, the kind of question that takes that tune.

### 7A · Hear

> **up at the end** — a yes/no question: *Do you check it?* ↗ *Does she know?* ↗
> **down at the end** — a question with a question word: *Where do you put it?* ↘ *How often does he look?* ↘

### 7B · Say

Say each one three times. Then say this pair:

> *Do you have a charger?* ↗
> *Where is the charger?* ↘

**Same room, same person, two tunes.**

### 7C · Make it mean something

Say these two:

> *You check your phone at night.* ↘ — *this is a fact, and I know it*
> *You check your phone at night?* ↗ — *this is a question, and I am surprised*

**Same seven words. The tune is the only difference.** Which one is a question?

### 7D · Fluency 20 ⏺

Twenty seconds: **six questions about one person, each with a different question word.**
Three times, faster.

## Part 8 · Writing ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

### A profile · 40–60 words

### 8A · Read the model

> My brother checks his phone before he opens his eyes. `[one strong first sentence, with the s]`
>
> He has ninety apps and he opens four. He does not use the camera. `[a number, and a negative]`
>
> He charges the battery twice a day. He says the phone is old. The phone is one year old. `[the
> gap between what he says and what is true]`
>
> He turns it off on Sunday morning, for two hours. `[the exception, at the end]`

**Questions about the writer's choices:**

1. The first sentence is about something that happens before he is awake. **Why start there?**
2. *The phone is one year old.* **Why is that one short sentence and not part of the one before?**
3. The last sentence is the only good news. **Why is it last?**

### 8B · The anti-model

> My brother has a phone. He uses it a lot. He has many apps. He likes his phone. It is a good phone.

Correct English, and it could be anybody. **Add two numbers and one negative, and make it one person.**

### 8C · Plan

> one habit nobody chooses → a number → something he or she does not do →
> the exception, at the end

### 8D · Write — 40 to 60 words
### 8E · Peer review — two things only

> ☐ Does every *he* and *she* verb have the **s**?
> ☐ Is there one number that is true about this person and nobody else?

### 8F · Publish

On the wall, with no names. **The class matches the profile to the writer's partner.**

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# The box by the door

Escola Monte wants to put a box by the door of room 3. You put your phone in it at the start of
the lesson and take it out at the end.

Teresa counted. In one ninety-minute lesson, eleven students picked up a phone sixty-four times.
Nobody noticed they were doing it.

But three students use the phone as a dictionary, every lesson. One student has a child at a
school that telephones. And the box has no lock.

### 11A · The evidence

> **FIGURE `fig_a11_u03_p11_v04b` · V4 Comparison Pair**
> The same ninety minutes, drawn twice: with the box and without it. **Left:** sixty-four pick-ups,
> and three of them are dictionary look-ups. **Right:** no pick-ups, and three hands up instead.
> **Six planted differences**, one of which is the clock on the wall.

🔊 **Track 3.6.** Yusuf, 20 seconds: *"I don't want the box. But I check my phone and I don't
know why. I don't even read it. How often? I don't want to know."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In threes. Use the frame. Everybody says one.

> *I think the phones should ______ , because ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *box · dictionary · message · child · lesson · nobody · notification*

### 11C · Decide

Write **two sentences**. Choose, and say why.

> I think the phones should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> No box. Put one phone on the desk at the front, on a stand, as the class dictionary, and leave
> the other ten where they are. The problem is not the phone. The problem is that looking a word
> up and leaving the room look exactly the same from the front.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** Teresa did not use a box. She put one phone at the front as the dictionary
> and asked everybody to put their own phone face down. Pick-ups fell from sixty-four to nineteen
> in a week.
>
> Then something she did not expect: the nineteen were almost all the same two students, and both
> of them were the ones who answered most questions. They were not leaving the room. They were
> checking a word and coming straight back.

**One question. You do not have to answer it out loud.**

> Teresa counted pick-ups. Was she counting the right thing?
