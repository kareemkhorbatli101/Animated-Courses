> **FIGURE `fig_a21_u03_p03_v09b` · V9 Grammar Visual · ladder**

### 3C · Know

| | Use it for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **used to + verb** | a habit or a state that has stopped | *She **used to start** at two.* |
| **past simple** | one finished action, or a habit with a number | *She **started** at two every day for thirty years.* |

> **negative** → *didn't use to* (no **d**) · **question** → *Did you use to…?*
> **anymore** goes at the end, and only with a negative → *I don't make it that way **anymore**.*
> **no longer** does the same job, earlier in the sentence, and sounds more formal.
> **still** says the opposite: it has not stopped → *I **still** make it the long way.*

> **Watch out.** *used to* needs a repeated past. **One Tuesday is not a habit.**
> ✗ *I used to go there last week.* ✓ *I used to go there every week.*

> **Why it matters.** *I made it the long way* tells me about one pot of soup.
> *I used to make it the long way* tells me about a life that changed.

## Part 5 · Reading ◆ **A · THE WORLD**

### 5A · Predict

### 5B · Read — one hundred seconds

---

**The pot**

When my grandmother was alive the broth took forty minutes and nobody thought that was long.
People used to stand in the lane and talk while they waited. The waiting was part of it. I was
eight, and my job was to carry the bowls out and bring them back, and I did it every summer until
I was fifteen.

She died in 2011. I kept the pot.

Last year a man came and stood in the queue for six minutes and then left. He wasn't rude about
it. He just looked at his phone twice and went to the stall at the end, which does a broth in
ninety seconds out of a packet, and it is not bad.

I still make it the long way. I start at two in the morning and I do not use a clock, because she
never used one and I have never needed one either. But I sell forty bowls on a Tuesday and she
used to sell a hundred and ten.

My cousin says I should change it. He says nobody is coming back for the forty minutes, they are
coming back for the taste, and the taste is in the bones and not in the time.

He may be right. He has never made it.

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# Forty minutes, or ninety seconds

The broth takes forty minutes. The lunch queue is twenty-five minutes long at its worst and people
leave. The stall at the end does a packet broth in ninety seconds and sells three times as much.

- **Keep it.** Forty minutes. Fewer bowls, and the thing stays what it is.
- **Change it.** Make the base the night before. Eight minutes a bowl. Nobody will know.
- **Both.** The long one until it runs out, the quick one after. Two prices.

### 11A · The evidence

1. **The two menus** from Part 5E — and what the handwritten line says.
2. **The chart** — bowls sold per hour, against how long people are willing to wait.
3. **The voice.** Her cousin, twenty seconds: *"They're not coming back for the forty minutes.
   Nobody has ever come back for the forty minutes."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In threes. Everybody says one. Use the frame.

> *I think Mei Ling should ______ , because ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *memory · queue · taste · her grandmother's · change · the same · money*

### 11C · Decide

Write **two sentences**. Choose, and say why.

> I think Mei Ling should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> She should make the base the night before and keep everything else. The forty minutes was never
> the point — her grandmother started at two in the morning precisely so that nobody would have to
> wait, and doing the slow thing at the wrong end of the day is not loyalty to her, it is just
> doing it in the hardest order.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** She made the base the night before. Service came down to nine minutes and
> sales went up by half in three weeks.
>
> Four regulars said it tasted different. She could not taste any difference and neither could her
> cousin, and two of the four were back within a month.
>
> She did not tell anybody she had changed it. She has told one person since: the man who waited
> six minutes and left, who came back and is now there on Tuesdays.

**Sales up by half. Four complaints. One secret she is not comfortable with.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say what you think of the secret.
