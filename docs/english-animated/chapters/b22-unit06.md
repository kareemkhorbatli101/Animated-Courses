## Part 3 · Grammar Lab 1 ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### Giving something away and keeping the sentence

### 3A · Notice

Look at the six sentences in Part 2 again.

1. In sentence 1, which half is the reader left holding? **Which clause is the main one?**
2. Sentence 3 begins with *for all*. **What does it concede, and in how many words?**
3. Sentence 2 uses *albeit*. **What can follow *albeit*, and what cannot?**
4. Sentences 4 and 5 split one move across two sentences. **What is the advantage of splitting it?**

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_b22_u06_p03_v09` · V9 Grammar Visual**

### 3C · Know

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| ***while* · *whilst*** | a full clause; the main clause comes second and wins | *While the cost is real, the case stands.* |
| ***albeit*** | a phrase, not a clause; no subject, no finite verb | *…the case stands, albeit at a cost.* |
| ***for all*** | a noun phrase; concedes a quantity of something | *For all the safety gain, ninety-four posts go.* |
| ***granted* · *admittedly*** | a whole move in one word, usually sentence-initial | *Granted, the gain is real.* |
| ***nonetheless* · *even so*** | holds the position after the concession | *Nonetheless, ninety-four posts go.* |
| ***notwithstanding*** | formal; takes a noun phrase, before or after it | *Notwithstanding the gain, the cost is real.* |

> **The reader keeps the main clause.** *While A, B* and *While B, A* contain identical
> information and leave the reader holding opposite things. Choosing which fact goes in the
> subordinate clause is the whole of this unit, and it is a moral choice before it is a
> grammatical one.

> ***Albeit* takes no verb.** *…albeit at a cost*, *…albeit slowly*, *…albeit a small one*. It
> cannot take a clause, and a learner who writes *albeit it is expensive* has been caught by the
> one irregularity in the set.

| | |
|---|---|
| **conceding in a phrase** | *to be fair · in fairness · that may be · there is something in* |
| **conceding and holding** | *at the same time · for all that · I accept that* |
| **limiting the concession** | *up to a point · I will grant you that · that does not follow* |
| **what people do in an argument** | *give way · back down · hold firm* |

> **Why it matters.** A position that concedes nothing is not read as strong; it is read as
> uninformed. A position that concedes everything has no position. The sentence that does both is
> the hardest one in professional English and the most quoted when it works.

## Part 5 · Reading ◆ **A · THE WORLD**

### 5A · Predict

The title is **"Both things are true."**
**Can one sentence carry a seventy-eight per cent fall in injuries and ninety-four redundancies
without favouring one?**

### 5B · Read — two minutes

---

**Both things are true**

Lost-time injuries at the two automated terminals have fallen from forty-one per million hours to
nine. Across the port's whole labour force that is about thirty-two fewer serious injuries a year,
and the people who are not injured are, on the whole, the same people whose jobs are going.

Ninety-four of three hundred and ten stevedore posts go. The retraining scheme places sixty-one.
Thirty-three it does not place, and the scheme's own documents say so on page four.

The port's study reports a twenty-two per cent productivity gain. The union's study points out
that the comparison excludes the eighteen months of transition, during which productivity fell by
a third, and that including it reduces the gain to about nine per cent. Both studies are correct
about what they measured.

The committee's draft position runs to four lines. Its first version began *While the programme
regrettably entails some reduction in headcount, the safety benefits are substantial*. The union
representative, a stevedore of thirty-four years, read it once and said that the first half of
the sentence was in the wrong clause.

He was right, and nobody in the room could say why for about a minute.

## Part 7 · Pronunciation & Fluency Lab ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

> **FIGURE `fig_b22_u06_p07_v10` · V10 Phonetics Visual**

### A concession rises and the position falls

### 7A · Hear 🔊 Track 6.3

> *While the cost is real,* ↗ *the case stands.* ↘ — the rise promises the main clause
> *Granted.* ↘ — a full stop: the concession is complete and nothing follows
> *Granted…* ↗ — and something is about to follow, and everybody waits for it

### 7B · Say

Say all three. Then say this:

> *Granted — and I do not say it lightly — the cost is real; nonetheless, the case stands.* — one
> rise, one level stretch, one fall, and the fall is where the position is.

### 7C · Make it mean something

Say these two:

> *I accept that.* ↘ — accepted, finished
> *I accept that…* ↗ — accepted, and here comes the limit

**A concession said with a falling tune is a concession; said with a rising one it is a run-up.**
Everybody in the room can hear the difference, which is why a rising *granted* never has to be
followed by *but*.

### 7D · Fluency 30 ⏺

Thirty seconds: **three concessions, each with a rising tail, each followed by a position that
falls.** Three times, faster.

### A position that holds two things · 200 words

### 8A · Read the model

> Lost-time injuries at the automated terminals have fallen from forty-one per million hours to
> nine, which is about thirty-two fewer serious injuries a year. `[the fact, unqualified, with its
> scope: nothing is conceded yet because nothing has been claimed]`
>
> Ninety-four posts go, of which the retraining scheme will place sixty-one; the remaining
> thirty-three are named in the scheme's own documents. `[the cost, in the main clause, with the
> number the port would rather not print]`
>
> For all the safety gain, the two facts fall on the same people: those not injured next year are,
> in the main, those whose jobs end this year. `[for all + noun phrase, and the main clause is the
> uncomfortable one]`
>
> The committee supports the programme, albeit with a recommendation that the scheme be extended
> to the thirty-three. `[albeit + a phrase, and the position survives the concession because the
> concession has a number in it]`

**Questions about the writer's choices:**

1. The first two paragraphs concede nothing. **Why not?**
2. Paragraph three puts the cost in the main clause. **What would the reverse have read as?**
3. The last sentence concedes with *albeit* and a recommendation. **What makes that concession
   credible rather than decorative?**

### 8B · The anti-model

> While the programme regrettably entails some reduction in headcount, the substantial safety benefits, which are considerable, clearly outweigh any short-term disruption, and the port remains fully committed to supporting all affected colleagues throughout this transition.

One sentence, thirty-six words, one concession with no number in it, and the cost in the
subordinate clause.
**Rewrite it with the cost in a main clause and a number in every claim.**

### 8C · Plan

> the gain, with its scope → the cost, in a main clause, with its number → the concession in

# STUDY FILE · The concession you make by accident

### 9A · But, but, but 🔊 Track 6.4

Learners concede constantly and almost always with *but*, which puts the concession in the first
half and the position in the second — the right way round — and then do it four times in a
paragraph, so that the listener stops being able to tell which half is the position.

| | |
|---|---|
| **what you say** | *It is good but expensive, but it works, but not always* |
| **what the listener hears** | four halves and no position |
| **what is missing** | one concession, in a different structure, used once |
| **the fix** | three structures, one each: *although*, *albeit*, *granted* |

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# The standing position

**Option one:** state the safety case. Injuries have fallen by seventy-eight per cent, that is
thirty-two people a year, and everything else is a transition cost.

**Option two:** state the job losses first and the safety case second, because the cost is certain
and concentrated and the benefit is statistical and spread.

**Option three:** one concessive sentence carrying both, published as the standing position, with
the numbers for each half printed beside it and the thirty-three named as a number.

### 11A · The evidence

> **FIGURE `fig_b22_u06_p11_v06` · V6 Data Visual · full page**

🔊 **Track 6.6.** Piet de Wit, stevedore, thirty-four years, 30 seconds: *"I have carried two men
off this quay. I am not going to stand here and tell you the injury figures do not matter, and I
notice nobody expects me to, which is its own kind of insult. What I want is the sentence written
the other way round once — the cost in the main clause, the gain in the subordinate — so that I
know somebody in that room has had to hold it that way for as long as it takes to type it."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In threes. Use the frame, and concede once per turn in a different structure.

> *Granted, ______ . Nonetheless, ______ , albeit ______ .*

### 11C · Decide

Write **three sentences**: the decision, the evidence, and the standing position itself.

### 11D · Model decision

> Option three. Option one is true and will be read as a refusal to look; option two is true and
> will be read as a refusal to decide. The single sentence is harder to write and is the only
> version that survives being quoted by either side, because both halves are in it and neither is
> in a subordinate clause by default. Naming the thirty-three as a number is the part that costs
> the port something, which is what makes the rest of it believable.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** The committee published one sentence with the cost in the main clause and the
> gain in a *for all* phrase, and printed both sets of figures beside it. The union quoted it
> unaltered in its own newsletter, which had not happened in nine years of the programme.
>
> Then something nobody predicted: take-up of the retraining scheme rose from fifty-one per cent
> to eighty-four. The scheme had not changed. What had changed was that the position named
> thirty-three people it would not place, and the sixty-one who could be placed now had a reason
> to believe the number applied to somebody else.

**One question. You do not have to answer it out loud.**

> The port conceded something and gained something. What exactly did it concede?
