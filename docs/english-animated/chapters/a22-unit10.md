## Part 3 · Grammar Lab 1 ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### The small words that decide what you mean

### 3A · Notice

Look at the four sentences in Part 2.

1. Sentences 3 and 4 differ by one word. **Which town is each about?**
2. In sentence 1, *a story* — why not *the story*?
3. **When do you use no article at all?** Try: *Water came in.* / *The water came in.*
4. *this town*, *that town*, *such a town*. **What does each one add?**
5. Try *"It's story about the town."* **What is missing, and does it matter?**

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_a22_u10_p03_v09` · V9 Grammar Visual · scope**

Now look at the second picture. **It runs from no article to *the*.
Where does a thing stop being any example and become **this** one?**

> **FIGURE `fig_a22_u10_p03_v09b` · V9 Grammar Visual · ladder**

### 3C · Know

| | Use it when | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **no article** | general, plural or uncountable | *Water came in.* · *Towns change.* |
| **a / an** | one of several, first mention | *It's **a** story about **a** town.* |
| **the** | we both know which one | ***The** water came in.* · ***The** story of this town.* |
| **this / that** | pointing, near or far | ***This** town* · ***that** winter* |
| **such** | of that kind | ***Such** a story.* |
| **own** | emphasising possession | *her **own** kitchen* |

> **Abstract nouns are the hard case.** Usually no article when general, *the* when specified.
> *Truth matters.* · ***The** truth about that night.* · *Memory is unreliable.* ·
> ***The** memory of it.*

> **First mention takes *a*; second mention takes *the*.**
> *They have offered **a** campaign. **The** campaign would run for three years.*

> **Watch out.** Dropping an article is not an informal option in English. It changes the
> meaning or breaks the sentence.
> ✗ *It's story about town.*

## Part 5 · Reading ◆ **A · THE WORLD**

### 5A · Predict

A feature article. The title is **"The town that came back, and the seven people who did not."**
**What do you expect the writer to be doing?** One sentence.

### 5B · Read — one hundred and twenty seconds

---

**The town that came back, and the seven people who did not**

The campaign is good. I want to begin there, because the easy piece to write about Ballinmore
this autumn is the cynical one and it would also be wrong.

The photographs are of real people doing real things and nobody is pretending the hall is
finished. The line — *the town that came back* — is three years of work by people who were not
paid for most of it, and the agency did not invent it. Somebody in the town said it first, in the
hall, in February, and nobody wrote down who.

Here is what it leaves out. Of the fifty-one households surveyed, seven have gone. Two sold, two
were bought out, and three are simply not here and the houses are shut. Seven out of fifty-one is
fourteen per cent, which is the figure that does not appear in the brochure, and I understand why
it does not: nobody markets a place with a number like that, and the people who left are not
available to be photographed.

The town did come back. It came back smaller, and it is carrying a sea wall decision it has not
made and a hall it has decided to keep without quite saying why.

This is not an objection to the campaign. It is a note that the story is true and the story is
also an edit, and that a place which has spent a year insisting on accurate times and honest
reporting is about to agree to a sentence about itself that it would not have accepted about
anybody else.

---

### 5C · Answer

1. Why does the writer say the cynical piece would be wrong?
2. Where did the campaign line come from?
3. **How many households have gone, and what happened to each group?**
4. What percentage is that, and why does the writer say it is not in the brochure?
5. **What does the writer mean by "it came back smaller"?**
6. What is the writer's last point, and who is it aimed at?

### 5D · Vocabulary in context

### 8A · Read the model

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# A sentence about yourselves

The agency will run *the town that came back* for three years. It would bring about nine thousand
visitors a year. Seven of the fifty-one households have gone.

- **Agree.** It is true. The town did come back, and the money would pay for the drain.
- **Refuse.** Eimear says it is a story about what happened to the town told by people it did not
  happen to, and that the town spent a year insisting on accuracy.
- **Agree, with a page.** The campaign runs. The town writes one page of the brochure, in its own
  words, including the seven.

### 11A · The evidence

1. **The brochure page** from Part 5E — and the numbers that are not on it.
2. **The chart** — households, before, after, and now.
3. **The voice.** One of the seven, by phone, twenty seconds: *"I've no objection at all. I'd
   like to be in it, actually. I'd like somebody to put in writing that we were there, because
   in about ten years the only record of it will be the brochure, and we are not in the
   brochure."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In threes. Everybody says one. Use the frame.

> *I think the town should ______ , because ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *identity · truth · story · reputation · community · belong · outsider · genuine*

### 11C · Decide

Write **two sentences**. Choose, and say why.

> I think the town should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> Agree, with the page, and put the seven on it by name with their permission. The line is true
> and refusing it costs the drain; but a town that has spent a year arguing about eleven-minute
> clocks and forty-one-word notices cannot then accept a sentence about itself without a footnote.
> The page is the footnote. It also solves the thing the woman on the phone actually asked for,
> which nobody in that room had thought to ask her about.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** They agreed, with the page. The agency accepted in one meeting and was,
> by every account, relieved — it had expected a fight about the line itself.
>
> Six of the seven agreed to be named. The seventh asked not to be, and the page says *and one
> household who has asked not to be named*, which Eimear says is the best sentence on it.
>
> The campaign ran. Visitor numbers were about six thousand in the first year, not nine. The
> drain was funded in the second year, partly from visitor levy and mostly from the county, who
> had been going to fund it anyway and had not said so.

**They got the page. The numbers were two thirds of the promise. And the money was coming
regardless.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say what the page is actually for.
