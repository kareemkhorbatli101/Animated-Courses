## Part 3 · Grammar Lab 1 ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### Saying which one you mean

### 3A · Notice

Look at the four sentences in Part 2.

1. In sentence 1, could you take out *who writes the rota*? **What would be left?**
2. In sentence 3, why *where* and not *who*?
3. Sentences 1 and 4 both pick one thing out of several. **Out of what, in each case?**
4. *who*, *which*, *where*. **Which one is for people, which for things, which for places?**
5. Try *"Mr Tan is the person which writes the rota."* **What is wrong?**

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_a21_u04_p03_v09` · V9 Grammar Visual**

Now look at the second picture. **A relative clause is a bracket. Where does it open and
where does it close?**

> **FIGURE `fig_a21_u04_p03_v09b` · V9 Grammar Visual · scope`**

### 3C · Know

| | Use it for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **who** | people | *the trader **who** opens the gate* |
| **which** | things | *the van **which** brings the ice* |
| **that** | people or things, and more common in speech | *the person **that** has the key* |
| **where** | places | *the office **where** you report it* |

> **The clause comes straight after the noun it describes.**
> ✓ *The person **who has the key** is Mr Tan.*
> ✗ *The person is Mr Tan who has the key.*

> **Watch out.** Do not say the subject twice.
> ✗ *the man who **he** opens the gate* ✓ *the man who opens the gate*

> **Why it matters.** There are twelve people in this market. *The man* tells me nothing.
> *The man who has the cold store key* tells me exactly one.

### 3D · Use — controlled

## Part 5 · Reading ◆ **A · THE WORLD**

### 5A · Predict

A personal essay by a market manager. The title is **"Twelve stalls and one corner."**
**What problem do you expect him to describe?** One sentence.

### 5B · Read — one hundred seconds

---

**Twelve stalls and one corner**

I have been the manager here for nine years. People think the job is the rota. It is not. The
rota takes an hour on a Sunday. The job is the corner.

There is one corner pitch. It is the pitch that everybody who walks in from the car park sees
first, and the trader who has it takes about forty per cent more than the trader who is six metres
further in. It came free in March.

Three people want it. One is the trader who has been here longest and has never asked me for
anything. One is the trader who pays the most and is, if I am honest, the most reliable person in
this market. One is a woman who started eleven months ago, who is on her own with two children,
and who is six metres further in.

My predecessor had a rule: longest serving. It is a good rule because it cannot be argued with
and a bad rule because it rewards nothing except staying.

I have read the handbook. The handbook says the manager decides. That is the sentence that tells
you the handbook was written by somebody who has never had to.

---

### 5C · Answer

1. How long has the writer been the manager?
2. What does he say the job really is?
3. How much more does the corner pitch take?
4. **Describe the three people who want it, in one sentence each.**
5. What was his predecessor's rule, and what does he say is wrong with it?
6. What is his complaint about the handbook?

### 5D · Vocabulary in context

Guess from the text. Then check.

> a pitch · six metres further in · my predecessor · rewards nothing except · if I am honest

### 5E · The counter-text

> **FIGURE `fig_a21_u04_p05_v05` · V5 Annotated Realia`**

**Read the market handbook extract and answer.**

1. Which section covers pitch allocation?
2. **What exactly does the handbook say the manager must do before deciding?**
3. How many days' notice must traders be given?
4. One sentence has been crossed out and initialled. What did it say?

### 5F · Two texts

**Put those two facts together. What has the writer left out, and why might he have?**

### 8A · Read the model

> Mr Tan is the manager who writes the rota and decides who gets which pitch. He is in the office
> by the gate from six. `[the role, then where and when — a newcomer can act on this]`
>
> Nurul is the inspector who arrives without warning. Do not try to be friendly with her; she is
> not here for that, and she is honest about it. `[one thing not to do, and why]`
>
> Zul drives the van which brings the ice. If the ice is late, it is almost never Zul.
> `[a warning about blame, not about the person]`

**Questions about the writer's choices:**

1. Each paragraph gives one thing you **should not** do. Why is that more useful than praise?
2. The last line defends somebody who was not accused. **Why?**

### 8B · The anti-model

> There are many people working here. Mr Tan is the manager and he is very nice. Nurul is the
> inspector. Zul drives the van. Everybody is friendly and works very hard as a team.

Correct English, and useless on a first morning. **Name two questions it does not answer.**

### 8C · Plan

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# One corner, three names

The corner pitch takes forty per cent more than the pitches six metres in. Three traders want it.

- **Siti.** Thirty-one years. Has never asked for anything. Would not ask now if you did not offer.
- **Mr Lim.** Pays the most, never late, the most reliable trader in the market. Also already
  does well.
- **Nurul's cousin Farah.** Eleven months. On her own with two children. Six metres in, and that
  six metres is most of what is wrong with her takings.

### 11A · The evidence

1. **The handbook extract** from Part 5E — and the sentence that was crossed out.
2. **The chart** — takings by position in the market, over twelve months.
3. **The voice.** Siti, fifteen seconds: *"Give it to the girl. I've had thirty-one years of
   people walking past me and I've done all right."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In threes. Everybody says one. Use the frame.

> *I think the pitch should go to ______ , because ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *fair · longest · reliable · needs it · rewards · rule · honest · team*

### 11C · Decide

Write **two sentences**. Choose, and say why.

> I think the pitch should go to ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> It should go to Farah, and Mr Tan should say out loud, at the committee, that he is not using
> the longest-serving rule and why. A rule you break quietly is worse than no rule. If he says it
> in the room, the next manager has something to argue with; if he does not, the next three
> corners go to whoever shouts.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** It went to Farah. The committee agreed in eleven minutes because Siti spoke
> first and nobody wanted to speak after her.
>
> Mr Lim did not complain and has not spoken to Mr Tan about anything except deliveries since.
>
> Farah's takings went up thirty-one per cent and she now opens at four, which she could not
> afford to do before because she was not taking enough to pay somebody to watch the children.

**It worked. And it cost him the trader who pays the most.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say what you would have said to Mr Lim.
