### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_a22_u02_p03_v09` · V9 Grammar Visual · mirror**

Now look at the second picture. **The ladder runs from a finished result to an activity with no
end in sight. Where does *for eight months* sit?**

> **FIGURE `fig_a22_u02_p03_v09b` · V9 Grammar Visual · ladder**

### 3C · Know

| | Shape | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **present perfect simple** | *have* + past participle | the **result**, and how many · *We'**ve done** the roof. I'**ve phoned** four times.* |
| **present perfect continuous** | *have been* + *-ing* | the **activity**, and how long · *We'**ve been doing** the roof since June.* |

> **The question each one answers.**
> *How many?* → simple. *How long?* → continuous.

> **Some verbs have no continuous.** *know, believe, own, belong, cost, mean*.
> ✓ *I've known him for years.* ✗ *I've been knowing him.*

> **Both take *for* and *since*.** *for* + a length, *since* + a point.
> You met this in A2.1 Unit 1 and it has not changed.

> **Watch out.** The continuous dislikes numbers.
> ✗ *I've been phoning him four times.* ✓ *I've phoned him four times.* ·
> ✓ *I've been phoning him all week.*

### 8A · Read the model

> The roof is finished. Two of the four windows are in. `[what is done, with numbers — and this
> comes first, because it is the only part the reader can rely on]`
>
> We have been waiting for plasterboard since June, which is twelve weeks. The supplier changed
> in July and the order has been re-placed twice. `[what is ongoing, with a length and the actual
> cause]`
>
> The floor has not started and will not start until the plasterboard arrives, so any date I give
> you for the floor is a date for the plasterboard. `[what has not started, and an unusually
> honest sentence about estimates]`

**Questions about the writer's choices:**

1. The numbers are in the first paragraph and the lengths are in the second. **Why that way?**
2. The last sentence refuses to give a date. **Is that unhelpful?**

### 8B · The anti-model

> Work is progressing well and we are pleased with how things are going. There have been some
> delays due to circumstances beyond our control but we are working hard to get back on track
> and hope to be finished soon.

Correct English, and a reader learns nothing. **Name three things it does not say.**

### 8C · Plan

> what is finished, with numbers → what is ongoing, with a length and a cause →
> what has not started → what any date actually depends on

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# Her footage, their broadcast

A national news channel has been using Aoife's phone footage of the flood for eight months.
Nobody asked her. The coverage has brought in about 90,000 in donations to the town.

- **Say nothing.** The money is real. She is eighteen and does not want a fight.
- **Ask for a credit.** Her name on the footage. No money. It is still her work.
- **Ask to be paid.** The channel has a rate card for user footage. It is about 250 a clip,
  and they have used four.

### 11A · The evidence

1. **The progress report** from Part 5E — what the donations have actually paid for.
2. **The chart** — donations over the eight months, against each broadcast.
3. **The voice.** Aoife, twenty seconds: *"I've been watching it on the television since
   January. I don't mind. What I mind is that my mother is in one of those clips and nobody
   asked her either, and she is not eighteen and she did not film anything."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In threes. Everybody says one. Use the frame.

> *I think Aoife should ______ , because ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *fair · her work · donations · credit · ask · permission · progress · funding*

### 11C · Decide

Write **two sentences**. Choose, and say why.

> I think Aoife should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> She should ask for the credit and raise her mother separately, in writing, as a different
> question. Mixing the two makes both easy to refuse: the credit is a small thing a producer can
> grant in an afternoon, and the consent is a thing a lawyer has to look at. Asked together they
> will be answered together, which means slowly.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** She asked for the credit by email on a Tuesday and had it by Friday. Her
> name has been on the footage since.
>
> She raised her mother in a separate letter. That took eleven weeks and three people, and ended
> with the clip being edited rather than removed, which her mother says she is fine with and
> Aoife is not sure about.
>
> She has not asked to be paid. She says that if she had asked for money first, she does not
> think she would have got the credit.

**The small ask worked in three days. The important one took eleven weeks and was half met.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say whether she should ask for the money now.
