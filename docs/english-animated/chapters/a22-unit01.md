## Part 3 · Grammar Lab 1 ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### If it happened again

### 3A · Notice

Look at the four sentences in Part 2.

1. In sentence 1, has it happened again? **So why is the verb in the past?**
2. Sentence 2 says *if I were you*. **Not *was*. Why?**
3. Sentence 3 compares two bad outcomes. **Which does he prefer, and is that a fact or a value?**
4. **First conditional or second?** *If it rains, we'll move the car.* / *If it flooded again,
   we'd move it.* What is the difference in how likely each one is?
5. Try *"If it would happen again, I'd move it."* **What is wrong?**

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_a22_u01_p03_v09` · V9 Grammar Visual · branch**

Now look at the second picture. **The ladder runs from certain to impossible.
Where does the second conditional sit?**

> **FIGURE `fig_a22_u01_p03_v09b` · V9 Grammar Visual · ladder**

### 3C · Know

| | Shape | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **first conditional** | *if* + present , *will* | a real possibility · *If it floods, we'**ll** move.* |
| **second conditional** | *if* + **past** , *would* | unlikely, imagined, or hypothetical · *If it **flooded** again, we'**d** move.* |

> **The past tense is not about time.** It is about distance. The further a thing is from
> happening, the further back the verb goes.

> ***were*, not *was*, after *if I*.** *If I **were** you* is the fixed form. *If I was you*
> is heard everywhere and is still the one to avoid in writing.

> **would in four jobs:** *I'd move it* (result) · *I'd rather* (preference) ·
> *Would you mind* (request) · *I'd have gone* (a past you cannot change)

> **Watch out.** ✗ *If it would happen* ✓ *If it happened*
> *would* lives in the result half, never in the *if* half.

### 3D · Use — controlled

> If it (1) __________ (happen) again, Donal (2) __________ (move) the car on the Wednesday.
> If Eimear (3) __________ (be) ready, she (4) __________ (not lose) the kitchen floor. If I
> (5) __________ (be) you, I (6) __________ (not wait) for the council. And if the wall
> (7) __________ (be) two feet higher, none of us (8) __________ (be) having this conversation.

### 3E · Use — guided

## Part 5 · Reading ◆ **A · THE WORLD**

### 5A · Predict

An interview transcript with a resident. The title is
**"I had sandbags in the shed since 2019."**
**What do you expect her to say about them?** One sentence.

### 5B · Read — one hundred seconds

---

**"I had sandbags in the shed since 2019"**
*Eimear Nolan, 14 Strand Road*

**When did you know it was coming in?**
About ten. The drain in the yard started going the wrong way. That is the thing nobody tells you.
It does not come in the door first.

**You had sandbags.**
I had sandbags. I had had them in the shed since 2019, when there was a warning that came to
nothing, and on the Thursday I thought about putting them out and I did not, because Donal had
moved his car and everyone was being funny about it and I did not want to be the second one.

**Would you do it differently?**
I would put them out. But I want to be careful here, because that is easy to say in September.
If you asked me in January, with a forecast and a shed and everybody watching, I honestly do not
know that I would.

**What would you change, then?**
I would not wait to see what anybody else did. That is the one. Not the sandbags — the waiting.

**And twelve centimetres.**
Twelve centimetres is nothing until it is in your kitchen. It takes eight months to dry a floor.
You cannot put a new one down until it is dry and you cannot tell when it is dry, so you wait,
and then you put one down and sometimes you are wrong.

---

### 5C · Answer

1. How did she know the water was coming in?
2. How long had the sandbags been in the shed, and why had she kept them?
3. **Why did she decide not to put them out on the Thursday?**
4. What does she say she would change, and what does she say she would **not** promise?
5. Why does it take eight months to dry a floor?
6. **What is the difference between "the sandbags" and "the waiting"?**

### What I would do differently · 90 words

> **FIGURE `fig_a22_u01_p08_v06` · V6 Data Visual**

### 8A · Read the model

> If it happened again I would not wait to see what the neighbours did. That is the only thing I
> would change and it is not the thing people expect me to say. `[the change, named, and the
> surprise acknowledged]`
>
> I would still keep the sandbags in the shed rather than by the door, because the shed is dry
> and the door is not, and a wet sandbag is a bag of mud. `[something kept, with a technical
> reason, which proves she has thought about it]`
>
> I would not promise to be braver. I would promise to be two hours earlier. `[the honest
> limit — and it is specific and achievable]`

**Questions about the writer's choices:**

1. The second paragraph defends something she did **right**. **Why include it?**
2. The last line refuses to promise courage. **Is that weakness or precision?**

### 8B · The anti-model

> If it happened again I would be much more prepared. I would act faster and I would not make the
> same mistakes. We have all learned a lot from this experience and we would all do things
> differently next time.

Correct English, and there is nothing in it. **Name two things it does not say.**

### 8C · Plan

> what happened, in one line → the one thing you would change → one thing you would keep,
> with a reason → what you will not promise

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# The wall, or the three houses

There is 1.8 million. It is enough to build the sea wall badly along its whole length, or to
move the three nearest houses and do nothing else.

- **The wall.** Protects fourteen houses to the standard that failed in January, plus about
  thirty centimetres.
- **The three houses.** Buys and demolishes them. The three families are rehoused and the gap
  becomes a flood meadow. The other eleven houses are no safer than they were.
- **Neither, yet.** Spend 90,000 on a proper survey and come back in a year with a number that
  means something.

### 11A · The evidence

1. **The policy summary** from Part 5E — and what is not covered.
2. **The chart** — water height at each house on the night, against floor level.
3. **The voice.** One of the three, twenty seconds: *"I would take the money. I want to be clear
   that I would take it, because I think people will say afterwards that we were pushed out, and
   we would not be. We would be glad."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In threes. Everybody says one. Use the frame.

> *If they built ______ , then ______ . So I think they should ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *risk · at risk · damage · protect · recover · cost · safe · in time*

### 11C · Decide

Write **two sentences**. Choose, and say why.

> I think they should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> They should do the survey. Both of the other options are being chosen on the basis of one
> night's water, measured afterwards by people wading through it, and a wall built to the wrong
> height is worse than no wall because it is believed. Ninety thousand to find out is cheap
> against 1.8 million to find out you were wrong.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** They did the survey. It took fourteen months rather than twelve and cost
> 118,000.
>
> It found that the water in January had not come over the wall at all. It had come **up through
> the old storm drain**, which was built in 1961 and connects to the harbour below the wall line.
> A higher wall would have made no difference to eleven of the fourteen houses.
>
> Fixing the drain is costed at 340,000. There is now an argument about what to do with the rest
> of the money, and two of the three families have said they no longer want to move.

**The survey was right. And it cost fourteen months, during which nothing was built.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say what you would do with the remaining 1.34
> million.
