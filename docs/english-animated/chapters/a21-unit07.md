Now look at the second picture. **The sentence has three slots. Which one can be deleted?**

> **FIGURE `fig_a21_u07_p03_v09b` · V9 Grammar Visual · scope**

### 3C · Know

| | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **present passive** | *am / is / are* + past participle | *The garlic **is grown** in China.* |
| **past passive** | *was / were* + past participle | *The sign **was put up** last year.* |
| **naming the agent** | **by** + who or what | *It was built **by** my grandfather.* |

> **Three reasons to use the passive, and only one of them is suspicious:**
> 1. Nobody knows who → *The sign was put up last year.*
> 2. It does not matter who → *The garlic is grown in China.*
> 3. Somebody would rather you did not ask → *Mistakes were made.*

> **Watch out.** The participle is not the infinitive.
> ✗ *is grow* ✓ *is grown* · ✗ *was build* ✓ *was built*

> **Why it matters.** The passive lets you put the important thing first and the person last —
> or nowhere. On a food label, that is a decision, not a grammar choice.

## Part 5 · Reading ◆ **A · THE WORLD**

### 5A · Predict

**What do you expect the article to find?** One sentence.

### 5B · Read — one hundred and ten seconds

---

**The word *local* is not regulated**

There is no law in this country about the word *local*. It is not defined anywhere. A sign saying
LOCAL PRODUCE can be hung above vegetables grown four hundred kilometres away and nothing has
been broken.

We looked at forty-one stalls in one market. Twenty-nine had a sign using the word. Of those
twenty-nine, eleven were selling at least one item that had been imported from another country,
and all eleven were doing it openly: the boxes were stacked behind the stall with the country of
origin printed on the side.

Nobody was hiding anything. That is the interesting part.

When we asked traders what *local* meant, we were given four answers. It meant *grown in
Malaysia*. It meant *not from a supermarket*. It meant *I know the man who brings it*. And, from
one trader who had been there thirty-one years, it meant *you are buying it from me and not from
somebody in an office*.

That last answer is the one the sign is really making. It is not a claim about geography. It is a
claim about who you are handing your money to, and on that measure every one of the twenty-nine
signs was telling the truth.

The boxes, however, are still behind the stall. Somebody chose to put the sign at eye level and
the boxes on the floor, and that choice was made by a person, even if nobody can now remember
which one.

---

### 5C · Answer

1. Is the word *local* defined in law?
2. How many of the forty-one stalls used the word, and how many of those sold imported items?
3. **Why does the writer say "nobody was hiding anything" is the interesting part?**
4. What were the four meanings traders gave?
5. Which meaning does the writer think the sign is really making?
6. What is the writer's last point about the boxes?

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# The sign, or the sourcing

The sign above the stall says LOCAL. Half the produce under it travels four hundred kilometres,
and the garlic comes from China by way of a packer in Selangor. Nothing illegal is happening.

- **Change the sign.** Take the word down. Lose whatever it was bringing in.
- **Change the sourcing.** Buy only Malaysian. Prices go up between nine and fourteen per cent
  and three products disappear for four months of the year.
- **Change what the sign says.** Keep it, and add the distances underneath, in small print.

### 11A · The evidence

1. **The three labels and the delivery record** from Part 5E.
2. **The chart** — where each product is grown, and how far it travels.
3. **The voice.** Siti, twenty seconds: *"I have never said the word local to a customer in
   thirty-one years. Somebody printed it and I let them hang it, which I suppose is the same
   thing."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In threes. Everybody says one. Use the frame.

> *I think they should ______ , because ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *honest · grown · imported · price · belong · roots · foreign · trust*

### 11C · Decide

Write **two sentences**. Choose, and say why.

> I think they should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> They should put the distances up. The feature is right that the sign is a claim about who you
> hand your money to, and that claim is true — but it is being made with a word that sounds like
> geography, and the fix is not to argue about the word. It is to put the four hundred kilometres
> next to it and let the customer decide what *local* meant all along.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** They printed the distances on small cards and put one in front of each
> crate. It took an afternoon.
>
> For three weeks almost nobody read them. Then a food writer photographed the cards and the
> photograph was shared about nine thousand times, mostly by people who had never been to the
> market, and for a month the stall was busier than it had ever been.
>
> Two other traders copied the cards. Four were angry about it, because customers had started
> asking them for distances they did not know.

**It worked, and then it worked too well, and it made four people's lives harder.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say what you would say to the four.
