### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_b13_u03_p03_v09` · V9 Grammar Visual · scope**

### 3C · Know

| | Followed by | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ***although* / *though*** | a clause | *Although he is right, nobody else spoke.* |
| ***despite* / *in spite of*** | a noun or *-ing* | *Despite being right, he lost the room.* |
| ***however* / *nevertheless***| a new sentence | *He is right. However, so was Thanh.* |
| ***whereas*** | a clause, for a parallel | *Marcus argues, whereas Thanh writes.* |
| ***yet* / *still*** | a clause, joined | *He is right, yet nobody agrees.* |

> **One concession word per sentence.** English does not allow *although… but…* or
> *despite… however…*. The second one is the one to delete.
> ✗ *Although he is right, but he is loud.* ✓ *Although he is right, he is loud.*

> ***Though* goes where *although* goes, and in one more place: the end.**
> *He is right. He is loud, though.* — informal, and extremely common in speech.

> ***Whereas* is not a concession at all.** It sets two things side by side without saying
> either is a problem. *Although* says the first one is in the way of the second.

> **Why it matters.** An argument that concedes nothing reads as if it has not understood the
> other side. An argument that concedes in the wrong clause gives away the thing it meant to keep.

## Part 5 · Reading ◆ **A · THE WORLD**

### 5A · Predict

A report extract, titled **"Participation: a note on measurement."**
**What do you expect it to recommend?** Two sentences.

### 5B · Read — two minutes and a quarter

---

**FACULTY OF ARTS · TEACHING QUALITY REVIEW · EXTRACT 4 OF 11**
**Participation: a note on measurement**

Participation is worth fifteen per cent of the mark in sixty-one of our units. In fifty-eight of
those, the published criterion is some version of *contributes regularly to seminar discussion*.
We asked eleven tutors what they record. Nine record nothing during the class and reconstruct a
figure in week twelve.

We then recorded airtime in nine seminars. The distribution is not unusual and it is not
defensible. In a seminar of eleven students over fifty minutes, the top speaker took a median of
17 minutes and the bottom four took a median of 40 seconds between them. The top speaker was
the same person in all nine weeks in seven of the nine groups.

Admittedly, airtime is a crude measure and nobody has proposed grading it directly. Still, it
is the only measure we have of a thing that carries fifteen per cent of a mark, and it says that
in most of our seminars the grade is being distributed on a quantity that four students in
eleven have almost no access to.

The obvious objection is that the quiet students could speak and choose not to. That is true up
to a point. It is also true that in every group we observed, the gap between the top speaker and
the rest widened between week three and week nine, which is not what a free choice usually does.

We are not recommending that tutors intervene. Intervention in a seminar is a blunt instrument
and the tutors who do it most are not the ones with the best participation spread. We are
recommending that the criterion be rewritten, because *contributes regularly to seminar
discussion* is a description of a behaviour and what we say we are assessing is a capacity.

One tutor, asked what she would do, said: "I would mark the question they ask, not the number of
times they open their mouth. And yet I know exactly who would lose by that too."

## Part 8 · Writing ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

### An argument that concedes · 180 words

> **FIGURE `fig_b13_u03_p08_v02` · V2 Cutaway**

### 8A · Read the model

> Marcus has read every text and he is right more often than I am. That is not in dispute and
> I am not going to pretend it is. `[the other side at its strongest, in the writer's own voice]`
>
> Admittedly, nobody is stopping the rest of us. The door is not locked and there is no rule
> about who speaks. `[the concession, and it is a real one — it costs the writer something]`
>
> Even so, the gap between him and the rest widened every week from week three, and a gap that
> widens is not a gap people are choosing. `[the turn, with the evidence inside the sentence]`
>
> What would change my mind is a group where the gap stayed flat. I have not seen one, and I
> have been in four. `[the falsifier — what evidence would move the writer]`

**Questions about the writer's choices:**

1. The first paragraph gives Marcus his best case. **What does the writer buy with it?**
2. The concession in paragraph two is true. **Why is it not enough on its own?**
3. The last paragraph names what would change the writer's mind. **What would an argument
   without that line be?**

### 8B · The anti-model

> While it is of course true that all students should feel able to contribute, it is also the
> case that some students are naturally more vocal than others. There are arguments on both
> sides and the issue is a complex one that deserves further consideration by all stakeholders.

Correct English, and it concedes nothing because it claims nothing. **Name the three moves it
is using to avoid taking a position.**

### 8C · Plan

> the other side at its strongest → a real concession → the turn, with evidence →
> what would change your mind

### 8D · Write — 180 words
### 8E · Peer review — three criteria

> ☐ Is the other side stated at its strongest, before any disagreement?
> ☐ Does the concession cost the writer something?
> ☐ Is there a falsifier — something that would change the writer's mind?

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# The best voice in the room, or the ten who are not using theirs

Marcus speaks for seventeen of fifty minutes and is usually right. Four students have spoken
for forty seconds in total across nine weeks. Priya has to mark all eleven on participation.

- **Intervene.** Cap contributions, go round the table, direct a question by name. It works
  immediately and it takes something from the person who prepared.
- **Let it run.** Mark what is said, not how often. Marcus earns his mark and the four get
  what the format gave them.
- **Change the format.** Written first, spoken second — everyone writes two minutes, then reads
  theirs aloud. Nobody is capped and nobody starts from nothing.

### 11A · The evidence

> **FIGURE `fig_b13_u03_p11_v06` · V6 Data Visual**

1. **The report and the transcript** from Part 5 — and the row the tutor left blank.
2. **The chart** — airtime and essay marks for all eleven students.
3. **The voice.** Thanh, thirty seconds: *"I have the answer about forty seconds after the
   moment has gone. I am not nervous and I am not unprepared. I am slow in the way that writing
   is slow, and the seminar is the one part of this degree that is not."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In fours. Everybody speaks twice. Use the frame.

> *______ (concession), ______ . And yet ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *argument · dissent · objection · position · premise · compromise ·
common ground · dominate · defer · the floor*

### 11C · Decide

Write **three sentences**. Choose, say why, and name what it costs.

> I think Priya should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .
> What this costs is ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> Change the format. Two minutes of writing before any discussion, then round the table once,
> reading what you wrote. It is the only one of the three options that does not require Priya to
> make a judgement about a person in front of the room, and the only one that gives the slow
> students the thing they are actually short of, which is not courage but time. Marcus loses
> four minutes of the hour. He will not notice.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** Priya changed the format in week ten. In the two remaining seminars, the
> number of students who spoke went from four to nine and from four to eleven. The median
> contribution was shorter and, on her own marking, better.
>
> Marcus's airtime fell from seventeen minutes to six. He came round to it in the second week,
> having spent the first one visibly annoyed, and said afterwards that he had not known most of
> the group had read the text.
>
> The faculty did not adopt it. The criterion still says *contributes regularly to seminar
> discussion*, because changing it requires a committee that meets twice a year and the paper
> missed the November meeting by four days.

**The format change worked in two weeks. The sentence that caused the problem is still there,
and it will be there next year, because of a deadline nobody argued about.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say what should happen to the criterion.
