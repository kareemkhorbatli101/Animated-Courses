## Part 3 · Grammar Lab 1 ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### Saying it happened without saying when

### 3A · Notice

Look at the four sentences in Part 2 again.

1. What two parts make this form of the verb?
2. Does any of the four say when it happened?
3. Where does *ever* go? Where does *never* go?
4. What is the difference between *I haven't done it* and *I didn't do it*?

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_a12_u08_p03_v09a` · V9 Grammar Visual**
> A timeline with the whole of a life shaded as one band, from birth to NOW, and no point marked
> anywhere inside it. Beside it, a second timeline with a single dot on it labelled *last
> Tuesday*. The first is the present perfect; the second is the past simple; the difference is
> whether there is a dot.

### 3C · Know

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **experience** | *have / has* + past participle | *I have worked abroad. She has done this for 23 years.* |
| **the question** | *Have you ever…?* | *Have you ever worked nights?* |
| **never** | one negative, already | *I have never been out of the country.* |
| **not yet** | it has not happened **so far** | *I haven't done the test yet.* |

> **No date, ever.** The moment you add a date — *last year*, *in 2019*, *when I was twenty* — you
> must change to the past simple. The present perfect and a date cannot stand in the same
> sentence.

> **FIGURE `fig_a12_u08_p03_v09b` · V9 Grammar Visual**
> Three columns of past participles: the ones that are the same as the past simple, the ones that
> are different, and the ones that are the same as the infinitive. *Been* and *gone* are drawn
> separately with a small person in each, one of them back and one of them not.

| | |
|---|---|
| **same as the past** | *worked · lived · made · bought · had* |
| **different** | *do → did → done · see → saw → seen · go → went → gone* |
| **been or gone** | *She's been to Jasper* — she is back · *She's gone to Jasper* — she is not |
| **already / yet** | *already* in positives, *yet* in negatives and questions |

> **Why it matters.** Every interview, every introduction and every conversation with somebody
> you have just met runs on this tense, and it is the first tense in this book that has no
> equivalent in most languages.

### 3D · Use — controlled

## Part 6 · Vocabulary Lab 2 ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### Been, gone, done, seen

> **FIGURE `fig_a12_u08_p06_v03a` · V3 Process Strip · six panels**
> Six past participles, one per panel, each drawn as a small scene: *been* (back), *gone* (not
> back), *done* (finished), *seen* (witnessed), *had* (possessed), *made* (produced). The first
> two are the same journey and they end in different places.

### 6A · The ones that catch everybody

| | |
|---|---|
| **been / gone** | *been* means you came back; *gone* means you are still there |
| **already / yet** | *already* in positives; *yet* at the end of negatives and questions |
| **ever** | only in questions: *Have you ever…?* — never in a statement |
| **never** | already negative, so no *haven't* with it |

### 6B · Say them

> ever · never · always · already · yet · been · gone · done · seen

### 6C · Chunk

1. **______** you ever been abroad?

4. I've **______** done it. *(and before you asked)*
5. **______ ______ .** *(two words — it has not happened so far)*
6. **______ ______ .** *(two words — it happened exactly one time)*

## Part 7 · Pronunciation & Fluency Lab ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### The *have* that is almost not there

> **FIGURE `fig_a12_u08_p07_v10` · V10 Phonetics Visual**
> Five present perfect phrases written at full length and then as they are said, with *have* and
> *has* shaded almost away. In two of the five, the word has disappeared into the pronoun
> completely.

### 7A · Hear

> **I have** → *I've*, one sound · **he has** → *he's* · **you have** → *you've*
> **have you** → *hav-yuh*, and the *h* almost goes · **has she** → *uz-shee*
> In a statement, *have* is never stressed. If it is, you are arguing.

### 7B · Say

Say each one three times. Then say this:

> *I've never been there, and he's already gone.* — two perfects, four beats.

### 7C · Make it mean something

Say these two:

> *I've done it.* — ordinary. *Have* has almost no sound at all.
> *I HAVE done it.* — you are contradicting somebody who said you had not.

**The full form is an argument.** Learners who always use the full form sound permanently
defensive. Which one would you use to answer a friendly question?

### 7D · Fluency 20 ⏺

Twenty seconds: **six things you have done in your life, no dates.** Three times, faster.

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# One box on a form

The company promotes about nine sleeping-car attendants a year. The form has one experience box
and it asks for years.

**Option one:** keep it. Years are cheap to check and impossible to argue with.

**Option two:** replace it with a checklist of twelve things — *have you ever run a service alone,
have you ever dealt with a medical event, have you ever worked in a language that is not your
first*. Takes four minutes to fill in and two hours a year to score.

**Option three:** a day's trial on a real train. Expensive, accurate, and it cannot be done for
more than about thirty people a year.

### 11A · The evidence

> **FIGURE `fig_a12_u08_p11_v04b` · V4 Comparison Pair**
> The two applicants' forms, drawn side by side. **Left:** the years box, 23 against 2, and
> everything else blank. **Right:** the same two people against the twelve-item checklist — eleven
> ticks and nine ticks, and two of the applicant's ticks are things Ruth has never done.
> **Six planted differences**, one of which is how long each form took to fill in.

🔊 **Track 8.6.** Ruth, 20 seconds: *"I've been here twenty-three years. Nineteen of them were the
same year. I'd rather be asked what I've done than how long I've been standing here."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In threes. Use the frame. Everybody says one.

> *I think the company should ______ , because ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *experience · skill · moment · learn a skill · try something new · win · lose*

### 11C · Decide

Write **two sentences**. Choose, and say why.

> I think the company should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> The checklist. Two hours a year is nothing and years of experience is not a measurement, it is
> a proxy for one. The trial is better and it does not scale, and the thing that does not scale
> is the thing that gets quietly dropped in a bad year.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** The company introduced the twelve-item checklist. In the first year the
> promotions went to almost the same nine people.
>
> Then something nobody expected: the checklist was being read by the people filling it in. Four
> attendants asked to be put on night services specifically because there was a line on the form
> about them, and the company had never once been asked that before.

**One question. You do not have to answer it out loud.**

> The checklist did not change who was promoted. What did it change?
