## Part 3 · Grammar Lab 1 ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### Compulsory, forbidden, and neither

### 3A · Notice

Look at the four sentences in Part 2 again.

1. Which two mean *this is forbidden*?
2. What does sentence 3 mean — forbidden, or not necessary?
3. What is the form of the verb after *must* and after *have to*?
4. Does *have to* change for *he* and *she*?

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_a12_u07_p03_v09a` · V9 Grammar Visual**
> A two-by-two square. Across the top, *positive* and *negative*. Down the side, *must* and *have
> to*. Three of the four boxes mean roughly the same thing. The fourth, *don't have to*, means
> something completely different, and it is drawn in a different colour.

### 3C · Know

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **compulsory** | *must* / *have to* | *You must wear a seatbelt. We have to check them.* |
| **forbidden** | *mustn't* | *You mustn't touch it. It mustn't be locked.* |
| **not necessary** | *don't have to* | *You don't have to sign it.* |
| **the difference** | *must* is the speaker's rule; *have to* is somebody else's | *I must stop smoking* / *I have to wear this.* |

> **Mustn't and don't have to are not opposites of each other.** *Mustn't* is a wall.
> *Don't have to* is an open door that you may walk through or not. Getting these two the wrong
> way round is the commonest serious error in this unit and it changes the meaning completely.

> **FIGURE `fig_a12_u07_p03_v09b` · V9 Grammar Visual**
> *Must* drawn as a rule with the speaker inside it, and *have to* drawn as a rule with the
> speaker outside it and an arrow coming in from off the page. The grammar is nearly identical
> and the source of the obligation is not.

| | |
|---|---|
| **must — from the speaker** | *I must get more sleep. You must come and see us.* |
| **have to — from outside** | *I have to wear a helmet. She has to fill in the form.* |
| **has to** | the only one of the four that changes for *he* and *she* |
| **no past for must** | *I had to* does the work of both |

> **Why it matters.** Every job, every country and every building you enter has a set of these,
> and knowing which of the four you are being given tells you whether to argue.

## Part 5 · Reading ◆ **A · THE WORLD**

### 5A · Predict

The title is **"Four times in eleven years."**
**What do you think has happened four times?**

### 5B · Read — sixty seconds

---

**Four times in eleven years**

There are twenty-two emergency door handles on this train. In eleven years, four have been
pulled.

Three of the four were mistakes. Somebody leaned on one. A child reached one. A passenger thought
it was the light switch. Each time, the train stopped, the crew walked the length of it, and
about fifty minutes were lost.

The fourth was in February, at two in the morning, when a man in car 7 had a seizure and the
nearest crew member was six cars away. A passenger pulled the handle. The train stopped. The crew
came. He is alive.

The company wants to put breakable covers over all twenty-two handles. The covers will stop the
three. They will also cost about eight seconds, and in February eight seconds would probably not
have mattered.

Probably.

---

### 5C · Answer

1. How many handles are there?
2. How many have been pulled?
3. How long is lost each time one is pulled by mistake?
4. What happened in February?
5. **Why does the writer end with the word *probably* on its own?**
6. **The covers stop three problems and create a possible fourth. How would you decide?**

### 5D · Vocabulary in context

Guess from the text. Then check.

> leaned on one · the length of it · the nearest crew member · breakable covers · would not have mattered

### 5E · The counter-text

> **FIGURE `fig_a12_u07_p05_v05` · V5 Annotated Realia**
> The **safety notice** beside the handle: four numbered instructions, a line about the penalty
> for improper use, the year it was printed, and a diagram with three parts. The penalty line is

## Part 7 · Pronunciation & Fluency Lab ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### Two words that became one, again

> **FIGURE `fig_a12_u07_p07_v10` · V10 Phonetics Visual**
> Four obligation phrases written at full length and then as they are said, with the letters that
> disappear shaded out: *have to* → *hafta*, *has to* → *hasta*, *must not* → *mussnt*, *got to* →
> *gotta*.

### 7A · Hear

> **have to** → *hafta* — and the *v* becomes an *f*
> **has to** → *hasta* · **mustn't** → *mussnt*, with no *t* in the middle at all
> **got to** → *gotta* — not in the exam, and in every conversation

### 7B · Say

Say each one three times. Then say this:

> *You have to fill it in, but you mustn't sign it.* — two obligations, four beats.

### 7C · Make it mean something

Say these two:

> *You don't have to come.* — you are free, and you are welcome
> *You mustn't come.* — you are forbidden, and somebody will be upset

**One is kindness and one is a wall.** Learners who swap them do not sound less fluent; they
sound rude. Which is which?

### 7D · Fluency 20 ⏺

Twenty seconds: **six rules of this building — two must, two mustn't, two don't have to.**
Three times, faster.

## Part 8 · Writing ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

### The rules of a place · 50–70 words

### 8A · Read the model

> You have to sign in at the desk. That one is the fire service, not us. `[the rule, and who
> made it]`
>
> You mustn't prop the back door open. It looks harmless and it is the one that matters.
> `[the prohibition, with the reason]`
>
> You don't have to wear the badge upstairs. Nobody does. `[the rule that exists and is not
> enforced, said plainly]`
>
> If you are going to break one of these, break the third. `[the sentence no official notice
> ever contains]`

**Questions about the writer's choices:**

1. *That one is the fire service, not us.* **Why name the source?**
2. *It looks harmless.* **Why admit that about a rule you are enforcing?**
3. The last line tells the reader which rule to break. **Does that undermine the other two?**

### 8B · The anti-model

> You must sign in. You must wear your badge at all times. You must not prop open any door. You must follow all safety instructions. Thank you for your cooperation.

Correct English, correct rules, and it is on the wall of every building on earth and read by
nobody. **Rewrite it so that each rule has a source, and one of them admits it is not enforced.**

### 8C · Plan

> the rule and who made it → the prohibition and the reason → the one nobody follows →
> the honest sentence

### 8D · Write — 50 to 70 words
### 8E · Peer review — two things only

> ☐ Does at least one rule say who made it?

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# Twenty-two red handles

Four pulls in eleven years. Three mistakes, costing about fifty minutes each. One real, in
February, and a man is alive.

**Option one:** fit breakable covers on all twenty-two. Stops the three. Costs about eight
seconds in a real emergency and eleven thousand dollars to fit.

**Option two:** leave them. Three false alarms in eleven years is one every four years, and
fifty minutes is fifty minutes.

**Option three:** fit covers on the eighteen handles in areas with no crew presence, and leave
the four near the crew cars uncovered.

### 11A · The evidence

> **FIGURE `fig_a12_u07_p11_v04b` · V4 Comparison Pair**
> The handle, drawn twice. **Left:** as it is — red, open, and a child's hand could reach it.
> **Right:** with the cover — the same handle behind a clear panel, with the words BREAK GLASS
> and a small hammer on a chain. **Six planted differences**, one of which is how far the nearest
> crew member is.

🔊 **Track 7.6.** Nadia, 20 seconds: *"In February I was six cars away. Somebody I have never met
decided in two seconds to do something that would cost them five hundred dollars if they were
wrong. I would like them to be able to keep doing that."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In threes. Use the frame. Everybody says one.

> *I think the company should ______ , because ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *safety · danger · emergency · rule · law · warning · allowed*

### 11C · Decide

Write **two sentences**. Choose, and say why.

> I think the company should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> Option three, and take the fine off the notice. The covers belong where nobody is watching. The
> five-hundred-dollar line belongs nowhere: it is the only part of this system that makes a
> stranger hesitate for two seconds, and two seconds is the entire thing we are trying to buy.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** The company fitted covers to eighteen handles and reprinted the notice with
> the penalty line in the smallest type instead of the largest.
>
> Then something nobody expected: in the next four years there were two pulls, both real, and
> both at covered handles. The eight seconds were spent and nobody noticed them. What had changed
> was that passengers now believed they were allowed.

**One question. You do not have to answer it out loud.**

> The covers were fitted to stop mistakes. What actually changed the numbers?
