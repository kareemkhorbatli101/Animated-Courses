## Part 3 · Grammar Lab 1 ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### One word, three jobs

### 3A · Notice

Look at the four sentences in Part 2 again.

1. Does *can* change after *I*, *you*, *she* or *they*?
2. What is the form of the verb straight after *can*?
3. How do you make a question with *can*?
4. One sentence is about something a body cannot do. Which?

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_a11_u04_p03_v09a` · V9 Grammar Visual**
> One word, *can*, with three arrows leaving it to three different places: **ability** (a body),
> **permission** (a person who decides), and **prohibition** (a sign with nobody's name on it).
> The same word, three destinations, and the destination is in the situation, not in the word.

### 3C · Know

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **ability** | what a body or a mind does | *I can carry it. I can't carry it up four floors.* |
| **permission** | somebody decided, and they could decide otherwise | *You can use the lift. Students can't.* |
| **prohibition** | a rule, and the person who made it is gone | *You can't smoke here.* |

> **Can never changes.** No **s** for *he* and *she*. No *do* in the question. The verb after it is
> always plain: *can go*, never *can goes* and never *can to go*.

> **FIGURE `fig_a11_u04_p03_v09b` · V9 Grammar Visual**
> Negatives and questions with *can*: *not* joining on behind it to make one word, and *can*
> itself jumping to the front for a question. Nothing else in the sentence moves at all.

| | |
|---|---|
| **–** | I **cannot** use it · I **can't** use it · She **can't** come today |
| **?** | **Can** I leave this here? · **Can** you help me? · **Can** she use the lift? |
| **short answers** | *Yes, you can.* · *No, I'm sorry, I can't.* |

> **The polite layer sits on top.** *Can I…?* is a question. *Could you…?* is the same question
> with the sharp edge taken off. *Is it OK if…?* asks whether the rule allows it rather than
> whether you allow it.

> **Why it matters.** Half of what you need in a building you do not own is permission, and the
> other half is finding out who gives it.

### 3D · Use — controlled

### 5B · Read — sixty seconds

---

**Who put that there?**

Escola Monte has eleven signs. Ana counted them.

Four of them are printed and screwed to the wall. They are about fire and about the law, and the
city put them there. Nobody argues with those four.

Four more are printed and taped. They are about the lift, the toilet and the corridor. The school

## Part 7 · Pronunciation & Fluency Lab ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### /æ/ and /ʌ/, and the *can* you can hardly hear

> **FIGURE `fig_a11_u04_p07_v10` · V10 Phonetics Visual**
> Two mouth cross-sections side by side for /æ/ and /ʌ/, with the tongue lower and further
> forward for the first, and minimal pairs under each.

### 7A · Hear

> **/æ/** — *can · bag · man · hand · that*
> **/ʌ/** — *come · one · but · much · up*

### 7B · Say

Say each group three times. Then say this:

> *I can come, but I can't come up.* — both vowels, twice each, in one sentence.

### 7C · Make it mean something

Say these two:

> *I can go.* — *can* is short and quiet. You hardly hear it.
> *I can't go.* — *can't* is long and loud, and it is the stressed word in the sentence.

**The negative is louder than the positive.** If you cannot hear the *t*, listen to the length
instead. Which one is which?

### 7D · Fluency 20 ⏺

Twenty seconds: **three things you can do and three things you can't do in this building.**
Three times, faster.

## Part 8 · Writing ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

### The rules of a place · 40–60 words

### 8A · Read the model

> You can come in before nine. The door is open from eight. `[what you can do, first]`
>
> You can't smoke anywhere, including the yard. `[the one hard rule, with the gap closed]`
>
> Please be quiet in the corridor on the second floor. There is an exam room. `[the rule, and
> then the reason]`
>
> If you need the lift, ask at reception. Somebody will say yes. `[the rule that is really a
> door, not a wall]`

**Questions about the writer's choices:**

1. The first sentence is about what you **can** do. **Why not start with the hard rule?**
2. *There is an exam room.* **What does giving the reason do to the rule?**
3. The last rule tells you who to ask. **Why is that more useful than the rule itself?**

### 8B · The anti-model

> No smoking. No food. No phones. Be quiet. Keep right. No bags in the corridor. Students can't use the lift.

Correct English, and nobody will read past the third line. **Rewrite it so that two of the seven
are things you can do, and one of them gives a reason.**

### 8C · Plan

> what you can do → the one real prohibition → one rule with a reason → who to ask

### 8D · Write — 40 to 60 words
### 8E · Peer review — two things only

> ☐ Is there at least one sentence with *can* and not *can't*?
> ☐ Is there one reason anywhere in it?

### 8F · Publish

On the door of the classroom. **Keep them up for a week and see which one people actually follow.**

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# The lift

Escola Monte has one lift. The notice says *Staff only*. It has said that for four years.

A student broke the door of the lift in the first year, and that is why the notice is there. That
student finished the course in the same year and left.

Dona Filipa carries a trolley up four floors twice a day. One student this term cannot use the
stairs at all. The school can leave the notice, take it down, or put a key on the lift and give
the key to reception.

### 11A · The evidence

> **FIGURE `fig_a11_u04_p11_v04b` · V4 Comparison Pair**
> The same lift door, drawn twice. **Left:** the notice as it is, taped, with a handwritten line
> under it. **Right:** a small printed sign with a name and a date on it, and a key hook beside
> the door. **Six planted differences**, one of which is the queue on the stairs behind.

🔊 **Track 4.6.** Dona Filipa, 20 seconds: *"I can use the lift. Nobody told me I can. I read the
paper on the door every morning for four years and I take the stairs."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In threes. Use the frame. Everybody says one.

> *I think the lift should be ______ , because ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *staff · student · key · stairs · trolley · broken · allowed*

### 11C · Decide

Write **two sentences**. Choose, and say why.

> I think the lift should be ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> Take the old notice down and put up a new one with a name and a date on it. The rule can stay
> exactly the same. What changes is that there is now somebody to ask, and a rule you can ask
> about is a different kind of rule.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** Ana printed a new notice: *Lift: ask at reception. A. Silva, March.* The rule
> did not change. In the first week eleven people asked, and she said yes to all eleven.
>
> Then something she did not expect: the asking stopped after two weeks, and people simply used
> the lift. Nobody broke it. Ana left the notice up anyway, because the name on it was doing
> something the rule was not.

**One question. You do not have to answer it out loud.**

> The rule did not change and the behaviour did. So what was the rule actually doing before?
