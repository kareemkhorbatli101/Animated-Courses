### A future that is already running

### 3A · Notice

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_a22_u07_p03_v09` · V9 Grammar Visual · mirror**

Now look at the second picture. **It runs from a decision made now to a year that is simply
running. Where does *going to* sit?**

> **FIGURE `fig_a22_u07_p03_v09b` · V9 Grammar Visual · ladder**

### 3C · Know

| | Shape | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **will** | *will* + verb | a decision made as you speak · *I'**ll** take it.* |
| **going to** | *am going to* + verb | an intention you already had · *I'**m going to** apply.* |
| **future continuous** | *will be* + *-ing* | a future that will simply be running · *I'**ll be** taking the boat out at four.* |

> **The future continuous is the least dramatic of the three**, and that is its point.
> It describes a future as **ordinary**, as something already in motion, with no decision
> being made in the sentence.

> **It is also the polite one.** *Will you be coming?* is softer than *Are you coming?*,
> because it asks about your arrangements rather than your intentions.

> **The time expressions it likes:** *this time next year · by then · by the end of ·
> in a year's time · eventually · meanwhile*

> **Watch out.** State verbs still have no continuous.
> ✗ *I'll be knowing by March.* ✓ *I'll know by March.*

### 3D · Use — controlled

> This time next year Peadar (1) __________ (live) in Galway and
> (2) __________ (take) a bigger boat out at four. The contract (3) __________ (start) in
> March. By then Aoife (4) __________ (study) in Dublin, and the hall
> (5) __________ (be) finished, or it (6) __________ (still / wait) for plasterboard.
> Sinéad says she (7) __________ (not go) anywhere, and that she

### 5A · Predict

### 5B · Read — one hundred and ten seconds

---

**Everybody here is waiting for somebody else's answer**

I counted it out on the back of an envelope in March and it frightened me.

Peadar will decide about Galway in February. If he goes, the boatyard loses its best customer and
Tomás's cousin does not take on the two apprentices, and one of those apprentices is my brother,
who will then be applying to the college in Sligo instead, which he does not want and which he
will probably get.

Sinéad will not leave. She has said so for eight months and I believe her. But the hall will not
reopen until the plasterboard arrives, and if it is still shut in January the committee will have
to decide whether to keep paying the insurance on a building nobody can use, and if they stop
paying it then Sinéad will be running a community with nowhere to put it.

I will be at college in Dublin by October. That is the only sentence in this piece with no *if*
in it, and I notice that I have put it last, and that it is also the only one that is entirely
about me.

A small town is not a set of individual plans. It is one plan with eleven authors, and nobody has
the whole document.

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# Galway, or the season after

Peadar is offered a berth on a bigger boat out of Galway. Two seasons, better money, three weeks
at a time away.

- **Take it.** The money is nearly double. The boat he had is not coming back and nobody is
  building him another.
- **Stay.** Wait a season. The yard thinks there will be a berth here by next spring, and the
  yard has thought that before.
- **Take it for one season.** The contract is for two. Ask for one, and risk being turned down
  for something he has already decided he wants.

### 11A · The evidence

1. **The job advert and the contract** from Part 5E — and the clause about the start date.
2. **The chart** — what a berth here has paid, each season, for ten years.
3. **The voice.** Aoife, twenty seconds: *"Everyone keeps telling him what it would do to the
   town. Nobody has asked him what he will be doing at four o'clock in the morning if he stays,
   because the answer is nothing, and he has been doing nothing at four o'clock in the morning
   since January."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In threes. Everybody says one. Use the frame.

> *This time next year Peadar will be ______ , because ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *career · chance · contract · season · leave · return · settle · future*

### 11C · Decide

Write **two sentences**. Choose, and say why.

> I think Peadar should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> He should take it for the two seasons and say so before January, not in February. Asking for
> one season is asking a skipper to carry the cost of his uncertainty, and skippers remember
> that. Two seasons is twenty months, and a man who has been awake at four every morning since
> January with nothing to go out in does not need a smaller version of the thing he wants.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** He took the two seasons and started in March.
>
> He is away three weeks at a time and says the work is easier than here and the boat is better
> than anything he will ever own. He has not sold the house. He is back for ten days in four,
> and on those ten days he is on the pier at four in the morning doing nothing, which his sister
> says is the only part of this that has not changed.
>
> The yard did not take on the two apprentices. Aoife's brother went to Sligo and is, by every
> account including his own, doing well.

**He went. The chain in the essay happened almost exactly as she wrote it. And the one person it
was supposed to be worst for is doing fine.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say what you make of the ten days in four.
