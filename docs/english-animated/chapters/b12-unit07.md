### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_b12_u07_p03_v09` · V9 Grammar Visual · mirror**

### 3C · Know

| Verb | + *-ing* | + *to* |
|---|---|---|
| **stop** | stop the activity · *I stopped **thinking**.* | stop in order to do · *I stopped **to think**.* |
| **remember** | the memory of doing it · *I remember **checking**.* | the duty, then the act · *I remembered **to check**.* |
| **forget** | *I'll never forget **seeing** it.* | *I forgot **to check**.* |
| **try** | an experiment · *Try **changing** the time.* | an effort · *Try **to change** it.* |
| **go on** | continue the same thing · *He went on **talking**.* | move to a new thing · *He went on **to say** that…* |
| **regret** | about the past · *I regret **saying** it.* | about now · *I regret **to say** that…* |
| **mean** | involve · *It means **leaving** at four.* | intend · *I meant **to leave** at four.* |
| **need** | passive meaning · *It needs **fixing**.* | *I need **to fix** it.* |

> **The pattern, as far as there is one.** The ***-ing* form points backwards or inwards**: to
> something already happening, or already remembered. The ***to* form points forwards**: to a
> purpose, an intention or a duty.

> **It is not a reliable rule.** It covers *stop*, *remember*, *forget*, *go on* and *regret*
> well, *try* and *mean* adequately, and *need* not at all. The eight verbs have to be learned.

> **Why it matters.** *I stopped checking* and *I stopped to check* are opposite accounts of the
> same night, and a hospital record contains both sentences.

## Part 5 · Reading ◆ **A · THE WORLD**

### 5A · Predict

An interview transcript. The title is
**"I didn't choose the eleventh one. I just didn't stop."**
**What do you expect the difference to be?** Two sentences.

### 5B · Read — two minutes and a quarter

---

**"I didn't choose the eleventh one. I just didn't stop"**
*Interview, night nurse, eleven consecutive nights*

**Why eleven?**
It wasn't eleven at the start. It was four, and then somebody was off, and then I picked up two
because the money is a third more at night and I have a daughter at university in Santiago.

**At what point did it become a decision?**
That is the question, and I have thought about it a lot since, and the honest answer is that it
never did. There is no night on which I decided to do eleven. Around night six I stopped thinking
about it. Not stopped caring — stopped thinking. It went from being a thing I was doing to being
the shape of the week.

**Did anybody say anything?**
Yes. On night eight somebody asked if I was all right and I said I was fine, and I remember
saying it and I remember meaning it.

**And on the eleventh?**
I remember checking a drug chart at about two. I do not remember checking it again, and I
believe I did, and I cannot tell you that I did.

Nothing happened. I want to be clear that nothing happened. That is not a defence, it is the
thing that worries me, because if something had happened there would be a form and a review and
the rota would have been looked at, and because nothing happened there is nothing except this
conversation.

**What would you say to somebody on night six?**
I would say: you are not going to notice night seven. Nobody notices night seven. Get somebody
else to count for you, because the thing that goes first is not your hands and it is not your
judgement, it is your ability to be the person who is counting.

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# Night twelve

Carla has worked eleven consecutive nights. She chose to, she needs the money, the rota permits
it, and a colleague has said she thinks it is unsafe. Night twelve is on the rota.

- **Let it run.** She is an adult, the policy permits twelve, nothing has happened, and she needs
  the money more than anybody else needs her to stop.
- **Stop her.** Take her off night twelve. She loses a third of a night's pay, somebody has to
  cover, and she will be the nurse who was taken off.
- **Change the policy.** Cap consecutive nights at seven for everybody. It costs about four
  shifts a month in agency cover and it takes the decision away from the person least able to
  make it at four in the morning.

### 11A · The evidence

> **FIGURE `fig_b12_u07_p11_v06` · V6 Data Visual**

1. **The policy and the rota** from Part 5E — and the clause added by hand.
2. **The chart** — error rates by consecutive night, from the literature and from this hospital.
3. **The voice.** Carla, thirty seconds: *"If you ask me now, I will tell you I am fine, and the
   reason I will tell you that is that I am the person being asked. I said it on night eight and
   I meant it. I would quite like somebody to stop asking me and decide."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In fours. Everybody speaks twice. Use the frame.

> *She stopped ______ , which is not the same as stopping ______ . So ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *habit · dependence · trigger · relapse · willpower · focus · attention ·
without noticing · the first week*

### 11C · Decide

Write **three sentences**. Choose, say why, and name what it costs.

> I think the hospital should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .
> What this costs is ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> Change the policy to seven and pay for the agency cover. Carla is right that she is the worst
> possible person to ask, and so is everybody else at four in the morning on their eleventh
> night; that is not a fact about her character, it is what the research says about everybody's.
> A cap is the only version of this that does not require one tired person to be heroic about
> their own tiredness, and it costs four shifts a month, which is a number the hospital can
> actually decide about.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** The cap was proposed at seven and agreed at nine, because seven would have
> required more agency cover than the budget had.
>
> Carla's income fell by about eleven per cent. She has said twice, publicly, that she supports
> the cap, and once, privately, that she has been looking at agency work herself because the
> agency rate for a single night is higher than the hospital's.
>
> Consecutive-night incidents in the following year fell from nine to four. Nobody can say
> whether that is the cap, because nine and four are small numbers and the department also got a
> new consultant in March.

**The cap worked, probably, and it cost one person eleven per cent of her income, definitely.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say what should be done about the agency rate.
