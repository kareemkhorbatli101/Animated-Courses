## Part 3 · Grammar Lab 1 ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### What you do every day

### 3A · Notice

Look at the four sentences in Part 2 again.

1. Is the verb different after *I*, *we*, *they* and *you*?
2. Where is the time — at the start of the sentence, or at the end?
3. Can the time go in the other place?
4. One of the four sentences is about more than one person. Which?

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_a11_u02_p03_v09a` · V9 Grammar Visual**
> Four columns — *I · you · we · they* — with the same verb under all four, identical, in the same
> colour. The lesson is the sameness: at this stage the verb does not change. A fifth column is
> greyed out with a question mark on it, and it is labelled **Unit 3**.

### 3C · Know

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | **work** | I work at six. |
| **you** | **work** | You work on Saturday. |
| **we** | **work** | We work together. |
| **they** | **work** | They work at night. |

> **The verb does not change.** Not yet. In Unit 3 it changes for *he*, *she* and *it*, and that
> is the only place it changes in the whole language.

> **FIGURE `fig_a11_u02_p03_v09b` · V9 Grammar Visual**
> Negatives and questions: *do not / don't* dropping in before the verb, and *do* jumping to the
> front of the sentence to make a question. The verb itself never moves.

| | |
|---|---|
| **–** | I **do not** work / I **don't** work · They **don't** open on Sunday |
| **?** | **Do** you work on Saturday? · **Do** they open early? |

> **Where the time goes.** *I get up at six.* · *At six I get up.* Both are English. The first one
> is normal. The second one puts the time first because the time is the news.

> **Why it matters.** This is how you say anything that is true every day, and most of what you
> say about your life is true every day.

### 7A · Hear

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# The six o'clock class

Escola Monte has an evening class at seven. Eleven students come.

Four of them work until seven and arrive late, every week. They miss the first fifteen minutes.
Teresa starts again for them, every week, and the seven students who came on time wait.

The school can move the class to **half past seven**. Then nobody is late. But the class finishes
at nine, and the last tram is at ten past nine, and three students walk home.

### 11A · The evidence

> **FIGURE `fig_a11_u02_p11_v04b` · V4 Comparison Pair**
> The same classroom at seven and at half past seven, drawn identically. **Left:** seven students,
> four empty chairs, Teresa at the board. **Right:** eleven students, and the window behind them
> is dark. **Six planted differences**, one of which is the clock on the wall of the corridor.

🔊 **Track 2.6.** Lin, 20 seconds: *"I come at seven. I wait. I am not angry. But I get up at
six and now I go home at half past nine."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In threes. Use the frame. Everybody says one.

> *I think the class should start at ______ , because ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *work · late · tired · tram · wait · early · every week*

### 11C · Decide

Write **two sentences**. Choose, and say why.

> I think the class should start at ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> Keep it at seven and do not start again. The first fifteen minutes is speaking practice, and
> you can walk into speaking practice late. Put the new grammar at half past. Nobody waits and
> nobody misses anything.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** Teresa moved the grammar to half past seven and left the first fifteen
> minutes for speaking. The four late students still came late. The seven others stopped waiting.
>
> Then something she did not expect: two of the seven started coming at twenty past instead of
> seven, because they had worked out what the first fifteen minutes was.

**One question. You do not have to answer it out loud.**

> Teresa solved the problem for the four. Did she make a new one for the seven?
