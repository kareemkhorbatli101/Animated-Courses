## Part 3 · Grammar Lab 1 ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM**

### Moving somebody's words

### 3A · Notice

Look at the four sentences in Part 2.

1. In sentence 1, what tense is *was*? In sentence 2?
2. Sentence 3 has *had checked*. **What did she actually say?**
3. *said* and *insisted* both report. **What does *insisted* add that *said* does not?**
4. **When does the tense not need to move back?** Try: *She said the drain is still blocked.*
5. Try *"She said me that she had checked."* **What is wrong?**

### 3B · See

> **FIGURE `fig_a22_u04_p03_v09` · V9 Grammar Visual · mirror**

Now look at the second picture. **The ladder runs from neutral to loaded.
Where would you put *claimed*?**

> **FIGURE `fig_a22_u04_p03_v09b` · V9 Grammar Visual · ladder**

### 3C · Know

| Direct | Reported |
|---|---|
| *"The water **is** at the door."* | She said the water **was** at the door. |
| *"I **checked** the clock."* | She said she **had checked** the clock. |
| *"I **will** phone you."* | She said she **would** phone me. |
| *"I **can** see it."* | She said she **could** see it. |

> **Everything shifts one step back**, and so do *I/you*, *here/there*, *now/then*,
> *today/that day*.

> **say** and **tell** are not the same shape.
> ✓ *She **said that** she had checked.* ✓ *She **told me that** she had checked.*
> ✗ *She said me…* ✗ *She told that…*

> **You do not have to shift** if the thing is still true.
> ✓ *She said the drain **is** still blocked.* — because it is.

> **The reporting verb is never neutral for long.** *said* is the flat one.
> *insisted*, *claimed*, *admitted* and *revealed* all add a judgement, and a reader hears it.

### 3D · Use — controlled

> Eimear said the water (1) __________ (be) at the door at eleven and that she
> (2) __________ (check) the clock. Donal insisted it (3) __________ (be) half past. Sinéad

> (5) __________ (go) by when the hall filled. The report added that the photographs

## Part 5 · Reading ◆ **A · THE WORLD**

### 5A · Predict

An extract from the inquiry's report. The title is
**"Section 4: reconciling the accounts."**
**What do you expect an official report to do with three different times?** One sentence.

### 5B · Read — one hundred and ten seconds

---

**SECTION 4: RECONCILING THE ACCOUNTS**

**4.1** Three residents gave times for the first water at door level. The times differ by forty
minutes. All three accounts are accepted as honest.

**4.2** Resident A stated that the water was at her door at eleven. She told the inquiry that she
had checked the kitchen clock. The inquiry notes that this clock was later found to be eleven
minutes fast.

**4.3** Resident B insisted that it was half past eleven. He said that he had not looked at a
clock but that he had been listening to a radio programme which he knew ended at half past. The
programme in fact ended at 23.15.

**4.4** Resident C said that she had not checked the time at all and went by the point at which
the hall was full. Hall records show the hall reached capacity at 23.25.

**4.5** Photographic metadata gives 23.30. The inquiry has adopted this time.

**4.6** The inquiry wishes to record that it does not regard any of the three accounts as
unreliable. Each witness used the best instrument available to them, and in each case the
instrument was wrong by between five and twenty minutes. This is normal. An account that matched
the metadata exactly would be more unusual than these three, and would deserve more scrutiny,
not less.

---

2. **What was wrong with Resident A's clock, and by how much?**

5. What time has the inquiry adopted, and from what source?
6. **What is the point of paragraph 4.6?** Put it in your own words.

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE**

# One time goes in the report

Three accounts, forty minutes apart, all honest. The report can carry one time in its summary.

- **23.30, the metadata.** The most accurate instrument. It is also the only one with no person
  behind it.
- **A range: 23.00 to 23.40.** Honest about the uncertainty. Harder to use, and a range in a
  summary tends to be read as its lowest number.
- **All three, named, with their instruments.** Six lines instead of one. Nobody is wrong, and
  nobody can quote it.

### 11A · The evidence

1. **The newspaper article** from Part 5E — and what it quoted.
2. **The chart** — each account, its instrument, and that instrument's error.
3. **The voice.** The inquiry chair, twenty seconds: *"A summary that cannot be quoted is not a
   summary. But a time that nobody in that town recognises is not a finding either, and I have
   written both and I do not like either of them."*

### 11B · Talk about it

In threes. Everybody says one. Use the frame.

> *I think the report should give ______ , because ______ .*

**Words you can use:** *accurate · exact · account · version · source · evidence · trust · doubt*

### 11C · Decide

Write **two sentences**. Choose, and say why.

> I think the report should ______________________ ,
> because ______________________ .

### 11D · Model decision

> It should give 23.30 in the summary and all three accounts in an annex, with the instruments
> and their errors. The summary has to be quotable or it will be replaced by whatever the
> newspaper writes instead, and the annex is where the town can find itself and see that it was
> not called unreliable. One number, and one page that explains why that number is not an
> accusation.

### 11E · The Reveal

> **What happened.** The report gave 23.30 in the summary and the three accounts in Annex C.
>
> Every newspaper used 23.30. Two of them also used Annex C, and one of those ran a long piece
> about the eleven-minute clock that Resident A says was the kindest thing written about her all
> year.
>
> The third newspaper ran the headline about witnesses contradicting each other. It printed a
> correction eleven days later, at the foot of page four, which Resident B says he did not see
> and still has not read.

**One number, quoted everywhere. One annex, read by two. And one correction nobody saw.**

> **Was it the right decision?** Say why — and say what a correction is for.
