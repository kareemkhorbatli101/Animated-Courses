# 05 · The Topic Matrix

> The brief: *"topics must be current interesting varied."*

Variety alone produces a magazine. English Animated gets variety **and** integration from a single
device: **ten permanent domains, each revisited once per book, at a higher altitude every time.**

- 10 domains × 14 books = **140 units.** Every book covers every domain exactly once.
- The order rotates per book, so no domain is always Unit 1 and no two consecutive books open the
  same way.
- A learner meets *Technology & Attention* fourteen times between A1 and C2 — as *my phone* at
  A1.1 and as *the political economy of algorithmic curation* at C2.1. Same thread, fourteen
  altitudes.

This is what makes the series **integrated and graded** rather than a shelf of unrelated books. It
also guarantees that no book is topic-starved in any area of life.

## 4 · Keeping "current" current

Topicality is a liability in a course meant to last eight years. English Animated separates the
permanent from the perishable.

| Layer | Lifespan | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Domain** | Permanent | The spine. Never revised. |
| **Unit Question** | 10+ years | Printed in the book. Written to be un-datable. |
| **Core texts** | 5–8 years | Printed. Dated examples avoided; principles preferred to news. |
| **The File** (Part 9) | 2–4 years | Printed, but written as a *format* that survives content refresh. |
| **The Live Panel** | 6–12 months | **Platform only.** One per unit: current data, a current case, a current artefact. Updated centrally; the book never goes stale. |

**The Live Panel is the mechanism.** A printed unit on attention design says what interfaces do and
why; the Live Panel attached to it carries this quarter's example. Teachers get currency; the
publisher does not reprint.

**Topics explicitly in scope** across the 140 units — because they are what adults in 2026 actually
read, argue about and need English for:

generative AI in work and study · remote and hybrid work · the gig economy · climate adaptation
(not only mitigation) · migration and the right to move · housing and who a city is for ·
mental health and the language around it · care work · ageing populations · attention and the
design of interfaces · misinformation and verification · privacy and data · supply chains ·
accessibility and universal design · renewable transition and its losers · food systems ·
language death and revival · sport as industry · the economics of culture · automation and
reskilling · trust in institutions · protest and its limits · the ethics of documentation ·
neurodiversity · open science · repair culture and the right to repair.

**Topics explicitly out of scope** in core texts: partisan electoral politics, sectarian religious
claims, graphic violence, and anything that requires a learner to take a position on their own
government. The Culture File handles contested ground as a *skill* — see §5 — never as a verdict.

## 5 · The Culture File: competence, not trivia

The corpus treats culture as national fact ("Cars in Syria", "Furniture-Making in Syria"). That
produces knowledge, not competence. English Animated treats culture as the **analysis of difference
in use**, following the CEFR Companion Volume's plurilingual/pluricultural scales.

| Format | What the learner does |
|---|---|
| **The Misunderstanding** | A critical incident is presented from both sides. The learner reconstructs each party's reasoning — *neither is wrong* — and proposes a repair. |
| **Register Map** | The same message in six versions across formality, distance and channel; the learner maps which lands where, and why. |
| **Unwritten Rules** | What a group assumes without stating. Learners surface the unwritten rules of their *own* contexts first, which is where the real insight is. |
| **What's Funny Here?** | Humour as the deepest cultural layer; what travels and what does not. |
| **Translating the Untranslatable** | A word with no equivalent, in both directions. Learners bring one from their own language. |
| **English as a Lingua Franca** | Explicitly: most English is now spoken between non-native users. What does "correct" mean then? (B2+) |

**Rule: every Culture File is reciprocal.** Learners examine an English-speaking practice *and*
their own, in the same lesson. The course never positions one culture as the norm and the learner's
as the variation.

## 6 · The Work and Study Files

**WORK FILE** (4 per Foundation/Independence book, 3 per Mastery book) — professional English as a
problem of *genre and behaviour*, not of vocabulary:

| Format | Deliverable |
|---|---|
| The Inbox | Triage a 6-message thread; write the two replies that matter |
| The Meeting | Chair it, minute it, and write the follow-up that makes it real |
| The Pitch | Three minutes, one slide, one objection handled |
| The Difficult Message | Bad news, a refusal, a correction, an apology that is not grovelling |
| The Handover | Make your work usable by the person who replaces you |
| The Review | Give and receive feedback that changes behaviour |
| The Remote Problem | Something that goes wrong across time zones and written channels |

**STUDY FILE** (3 per book; 4 at C-level) — academic literacy and learning strategy:

| Format | Deliverable |
|---|---|
| Source Check | Evaluate three sources on one claim; rank and justify |
| Note Systems | Four note methods tested against four text types |
| Paraphrase & Citation | Where paraphrase becomes plagiarism, practically demonstrated |
| The Seminar | Prepare, contribute, disagree, and build on someone else |
| Exam Decode | What a task is actually asking for; rubric reverse-engineering |
| Reading at Speed | Survey, question, read, recall — timed and measured |
| The Literature Review | C-level: synthesise five sources into one position |
| Working With AI | Prompt, verify, edit, attribute — and decide what not to outsource |

Every File ends with the same move: **take this into your own life this week and report back.** It
is the only part of the course that leaves the room.
