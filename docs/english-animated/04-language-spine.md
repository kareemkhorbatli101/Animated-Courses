# 04 · The Language Spine — 140 Units, A1 → C2

Every unit carries **G1** (a new grammar target), **G2** (a returning target re-entered at higher
load), **L1** (a topic lexical set) and **L2** (a system lexical set), plus a pronunciation focus
and a primary function. The G1/G2 pairing is the mechanism that keeps a unit from collapsing into
one line (`02-unit-architecture.md` §2).

**Domain codes** (see `05-topic-matrix.md`): **D1** Identity · **D2** Work · **D3** Learning ·
**D4** Health & Mind · **D5** Place & Movement · **D6** Food, Land & Climate · **D7** Technology
& Attention · **D8** Money & Value · **D9** Story, Art & Play · **D10** Power & Fairness.

## Reading this spine

**Note the shape across the shelf.** New-structure counts fall after B2.1 (`03-grading-spine.md`
§6) and G1 targets stop being *forms* and start being *choices*: "aspect as meaning", "passive as
an information-structure choice", "the grammar of authority". This is the single most important
difference between a course that stops at B2 and one that genuinely reaches C2. A C1 learner does
not need another tense; they need to know why a writer chose the one they chose.

**Note also that G2 is never revision.** The returning structure always re-enters against new
meaning or new register. *Passive* appears at A2.1 (form), B1.1 (procedure), B1.2 (causative),
B2.2 (information structure) and C1.1 (abstraction and authority) — five encounters, five
different jobs, no repetition.
