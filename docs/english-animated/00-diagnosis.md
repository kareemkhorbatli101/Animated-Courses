# 00 · Diagnosis of the Five Source Books

**Corpus examined.** AlHasan International Student Book 1 (A2, trade) · AlHasan International
Student Book 2 (B1, trade) · EAM — English for Auto Maintenance (upper A2) · EAT — English
for Auto Technicians (B1.1–B1.2) · EFD — English for Furniture Design (A2).
Five books, 50 units, ~292,000 words of running text, 635 illustrations. Every figure below
is counted from the extracted `document.xml`, not estimated.

---

## 1 · The shared house template

All five books run one template. It is a good template, and the new series inherits its bones:

| Slot | AlHasan 1 & 2 | EAM / EFD | EAT |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opener | Warm Up (+ track tag) | Warm Up | Warm Up |
| 1 | Vocabulary and Terminology | Vocabulary and Terminology | Vocabulary and Terminology |
| 2 | Grammar | Grammar | Grammar |
| 3 | Listening and Dialogue | Listening | Listening and Dialogue |
| 4 | Speaking and Your Role | Speaking | Speaking |
| 5 | Extended Reading | Reading | Extended Reading |
| 6 | Writing (a real email) | Writing | Writing |
| 7 | Trade-Skills Track | Management File | Service Adviser Track |
| 8 | Consolidation | Global Case Study | Consolidation |
| 9 | Case Studies and Decisions | Local Case Study + Decision Task | Decision Task |
| 10 | Review and Can-Do | Review, Can-Do, Glossary | Review and Can-Do |
| 11 | Foundations for Further Study | — | — |

Unit length is remarkably consistent: **EAT Unit 1 = 6,260 words**, and the other units sit
within ±15% of that. This is a book of roughly 60,000–90,000 running words per level — the
right order of magnitude, and the new series holds it.

## 2 · What is genuinely good, and is being kept

**2.1 A single persistent world.** Barada Auto Service has a foreman (Maher), a junior
technician (Ziad), an adviser (Rasha), a diagnostics specialist (Bassel), a thirty-year veteran
(Abu Salim) and an apprentice (Tarek). They recur in every unit, in every skill, for ten units.
Learners stop re-orienting and start listening. Most commercial coursebooks throw away this
advantage by changing cast every spread.

**2.2 The Decision Task.** Part 9 poses a real dilemma with a cost attached — *fit the coil and
hope, replace three parts, or test for an hour* — supplies an answer frame (`I think Ziad
should ___, because ___`), a phrase bank, and a **model decision** that argues rather than
summarises. This is the single best thing in the corpus. It is rare, it is hard to write, and it
produces language worth producing.

**2.3 Model-before-task writing.** Every writing task gives a full model paragraph, a planning
frame, and a three-box `Check before you finish` list. Compliance scaffolding done properly.

**2.4 Ambient glossing.** `Gloss:` lines sit under the exercise that needs them, not in a back
matter nobody opens.

**2.5 "Foundations for Further Study"** (AlHasan only). A Concept Spotlight that lifts the unit's
content to a transferable idea — *a holding group is many specialists acting as one; so is a
department, a city, a team* — then asks the learner to apply it to their own world. This is the
most intellectually ambitious section in the corpus and it is **promoted to a permanent fixture**
in the new series.

**2.6 Discipline.** Numbering, example items (`0. is done`), word-count ranges, can-do
checklists, per-unit glossaries. The books are *finished*. That is worth more than it sounds.

## 3 · What fails, with the evidence

### 3.1 One line per unit, hammered twenty times

EAT Unit 1 targets *present simple vs present continuous*. In 6,260 words it names that target
**33 times**, and the target owns:

> Warm-up gap-fill → Part 1 "Write Two Sentences" → Part 2 Notice → Grammar Box → Controlled
> Practice → State Verbs → Correct the Sentence → Freer Practice → Grammar Review → Part 3
> Listening 1 (*a character explains the two tenses aloud*) → Listening 2 → Listening 3 →
> Part 4 Speaking ×3 → Part 5 Reading (*a text about the two present tenses*) → Part 6 Writing
> ×4 → Part 10 Spiral Review

**Nineteen slots. One grammar point.** Everything else in the unit — the workshop lexis, the
symptom/cause distinction, the register shift from technician to customer — is carried
incidentally, unsequenced and untested. This is precisely the "narrow focus and too much
iteration through the same stuff in different parts" the brief names.

### 3.2 Reading and listening are conscripted as grammar delivery

Part 3 Listening 1 is a monologue in which the foreman *teaches the grammar*: "Use the present
simple for what normally happens… Now, one warning. Some verbs describe a state." Part 5
Extended Reading is an essay titled "Two Present Tenses". Two of the four skills have been spent
on metalanguage. The learner never practises listening for gist, for attitude, for what was *not*
said, or for a number said fast — because the audio exists to carry a tense.

### 3.3 Ten task types carry five books

Counted across the corpus:

| Rubric family | Occurrences |
|---|---|
| Match with one redundant option | **110** |
| Open comprehension questions ("Answer the questions. 0 is done.") | 139 |
| 4-option multiple choice on a text | 70 |
| True/False (±Not Given) | 29 |
| Gap-fill from a word bank | 40+ |
| Single-error correction | 30 |

Six families, approximately 420 exercise instances. The redundant distractor **"a mistake in the
code" appears 29 times verbatim** and **"a paid holiday" 12 times** — in a furniture book, an
auto book and a trade book alike. The learner learns the *exercise*, not the language.

### 3.4 The illustrations decorate; they do not teach

635 figures. Three visual genres account for nearly all of them:

1. **Avatar bust + role label** (`Maher · foreman`, `Tarek · apprentice`)
2. **Avatar bust + speech bubble** containing a line already printed in the text below
3. **Icon strip** — eight flat glyphs in rounded cards with the word underneath

Measured properties: AlHasan figures are 1180 × 280–360 px — a letterbox strip, ~12% of it
occupied by content; EAT figures are 3000 × 1680 with the lower 40% empty. Observed defects
include speech text clipped mid-sentence, labels overlapping the rule below them, and no figure
anywhere in the corpus that is *referenced by a task*. Not one exercise says "look at the
picture". A cutaway, an annotated document, a process sequence, a chart, a floor plan, a labelled
tool board — none of these exist. **The pictures show who is in the room. They never show how
anything works.**

### 3.5 Missing entirely

- **No listening sub-skills.** Transcripts are printed in full on the page beside the task.
- **No pronunciation system.** Word-stress lists only (`tech-NI-cian`). No phonemes, no connected
  speech, no sentence stress, no intonation, no fluency work.
- **No recycling architecture.** "Spiral Review" is four gapped sentences from the unit just
  finished. Nothing returns from Unit 3 in Unit 8.
- **No CEFR Companion Volume (2020) strands.** No mediation, no online interaction, no
  plurilingual/pluricultural competence. These are now the defining dimensions of a modern
  syllabus and the corpus predates them.
- **No assessment beyond self-report.** One can-do checklist per unit; no diagnostic, no progress
  test, no portfolio, no exit test, no exam mapping.
- **No learner-strategy training.** Nothing teaches how to guess from context, take notes, repair
  a conversation, or plan a text.
- **Output targets below level.** EAT is labelled B1.1–B1.2 and asks for **80–130 words**. B1
  productive writing should reach 150–200.

### 3.6 Context is welded in

"Syria in Focus", Damascus, the Barada river, Aleppo factories, `ustaz` glossed as a loanword.
Excellent for its commissioned purpose; wrong for a general international course. The new series
replaces hard-coded locale with a **rotating international settings system** and an explicit
localisation layer.

## 4 · The eleven design commitments that follow

Each is traceable to a finding above and is enforced by a gate in `10-production-plan.md`.

| # | Commitment | Fixes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Every unit runs **three declared lines** (World / System / Performance), tagged on every part | 3.1 |
| 2 | **No language point may own more than 4 of the 12 parts.** Two grammar targets and two lexical sets per unit | 3.1 |
| 3 | **Audio and reading texts never explain grammar.** Grammar is noticed in text, taught in the Grammar Lab | 3.2 |
| 4 | **64-type task taxonomy**; no type twice in a unit without variation; a banded minimum of evaluate/create tasks per unit (2 at A, rising to 6 at C2) | 3.3 |
| 5 | **No figure without a task.** Every illustration is the object of at least one exercise | 3.4 |
| 6 | **Twelve figure types**, realistically detailed and information-dense, each with a declared pedagogical job | 3.4 |
| 7 | A **Pronunciation & Fluency Lab** in every unit, on a six-year phonological spine | 3.5 |
| 8 | **Mediation & Interaction** is a permanent part (CEFR CV 2020) | 3.5 |
| 9 | **Hard recycling**: every unit re-tests two items from earlier units and one from the earlier book | 3.5 |
| 10 | **Six-layer assessment** from placement to exit test and portfolio | 3.5 |
| 11 | **Culture-neutral core + localisation layer**; settings rotate across six world regions | 3.6 |
