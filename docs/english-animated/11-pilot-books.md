# 11 · Pilot Books — Recommendation

> *"I will approve the first few books, possibly ones you recommend."*

---

## 1 · The recommendation

**Build two books first, plus one calibration unit.**

| | Why this one |
|---|---|
| **1 · B1.1 · *Taking a Position*** — a shared workspace, Nairobi | **Build this one first.** B1 is the commercial centre of gravity of every general English list — the largest population, the longest dwell time, the level most learners buy into and most institutions place into. It is also the level at which all twelve parts are genuinely exercised: Mediation, the Decision and the Files all work at full strength at B1, whereas at A1 they are necessarily compressed. If the architecture is going to fail, it fails visibly here, where there is still time to change it. |
| **2 · A1.1 · *Starting Out*** — a language school and its street, Lisbon | **Build this second, in parallel.** A1.1 sets the floor of the entire series and is the hardest book in the shelf to write well: every device in the architecture has to work with 350 words and no past tense. Authoring it alongside B1.1 forces the architecture to prove it stretches across four CEFR bands *before* 120 more units are committed to it. It is also the book that acquires learners. |
| **3 · C2.2 Unit 1 only** — *Voice*, a documentary production | **A single unit, as a calibration sample.** Not a book. Its only job is to prove that the twelve-part architecture still makes sense at the top of the shelf before the C-level team inherits habits from the B-level team. One unit, ~5 weeks, and it de-risks the last two years of the programme. `10-production-plan.md` §8 lists "C-level authored by B-level habits" as a high-impact risk; this is the mitigation. |

**Why not A1.1 and A1.2 together?** Two adjacent books test one altitude twice. The grading question
— does the spine actually hold from 350 words to 6,650? — goes unanswered for two years.

**Why not B1.1 alone?** A single pilot book cannot reveal the grading gradient, and the architecture
decisions that will hurt most at A1 (how do you run a Decision Task with no conditionals?) stay
hidden.

**Sequence:** B1.1 Unit 1 as the specimen unit (`12-specimen-unit.md`), approved before anything
else is written; then B1.1 and A1.1 authored in parallel; the C2.2 unit written during the pilot
trial window, when the editorial team has capacity and the architecture is fresh.

**File placement.** A1.1 follows the canonical pattern in `15-rotation-maps.md` §2 —
W S C W S C W S C W — so no File type falls in two consecutive units. An earlier draft of this
table did not, and was corrected in calibration pass 3.

**Note on A1 Decisions.** At A1 the Decision Task is real but small: two options, a visual, a
four-line answer frame, and a model decision of 40–55 words. The cognitive move — *weigh, choose,
justify* — is identical to B1; only the language is smaller. The corpus never attempts this below
B1; there is no reason not to.

**Milestones:** after Unit 5 — *The School Guide* (a one-page guide for a new student, made in
groups). After Unit 10 — *My Street* (a photo-and-text wall display with recorded audio).

## 4 · What approval of these two buys

| Proved by B1.1 | Proved by A1.1 | Proved by C2.2 U1 |
|---|---|---|
| All 12 parts at full strength | The architecture under severe language constraint | The architecture at the ceiling |
| The three-line braid | A1 Decision Tasks are possible | G1 targets as *choices*, not forms |
| Mediation at the level it matters most | 15 figures/unit visual density | Authentic unedited text at 1,600 words |
| The Decision with full evidence | The Part 0/12 replay with beginners | Mediation at expert level |
| The File rotation across all three types | Three lesson paths in a short course | That C-level needs a different author brief |

Together: **the grading spine tested across four CEFR bands, 21 of 140 units, roughly 15% of the
programme, before the remaining 85% is committed.**
