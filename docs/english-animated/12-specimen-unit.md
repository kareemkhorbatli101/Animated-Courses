# 12 · Specimen Unit — B1.1 Unit 1

A complete blueprint of one unit at spec, so that the architecture can be judged on something real
rather than on a diagram. This is the first thing to be authored and the template everything after
it is held to.

---

## The header

| | |
|---|---|
| **Book** | B1.1 · *Taking a Position* |
| **Unit** | 1 · **Ground Floor** |
| **Setting** | Jengo House — a shared workspace on a street in Nairobi that is being rebuilt around it |
| **Domain** | D5 · Place, Movement & Cities |
| **Unit Question** | **Who is a city built for?** |
| **Outcome Task** | Tell the story of how one street changed, in 90 seconds, to someone who has never seen it |
| **CEFR** | B1.1 · ~45 active items · 11 hours core |

### The three lines, as printed on the opening spread

| | |
|---|---|
| ◆ **A · THE WORLD** | How a street changes, who changes it, and who is still there afterwards |
| ◆ **B · THE SYSTEM** | **G1** narrative tenses contrasted · **G2** past perfect (returning) · **L1** urban space & change (24) · **L2** time and sequence markers (21) · **Pron** elision in connected speech |
| ◆ **C · THE PERFORMANCE** | A 90-second spoken account of a changed place, and a 140-word written one |

### Can-dos

1. I can tell a story about the past using three tenses together without losing the reader.
2. I can describe how a place has changed and say who gained and who lost.
3. I can take notes while someone speaks and retell what they said accurately.
4. I can state a position on a local decision and give two reasons for it.

## Part 1 · Vocabulary Lab 1 ◆B — 2 pp, 50 min

**L1 · Urban space and change — 24 items.** 11 single words, 8 collocations, 3 phrasal verbs,
2 fixed expressions.

> *pavement · kerb · plot · frontage · hoarding · scaffolding · demolish · redevelop · tenant ·
> landlord · rent · planning permission · put up (a building) · knock down · move out · set back
> from the road · mixed use · rising rents · a block of flats · a change of use · the ground floor ·
> on the corner · priced out · the way things were*

**Sequence — meet · sort · stretch · own**

| Stage | Task | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Meet | Items are harvested from the Part 0 scene by hotspot or word bank. No list. | L1 |
| Sort | **Cline:** place 7 verbs on a scale of destructiveness — *repaint · refit · extend · convert · redevelop · demolish · clear*. Learners disagree; that is the task. | L3 |
| Sort | **Odd one out with reason:** *tenant · landlord · resident · developer*. At least two defensible answers; the criterion is the answer. | L4 |
| Stretch | **Collocation grid:** which of *rent · permission · a block · a stall · the kerb* goes with *raise · grant · put up · run · re-lay*? Then: which combination does nobody say? | L5 |
| Own | Describe a street you know using six of these items and one you had to look up. | — |

**Figure:** `fig_b11_u01_p01_v08` — **V8 Map / Network.** Sixty metres of the street drawn as a
plan, each frontage shaded by what it is now and outlined by what it was in 2010. The tailor's
stall is the only shape that does not change. Feeds the cline task directly: learners place the
seven verbs against the seven plots. Animation: MORPH (2010 ⇄ 2026), HOTSPOT.

**Corpus Note.** *"In a 1.2-billion-word corpus, ***redevelop*** appears beside* **site**, **area**
*and* **scheme** *— almost never beside* **home**. *What is that choice doing?"* → L11.

## Part 2 · Listening ◆A — 2 pp, 60 min

**2A · *Three people on the same street.*** 2 min 20 s · 128 wpm · three speakers: Grace (cleaner,
Kenyan English), Mr Kamau (landlord, Kenyan English), Tom (consultant, Scottish). Semi-scripted.
**Transcript: back of the book, p. 171.**

Three accounts of the same ten years. They do not contradict each other on fact and they disagree
completely on meaning.

| Task | Type | Cog |
|---|---|---|
| **Before** — from the Part 0 image, predict who will be pleased and who will not | R1 | UN |
| **Gist** — one listen, one question: *Which of the three would move away tomorrow if they could?* | S1 | UN |
| **Detail** — complete a three-column note frame: what each speaker says the street *was*, *is*, and *will be* | S2 | AP |
| **Inference** — *Grace never says she is worried. How do you know she is?* Cite the evidence | S5 | AN |
| **Attitude** — Mr Kamau says "it's progress". Which *sound* tells you how he feels about it? | S4 | AN |
| **Noticing** — six sentences from the audio on the page. *Which describe the background? Which describe a single completed event? Which describe something that had already happened before the story started?* **→ feeds Part 3. The grammar is not named.** | G1 | AN |

**Figure:** `fig_b11_u01_p02_v07` — **V7 Dialogue Stage.** The three speakers, drawn full-figure
in the places they actually stand: Grace in the stairwell with a mop bucket, Mr Kamau on the
pavement facing the hoarding, Tom at a window looking out. Body language carries the attitude the
audio carries — who is turned towards the new building and who is turned away. Speech balloons for
one line each; thought balloons for what none of them says aloud. Serves the attitude-read task.

**2B · Decoding clinic.** 42 seconds of Grace at full speed, four replays:
*(1) how many words in the first sentence? (2) catch the three numbers (3) where do two words join
into one sound? (4) what disappeared from "didn't know"?*

## Part 3 · Grammar Lab 1 ◆B — 3 pp, 70 min

**G1 · Narrative tenses contrasted — past simple · past continuous · past perfect.**

| Stage | Content |
|---|---|
| **Notice** | Return to the six sentences from Part 2. Five questions whose answers *are* the rule. Nothing is stated first. |
| **See** | `fig_b11_u01_p03_v09` — **V9 Grammar Visual.** One timeline, three bands: a **line** for the single completed event, a **lens bracket** for the ongoing background, a **dotted span with a boundary** for what had already happened. Animation: REVEAL, one band at a time under narration. |
| **Know** | Compact form table. **Watch out:** *the past perfect is not "more past" — it marks what was already true when the story started.* **Why it matters:** *get this wrong and your reader puts the events in the wrong order.* |
| **Use** | Controlled: a continuous 90-word account of the street with 10 gaps, never disconnected sentences. Guided: reorder a scrambled six-event account, then write the connective tissue. Open: **tell the story of a place that changed on you** — a real purpose, to a partner who must then retell it. |
| **Check** | 6 mixed items, self-scored, with routing: *0–3 → Workbook pp. 6–8 · 4–5 → Part 6 Contrast Clinic · 6 → the extension on p. 19* |

**Figure:** `fig_b11_u01_p03_v11` — **V11 Error Autopsy.** *"By the time I arrived, they demolished
it."* The arrow sits on the wrong side of the boundary marker. The misconception is named: *the
writer treated the past perfect as optional decoration rather than as the thing that fixes the
order.*

## Part 4 · Speaking ◆C — 2 pp, 60 min

| Stage | Activity |
|---|---|
| **Fluency sprint** | **4/3/2** — describe your street. Four minutes, then three, then two, same content, new partner each time. Fluency measured, not accuracy. (P1) |
| **Functional core** | *Describing change and attributing it.* Language Bank, 11 chunks across three formality bands: *it's not what it was · the whole area's been done up · the character of the street has altered considerably.* Information gap: two learners hold photographs of the same street in 2010 and 2026. (P2) |
| **Performance** | **The residents' meeting.** Six role cards, each with a stated position and a **hidden brief** the others do not see — Grace needs the hours, not the building; Mr Kamau has already signed something. Twenty minutes, unmanaged turn-taking. (P4) |

| **Observer** | One learner per group listens only for: *who was interrupted, and who was never asked a question?* Reports in 30 seconds. |
| **Self-assessment** | Two criteria only: *Did I give a reason every time I disagreed?* · *Did I bring in someone who had not spoken?* |

## Part 5 · Reading ◆A — 3 pp, 70 min

**5A · The long text** — 290 words. *"The last shop on the corner."* A feature piece on a tailor's
stall that has outlasted three buildings. Adapted authentic: real source, lexis levelled to B1,
structure and voice untouched. Genre: feature article.

**5B · The counter-text** — 120 words. The developer's planning statement for the site next door.
Genre: administrative document. Same facts, no people in it at all.

| Task | Type | Cog |
|---|---|---|
| Predict from the headline and the first line only | R1 | UN |
| Skim for shape — where does the article turn? | R2 | UN |
| Close read — 6 items, half inference | R5 | AN |
| Vocabulary in context — 8 items, inferred before glossed, with a confidence rating | R6 | AP |
| **Writer analysis** — *The article names four people. The planning statement names none. What is each document for?* | R7 | EV |
| **Text vs text** — *Both are true. Which would you put on the hoarding?* | R8 | EV |

**Figure:** `fig_b11_u01_p05_v05` — **V5 Annotated Realia.** The actual planning notice, reproduced
at full legibility: reference number, dates, the clause about objections, and the small print about
the consultation window **that the article never mentions**. Seven callouts. Learners find the
window has closed. That is the lesson.

## Part 11 · The Decision ◆C — 2 pp, 60 min

### *The offer on the ground floor*

A developer offers to buy Jengo House. The price is good. Six tenants, three positions:

- **Obi** wants to take it — the money would fund two years of the logistics business.
- **Mariam** wants to refuse — her workshop cannot move; the machines are built into the floor.
- **Grace** has not been asked, and has worked there six years on a handshake.

**Evidence in three formats:** the offer letter, reproduced in full as text · `fig_b11_u01_p11_v06`
— **V6 Data Visual**, what comparable tenants paid in the three years after four similar sales
nearby, with the one outlier that everybody quotes · Grace's recorded voice, 40 seconds, lifted
from Part 2. The learner must integrate all three; no two of them agree.

| Stage | |
|---|---|
| **Deliberate** | In threes, against the frame: *I think they should ___, because ___. The strongest argument against me is ___, and my answer to it is ___.* |
| **Steelman** | Each learner argues the position they disagree with, first. (T3) |
| **Decide** | Commit in writing, 80 words, with two reasons |
| **The Reveal** | What actually happened: they refused, and two years later rents rose anyway and Mariam left. **Was the reasoning sound even though the outcome was bad?** (T2) |
| **Model Decision** | 160 words — an argument, not a summary, using narrative tenses and sequence markers under pressure |
