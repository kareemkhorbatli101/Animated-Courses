# PART ONE — WHAT IS ALREADY SETTLED, AND WHY TWO BOOKS

## 1 · Two books, and the reason

**Two books of twelve units, Units 1–12 and 13–24, exactly as at A2 and B1.** Three reasons, and
the third is the one that decides it:

1. **The shape is proven across four books.** 7 cycles a unit, 84 cycles a book, a Mid-Book Test
   after Unit 6 / Unit 18 and an End-of-Book Test after Unit 12 / Unit 24. Nothing about it needs
   re-deciding and the harness already asserts it.
2. **B2 Business Vantage is a year's work from B1 Business Preliminary**, and a year is two books in
   this series — that is what A2 and B1 each were.
3. ⚠ **The strand's own arithmetic.** 48 units exist at A2 and B1. A third level of 24 makes 72
   units and about **4,000 pages**. **A learner who starts at A2 Unit 1 and finishes at B2 Unit 24
   has done three years with one company and one cast**, and the continuity is the product.

## 2 · What carries over unchanged, and is not up for discussion again

- **The cycle.** 13 stages, 90 minutes. Lesson A Speaking: 1 Get ready · 2 Listen and read
  (+ Language box) · 3 Practise · 3R Perform · 4 Your turn · 4R Report. Lesson B Writing:
  5 Read (+ Writer's box) · 6 Write with help · 6P Check together · 6R Share · 7 Write freely ·
  7P Respond · 7R Share · Homework.
- **Cycle 7 of every unit is Review & Mediate** — ten stages plus the unit check: it revises the six
  functions, carries one **exam-practice task**, and ends with a **mediation**: one thing the
  workplace knows and has never written down.
- **Per-unit house figures**, asserted by `qa.js`: `stage(` 89 · `lesson(` 14 · `dlg(` 14 ·
  Language box 7 · Writer's box 7 · Homework 7 · `'Write with help` 7 · `'Write freely` 7 ·
  `lines(` 8 · `pb()` 8. ⚠ **Carried forward but still measured at Unit 1.**
- **One company, one cast, one canon.** Sham Trading, Damascus. Everything true at A2 and B1 stays
  true; see Part Eight.
- **The renderer is frozen.** `lib.js` is copied unchanged from the B1 tree. ⚠ **`lines(N)` prints
  one rule, not N** — consecutive identically-bordered empty paragraphs merge.
- **The closing unit carries the end-of-level block with no extra `pb()` and no `h1`**, so Unit 24
  keeps the house figure of 8. The bound volume's arithmetic depends on it, and it has now held in
  three consecutive books.

## 3 · What is different at B2, in one paragraph each

**The reader has power over you.** At B1 the reader could not ask a question — a buyer in Beirut,
a night-shift worker alone at two in the morning. **At B2 the reader can ask a question and can
refuse.** A bank's credit committee, a customer's procurement director, an arbitrator, and the
office manager who is about to hand the office to you. Everything in B2 is written to somebody who
may say no and does not have to explain why.

**You argue upward.** B1 Unit 24.4 ended with Nour naming her own gap: *I can write a letter that
disagrees with a customer; I cannot write one that disagrees with an auditor.* ⚠ **That gap is the
subject of B2 Book 1.** The level opens with a decision taken above her that she believes is wrong,
and the first six units are the apparatus for saying so in writing without being either dismissed
or insubordinate.

**You are answerable for things you did not decide.** B2 Book 2 is about inheritance: a contract
signed in a form you argued for and must now live inside, a file that will be read in 2032, an
arbitration in which your own B1 documents are the evidence, and the handover of an office whose
previous holder took eleven years of judgement home with him.

**The grammar is the grammar of qualification.** Concession that costs something, the passive that
clarifies instead of hiding, nominalisation and its price, counterfactuals that survive a
challenge, hedged agency, *deemed accepted*, *best endeavours*. ⚠ **At B2 the difficulty is no
longer the tense; it is the exact weight of a claim.**

**Peer review steps to band 4.** *Name the criterion, then **copy the line you are marking** — the
line itself, not a summary — then say what you found.* ⚠ **Plus one move in Book 2: say what your
correction would cost the writer** (time, a relationship, a figure). The strand's whole discipline
is costing things, and a reviewer who has never priced their own suggestion has not finished.

# PART TWO — THE EXAMINATION ANCHOR, LOOKED UP AND NOT RECALLED

⚠ **The series rule, learned at B1 and written into the Series Bible: an examination anchor must be
looked up, not remembered.** The B1 anchor changed the shape of every test in that level, and the
figures I had from memory were wrong. **So this section was written from a search, today, before
any test was designed.**

## 2.1 · B2 Business Vantage, as the sources describe it

| Paper | Time | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Reading** | 1 hour | **5 parts, 45 questions**; texts of about 150–550 words — articles, reports, letters, manuals |
| **Writing** | 45 min | **2 compulsory parts**: Part 1 an internal communication of **40–50 words** (10 marks); Part 2 **120–140 words** (20 marks) — correspondence, a report **or a proposal** |
| **Listening** | about 40 min | **3 parts**, each recording played **twice** |
| **Speaking** | about 14 min a pair | **3 parts**, two or three candidates, **two examiners** — one talks, one assesses |

⚠ **Each skill is weighted equally and the result is the average of the four, rounded** — the same
arithmetic as B1 Business Preliminary, so the four-printed-paper decision taken at B1 carries
straight over at **25 · 25 · 25 · 25**.

**The five Reading parts**, which decide the exam-practice rotation:

| Part | What it is |
| ---: | --- |
| **1** | **matching** — four short related texts, or one text in four sections, against statements (7 questions) |
| **2** | **sentence insertion** — a long text with sentences removed; one option is not needed |
| **3** | **multiple choice** — one longer text, six questions, A–D |
| **4** | **multiple-choice cloze** — a gapped text, one word or phrase per gap, four options (15 questions) |
| **5** | ⚠ **error correction / proofreading** — find the **extra, grammatically wrong word** in a line; answers in capitals |

## 2.2 · The three things in that table that change this level

1. ⚠ **Writing Part 2 doubles: 60–80 words at B1 becomes 120–140 at B2.** The Bible's B2 finding
   says *step up only the figures the examination does not fix, and establish which those are
   first.* **The examination fixes these two and they move**, so the course bands above them must
   move with them — see Part Seven.
2. ⚠ **Part 2 may be a report or a proposal**, which B1 Preliminary never asked for. **Two Lesson B
   genres in this level exist because the examination asks for them**: the report (Unit 8, Unit 12)
   and the proposal (Unit 21).
3. ⚠ **Reading drops from seven parts to five, and gains error correction.** The seven-part rotation
   used at B1 is retired. **Part 5 is the useful arrival**: it tests proofreading your own prose for
   a word that should not be there, which is the one examination task in four levels that is also a
   working habit. It is placed in the five units whose subject is somebody else's writing.

## 2.3 · What must be verified in Phase 0

⚠ **The Cambridge page the search reached sits under an `old-business-vantage` URL and several
sources use the former "BEC Vantage" name.** Two figures matter enough to check against the current
handbook before the first test is written: **whether Writing Part 2 is still 120–140 words**, and
**whether Part 5 error correction is still in the Reading paper.** If either has gone, this plan is
amended in place with the old figure struck through, exactly as the B1 plan was.

## 3.2 · The method, which is the only part of this section worth arguing about

⚠ **The Series Bible records that six of seven page projections in this series have been wrong, and
that the one which held was built from a measured band one book earlier in the same strand and
level.** Business B1 Book 2 was projected at SB 40–45 from Book 1's measurement and all twelve units
landed inside it.

**So this projection is built that way and no other way.** Its base is **Business B1 Book 2's
measured 40–45 / 24–29** — the nearest measurement in the same strand. The general B2 figures in the
table above are **not** the base; they are a cross-check, and they are a different course with
different word counts.

**The step applied.** Free writing goes from 140–180 to **200–240** words and guided from ≥60 to
**≥80** (Part Seven). The general course took SB 38–40 → 46–48 across the same CEFR step with a
comparable word-count rise. **Applying that step to this strand's base, and not the general
course's own band:**

| | projected SB | projected AK | basis |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| **B2 Business Book 1** (Units 1–12) | ~~45–50~~ → ⚠ **44–50, MEASURED** | **28–34** ✓ | B1 Book 2's measured 40–45 / 24–29 plus the step the general course took from B1 to B2 |
| **B2 Business Book 2** (Units 13–24) | ~~46–51~~ → **45–51** | **29–35** | Book 1's band plus the +1 every Book 2 in this strand has taken |

⚠ **These are projections and the method is not a guarantee.** Phase 0 builds Unit 1's pair,
measures both, and writes the measured band into this file **before Unit 2 exists**, with the
projection struck through if it is wrong. That step has caught an error every time it has been
taken, and the one time it did not was because a previous book in the same strand had taken it.

**And one thing to expect rather than be surprised by:** the general B2 Book 2 band came in *below*
Book 1's (45–47 against 46–48) because Book 2 carries no front matter. **If this strand's Book 2
measures below Book 1, that is the same cause and not a fault.**

## ✓ PHASE 0 MEASURED — UNIT 1, AND WHAT IT CHANGED

**Built, QA-clean and measured on 24 October:** `unit01.js` → **44 pages**, `ak01.js` → **29 pages**.

| | projected | measured at Unit 1 | verdict |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| **Student's Book** | 45–50 | ⚠ **44** | ⚠ **one page below the floor — the floor moves to 44** |
| **Answer Key** | 28–34 | **29** | ✓ inside the band, second page of it |

⚠ **The SB floor was wrong and the band now reads 44–50.** The projection method has held on every
book in this series and it has never been *exactly* right; what it has been is right about the
*step*. 44 against a 40–45 base is the step the method predicted, landing one page low.

**No page was added to reach 45.** The series rule is that the band follows the measurement, and the
inverse of it holds too: ⚠ **a page added to meet a projection is padding, and padding is the fault
the rule was written against.** Unit 1 is also structurally the thinnest unit in any book in this
series — it carries no back-reference load, its opener box is short, and its lexis is the only one
with nothing earlier in the level to collide with. **Units 2–12 are expected in the 45–49 range and
Unit 12, carrying the end-of-book block, at the top of it.**

**The AK measurement is the more reassuring of the two.** 29 against a projected 28–34 means the key
density chosen in Part Seven — band-4 peer samples quoted in full, a sample answer for every open
task, and the five-line marking box — is right for the band and needs no adjustment.

**House figures at Unit 1: all ten exact on the first build.** `stage(` 89 · `lesson(` 14 · `dlg(`
14 · Language box 7 · Writer's box 7 · Homework 7 · `'Write with help` 7 · `'Write freely` 7 ·
`lines(` 8 · `pb()` 8. Key: 7 cycle banners, 7 page breaks, every stage code covered, 8 answers.

**Three faults the new harness caught that no earlier harness would have:**

1. ⚠ **30 occurrences of `§`** — a character this series does not use. The B2 register invited it
   (a paper with numbered paragraphs is exactly where `§` belongs) and the character check refused
   it. Every one is now **"paragraph 11"** written out, which is also better for a B2 reader.
2. ⚠ **A skeleton dialogue with two consecutive `B` turns** — an ask-and-self-answer pair, which the
   harness flags as always a fault. It was: B asks for the rewrite, B then reports what they now
   know, with A's rewrite hidden in a stage direction. Fixed by giving A the turn.
3. ⚠ **A cycle banner the cycle-map regex could not see.** `// ===== CYCLE 1.7 — REVIEW & MEDIATE
   =====` does not match `/\/\/ =+ ?(?:CYCLE )?(\d+\.\d+) ?=+/`, so cycle 1.7 was **absent from
   the unit's cycle map** — and the key's stage-coverage check compares against that map, which
   means **the key could have omitted all eleven of cycle 7's stages silently.** This is a new
   finding for the Series Bible: *a decorated cycle banner disables the coverage check that depends
   on it, and nothing fails — the count of cycles is only checked on the key.*

**And one fault the harness did not catch, found by reading:** the key's cycle 1 had no `stage('2')`
entry — the listening answers were missing — because the Language-box practise sample had been
written straight after stage 1. The key's stage-coverage check compares the *key* against the
*unit*, and would have caught it; it did not fail because at that point the key was one cycle long
and `checkKey` had not yet been reached. ⚠ **The coverage check only works on a complete key, so a
key written cycle by cycle must be re-run at the end, not only as it goes.**

# PART FOUR — BOOK 1 · UNITS 1–12, CYCLE BY CYCLE

**Book 1's spine: the contract Nour thinks is a mistake.**

In January, **Mr Rafiq Dallal**, procurement director of Ms Tannous's own customer in Beirut, writes
to Sham Trading directly. He offers a **three-year supply contract at four times the Tannous
volume** — and it cuts Ms Tannous, who found them, out of the chain. **Mr Ayoub wants it.**

⚠ **Nour believes it is a mistake, for three reasons she can evidence and one she cannot.** It makes
one customer about sixty per cent of turnover; it ends a relationship built over eighteen months by
somebody who behaved well; the contract carries a liquidated-damages clause nobody here can model;
and the fourth reason is that the company has never in twenty-eight years had a customer it could
not afford to lose. **Her problem is not the argument. It is that she has never written one to
somebody who can overrule her** — which is the gap she named in the last unit of B1.

# PART SEVEN — WORD COUNTS, PEER REVIEW, AND THE ONE REAL CONFLICT

## 7.1 · The conflict, and it is a different conflict from B1's

**At B1 the conflict was that the course asked for more words than the examination.** Preliminary
wanted 35–45 and 60–80; the course asked for ≥60 and 140–180, and the plan kept the course above
the examination deliberately, because a learner who can write 170 words of a real document can
write 70 of an examination answer and not the reverse.

⚠ **At B2 the examination has moved and the gap has narrowed.** Vantage asks for **120–140 words** in
Part 2 — against Preliminary's 60–80. **A course that kept B1's 140–180 would be asking for barely
more than the examination**, which is not a ladder.

## 7.2 · The recommendation

| | guided (stage 6) | free (stage 7) | models | test Part 1 | test Part 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A2 Book 1 | ≥25 | 35–60 | 45–100 | — | — |
| A2 Book 2 | ≥35 | 60–100 | 100–180 | — | — |
| B1 Book 1 | ≥50 | 100–140 | 180–260 | 35–45 | 60–80 |
| B1 Book 2 | ≥60 | 140–180 | 180–260 | 35–45 | 60–80 |
| ⚠ **B2 Book 1** | ⚠ **≥80** | ⚠ **200–240** | **250–350** | **40–50** | **120–140** |
| ⚠ **B2 Book 2** | ⚠ **≥80** | ⚠ **200–240** | **250–350** | **40–50** | **120–140** |

**Three things to notice, and the third is the one the Bible warns about.**

1. **The free band rises to 200–240**, which keeps the course a clear step above the examination's
   120–140 — the same relationship B1 had to Preliminary.
2. ⚠ **The guided band stops rising at 80.** Stage 6 is a frame with blanks; past about eighty words
   the frame stops teaching and starts transcribing. A2 → B1 → B2 is 25 → 35 → 50 → 60 → **80**,
   and 80 is where it stays.
3. ⚠ **The B2 bands are identical across both books, and that is deliberate.** The Bible's finding
   from the general B2 course: *four of the six word-count bands projected for Book 2 came back
   identical to Book 1's, because the examination fixes them and does not ask for more in the second
   year.* **Vantage fixes Part 1, Part 2 and, through them, the course bands.** **Only the page band
   and the test length should be expected to move between the two books.**

## 7.3 · Peer review — band 4, and one move in Book 2

| | band | what the slip says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A2 Book 1 | 1–2 | tick the criterion; find one thing |

## 9.3 · Fidelity criteria — what "done" means for a unit

A unit is finished when **all** of these hold:

1. `node qa.js` passes the whole tree with **0 faults**.
2. The unit measures inside the band, **or** the band is widened on the measurement and the widening
   is recorded in this file with the trim that was tested.
3. Every lexis list was cleared by `freecheck.js` **before the unit was written**.
4. The exam-practice task is the part the rotation says, and the Writing part alternates correctly.
5. Cycle 7 ends in a mediation that is **a fact somebody holds and nobody has written down** — not a
   summary of the unit.
6. ⚠ **Every number in every model document has a reason or a source beside it.** This is the
   strand's rule from A2 Unit 4 and it is checked by reading, not by the harness.
7. The Answer Key gives **a full sample answer for every open task**, and its teacher's note names
