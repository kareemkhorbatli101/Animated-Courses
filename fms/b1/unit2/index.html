Part 8 - Can-Do and glossary

1 card
  • I can say what work goes on behind a successful bilateral meeting, describe what a liaison officer actually does, and use present simple and present continuous accurately again after Unit 1.
  • I can use this unit's seven coordination and protocol terms correctly, arrange a room, interpretation and equipment from the details on a scenario card, and say what a protocol brief must contain.
  • I can choose the right modal verb for an obligation, a recommendation, a possibility and a polite request, give the ministry example for each, correct modal errors in coordination writing, and make a request of a counterpart at the level of politeness the situation needs.
  • I can predict the modal language a coordination call will use, follow four calls between a protocol officer and an embassy closely enough to extract each decision and deadline, choose a correct professional response to a request, and write down five coordination phrases.
  • I can make a coordination call that settles date, venue, interpretation and equipment, open a bilateral meeting formally, and say how a meeting is held to its agenda and its time.
  • I can read an account of a bilateral working group session for its preparation steps, its structure and its outcomes, discuss why each step was taken, and use modal verbs correctly when I set out a procedure.
  • I can recognise the five sections of a meeting agenda and what each one is for, write an agenda for a bilateral session, and write a meeting confirmation email of 80 to 100 words.
  • I can stress this unit's meeting and coordination vocabulary correctly, use the intonation that keeps meeting-room language formal rather than abrupt, and record a meeting opening on a topic from my own work.
  • I can describe how meeting behaviour differs across three cultural contexts, compare punctuality, turn-taking and decision-making in a table, and open a meeting appropriately for a European environmental NGO and for a Gulf Arab ministerial delegation.

The directions and every line of the acted conversations carry Arabic. The questions, the options and the answers are in English on purpose — they are the part of the exercise that has to be done.
