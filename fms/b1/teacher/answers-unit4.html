W-AExercise 1 in the book’s key

Pair Discussion – Your Country's Future in English

Question 1

B1 targetMy country is open and ready to work. We are a reliable partner that keeps its commitments and reports on them honestly. That is the message.

B2 extensionMy country is open — for partnership, for investment, and for scrutiny. The message I would send is not that we are ambitious but that we are reliable: frameworks are signed, committees meet on schedule, and reports are published whether or not the news is good. Ambition is cheap in diplomacy and every country claims it. Reliability is scarce, and it is the only claim a partner can verify before committing capital.

Question 2

B1 targetA note verbale is written entirely in the third person and uses fixed formulas. A business email uses the first person and ordinary professional language. Using the wrong one tells the recipient that the sender does not know diplomatic practice.

B2 extensionA note verbale is a formal communication between governments, written entirely in the third person — never "I" or "we" — and opening and closing with fixed compliment formulas. A business email is first-person and functional. The distinction matters because the form carries institutional meaning: a note verbale is the ministry speaking as an institution, and it creates a record in both chanceries. Sending a first-person letter where a note verbale is required does not merely look informal; it changes the legal and institutional weight of the communication, and the receiving ministry will notice. The reverse error is just as visible to the recipient.

Question 3

B1 targetMy country should show that its institutions work, that its commitments are specific — and that it reports honestly on progress. Partners can check all three.

B2 extensionThree things above all. First, that the institutions function independently of individuals — committees with fixed reporting cycles rather than relationships with particular officials. Second, that commitments are specific enough to be checked: dates, sectors, named mechanisms. Third, that the reporting is honest when the news is bad — because that is the only evidence a partner has that the good reports are true. Everything else a ministry communicates — the ceremonies, the frameworks, the visions — is read through those three. The fourth thing, which is harder to communicate, is that the institutions are not dependent on the individuals currently running them — because a partner is committing to a five-year framework — not to a minister.

W-BExercise 2 in the book’s key

Group Brainstorm – Types of Written Communication at the Ministry

Question 1

sample1. Note verbale — Category A — written by the Protocol or Political Affairs Directorate, signed institutionally rather than personally; its purpose is formal inter-governmental communication, in the third person throughout. 2. Memorandum of Understanding — Category A — drafted jointly by the relevant technical directorate and the Legal Directorate; it records agreed intentions and establishes the institutional machinery. 3. Aide-mémoire — Category A — prepared by the liaison officer after a meeting; it records what both sides understood to have been agreed. 4. Bilateral communiqué — Category B — drafted jointly and cleared at ministerial level; it is the public statement of a meeting's outcome. 5. Formal invitation — Category A — issued by Protocol, in the note verbale register for inter-governmental events. 6. Congratulatory note — Category A — from the minister to a counterpart, in the third person, on an appointment or a national day. 7. Circular — Category C — internal, from a directorate to ministry staff and missions, conveying procedure. 8. Press statement — Category B — Press and Information Office, for international media. 9. Annual report — Category B and C — prepared across directorates, published for partners and the public and used internally as the record of the year.

Spoken sample answer
Senior Officer: The thing to understand about a note verbale is that it is not a letter. A note verbale is written in the third person throughout — no "I" or "we." Instead: "The Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the Republic of Kazakhstan presents its compliments to the Embassy of the Federal Republic of Germany and has the honor to inform..." Every note verbale begins with this formula. Every note verbale ends with a closing compliments formula: "The Ministry of Foreign Affairs avails itself of this opportunity to renew to the Embassy the assurances of its highest consideration." These are not optional. They are the form through which the institution speaks — and getting them right signals that the diplomatic service knows what it is doing.
Junior Officer: And if I write it in first person?
Senior Officer: Then it is not a note verbale. It is a letter — and a letter carries different weight and a different institutional meaning. The distinction matters to the receiving party. Always use the correct form for the correct purpose.
Senior Officer: Once the note verbale is cleared, it goes to Consular Affairs for authentication before dispatch. Nurlan's section handles that — have your document ready 24 hours before it needs to leave the building.

1CExercise 6 in the book’s key

Group Discussion – Formal Correspondence Norms

Question 1

B1 targetFormal correspondence, such as a note verbale, is third-person and uses fixed formulas; it is the institution speaking. Semi-formal correspondence, such as a coordination email to a counterpart, is first-person and functional. A note verbale invites an ambassador; an email confirms a room booking.

B2 extensionFormal diplomatic correspondence is third-person, formulaic, and institutional — the note verbale, the aide-mémoire, the formal invitation. Semi-formal correspondence is first-person and operational: the coordination emails and logistics notes that make the formal instruments possible. An example of each: a note verbale inviting His Excellency the Ambassador to a signing ceremony, against an email to the embassy's logistics officer confirming that twelve seats and German interpretation are arranged. Both are necessary and neither substitutes for the other. The error that matters is mixing the registers inside one document. The second error, which is subtler, is sending a note verbale where an email would have done — it reads as an attempt to add weight the subject does not carry.

Question 2

B1 targetSome phrases are only for very formal correspondence: "presents its compliments," "has the honor to," "avails itself of this opportunity to renew the assurances of its highest consideration." They should never appear in a coordination email, where they would look like parody.

B2 extension"Presents its compliments," "has the honor to," and "avails itself of this opportunity to renew to the Embassy the assurances of its highest consideration" belong exclusively to the note verbale register. So do "Your Excellency" as a written form of address and "it is the honor of this Ministry to inform." None of them should appear in a coordination email: in that context they read either as parody or as evidence that the writer has learned formulas without understanding what they are for. The register signals the weight of the communication, so using the heaviest register for a room booking devalues it for the occasions that need it.

Question 3

B1 targetThe most serious mistakes are a wrong name or title, a lapse into the first person, and a missing closing formula. Each signals that the ministry did not check its own document, which is the impression a note verbale exists to prevent.

B2 extensionThree critical errors. First, an incorrect name, title, or form of address — this is the one the receiving ministry will remember, and it is read as a judgement about how seriously the relationship is taken. Second, slipping into the first person, which means the document is no longer a note verbale at all. Third, omitting or mangling the closing compliments formula — which looks like carelessness in the one part of the document that is entirely within the drafter's control. All three signal the same thing: that the document was not checked. A note verbale is a demonstration of institutional competence, so a sloppy one actively damages the message it carries. The practical safeguard is the three-pass check, and it should be done by someone other than the drafter wherever the document is going outside the building.

Spoken sample answer
Senior Officer: Here is the rule I give every new drafter. Read the document once for content, then read it a second time looking only at names and titles, and a third time looking only at the opening and closing formulas. Three passes, and never fewer. The content is usually fine — because you have thought about it. What goes wrong is the thing you did not think about — a surname spelled from memory, an "Excellency" where the rank does not take one, a closing formula pasted from a note to a different mission. The receiving chancery files our document. A mistake in it outlives everyone in this room.

2EExercise 12 in the book’s key

Speaking Practice

Question 1

B1 targetOver the next two years, the ministry will expand its network of bilateral cooperation frameworks in Europe and Asia. We are going to open two new liaison offices — because the budget has already been approved. We will also strengthen the diaspora services program.

B2 extensionOver the next two years the ministry will expand its network of bilateral cooperation frameworks in Europe and Asia — that is a projection based on the current pace of negotiation. We are going to open two new liaison offices and expand the digital connectivity unit — because both have been approved and budgeted. The ministry will also, I expect, come under more scrutiny as the first quarterly implementation reports are published, and preparing for that honestly is itself one of the priorities. The honest framing in a briefing is to separate the three categories explicitly rather than let a senior official guess which is which.

Question 2

B1 targetKazakhstan–France Partnership Plan. Point one: the Joint Cultural Commission is meeting in October — that is confirmed. Point two: we are going to launch the renewable energy research program in September. Point three: the digital connectivity roadmap shall be delivered by December 2025, as the Framework requires.

B2 extensionKazakhstan–France Partnership Plan, in three points. One: the Joint Cultural Commission is meeting in Astana in October — Present Continuous — because the date is fixed and the invitations have gone out. Two: we are going to launch the joint renewable energy research programme in September, which is going to rather than will because the funding decision has already been taken on both sides. Three: the digital connectivity roadmap shall be delivered by December 2025 — shall — because that is a binding obligation under the Framework rather than an expectation. And a fourth — which is only a projection: French technical expertise will, I think, prove most valuable in the e-government work.

Question 3

B1 targetConfirmed: We are signing the Cultural Exchange Protocol with Germany on 18 June. We are briefing the minister's office on 20 August. Predictions: Our partnerships in Europe will deepen over the next three years. Interest from Asian investors will grow once the first projects are operational.

B2 extensionConfirmed arrangements, in Present Continuous: we are signing the Kazakhstan–Germany Cultural Exchange Protocol on 18 June at eleven o'clock, and we are briefing the minister's office on the investment catalogue on 20 August. Both are in the diary and the invitations are issued. Predictions, with will: our European partnerships will deepen over the next three years, and interest from Asian investors will grow once the first infrastructure projects are visibly operational rather than merely announced. The distinction matters in a briefing — because a senior official hearing will assumes you are forecasting and hearing are meeting assumes it is fixed.

3AExercise 13 in the book’s key

Before You Listen: Predict and Discuss

Question 1

sampleWe expect formal future language — will for the significance of the agreement, going to for the arrangements that have already been decided. The tone will be ceremonial but restrained. Expect first-person plural on behalf of the country rather than the individual, and a forward-looking closing sentence.

Question 2

sampleSpeakers will use both forms: 'will' for projections about the investment climate and 'going to' for programmes already designed and funded. Expect shall at least once, for an institutional commitment with a deadline. The tone will be confident and specific — because the audience is deciding whether to commit money.

Question 3

sampleA toast calls for ceremonial future language and the warmest register of the four. Expect will for the vision — "a future that will be built together" — and going to for the programmes. The formality is high but the content is deliberately general; a toast is not the place for dates and figures.

Question 4

samplePrivate professional language is more precise and less ceremonial. Expect Present Continuous throughout for confirmed arrangements — "we are holding," "we are publishing" — with going to reserved for what has been decided but not yet released, and shall for formal institutional acts. The contrast with the public speeches is that every sentence carries a date.

3BExercise 14 in the book’s key

Listening: Four Formal Cooperation Contexts

Question 1

sampleExcerpt 1: A joint technical committee is going to meet within sixty days; the MoU will create a structured framework for cooperation in infrastructure, education, and trade; the partnership will grow and deepen. Excerpt 2: Modernization programmes are going to be implemented over three years; the ministry shall release an investment opportunity catalogue within thirty days; the delegation is meeting more than forty delegations during the conference. Excerpt 3: The signed framework will define the next chapter of the relationship; the programmes they are going to implement in culture, education, and economic partnership will benefit both peoples. Excerpt 4: The first technical committee meeting is on 15 September; the investment catalogue is being published on 30 August and is going to include twelve priority projects; the minister's office is being briefed on 20 August; formal invitations shall be issued by the end of the month.

Question 2

sampleIn the public speeches (1–3), future forms carry rhetorical weight: will for vision and significance, going to for programmes already decided, and shall once for a formal institutional commitment. Dates are rare and deliberately so. In the internal planning session (4), the dominant form is Present Continuous and every sentence has a date attached — because the purpose is coordination rather than persuasion. Going to appears only for content already decided but not yet released, and shall only for the formal act of issuing invitations. The grammar follows the function exactly. The practical lesson for a drafter is that the public register tolerates vagueness about timing and the internal register does not, so the same content has to be rewritten rather than reused.

Question 3

B1 targetAigerim uses Present Continuous for everything that is confirmed — "we are holding," "we are publishing," "we are briefing." She reserves going to for the catalogue contents and shall for the formal invitations. This precision signals that her team is reliable and professionally coordinated.

B2 extensionAigerim uses Present Continuous for every confirmed arrangement — "we are holding the first technical committee meeting on September 15th," "we are publishing that on August 30th," "we are briefing them on August 20th" — and attaches a date to each. She shifts to going to only for the catalogue's contents, which have been decided but not yet released, and to shall only for the institutional act of issuing invitations. The effect on a senior listener is immediate: nothing in her report is a hope, and the grammar itself tells them which items are fixed. That distinction is what professional coordination sounds like.

3CExercise 15 in the book’s key

Choose the Correct Formal Response

Question 1

sampleCorrect answer: (b) Thank you, Your Excellency. The joint technical committee is going to meet within sixty days to begin implementation planning — concrete results will follow that process. Kazakhstan is committed to demonstrating through action that this partnership is more than a signature. — Why (a) is wrong: "We will try our best" commits to nothing that can be checked. At a signing ceremony the other delegation is listening for the mechanism and the timetable - a committee, a date - not for goodwill.

Question 2

sampleCorrect answer: (b) That is the right question to ask, and it is one we take seriously. Each framework includes a joint monitoring committee with quarterly reporting obligations. The first progress reports will be published within ninety days of each committee's inaugural session. We are also meeting with all partner delegations at this conference specifically to review implementation timelines — accountability is built into the architecture. — Why (a) is wrong: It over-promises. No ministry can guarantee everything, and an investor who hears a guarantee that broad discounts the rest of what you say. The correct reply treats the question as reasonable and answers it.

Question 3

sampleCorrect answer: (a) Kazakhstan is very interested in renewable energy cooperation — it is going to be one of our central modernization priorities. I would like to schedule a follow-up session with our Energy Affairs team. We are meeting on Thursday — are you available to join us then? — Why (b) is wrong: It hands the question back at the moment it was asked. A ministry representative at an international conference is expected to be able to state a national priority without checking first.

Question 4

sampleCorrect answer: (b) Kazakhstan is committed to participating in the Berlin Forum. Our Economic Affairs team is going to lead the delegation, and the ministry shall communicate our participation officially through formal channels. I will ensure you receive a formal confirmation letter within the next ten working days. — Why (a) is wrong: "Someone" and "probably" leave the other side unable to plan. Commitment language names who is coming, what they will do and when - which is exactly what the future forms in this unit are for.

4CExercise 19 in the book’s key

Discussion: Bridge-Builders and Future Vision

Question 1

B1 targetI think reliability is the most important quality. If you do what you said you would do, by the day you said, everything else becomes possible. Without it, curiosity and emotional intelligence have nothing to build on.

B2 extensionA: Reliability is the foundation — because it is the only one of the five that a counterpart can verify independently. Everything else they have to take on trust, and reliability is what earns that trust. B: I would put precision first. Getting a name or a title wrong undoes a year of goodwill in a single document, and the damage is disproportionate to the effort of checking. C: My choice is patience. Diplomatic work has long flat periods where nothing visible happens, and the people who sustain engagement through those periods are the ones who are there when something becomes possible. D: What is striking is that nobody chose genuine curiosity, although all of us think it matters. Perhaps because it cannot be demanded of anyone — it is either there or it is not. E: What the ranking exercise really reveals is that four of the five can be trained and one cannot, which is worth knowing when you are recruiting.

Question 2

B1 targetKazakhstan sits between Europe, Russia, China, and the Turkic world. This creates opportunity — because we can host conversations that would be difficult elsewhere. It also creates challenge — because every partner watches how we deal with the others.

B2 extensionA: Kazakhstan sits at a genuine crossroads — between Europe, Russia, China, and the Turkic world — and that creates a diplomatic opportunity no amount of effort could manufacture: we can convene conversations that would be awkward or impossible in other capitals. B: The challenge is the same fact seen from the other side. Every partner reads our relationship with every other partner, so a framework signed with one is assessed by all of them. C: That means our diplomacy has to be unusually consistent. A country in this position cannot say different things in different rooms — because the rooms compare notes. D: Handled well it makes us a bridge; handled carelessly it makes us a country that nobody fully trusts. The margin between the two is narrower than it looks. E: The practical discipline is to keep one version of every substantive position, written down, and to use it in every room — adapting the register and nothing else.

Question 3

B1 targetI am going to write one formal document in English every week, and I will ask a colleague to check it. I am also going to keep a list of the stress patterns I get wrong.

B2 extensionA: I am going to practise one note verbale a week against a real scenario and have a colleague check it — three passes, as the unit recommends, with the third for formulas only. B: I will record myself delivering a formal welcome once a week and listen only to the endings of sentences — because that is my weakest point. C: I am going to keep a working list of the words whose stress I get wrong, and add to it rather than hoping to remember. D: And the one that is hardest: I am going to say "I do not have that figure" when I do not have it, rather than estimating. That is a habit — not a skill, and it will take the longest to build.

5BExercise 21 in the book’s key

Reading Comprehension: Discussion Questions

Question 1

B1 targetThis framework is credible because it is specific. It names the bodies, the numbers, and the dates: fifty students a year, a roadmap by December 2025, a review at three years. A framework without those would be a declaration.

B2 extensionA: It is credible because almost every commitment is countable. Fifty students a year, ten researchers, four university partnerships, six committee members from each side, a roadmap by December 2025, a review at three years. A reader can check every one of those in eighteen months. B: The institutional design is also realistic rather than grand: standing commissions with annual meetings — not a permanent secretariat that would need staffing neither side has committed. C: What I would question is the three-year review. Cultural and academic programmes take longer than that to show results, and a review too early can kill something that was working. D: But on balance the specificity is what makes it believable. Vagueness in a framework is almost always a sign that one side was not ready to commit. E: What is missing is a mechanism for publishing the committee's reports. A framework this specific deserves to be checkable by people who are not in either ministry.

Question 2

B1 targetKazakhstan brings its cultural heritage, its universities, and its position between Europe and Asia. It also brings an existing e-government platform, which gives French partners a working environment to build on rather than a blank sheet.

B2 extensionA: Kazakhstan brings its own heritage and a serious cultural sector — the exhibitions travelling to Paris, Lyon, and Marseille are not a favour to us, they are content French institutions want. B: In the academic strand we bring research sites and conditions France does not have, particularly for renewable energy at scale in a continental climate. C: In the digital strand we bring a functioning national e-government base, which is a test environment rather than a blank sheet, and a geographic position that makes connectivity infrastructure here strategically valuable. D: The framing matters. A country that presents itself as a recipient gets treated as one, and the agreement itself is written between equals — "fifty Kazakh students to France" sits alongside "ten French specialists to Kazakhstan" in the same paragraph. The reciprocity is the strongest evidence that this was negotiated rather than offered.

Question 3

B1 targetThe word 'shall' signals a binding obligation. 'Going to' signals that the planning has already happened. 'Will' is used for projected outcomes. Together they create a graduated scale of certainty, and a careful reader can see exactly how firm each commitment is.

B2 extensionA: The use of shall marks the commitments that are binding — the Film and Literature Festival shall be established, the annual committee shall oversee implementation. These are obligations — not intentions. B: Going to marks what has already been planned and funded: the heritage exhibitions are going to be hosted, the research programme is going to be launched in September. The decision has been taken; only the execution remains. C: Will carries the projections and the outcomes — French expertise will support the e-government work, the Commission will meet annually. D: The combined effect is a graduated scale of certainty that a lawyer and a journalist can both read: shall, then going to, then will. A framework that used one form throughout would tell the reader nothing about which parts are enforceable. E: It is also why a drafter should never 'tidy' the forms into consistency.

Question 4

B1 targetI would prioritize healthcare cooperation and environmental rehabilitation. Both affect people directly and both need expertise we do not yet have at scale. Vocational training is the third — because every framework in this unit assumes a workforce that can maintain what it builds.

B2 extensionA: Healthcare and medical training — because the benefit reaches people who will never see a framework document, and because a partnership measured in trained clinicians is very hard to present as symbolic. B: Environmental rehabilitation — we have specific, well-documented legacy problems and European partners have both the technical capacity and a political interest in addressing them. C: Legal and judicial modernization, which is the least glamorous and probably the most consequential: investment frameworks are only worth what the courts behind them are worth, so this strand quietly determines whether the economic strands work at all. D: And one that is often forgotten: vocational and technical training. Every infrastructure framework in this unit assumes a workforce that can build and then maintain what is built — and that assumption is usually the weakest part of the plan. E: And one I would not prioritize: another cultural framework. We have a good one, and a second would be an easy signature rather than a hard commitment. F: The test for any new strand is whether we can name in advance the numbers by which we would judge it in three years.

Question 4

sampleFRAME-work: two syllables, stress on the first, both clearly pronounced. — EN-voy: two syllables, stress on the first; the second is short — not 'en-VOY.' Mispronouncing a title in front of the person who holds it is the one stress error that is actually costly.

7BExercise 29 in the book’s key

Formal Intonation — Warm but Dignified

Question 1

sampleSlight elevation on 'EVENING,' then a short pause, then a steady warm pace on 'partnership.' The elevation carries the warmth and the pause carries the dignity. Flat delivery sounds like reading a list; rising delivery throughout sounds like a sales conference. The contrast between the two halves is the whole effect.

Question 2

sampleStress: HON-or, PRO-pose, TOAST, EVE-ning. Warmth and slight elevation on 'honor,' then settling into a falling line by the end of the sentence. Do not rush 'it is my honor' — it is the phrase that establishes the register for everything that follows.

Question 3

sampleStress: FRIEND-ship, co-op-er-A-tion, FU-ture, to-GETH-er. Three rising beats on the list, then a clear fall on 'together.' The rise-rise-rise-fall pattern is what makes a toast sound like a toast rather than a sentence; the final fall is the cue for the room to raise glasses.

7DExercise 31 in the book’s key

Practice and Record

Question 1

Question 2

sampleTwo-part check: (1) On 'Your Excellency,' is there warmth and a real pause, or did you run straight on? (2) On each declaration, does the line fall at the end? Those two account for almost everything that separates a formal statement that works from one that does not. Listen for them separately — you cannot hear both at once.

Question 3

sampleTime check: 30 seconds for 5–6 sentences means roughly five seconds a sentence, which is slower than it feels. If your recording runs to 22 seconds you are rushing, however clear the words are. Record again with a deliberate pause after the address and between the acknowledgment and the forward-looking sentence. A formal statement delivered quickly reads as nervous, whatever it says.

One Dinner, Three Cultures

Question 1

B1 targetThe safest approach is to seat by institutional rank, with the most senior guest of each delegation closest to the host. Tell each delegation the seating plan in advance so nobody is surprised. For the East Asian delegation, remember that the place of honour is furthest from the door, and arrange their section accordingly. Circulating the plan in advance is what converts protocol knowledge into a working arrangement.

B2 extensionA: The most senior guest of each delegation sits closest to the host, and within each delegation the order follows that delegation's own hierarchy rather than a single rule imposed across the table. B: Three practical details decide whether it works. The East Asian place of honour is furthest from the door, so that section is arranged on a different axis from the European one. The Gulf delegation expects clear and visible deference to their lead throughout the evening — not only at seating. And the European delegation will care least about position and most about being able to talk to the host at some point. C: Above all, circulate the plan in advance to each delegation's protocol contact. A seating error discovered at the table cannot be fixed; one discovered the day before is a five-minute conversation.

Question 2

B1 targetThe host should place non-alcoholic drinks of the same quality and in the same glassware at every place, and raise a toast that does not refer to wine. Participation matters more than the contents of the glass, and nobody should have to explain their choice.

B2 extensionA: The host ensures that a serious non-alcoholic option — not an afterthought — is at every place, in the same glassware, poured at the same time. The point is that no guest has to decline anything in front of the table. B: The toast itself is worded so that it works for every glass: "to friendship, to cooperation, and to a future that will be built together," never "let us drink to." C: The host should also not toast twice in a way that forces repeated raising — because that multiplies the moments in which a difference becomes visible. D: Handled properly, the Gulf delegation participates fully in the toast and the question never arises. Handled badly, one delegation spends the evening being conspicuously accommodated, which is worse than being ignored. The same principle applies to the menu — which should be halal throughout rather than selectively.

Question 3

B1 targetIn Japanese culture, gifts are received with both hands and a slight bow, and they are typically not opened at the table. Opening it publicly would put the giver in the position of having their choice evaluated in front of others. The host should not press him to open it, and should not read the delay as indifference.

B2 extensionA: In Japanese practice a gift is received with both hands and a small bow, and it is not opened in front of the giver or the table. Opening it publicly would turn a private courtesy into a public evaluation of the giver's judgement. B: The host should therefore neither encourage him to open it nor interpret the closed package as coolness — the bow and the two-handed acceptance already carried the thanks. C: The practical lesson for the host is reciprocal: offer gifts of good craftsmanship rather than high price, present them with both hands, and avoid anything containing alcohol at a table that includes a Gulf delegation. D: And expect that the European guests, who will open theirs immediately with visible pleasure, are doing exactly the right thing in their own register. Three correct behaviours at one table is not a contradiction to be resolved. D: What the host must not do is apply one rule to all three and hope that consistency will be read as fairness.

On behalf of the French and German delegations, we accept this toast with pleasure and look forward to the first Commission session in October. To our countries, and to the work ahead.

8EExercise 36 in the book’s key

Closing Discussion: Your Country's Dual Register and Your Commitment

Question 1

B1 targetKazakhstan's position between Europe, Russia, China, and the Turkic world gives it a cultural range that is hard to replicate. We can move between registers that most countries only operate in one of.

B2 extensionA: Kazakhstan's dual cultural fluency is the strength. We are at home in the formal, relational register that Gulf and East Asian partners expect, and equally at home in the direct, egalitarian register of our European partners — and we can switch between them in the same building on the same day. B: That is not a small thing. Most ministries are genuinely comfortable in one register and are visibly performing the other. C: The second strength is geographic and follows from the first: we can host conversations between parties who would find a meeting in each other's capitals difficult. D: The constraint is consistency. A country that can speak to everybody is only trusted if it says the same thing to everybody, and the temptation to tailor the substance rather than the register is the real professional risk in a position like ours. E: The practical safeguard is to write down the position once and use the same words everywhere, which also makes it much easier to defend when someone eventually quotes it back.

Question 2

B1 targetI am going to write one formal document in English every week and have it checked. I will also stop estimating figures I do not have, and say so instead.

B2 extensionA: I am going to make the three-pass check a habit on every document I draft — once for content, once for names and titles, once for formulas. It costs five minutes and it is the difference between a document that works and one that is remembered for the wrong reason. B: I will record one formal statement a week and listen only to how my sentences end — because that is my weakest point and I cannot hear it while I am speaking. C: I am going to keep the stress list and add to it rather than trusting my memory. D: And the hardest commitment: I will say "I do not have that figure" when I do not have it. That is not a skill I need to learn; it is a habit I need to build, and it will take the longest.

Question 3

B1 targetEvery email, meeting, and document is the ministry speaking — not just me. That is the message I want to carry. Precision and reliability are not administrative details — they are how a country is judged.

B2 extensionA: That every interaction is the institution speaking. A misspelled name in my email is the ministry's error — not mine, and a figure I estimated is the country's figure once it leaves my outbox. B: That precision and reliability are not administrative virtues but diplomatic ones. The partners in this unit — French, German, Gulf, Japanese — all decide what to commit on the basis of whether our details hold up. C: That the registers are a form of respect and not a performance, which is why learning them properly matters. D: And that the quiet work is the work. Nobody will remember who coordinated the room, checked the formulas, or caught the wrong title. The relationship that holds for five years will have been built by people whose names are not on the framework. E: And that this is a long game. The frameworks in this course all run five years or more — which means the person who signs is rarely the person who delivers.
