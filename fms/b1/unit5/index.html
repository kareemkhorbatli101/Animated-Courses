Part 8 - Can-Do and glossary

1 card
  • I can say what economic diplomacy opens doors to, name my country's main investment partners and priority sectors, and use future forms accurately again after Unit 4.
  • I can use this unit's seven investment and facilitation terms correctly, read an investor's role card to identify their sector and what they need from the ministry, and match an investor to the ministry resources that can actually help them.
  • I can tell a real possibility from a hypothetical one, form the first and second conditional correctly, correct conditional errors in investment talk, and answer an investor's questions using each conditional for what it is for.
  • I can predict how an investment conversation will go, follow four investment diplomacy scenarios closely enough to identify each investor's interest and what the officer commits to, choose a correct officer response to an investor's question, and write down five investment phrases.
  • I can deliver an investment briefing script, take part in a panel discussion at a national investment forum, and say what makes an investment pitch work and what makes one fail.
  • I can read an investment forum profile for its purpose, its sectors, its delegations and its outcomes, discuss what the forum achieved, and use conditional structures the way investment writing uses them.
  • I can recognise the parts of a formal investment letter and what each one is for, write investment correspondence from a table of data, and write a formal investment invitation letter of 100 to 120 words.
  • I can stress this unit's investment and legal vocabulary correctly, use the rise-and-fall rhythm that makes a conditional sentence sound confident rather than hesitant, and record a dialogue using a real investment question from my own work.
  • I can describe how business culture shapes an investment meeting, compare directness, relationship-building and the pace of decisions in a table, and host both a German and a Gulf investor meeting appropriately.

The directions and every line of the acted conversations carry Arabic. The questions, the options and the answers are in English on purpose — they are the part of the exercise that has to be done.
