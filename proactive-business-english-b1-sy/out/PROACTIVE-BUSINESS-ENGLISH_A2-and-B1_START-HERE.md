## 3 · How a unit works — the same in all 48

**Seven cycles a unit. One cycle = one 90-minute lesson in thirteen stages.**

| Lesson A — Speaking | Lesson B — Writing |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Get ready · 2 Listen and read (+ Language box) | 5 Read (+ Writer's box) |
| 3 Practise · 3R Perform | 6 Write with help · 6P Check together · 6R Share |
| 4 Your turn · 4R Report | 7 Write freely · 7P Respond · 7R Share · Homework |

**Cycle 7 of every unit is Review & Mediate**: it revises the six functions, carries one
**exam-practice task** from the level's examination, and ends with a **mediation** — one thing the
workplace knows and has never written down.

**Word counts:** A2 Book 1 ≥25 / 35–60 · A2 Book 2 ≥35 / 60–100 · B1 Book 1 ≥50 / 100–140 ·
B1 Book 2 ≥60 / 140–180, with models at 45–100 rising to 180–260.
**Peer review** rises across the four books: bands 1–2 at A2 Book 1, band 3 at A2 Book 2 and B1
Book 1, **band 3 + *name the reader you are being*** at B1 Book 2.

## 5 · What the two levels are each for

**A2 teaches a learner to write the thing down.** By the end of A2 Book 2 they can write a CV, a
forecast, a problem report, a complaint, an email that asks for one thing, and a description of
their own company.

**B1 teaches them to write it for somebody who cannot ask a question.** Book 1 builds the ten
documents a coordinator writes — a handover note, a procedure page, an authority matrix, an
incident report, an options paper, an inspection note, minutes, a correction to terms, a position
paper — against a reader who is paid to find the gap. Book 2 is the export book: every document has
to work for a buyer in Beirut who cannot telephone the warehouse, and for a night-shift worker who
is alone and cannot ask.

**Four things run through B1 Book 2, and they are what its End-of-Book Test marks:**

1. **Write for the reader who is not in the building.**
2. **Say what is not known.**
3. **Put the number where the work is** — a control kept away from the work will be forgotten.
4. **Ask the person who does the work, and then ask who has never been asked.**

## 7 · Rebuilding any of it

`Source-and-Plans/` holds `pipeline_A2.zip`, `pipeline_B1.zip` and the three plans. Each pipeline
is one JavaScript file per unit, one per key, the test scripts, a frozen renderer (`lib.js`), the
bound-volume builder, and the QA harness.

```
unzip pipeline_B1.zip && cd pipeline
node qa.js                     # whole tree: 720 lexis items, 0 faults
./build.sh bound               # Book 1 bound volumes
PE_BOOK=2 ./build.sh bound     # Book 2 bound volumes
```

⚠ **Load a script before you build it** — `node -e "require('./unit20.js')"`. The loader names the
bad line; the renderer does not.

## 8 · One thing to do with the last unit

B1 Unit 24.7 reports a count taken at this company: **eleven people took the A2 course, three still
write anything in English two years later, and they are not the three with the best marks.** What
the three have in common is that **somebody is waiting for a document from each of them every week
and would notice if it did not come.**

⚠ **So the last session of the B1 course is not a test.** It is one document, one reader, one date
inside the following fortnight, written on a list the teacher keeps — **and the teacher asks in
October.** It is the only instruction in either level for something that happens after the course,
and it is the one that decides whether any of the other 2,950 pages survive.
