## 2 · What is different at B1, in one paragraph each

**The learner is no longer new.** At A2 Unit 1 Nour was introducing herself as the new
assistant; at A2 Unit 24 she was writing a proposal with an exit in it. B1 opens with her as
**administration coordinator across two sites**, and the book's subject is what that costs:
explaining a cause in writing, carrying a rule she did not make, reporting what somebody else
said, negotiating, and training a junior. The arc of B1 is **from doing the work to being
answerable for it.**

**The grammar ladder is the general B1 Syria Edition's, in its order, unchanged.** That was the
decision the author took at A2 and it has paid: the two books interleave, a class can run either
or both, and a learner moving between them is never ahead or behind. Part Four and Part Five
below name the general B1 unit each Business unit interleaves with.

**The examination anchor changes, and this time a business examination exists.** At A2 there is
no Cambridge business examination, so A2 Key was the anchor. At B1 there is: **B1 Business
Preliminary**. Its weighting is not 30·30·20·20 and the tests must follow it. §6 below sets this
out, and it is Decision 1.

**The writing lengths pull in two directions for the first time in this series**, because the
examination asks for *fewer* words than the course ladder. §7 below sets that out, and it is
Decision 2. It is the most consequential thing in this plan.

# PART TWO — WHAT THE MEASURED RECORD SAYS, BEFORE ANY PROJECTION IS MADE

Six books have been measured in this container. **Nothing below is a projection:**

| level and book | SB per unit | AK per unit | guided / free writing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| General A2 Book 1 | 31 | 17–22 | ≥25 / 35–60 |
| General A2 Book 2 | 31–33 | 18–22 | ≥35 / 60–100 |
| **Business A2 Book 1** | **29–32** (U12 = 33) | **15–17** (U12 = 18) | ≥25 / 35–60 |
| **Business A2 Book 2** | **35–36** (U24 = 37) | **18–21** (U24 = 22) | ≥35 / 60–100 |
| General B1 Book 1 | 35–38 | 22–30 | ≥50 / 100–140 |
| General B1 Book 2 | 38–40 | 29–31 | ≥60 / 140–180 |

Four facts follow from that table and they govern every number in this plan.

**1 · A strand that changes its word counts has changed its page band.** Business A2's two books
have identical structure and differ by five pages a unit. The cause is not content, it is the
word counts: ≥25/35–60 against ≥35/60–100. **So the band must be measured again at the first
unit of each book and never inherited from the book before it.**

**2 · The Business strand runs heavier per cycle than the general course at the same word
counts.** Business A2 Book 2 measured 35–36 on word counts a whole level below general B1 Book
1's 35–38. The reason is apparatus: role cards, phrase cards, checklists, board-clinic lines and
the *what to expect* boxes that a workplace book needs and a general book does not. **A Business
page is denser than a general page, so do not read a general band across.**

**3 · Page counts are priced in unsplittable blocks, not in words.** `box()` is `cantSplit`.
Measured five times in the A2 Business strand alone: two units and three tests each had a final
page carrying nothing but one box. Two were bought back and three were not, and the two that
moved were bought **one line at a time**. ⚠ **The rule, which is the useful half: a trim that
does not move a page is reverted.** A cut that buys nothing has not improved the text; it has
only shortened it, and the material goes back in.

**4 · Test lengths have been mis-projected at every level in this series, in both directions.**
B1 general projected 10–11 and delivered 12. B2 projected 14–15 with a key of 11–12 and
delivered 14 with a key of 10. Business A2 Book 1 hit 8+6 and 9+8 exactly and Book 2 missed
three of its four figures. **A test page is denser than a unit page**, which is why projections
built from unit density fail. The figures in §6 are therefore marked as projections and Phase 2
replaces them with measurements.

# PART THREE — THE PROJECTIONS, AND THE RULE THAT REPLACES THEM

⚠ **Everything in this part is a projection and is expected to be wrong.** It exists so that
Phase 0 has something to disprove. **Unit 1's Student's Book and Answer Key are built and
measured before Unit 2 is written**, and the measured band replaces this table on the day it is
taken, in this file, with the old figure struck through rather than deleted.

| | projected SB | projected AK | basis |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| **B1 Business Book 1** (Units 1–12) | **39–43** | **24–29** | Business A2 Book 2's measured 35–36, plus the step the general course took from A2 to B1 (31 → 35–38) |
| **B1 Business Book 2** (Units 13–24) | **41–45** | **26–31** | Book 1's band plus the +2 that every Book 2 in this series has taken, less the front matter Book 2 does not carry |

**Stated exceptions, projected now so that they are not treated as failures later.** Unit 12
carries the end-of-book block and Unit 24 carries the end-of-level block; both ran over band in
Business A2 (U12 at SB 33 against 29–32; U24 at SB 37 against 35–36) and both are expected to
again. **Unit 24 is also where the *letter to your B2 self* lives and should be allowed to run
long.**

⚠ **Hold the band; do not aim at its midpoint.** A unit built to the middle of a band has been
padded or trimmed against its own content, and both show on the page.

⚠ **Measure the bound volume as well as the sum of the units.** `bound.js` puts a page break
before every component, and one C1 unit measured 50 alone and 51 bound because the extra line at
the top of the first page pushed a `cantSplit` block over. Business A2 Book 1's bound Answer Key
carries one **blank** page for the same reason; Book 2's happens not to. `bound.js` is the
series' own file and is **not** to be changed to suppress this without the author's say-so.

# PART SIX — TESTS, AND THE EXAMINATION ANCHOR

## 6.1 · The anchor changes at B1, and it is a business examination

At A2 there is no Cambridge business examination, so **A2 Key** was the anchor and the split was
30·30·20·20. At B1 there is one: **B1 Business Preliminary**. As I have it, the examination is
three papers — **Reading and Writing as one paper at 50 %, Listening at 25 %, Speaking at 25 %**
— with Writing Part 1 a short internal message and Part 2 a longer piece of business
correspondence, Listening in four parts, and Speaking in pairs in three parts of about twelve
minutes in all.

⚠ **This must be checked against the current handbook before the first test is written.**
Examination specifications move, my figures are from training and not from the page in front of
me, and the series' own rule is to read the examination's own weighting rather than to remember
it. Phase 0 includes that check and this section is amended in place if it is wrong.

**What that means for the papers.** Printing a single 50-mark Reading-and-Writing paper is
faithful to the examination and bad for teaching: a teacher cannot see where a class lost
marks. **My recommendation is four printed papers at Reading 25 · Writing 25 · Listening 25 ·
Speaking 25**, with a line in the candidate instructions saying that the real examination
combines the first two into one paper of 50. That keeps the weighting exact and the diagnosis
possible. **This is Decision 1.**

## 6.2 · The two tests

| | marks | split | questions | projected pages |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| **Mid-Book Test** (after Unit 6 / Unit 18) | 60 | R 15 · W 15 · L 15 · S 15 | 30 | **10 + 8** |
| **End-of-Book Test** (after Unit 12 / Unit 24) | 100 | R 25 · W 25 · L 25 · S 25 | 50 | **13 + 9** |

⚠ Both page figures are projections and Phase 2 replaces them with measurements. The basis is
Business A2's measured 8+7 and 10+7 plus the step the general course took from A2 to B1.

**The key's page-break convention, which is series canon and was got wrong twice at A2:** a
**unit** key puts a `pb()` before each cycle after the first; a **Mid-Book** key uses **three**;
an **End-of-Book** key uses **five** — one before each of the four papers and one before the
closing band table.

**One deliberate test-only mark, inherited from A2 and worth keeping.** A2 Business put 5 of 20
Writing Part 2 marks on **the exit** — how a proposed trial would be stopped and what stopping
would cost. No unit marked it; the test did. At B1 the equivalent is **the assumption**: the
End-of-Book Writing Part 2 should put marks on the assumptions printed under a forecast or a
recommendation. ⚠ It is declared here so that it is a decision and not a surprise.

# PART SEVEN — WORD COUNTS, AND THE ONE REAL CONFLICT IN THIS PLAN

## 7.1 · The conflict

The general B1 course ladder asks for **guided ≥50 / free 100–140** in Book 1 and **≥60 /
140–180** in Book 2, with models of 180–260 and 250–350. That is the ladder a learner needs to
be ready for B2.

**B1 Business Preliminary asks for less.** Its Writing Part 1 is a short internal message of
about 30–40 words and its Part 2 about 60–80 words. A test written to the examination asks a
learner for **a third of what the unit asked for the week before.**

B2 taught this series the governing rule: **step up only the figures the examination does not
fix, and establish which those are before projecting any of them.** Four of six word-count bands
projected for B2 Book 2 came back identical to Book 1's, because B2 First fixes them.

## 7.2 · The recommendation

**Keep the course bands above the examination's, and say so on the test paper.**

| | guided (stage 6) | free (stage 7) | models | test Part 1 | test Part 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Book 1** | ≥50 | 100–140 | 180–260 | 35–45 | 60–80 |
| **Book 2** | ≥60 | 140–180 | 250–350 | 35–45 | 60–80 |

And in the candidate instructions of both tests, in as many words: *the examination asks for
fewer words than this book does, on purpose. A learner who can write a hundred and forty can
write eighty; the reverse is not true.* **This is Decision 2, and it is the most consequential
line in this plan** — it decides whether the course trains for the certificate or for the job.
My recommendation is the job, with the certificate as a by-product.

## 7.3 · Peer review

| | band | the move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Book 1**, Units 1–12 | **band 3** | quote the criterion from the Writer's box, ask a question, and do not rewrite the sentence for the writer |
| **Book 2**, Units 13–24 | **band 3 + one move** | first **name the reader you are being** — *"I am reading this as the auditor"* — then quote, ask, stop |

Business A2 ran bands 1–2 for the whole of Book 1 and band 3 for the whole of Book 2, against a
general course that moves through all four in two books. **That slower progression is correct
for an ESP strand** and is kept: these learners are at work, criticising a colleague's real
document, and one band done properly for a whole book is worth four rehearsed. **Full band 4 —
with the sentence you would put in the writer's place, which the writer may refuse — waits for
B2 Business.**

## 8.3 · Lexis — 30 items a unit, and the rule that makes it possible

**720 items across the level, no repeat inside it.** And the harder constraint: **no repeat
against Business A2's 624**, which is the same company and the same people and therefore the
same words. Thirteen collisions were caught inside A2 alone and every one pointed backwards to a
unit written months earlier.

⚠ **Two obvious answers to that are both wrong.** Forbidding every A2 item pushes B1 towards the
rare and the ornamental, and permitting repeats lets a unit recycle last year's list. **So the
C1 three-part rule is adopted here, one level down, where the cast is reused and it is actually
needed:**

1. no repeat inside B1's own 24 units;
2. no repeat against Business A2's 624;
3. **A2 items permitted only on a second declared line of two or three items, called *Back at
   B1*, each taught for a sense or register it did not have at A2.** *quote* (a price, A2) →
   *quote* (to cite a clause, B1). *claim* (a complaint, A2) → *claim* (to assert, B1).

**The check runs in both directions and before a cycle is written, not after.** It rejected
items in four of twelve units at C1 and it will here.

# PART NINE — HOUSE FIGURES AND FIDELITY CRITERIA

The harness is `qa.js`, copied from the A2 Business tree, which is already book-aware. **Every
rule below was proved to fire at A2 by breaking a file and restoring it; a rule nobody has seen
fail is not a rule, and that standard carries.**

| check | what it refuses |
| --- | --- |
| `checkLoads` | a script that does not parse, exports a non-array, exports nothing, or holds an `undefined` element — **and it loads the test papers too**, which is the only way to catch a `table()` whose widths array is shorter than its headings. That one writes **no file** and throws from inside `docx`; it occurred three times at A2 and the message is rewritten into one readable sentence |
| `checkChars` | any character outside the house set. A Cyrillic word, a U+2212 minus and a CJK character all reached built pages before this existed |
| `checkUnit` | the structural counts of §1 above, plus the cast list — which is how three undeclared speakers were found |
| `checkKey` | `pb()` count, **derived from the unit number and the book**, and every Student's Book stage code answered **in its own cycle** |
| `checkUnitCheck` | 8 language questions, an answer per question, each answer letter pointing at an option that exists, **no option equal to the word after the blank**, a marking box and a teacher's note |
| `checkOrdinals` | the cycle-7 ordinal claim, **counted within the book** and read **only from `CYCLE n.7` onwards** — both fixes are needed and the second was found at A2 |
| `checkLexis` | collisions across all 24 declared sets, **and across A2's 624**, with the *Back at B1* line exempted and checked separately |
| `checkTest` | four papers, the right total, the right split, part marks adding to their paper, unbroken question numbering, the right **number** of questions, one answer letter per question, and **an answer in the key for every numbered question** — gap tables carry their numbers in the **headings**, which is the convention the rule was written against |
| `checkNames` | a cast first name with the wrong surname |

**Four things to add to the harness at Phase 0, all of them B1-specific:**

1. **`checkBackAtB1`** — every unit declares exactly one *Back at B1* line of two or three items,
   and each item must exist in A2's declared sets. An item that is not an A2 repeat has no
   business on that line.
2. **`checkReportingVerbs`** — Units 10 and 23 teach that *said · claimed · admitted · denied*
   are four different claims about a person. Assert that no key uses *admitted* or *denied* in a
   sample answer where the Student's Book text does not support it. ⚠ This is the one new check
   I am least sure can be automated; if it cannot, it becomes a reading pass and is listed as one.
3. **`checkWordBands`** — stage 6 and stage 7 rubrics must state the book's own figures (≥50 /
   100–140 in Book 1, ≥60 / 140–180 in Book 2) and the test rubrics must state the examination's.
   A unit that quietly carries A2's figures is the likeliest single error in Phase 1.
4. **`checkCanon`** — the A2 dates of §8.2 as literal strings: 8 June, 12 May, 19 July, nineteen
   of thirty, fourteen days, one until four. A B1 unit that contradicts one is refused.

**And the rules that are not scripts.** Each finished unit is **read against the house pattern** —
cast list, boxes, reports, dialogues — because a count that passes an empty list is not a check:
one C1 unit was built with no Writer's box at all and the harness printed `writers box : [] OK`.
Every unit and key script ends with **both** `module.exports = out;` **and** the `require.main`
build line; without the second the build writes no file and exits silently.
