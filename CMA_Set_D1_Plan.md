# CMA Part 1 — Interactive Handouts
## The general framework, and Set D1 (absorption vs variable costing) framed in it

---

## Part 1 · The general framework

This is the reusable machine. Any CSO requirement can be taught through it.

### 1.1 The unit of work is the **Set**

A Set is 6 handouts that together summarise one testable area, plus one
answer key serving all 6, plus a glossary. Set D1 is the worked example:
~113 pages, 55 figures, 83 generative blanks, 51 exam questions,
60 glossary terms.

Handouts run in a fixed dramatic order, because the order is what makes a
summary teachable rather than merely complete:

| # | Role | What the student can do afterwards |
|---|------|------------------------------------|
| 1 | **The distinction** | Classify the raw material of the topic |
| 2 | **The competing methods** | Say what each method does differently |
| 3 | **The artefacts** | Read and build the statements |
| 4 | **Over time** | Explain the behaviour across periods |
| 5 | **The ledger** | Post it, and prove the ledger agrees with the statement |
| 6 | **The incentive and the exam** | Spot the manipulation; answer exam-style |

### 1.2 Exercises are generative, not illustrative

The student writes the book. Every exercise type produces text that becomes
the summary:

- **Fill-in** — written as `The cost is {inventoriable} until sold.` A single
  string produces the exercise, the numbered blank, the word bank entry and
  the answer-key line. The key cannot drift from the exercise, because it is
  not written separately.
- **Word bank** — generated from that exercise's own answers plus authored
  distractors, so it can never omit a word the student needs, and the wrong
  choices are the ones the exam actually offers.
- **Rearrange** — statement lines, journal entries or procedure steps in the
  wrong order.
- **Match / sort grid** — term to definition, cost to bucket, treatment to
  method.
- **Build** — a blank statement or T-account the student completes from data.
- **Trap tables** — the mistake the exam sets on purpose, worked before the
  answer is given.
- **CMA-style questions** — multiple choice and Case-Based style, leaning
  harder than the teaching text.

### 1.3 Every exercise carries the same scaffolding

A fixed three-row panel above each exercise, enforced by the checker:

- **OBJECTIVE** — one sentence, what this exercise is for (min. 25 characters)
- **YOU WILL NEED** — the earlier exercises it depends on
- **HOW TO START** — bulleted ways in, so nobody stalls on a blank page

And at least one figure per exercise. No exceptions; the build fails without
one.

### 1.4 Three-register language architecture

The learners are Syrian accounting students and professionals who learned
this material in another language. Language is graded deliberately:

- **R1 — Teaching English.** Short sentences, one idea each.
- **R2 — Textbook English.** The register of a reference text.
- **R3 — Exam English.** Full CMA stem complexity, including the distractor
  phrasing.

Each handout opens in a lower register than it closes. Practice questions sit
at R3 regardless, so authenticity is never traded away. Each handout carries a
**LANGUAGE FOCUS** box: the register, the exact collocations to reuse, the
word pairs that are not the same thing, and one thing to remember.

The glossary gives every term with its Arabic equivalent and the place it
misleads — recognition only, since the exam is worked in English.

### 1.5 Three scenarios, carried across the whole Set

One company, three situations matching the likeliest exam framings:

- **Scenario 1** — production exceeds sales (absorption income higher)
- **Scenario 2** — three periods that net to nil (the reconciliation proof)
- **Scenario 3** — the bonus/earnings-management case (the ethics hook)

Every figure in the Set derives from one data file. No number is typed twice.

### 1.6 Correctness is structural, not reviewed

This is the part that matters most. The build refuses to produce a document
that violates any of these:

| Check | What it prevents |
|-------|------------------|
| Arithmetic recomputed from the data layer | A figure that disagrees with itself |
| Every journal entry must balance | A broken entry shipping |
| Ledger must reconcile to the statement formula | Handout 5 contradicting Handout 3 |
| No term used before the handout that teaches it | Vocabulary arriving out of order |
| No term glossed that the handout never uses | Dead glossary entries |
| Paragraphs capped at 95 words | Walls of text |
| ≥3 distractors per word bank, no overlap with answers | A bank that gives the game away |
| Every exercise has objective, dependencies, way in, figure | Unscaffolded work |
| **Figures drawn and measured against the canvas** | Labels printed off the page |
| **Labels measured against their neighbours** | Two labels printed on top of each other |
| **T-account headers from the costing palette only** | Colour meaning two things at once |
| **No sentence set in capitals** | Shouting standing in for emphasis |
| No italics anywhere | — |

### The colour system

Expanded into five bands, each stating the question its colours answer. The
bands are what let a colour be reused without contradicting itself — plum is
"absorption costing" in one band and "still held in inventory" in the next,
and the band heading makes that legible rather than confusing.

| Band | The question it answers |
|------|-------------------------|
| The three costing systems | Which system am I reading? |
| Where a cost is sitting | Has this cost reached the income statement? |
| The two sides of the ledger | Which side does this posting go on? |
| Variance direction | Which way did the variance go? |
| Signposts on the page | What is this box telling me to do? |

### Visual vocabulary

Fourteen reusable diagram archetypes, so a student learns to read each shape
once: ladder, tank, bridge (waterfall), fork, spine, scale, T-accounts,
workplace (people and props, for organisational context), buckets, behaviour,
matrix, stacks, timeline, formula, threshold. Any of them can be drawn
complete or with labels removed — a blank diagram is a question.

T-accounts carry posting references with an ordered legend underneath, two
accounts to a row, which is what keeps the inside of each T to two short
columns and stops the labels colliding.

### Page furniture

Each handout runs its own header — the handout name on the left, and
**"Handout N — Page N"** on the right, with page numbering restarting at 1 per
handout, so a student holding a loose sheet can say exactly where they are.

## Part 4 · Section A — the financial accounting production list

Source: **IMA, CMA Learning Outcome Statements, Content Specification Outlines
effective 1 September 2024.** The IMA has confirmed the CSO and LOS do not
change for 2026. Section A is headed *External Financial Reporting Decisions
(15% — Levels A, B, and C)*.

### What the LOS actually contains

**48 learning outcome statements**, not the intermediate-accounting syllabus
it resembles:

- **A.1 Financial statements — 16 LOS.** The four statements (a–g),
  consolidated statements (h–k), integrated reporting (l–p).
- **A.2 Recognition, measurement, and valuation — 32 LOS.** Asset valuation
  (a–n), valuation of liabilities (o–p), income taxes (q–s), leases (t–u),
  equity transactions (v–w), revenue recognition (x–z), income measurement
  (aa–ee), GAAP–IFRS differences (ff).

### Three things that are NOT in scope

Worth recording, because each one looks like it must be there and is not. Any
of them would have cost a Set to write and would never have been examined:

- **No bonds.** No premium or discount amortisation, no effective interest
  method, no extinguishment. The whole of "valuation of liabilities" is two
  statements: short-term debt expected to be refinanced (o), and the
  assurance versus service warranty approaches (p).
- **No EPS.** Equity is two statements: what affects paid-in capital versus
  retained earnings (v), and the effect of large and small stock dividends
  and splits (w).
- **No direct-method statement of cash flows.** A.1(g) asks for the indirect
  method only.

Two things are in scope that are easy to underweight: **consolidations**
(A.1 h–k, including the variable interest and voting interest models) and
**integrated reporting** (A.1 l–p, five statements covering the six capitals,
the value creation process and the eight elements of an integrated report).

### The production rate, measured

Set D1 covers D.1(f), (g), (h) and (i) — four core learning outcome
statements, plus supporting material from D.1(a), D.1(e) and D.3(k) — in
6 handouts and 113 pages. The rate that falls out of the built artifact:

> **One handout per learning outcome statement, about 19 pages each.**

### Three sequencing decisions, recorded so they can be revisited

- **Revenue sits at Volume 2.** That follows current intermediate texts. Older
  books, including the ones these learners may have trained on in Arabic, put
  revenue near the end; moving it after Volume 5 would match that memory.
- **Consolidations is at Volume 10, and is the real judgement call.** Textbooks
  place it in advanced accounting, not intermediate. The equity method in
  handout 33 is its natural on-ramp, so it would read more smoothly straight
  after Volume 6. It is late here only because it is conceptually heavier than
  Volumes 7 to 9.
- **Volumes 11 and 12 have no textbook home.** Integrated reporting is not in
  accounting texts, and GAAP/IFRS contrasts are normally end-of-chapter boxes.
  They go last by necessity — and Volume 12 is cheap there, because every
  handout contrasts against a volume already worked through.

### Where to start, when that is decided

Volume 4 (Inventory) is the densest block in Section A and the strongest second
test of the toolchain after D1: seven statements, nearly all computational,
including a Level C *recommend*. Volume 1 is the alternative, on the argument
that it is the foundation everything else refers back to.

### What the figures say against the estimate

Part 4 estimated roughly one handout per learning outcome statement at about
19 pages each. The 33 handouts delivered average **13 pages**, not 19. The
estimate was drawn from Set D1, whose topic is a single subtle distinction
worked from six angles; most Section A statements are narrower than that, and
the conceptual ones much narrower. The remaining 22 handouts should be
estimated at the rate actually achieved rather than at D1's.

### The thing that made it hold together

One company, one year, one data layer. `facheck.py` recomputes every identity
on every build of every volume, so a figure cannot drift between books:

- The balance sheet balances in both years, the roll-forwards close, and the
  cash flow statement lands on the cash balance.
- The Volume 3 aging schedule adds to Volume 1's gross receivables and its
  estimate equals Volume 1's allowance.
- Each Volume 4 cost flow assumption splits the same goods available for sale,
  and the tax LIFO defers equals the reserve at the tax rate.
- Every Volume 5 depreciation method writes off the whole depreciable amount
  and ends exactly at the residual value.
- The Volume 6 portfolio's unrealised gain is the one Volume 1 took to other
  comprehensive income.

A student who finds a contradiction between two volumes has found a bug in the
data layer, not a judgement call.
