# Chapter 1 as handouts — the plan, under the new standard

Chapter 1, *The Language and Framework of Financial Reporting*, is six sections and an end-of-chapter case set. It becomes **seven sheets**: one for each section, plus one that closes the chapter on its case set and whatever the sections did not use.

The last column is the one that matters. A sheet is meant to be used instead of the chapter, so every main point the section makes has to be asked about by some question on the sheet. A main point is one of three things, all of them the section’s own: a row of one of its tables, a term it states the meaning of, or a sentence that asserts something about one of its terms. Narration and encouragement carry no point and are left out. A point merely printed in a reference table does not count as covered, and nor does one sitting in the unblanked prose of a gapped summary — a reader is handed both without being asked anything.
