### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- How is negotiating by letter different from negotiating in a room?
- What happens to a concession once it is written down?
- What should every concession in a letter be attached to?

Negotiation by letter is slower than conversation, and it is also permanent. What is written in the second letter is still available to the other side in the ninth.

A concession made in writing cannot be withdrawn without a reason, and a reason that amounts to having changed your mind weakens everything you write afterwards.

So the sequence is planned before the first letter goes. Four letters, perhaps five, with the opening position, the points you will concede, the order in which you will concede them, and what you want in exchange for each.

Every concession is linked. We are prepared to extend the credit period to forty-five days, provided that the minimum order is raised to 2,000 metres, is a sentence that costs nothing if it is refused.

Where a point is still open, the position is reserved expressly: our silence on clause 11 is not to be taken as acceptance of it. A point not reserved is a point conceded by inattention.

### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: contract register: hereby, herein, the parties, the said**

- Hereby means by this document and is used once, in the operative sentence: the parties hereby agree as follows.
- Herein means in this document: the terms set out herein supersede all earlier drafts.
- The parties and the said are definite references back to something already identified: the said territory comprises Jordan and Lebanon.
- Use this register sparingly and only where it adds precision. A letter written entirely in it reads as a parody and conceals the point you are making.

**Figure 1.2** — One marker to a sentence, used where it narrows the meaning and not for ornament.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A negotiation by letter is won by whoever planned the sequence before writing**

**Figure 1.5** — Three concessions, three things gained, and three clauses reserved to the end.

Correspondence in a negotiation is written letter by letter, each one answering the last, and the party who does that is negotiating inside the other party’s plan.

A negotiating position written as a sequence of letters is useful work to hand over, because the structure can be set out before the first word of prose is written.

Give it the draft under negotiation, your list of points by priority, and the concessions you are willing to make. Ask for a sequence of four letters, with each concession placed in a letter and linked to something sought in exchange.

Require every point not addressed in a letter to appear in an express reservation in that same letter, so that nothing is conceded by silence while the sequence runs.

What it cannot know is what the other side needs. A concession that costs you little may be the one thing their board has asked for, and only a conversation will tell you that.

So the plan is drafted, the first letter is sent, and the plan is revised after every reply, because a sequence written in advance is a plan and not a script.

> **DATA POINT · Negotiation records** — Commercial negotiation practice commonly treats written exchanges as a record of the parties’ positions, and silence on a proposed term may later be argued to indicate acceptance of it.  
> Source: general commercial practice guidance, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Reservations** — In commercial correspondence it is common practice to reserve a party’s position expressly on terms not yet addressed, so that continued negotiation is not taken as acceptance.  
> Source: general commercial practice guidance, 2025–2026.

## Unit 2 · An Agency Agreement

*CEFR B2 · Grammar: obligation, permission and prohibition — shall, may, shall not · AI: an agency agreement read for what it obliges you to do*

**Figure 2.0** — EVERY PARTY READS AN AGREEMENT FOR WHAT THE OTHER SIDE MUST DO, WHICH IS WHY NOBODY NOTICES THEIR OWN OBLIGATIONS UNTIL THEY ARE BREACHED: Nineteen clauses marked up by party, which is the first reading nobody makes.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- Whose obligations do you read first in an agreement?
- What is the difference between an agent who may sell and one who shall sell?
- Which obligations outlive the agreement itself?

An agency agreement is read, by both parties, for what the other side is required to do, and the result is that each side knows the other’s duties better than its own.

The first useful reading is therefore a reading of your own obligations only: every clause in which your company is the subject of a shall, and every clause in which something is required of you by implication.

The modal verbs carry the whole structure. Shall imposes a duty, may grants a permission, and shall not imposes a prohibition; a clause that says the principal will endeavour to supply is none of the three and means very little.

The authority given to an agent matters more than the commission. An agent authorised to conclude contracts can bind you to a price you have never seen, whereas an agent authorised only to introduce customers cannot.

Finally, three or four obligations usually survive termination: confidentiality, non-competition, post-termination commission on orders already introduced. Those are the clauses that are read for the first time in the month the agreement ends.

### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: obligation, permission and prohibition: shall, may, shall not**

- Shall imposes an obligation on the party named as its subject: the agent shall submit a written report each month.
- May grants a permission and is not a duty: the agent may appoint sub-agents with our written consent, which he need not do at all.
- Shall not imposes a prohibition and is stronger than may not in commercial drafting: the agent shall not represent any competing product.
- Shall use best endeavours and shall endeavour to are obligations of effort rather than of result, and the difference between them and shall is where most disputes begin.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**Read an agreement twice: once for their duties, and once for your own**

**Figure 2.5** — Six of nineteen clauses, and the three that survive are all obligations of ours.

An agreement is read for the other party’s duties because that is where the negotiation feels as if it lies, and the consequence is that your own obligations arrive as a surprise in the month somebody enforces them.

An agency agreement read for what it obliges you to do is good work to hand over, because the task is a mechanical classification of every clause by party and by the strength of its modal verb.

Give it the draft and ask for a table of every clause, the party it binds, whether it creates an obligation, a permission or a prohibition, and whether it operates after termination.

Require the clauses that bind you to be listed first and separately, because a list of your own duties, read in one place, produces a different negotiation from the same clauses read between the agent’s.

What it cannot judge is which obligation is commercially dangerous. A price guarantee for one year is three lines of ordinary drafting and the most expensive sentence in the document in a year when cotton moves.

So the table comes back, you read your own column first, and the clauses you amend are chosen for what they could cost rather than for how unusual they look.

> **DATA POINT · Surviving terms** — Commercial agreements commonly provide that confidentiality, non-competition and commission entitlements continue for a stated period after termination, and those provisions are frequently the subject of later dispute.

## Unit 3 · A Distribution Territory

*CEFR B2 · Grammar: defining scope — in respect of, in relation to, for the purposes of · AI: a territory defined so that two agents never meet in it*

**Figure 3.0** — A TERRITORY IS NOT A PLACE ON A MAP; IT IS A RULE FOR DECIDING WHOSE CUSTOMER A PARTICULAR BUYER IS: One country, two agents, and a definition that has to decide every borderline buyer.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- What does a territory actually decide?
- What happens when a customer has offices in two territories?
- Which sales belong to an agent who did not make them?

A territory looks like geography and works like a rule of attribution: when an order arrives, whose order is it, and who is paid commission on it?

Geography alone cannot answer that. A buyer with a head office in Amman and a warehouse in Irbid is in both halves of a country divided between two agents, and the order will arrive from whichever office happens to send it.

So a workable definition has three layers: the geographical area, a named customer list where the geography is ambiguous, and a rule about conduct that distinguishes active solicitation from a sale that merely arrives.

House accounts, which the principal keeps for itself, must be reserved by name rather than by description, because a description of a house account is an invitation to argue about which accounts fit it.

Exclusivity should be conditional on performance. An exclusive territory with no minimum volume is a territory the principal cannot sell in and the agent need not sell in.

**Grammar: defining scope: in respect of, in relation to, for the purposes of**

- In respect of narrows an entitlement or obligation to a class of things: commission is payable in respect of orders from the territory.
- In relation to connects a duty to a subject matter: the agent’s duties in relation to stock are set out in clause 9.

**A territory is a rule of attribution, and a map is only a picture of one**

**Figure 3.5** — Six provisions, of which only the first looks like a territory at all.

A territory is drafted as a line on a map because that is how it is discussed, and the line then has to answer questions about customers, conduct and group companies that no line can answer.

A territory defined so that two agents never meet in it is good work to hand over, because the ambiguities are predictable and can be enumerated before any dispute arises.

Give it the proposed area, the customer list and the group structures of your six largest buyers. Ask for every case in which two agents could claim the same order, with the provision that would decide it.

Require each ambiguity to be resolved by a named customer, a named house account or a stated rule of conduct, and not by a general description that will be argued about later.

What it cannot know is which agent you would rather keep. A definition that resolves every ambiguity in favour of the stronger agent is clear, enforceable and the end of the weaker agency.

So the list of ambiguities comes back, you decide each one commercially, and the schedules are reviewed at every renewal, because customers merge, move and open warehouses in the other half of the country.

## Unit 4 · The Terms and Conditions Nobody Reads

*CEFR B2 · Grammar: exceptions and provisos — notwithstanding, save as provided, except where · AI: standard terms compared against the terms you are sent*

**Figure 4.0** — EVERY ORDER CARRIES TERMS ON ITS REVERSE, AND BOTH PARTIES BELIEVE THEIR OWN SET APPLIES: Two sets of printed terms, each asserting that it governs, and a shipment between them.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- Whose standard terms apply to an order?
- What happens when two sets conflict?
- Which differences between two sets actually matter?

Both parties to most transactions have printed terms, and both sets say that they alone govern. The buyer’s terms are on the reverse of his order and ours are on the reverse of our confirmation.

Where the two conflict and neither side addresses the conflict, the outcome is decided by which document was sent last and what each party did afterwards, which is not a basis on which anybody should plan a business.

So the terms we are sent are compared against our own, clause by clause, and the comparison separates three kinds of difference: those that change who carries a loss, those that change a procedure, and those that are only drafting.

A handful of clauses change who carries a loss: the limitation of liability, the exclusion of consequential loss, the retention of title, the interest provision, and the jurisdiction clause.

The rest can usually be accepted without a letter. An order of precedence clause, agreed once, disposes of most of the argument: in the event of conflict, the confirmation prevails over the order and the schedules prevail over both.

**Grammar: exceptions and provisos: notwithstanding, save as provided, except where**

## Unit 5 · A Contract in Two Languages

*CEFR B2 · Grammar: parallel drafting of clauses and sub-clauses · AI: a bilingual contract checked clause against clause*

**Figure 5.0** — A CONTRACT IN TWO LANGUAGES IS TWO CONTRACTS UNTIL SOMEBODY SAYS WHICH ONE GOVERNS: Two texts of one agreement, aligned clause by clause, with four differences found.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- Why are commercial contracts often made in two languages?
- What does a governing language clause do?
- What happens when the two texts differ?

Contracts in this market are commonly made in two languages, because one text is needed for the parties and another for an authority, a bank or a court.

Two texts are two documents. They are intended to say the same thing, and the moment they do not, the agreement depends on a single clause saying which of them governs.

That clause is usually the shortest in the document and the one most often translated carelessly, with the result that each text names itself as the governing one.

The check is mechanical and tedious: clause one against clause one, sub-clause by sub-clause, with the numbering compared as well as the words, because a sub-clause that exists in one text and not the other is the commonest and most expensive discrepancy.

Some terms have no equivalent. Best endeavours, consequential loss and without prejudice translate into phrases that mean something slightly different, and the sensible course is to define them in the contract rather than rely on the translation.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- What does a force majeure clause actually give you?
- What must be done to obtain it?
- Why is a late notice fatal?

A force majeure clause does not excuse a failure by itself. It gives relief to a party that satisfies the conditions written into the clause, and the first of those conditions is almost always notice within a stated period.

That notice requirement is a condition precedent: relief is available only if the notice has been given in time and in the form the clause requires. A notice on the eighth day under a seven-day clause is a nullity.

The second requirement is causation. The event must have caused the failure, which means a party claiming relief for a closed road must show that the road was the reason the goods did not move, and not that the dyeing was late in any case.

The third is mitigation. A party is commonly obliged to do what it reasonably can during the event, which in practice means a record of what was attempted, dated as it happened.

None of this can be reconstructed afterwards. The notice is drafted while the event is running, from a file kept that week, and the alternative is a convincing letter written in March that proves nothing at all.

### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: conditions precedent and subsequent in commercial terms**

- A condition precedent is expressed with only if, provided that, or subject to: relief is available only if notice is given within seven days.
- A condition subsequent is expressed with if or unless and removes a right that has already arisen: relief shall lapse if the party fails to mitigate.
- Use while for the period during which obligations are suspended and once for the moment they revive: performance is suspended while the event continues; the obligation revives once the event has ceased.
- Keep the condition and the consequence in the same sentence. A condition in one clause and its consequence in another is an invitation to argue about both.

**A notice written on the second day is worth more than a letter written in March**

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- How are confidentiality obligations usually breached?
- What makes a confidentiality clause workable rather than absolute?
- What record should exist of what you have sent?

Confidentiality is discussed as though the risk were indiscretion, and the actual risk is ordinary administrative haste: a file attached to a message, a distribution list that has grown, a forwarded thread with eleven earlier messages beneath it.

A workable clause has exclusions, and the exclusions are what make the obligation possible to comply with: information already public, information already known to the recipient, and information a party is required by law to disclose.

It also has permitted disclosures. An obligation that prevents you from showing a document to your own bank or your own lawyer is an obligation you will breach in the first month.

Duration is expressed in two different ways, and both must be checked. An obligation may run for a period of three years from disclosure, and it may also survive termination of the agreement, which are not the same thing.

The only practical control is a record: what was sent, to whom, on what date, and under which agreement. Without it, a question about a leak two years later cannot be answered at all.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- What four things must a dispute clause settle?
- What makes a clause unworkable?
- How does the cost of a forum compare with a typical dispute?

The dispute clause is agreed last, briefly, by people who have spent four weeks on commission and territory and have no appetite left for procedure.

It must settle four things: the law that governs the contract, the forum in which disputes are resolved, the language of the proceedings, and who appoints the tribunal if the parties cannot agree.

A clause that names no institution and no appointing authority is pathological: both parties have agreed to arbitrate and neither can start an arbitration, which is worse than having no clause at all.

The cost of the forum must be weighed against the size of the typical dispute. An international arbitration with three arbitrators is a reasonable forum for a claim of two million and an absurd one for a claim of nine thousand.

Enforceability is the last question and the first in importance. An award is worth what it can be enforced against, and a judgment from a court whose decisions are not recognised where the debtor’s assets are is a document and not a remedy.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A dispute clause is read for four things, and for the cost of using it**

**Figure 8.5** — Six requirements, and a clause missing any one of the first four cannot be used.

The dispute clause is the only clause in a commercial agreement that is used exactly when every other clause has failed, and it is drafted by people who assume that will not happen.

A dispute clause read for where, in what language and before whom is good work to hand over, because the four elements are fixed and their presence or absence is a matter of fact.

Give it the clause and ask whether it states the governing law, the forum or seat, the rules, the appointing authority and the language, and what each of those states.

Require any missing element to be reported as missing rather than inferred. A clause that says a neutral arbitrator does not say who appoints him, and no amount of good will supplies the omission.

What it cannot judge is proportion. Three arbitrators abroad is an excellent forum for a dispute you will never have and a bar on every dispute you are likely to have.

So the four elements are checked, the cost of the forum is set against your typical claim, a threshold is proposed if the figures require one, and the clause is agreed before the last ten minutes of the negotiation.

> **DATA POINT · Proportionality** — Commercial practice commonly matches the complexity and cost of a dispute procedure to the likely value of disputes, sometimes by providing for a sole arbitrator below a stated threshold.

## Unit 9 · A Memorandum After a Meeting

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- Who should write the memorandum of a meeting?
- How soon should it be circulated?
- What is the difference between a decision and an observation?

Four people leave a three-hour meeting with four honest and different accounts of it, and the differences grow for a fortnight until somebody writes something down.

Whoever writes first sets the record. That is not a dishonest advantage; it is simply that a written account circulated on the evening of the meeting is the only version anybody can check against a memory that is still fresh.

A memorandum distinguishes three kinds of paragraph. It was agreed introduces a decision; it was noted introduces something stated but not decided; and action by names a person and a date.

Nothing else belongs in it. Argument, atmosphere and the writer’s own view of who was unreasonable belong in a private note, because a memorandum that argues invites a reply that argues back.

Circulation matters as much as drafting. A memorandum sent the same evening attracts corrections within two days; one sent a week later attracts a different memorandum from the other side.

## Unit 10 · Agreed, or Only Discussed

*CEFR B2 · Grammar: distinguishing agreement from discussion in a written record · AI: a record of what was agreed, and of what was only discussed*

**Figure 10.0** — A NEGOTIATION ENDS WITH THREE KINDS OF POINT, AND A FILE THAT DOES NOT DISTINGUISH THEM WILL BE READ IN TWO YEARS BY SOMEBODY WHO NEEDS IT TO: Nineteen points labelled in three categories, in the week they were discussed.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- What are the three states a negotiating point can be in?
- Why does the distinction matter two years later?
- When is an ambiguity worth preserving?

At the end of a negotiation every point is in one of three states: agreed, discussed without agreement, or never reached. A file that records all three in the same voice is a file that answers no question at all.

The reader who needs the distinction is somebody in two years, under pressure, looking for a single sentence. If each paragraph in the file says which state it records, that reader finds the answer in a minute.

A disagreement is worth recording carefully. Our position was 2,000 metres, his was 1,200, and the point was left open is a better record than any attempt to make the difference look smaller than it was.

Some ambiguity is deliberate. Where two parties can live with a form of words and cannot agree on its meaning, writing the words and recording that each side reserved its interpretation is sometimes the only way to sign anything at all.

Accidental ambiguity is different, and it is created by writing in a single voice: a paragraph that does not say whether something was agreed will be read as agreement by the party it suits.

**A file is written for a reader in two years who has ten minutes**

**Figure 10.5** — Three states, nine positions named, and not one paragraph left unlabelled.

A record of a negotiation is written as a narrative, because a narrative is what the writer remembers, and a narrative conceals the one distinction the file exists to preserve.

A record of what was agreed, and of what was only discussed, is good work to hand over, because the three states are exhaustive and every paragraph belongs to exactly one of them.

Give it the correspondence, the memoranda and the list of points. Ask for every point to be labelled agreed, discussed without agreement, or outstanding, with the agreed points collected separately.
