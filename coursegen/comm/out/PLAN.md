# English for Commercial Correspondence · Syria Edition
## Design brief, written before any content

### 1 · Who it is for

People who write the letters a company is judged by, in Syria, now: export and
import staff, sales and purchasing officers, documentary credit clerks at
banks, freight forwarders, customs brokers, credit controllers, commercial
agents, and the owners of small and medium firms who still write their own
correspondence.

The reader's English is a working tool, not an accomplishment. They are writing
to a buyer in Dubai, a bank in Frankfurt, a forwarder in Mersin and an agent in
Amman — none of whom shares their first language, several of whom have never
visited the country, and all of whom will decide what the company is like from
a page of English.

### 2 · Why now

The commercial letter has become the whole relationship. Firms are reopening
export lines after years in which many could not; banks are rebuilding
correspondent relationships and asking documentary questions they did not ask
before; buyers abroad are cautious, well informed and quick to go elsewhere;
and the first contact is almost always written. A quotation that answers every
question becomes an order. One that does not is never answered at all.

The second reason is the obvious one: drafting assistance is now available to
every desk, and it is extremely good at producing a letter that reads well and
commits the company to something nobody intended. A course for this audience
has to teach both the letter and the machine.

### 4 · The unit, twelve parts

Unchanged from the series architecture: Warm Up and Read · Vocabulary · Grammar
and Dialogues · Speaking · Extended Reading · Guided Writing · Working with AI ·
The Decision Room · The Brief Lab · **The Money Conversation** · Six-Skill
Review · Can-Do and Foundations.

### 5 · The signature part: The Money Conversation

Every unit ends with a spoken round at the moment correspondence has failed and
somebody has to speak: an overdue invoice, a rejected document set, a claim for
damaged goods, a price increase, a container held at the port, a customer
asking for credit the company cannot give, an agent who has lost a market.

Each round has a three-step ladder showing how the position was reached, three
turns of live dialogue, six repair phrases that hold a position without
breaking a relationship, a red line that must not be crossed on the telephone,
and a five-row score sheet. It is unscripted and must not be prepared.

The pedagogical argument: in trade, writing is evidence and speech is repair.
Students who can only write lose money slowly; students who can only speak lose
cases. The course teaches both, and teaches which is which.

### 6 · The three ladders

**AI capability** — 60 points, one per unit, each with a boundary. An enquiry
sorted into what is asked and what is assumed; a price list converted into one
currency and one unit; a letter of credit read for the conditions you must
meet; a set of documents checked field by field against the credit; an
escalating sequence of reminders carrying the same facts; a bilingual contract
checked clause against clause; a compliance questionnaire answered with
evidence attached.

**Grammar** — 60 points, graded A2.1 to B2.2 and chosen for this register: the
present simple for what a company does, through conditional obligations
(provided that, subject to, unless), to contract register (shall, may, save as
provided, notwithstanding) and the language of a written record.

**Precision faults** — the eight faults of a bad instruction, introduced
progressively, three per Brief Lab, two phrases each.

### 7 · Neutrality rules

No vendor, product, model or platform anywhere. No serving official, no party,
no political claim, no reference to any dispute. Firms are invented; cities and
trading partners are real; commercial instruments appear under their own names
because a trader must use them. Every capability is taught with a stated limit,
and no sentence in the six books predicts what a system will be able to do
later.

### 8 · Quality gates

Twenty-three per book, three across the series: word count, figure count,
keyline count, part order, vocabulary uniqueness across all 1,200 terms,
register band by measured mean sentence length, long-sentence ceiling, answer
key completeness, MCQ validity, dialogue length, an open second dialogue,
sourced data points, neutrality, no capability forecast, Brief Lab integrity,
the fault ladder, the AI ladder in order, a boundary line on every capability,
dissent in the clinics, and a complete Money Conversation in every unit.
