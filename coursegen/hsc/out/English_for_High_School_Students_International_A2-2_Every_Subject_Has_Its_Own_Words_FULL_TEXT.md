## Unit 1 · A Definition That Actually Defines

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- Define a river in one sentence.
- Does your sentence rule anything out?
- What else would it also describe?

Every subject asks you to define things, and almost every wrong definition fails in the same two ways.

The first way is no category. Photosynthesis is when plants use sunlight tells a marker nothing about what kind of thing it is.

The second way is no difference. A mammal is an animal is true, and it also describes a fish, a beetle and a bird.

So a definition has two parts. A mammal is an animal that feeds its young on milk. Animal is the category. Milk is the difference.

And test it. Think of the hardest case you know and check that your sentence still works, because the hardest case is the one in the exercise.

### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: a definition: a thing that, used for, made of**

- A thing that takes the category and then the difference. A bird is an animal that has feathers.
- Used for takes an instrument and its purpose. A thermometer is an instrument used for measuring temperature.
- Made of takes a material or a landform. A delta is a landform made of sediment deposited at a river mouth.
- Which or who replaces that where the category is a place or a person. A geologist is a scientist who studies rocks.

**Dialogue 2 · The circle**

**Figure 1.4** — Ms Clara Benedek and Noor Haddad, over a glossary task.

**Noor:** That is a quick test.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A definition checked against the one the textbook gives**

**Figure 1.5** — Four checks, one sentence, and a mark for each of the first two.

Checking a definition is the one piece of study work where a tool can be useful without doing the thinking. The thinking is the sentence and the checking is mechanical.

Hand it the sentence you wrote yourself, the version the textbook gives, the subject, and the strangest example of the thing you can name.

Ask it to name the category and the difference in your sentence, and to say plainly if either is missing.

Ask it to say whether your sentence uses the word it is defining, which is the test for a circular definition.

Ask it to test your sentence against the hard case you supplied, and to say whether your definition includes or excludes it.

What it cannot do is write the definition you will remember. A definition in your own words is remembered because of the work of making it. A definition handed to you is a sentence you will recognise in June and not be able to produce.

> **DATA POINT · Definitions in subjects** — Subject glossaries commonly define a term by naming the class of thing it belongs to and then what distinguishes it. How much detail is expected is set by each subject and each school.  
> Source: general practice overviews, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Mark schemes** — Mark schemes for definition questions commonly award one mark for the class of thing and one for the distinguishing feature. What is awarded is set by each subject.

## Unit 2 · A Process Somebody Else Could Follow

*CEFR A2.2 · Grammar: a process in order: first, then, after that, finally · AI: a process written as numbered steps a classmate could follow*

**Figure 2.0** — A PROCESS YOU CAN FOLLOW IS NOT A PROCESS SOMEBODY ELSE CAN.: Noor Haddad wrote nine steps. Zofia followed them exactly and ended up with the wrong liquid in the wrong beaker.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- Describe how you make a cup of tea.
- Did you say where the water comes from?
- Could somebody who had never seen tea follow you?

Writing a process is the first subject skill that fails for a reason that is nothing to do with English.

It fails because you already know the answer. You skip the step you always do without thinking, and the reader stops there.

So number the steps, and put them in the order they actually happen. First, then, after that, finally.

Name the equipment before step one. A process that needs a thing you never mentioned is a process that stops at that line.

And test it on a person. Give it to somebody who was not in the lesson and watch where they stop. That place is your missing step.

**A. Comprehension.**
0. Why does writing a process fail?  — *because you already know the answer*

**A process written as numbered steps a classmate could follow**

**Figure 2.5** — Four parts, numbered, and a stranger who gets to the end.

A process is the one kind of writing where your own knowledge works against you. The step you never think about is the step you never write.

Give the system your process as you first wrote it, the equipment you actually used, and the subject it belongs to.

Ask it to split any step that contains more than one verb, because a step with three verbs in it is three steps badly joined.

**Grammar: cause and effect: because, so, which means**

- Because puts the cause after the effect. The air rises because the land has heated it.

- Which means states what follows from the whole clause before it. Air flows in from the sea, which means a breeze forms.

Science writing hides the person on purpose, and most students meet the passive without ever being told why.
