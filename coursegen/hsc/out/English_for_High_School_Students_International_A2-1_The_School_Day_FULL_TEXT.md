### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- What is your first lesson tomorrow?
- Which room is it in?
- How long does it take to get there?

A school timetable is a grid. It has days across the top and periods down the side.

Your day is not a grid. It is a line: this room, then that room, then lunch.

The timetable does not know the building. It says B14. It does not say that B14 is up two flights of stairs.

So turn the grid into a line. Write Monday as six rows, in order, with the room beside each one.

Then add the walk. Four minutes from B14 to the science block is a fact about your Monday, not about the timetable.

### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: the timetable: on Mondays, at nine, in room**

- On takes a day. On Mondays I have double physics.
- At takes a clock time. The lesson starts at nine.
- In takes a room. We are in room B14.
- From and until take the two ends of a lesson. The lesson runs from nine until ten twenty.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A timetable rewritten in the order your day happens**

**Figure 1.5** — Four columns, six rows, one day. Twenty minutes to build.

A timetable is given to you as a grid because a grid fits one week on one page.

You do not live a week on one page. You live one day at a time, in order, with a walk between each lesson.

Give the system your timetable, the room codes, and the building plan or your own notes about where things are.

Ask it to produce one day at a time, in order, with the start time, the subject, the room and the floor.

Ask it to add a walk column, and to mark any walk you have not measured as unknown rather than guessing it.

What it cannot do is walk the building. Four minutes from B14 to the science block is something you find out with your feet, once, on a quiet afternoon.

### Part 8 · The Decision Room · The grid, or the day

**STAGE 1 · The Brief**

A school looked at lateness in the first three weeks of term, across 380 new students.

126 of them had been given a day-order timetable at registration. The other 254 were given only the weekly grid.

**A catch-up list for the lessons you missed**

Being away is a problem with an arithmetic answer, and the arithmetic is almost never done: three days is fifteen lessons and about six subjects.
