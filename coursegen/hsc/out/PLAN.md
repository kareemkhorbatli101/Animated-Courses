# English for High School Students — International Edition
## Series plan

### The spine

**Every subject has a language, and nobody teaches you it.**

A physics answer, a history paragraph, a biology diagram caption and a maths
explanation are four different kinds of English. A student who writes all four
the same way is marked down in at least three of them, and is almost never told
which three or why. Secondary schools teach the subjects. The language each
subject actually requires is left to be absorbed, which works for the students
who grew up inside it and not for anybody else.

This course teaches that language explicitly, subject by subject, at six
levels, on school material: the definition that has to contain a category and a
distinguishing feature, the process a classmate could actually follow, the
passive that belongs in a lab report and nowhere else, the causal verb a graph
does not license, the transition that is a claim about a relation rather than a
noise between sentences.

### Why an international edition

The school is invented and so is the town. Brackenhill International School,
Quillon Sixth Form College, the Eldermere Debating Union and Westmere
University are not anywhere, which means a student in Lagos, Lima, Almaty or
Beirut can put their own school in this one's place without first having to
translate somebody else's system.

The students' home cities are real, because that part is. Eleven people from
eleven places sit in the same classroom and none of them learnt English first.
The series never treats that as a deficit. It treats it as the ordinary
condition of secondary education now, and it teaches the conventions that are
genuinely subject conventions rather than the ones that are only somebody's
accent.

### The two assessments

Most of these students will eventually sit two kinds of assessment in English:
a timed reading-and-writing paper used for university admission, and a
four-skill English proficiency test. Both are redesigned periodically, and both
reward exactly the competences above.

This course therefore builds those competences and names no test. There is no
test technique in it, no timing drill, no answer-elimination trick and no
mention anywhere in any of the sixty units of any assessment, awarding body or
examination. A student who finishes Book 6 can do the things both assessments
measure, because they are things worth being able to do. `COVERAGE.md` sets out,
for the author rather than the student, which unit carries which competence.

The arc is deliberate. Books 1 and 2 are the school day and the subjects, at
A2, where the English is the obstacle. Book 3 is the classroom argument, where
the obstacle becomes thinking. Book 4 is writing out of other people's work.
Book 5 is the point where a number disagrees with a feeling. Book 6 is the year
of applying, where a student has to write one true paragraph about themselves.

### The twelve-part unit

Every one of the sixty units has the same shape: hero statement, warm-up,
reading, six kinds of comprehension, twenty key terms, a grammar point with a
loose-and-precise contrast, two dialogues of twelve lines, three speaking
tasks, the AI capability with its boundary, the series signature, a five-stage
Decision Room, and a Brief Lab.

### The series signature

Part 10 of every unit is **the question you ask instead of guessing**. A
student who does not understand an instruction, a word, a diagram or a mark has
four seconds of discomfort available to them and a term of consequences if they
spend it instead on guessing. Each unit names one specific misunderstanding,
gives a three-turn ladder for raising it, six lines for holding the ground when
the answer is vague, and a redline.

### The two ladders

**Grammar** runs from *the timetable: on Mondays, at nine, in room* to
*authorship: what is mine, what is not* — sixty distinct points, each
introduced once and never repeated. From Book 3 onwards the points are the ones
that decide whether a sentence is correct rather than whether it is
comprehensible: sentence boundaries, the comma splice, pronoun reference,
modifier attachment, parallel structure, possessives, transitions, agreement
with data, conditionals.

**AI capability** runs from *a timetable rewritten in the order your day
happens* to *what nothing can write for you about your own life* — sixty
distinct capabilities, each with a stated boundary. The series teaches a
fifteen-year-old to ask well for what a system can do, and ends by naming what
cannot be delegated in an application written about themselves.

### The eight faults

Every Brief Lab diagnoses exactly three fault types, two entries each:
unscoped set · unbounded quantity · unstated criterion · undefined done ·
ambiguous referent · missing condition · unstated exception · hidden
assumption. Three are available at A2.1, five at A2.2, six at B1.1 and all
eight from B1.2.

### Standing rules

- No real school, university, examination, test, awarding body, vendor or
  product is named anywhere in the series.
- No test technique. The course teaches competences, not strategies.
- No deficit framing. A student who did not know a convention was not told it.
- Nothing is presented as universal where schools differ: the text says so and
  tells the student to ask.
- Subject content is correct but never the point: the English is the lesson.
