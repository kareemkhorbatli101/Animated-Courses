# English for High School Students
## International Edition

Six books. Sixty units. One argument: **every subject has a language, and
nobody teaches you it.**

---

### What this is

A six-book course in English for secondary school students aged roughly
thirteen to eighteen, taking a learner from CEFR A2 to CEFR B2 across
sixty units of identical architecture. It is written for a student in any
country whose school subjects are taught in English, or who is preparing
to study in English, and it assumes nothing about where that student
lives.

The subject matter is school itself: the timetable, the subjects, the
arguments in a classroom, the essay written from reading, the figure that
disagrees with a feeling, and the year of applying to a university. The
cast is a group of pupils and teachers at an invented international school
whose problems are the ordinary ones.

### The six books

| Book | Level | Title | Protagonist |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A2.1 | The School Day | Noor Haddad |
| 2 | A2.2 | Every Subject Has Its Own Words | Noor Haddad |
| 3 | B1.1 | Saying What You Think, and Why | Mateo Alvarez |
| 4 | B1.2 | Writing From What You Have Read | Sunita Pillai |
| 5 | B2.1 | Evidence, Numbers and What They Do Not Show | Oleh Danyliuk |
| 6 | B2.2 | Applying: Choosing, Writing and Being Asked Why | Fatima Sow |

The arc runs from finding a room on a timetable to being asked, out loud,
why you want to study a subject — and to the last unit, which is about
which sentences in a document are yours.

### Every unit, twelve parts

1. **Opening spread** — a headline figure and a number that hurts
2. **Reading** — five paragraphs on one problem of language
3. **Comprehension** — six question types, including a classification task
4. **Vocabulary** — twenty terms, with a figure and two exercise types
5. **Grammar** — one point, a loose-against-precise figure, two exercises
6. **Two dialogues** — one resolved, one ending on an open objection
7. **Speaking** — three paired tasks, six turns each
8. **The capability** — one thing a system can do, and what it cannot
9. **Writing task** — a six-line tasking, with a model and a bank
10. **The Question You Ask Instead of Guessing** — the series signature
11. **The Decision Room** — a case with exhibits, voices and a decision
12. **Brief Lab** — a six-line draft, diagnosed and rewritten

Plus a review spread, four foundations and a closing figure.

### Two assessments, never named

The course prepares a student for the reading-and-writing paper most
widely used in admissions and for the four-skill proficiency test most
widely used for study in English — **indirectly, and without naming
either, and without teaching any test technique.**

It does this structurally rather than by bolting practice on. The Decision
Room is a quantitative evidence task. The Brief Lab is a revision task.
The two dialogues in every unit are campus conversations held inside a
school. Part 10 is a short spoken response. The vocabulary gaps are
word-completion items; the grammar items are sentence construction; the
speaking tasks are interview turns.

`COVERAGE.md` maps every current task type of both assessments to the
units and the slots that carry it. **That document is for the author.
Nothing in it appears in any of the sixty units.**

### Five standing rules

- No real school, university, examination, test, awarding body, vendor or
  product is named anywhere.
- No test technique is taught. No question is labelled as belonging to any
  assessment.
- No deficit framing. A student who has not met a convention has not met
  it; nothing about them is deficient.
- Nothing is presented as universal. Where a convention differs between
  subjects or institutions, the unit says so and tells the student to ask.
- Subject content is correct and never the point. The physics is right;
  the unit is about the sentence.

### What is in the package

Six books as `.docx` and as `.pdf`. Six full-text `.md` records, one per
book, carrying every word including the answer keys. `PLAN.md`, the design
document. `COVERAGE.md`, the author-facing assessment map. `ALIGNMENT.md`,
the unit-by-unit table of grammar points and capabilities.
`BUILD_RECORD.md`, the quality record. `vocab_ledger.json`, the 1,200-term
vocabulary ledger with the unit that introduces each term.
