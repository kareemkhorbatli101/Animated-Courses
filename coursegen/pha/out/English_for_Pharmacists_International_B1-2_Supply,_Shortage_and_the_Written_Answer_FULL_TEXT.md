A shortage is a supply fact with an operational consequence, and the consequence arrives first if nobody has written the fact down.

The notice carries two dates. The date from which supply fails, which is usually known, and the date until which it is expected to last, which is usually not.

Say which of the two is uncertain. A return date that has already moved twice is a different object from one a manufacturer has confirmed.

And put one action in the notice. Not five. A notice with five actions is read as a notice with none.

### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: announcing a shortage: from, until, and affecting**

- Use from with the first date. Supply fails from the fourteenth of October. The preposition carries the certainty.
- Use until and expected for the second. It is expected to last until mid-December. The word expected is doing the real work.
- Use affecting with a list, and then a second sentence naming what is not affected. This shortage is affecting the day unit and two wards; it is not affecting outpatient supply.
- Use has already moved for a date you do not trust. The return date has already moved twice, so we are not planning on it.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A shortage notice drafted with its dates and the service affected**

**Figure 1.5** — Six lines, and the fifth one is the one that stops the telephone.

A shortage notice drafted with its dates and the service affected is a short document that must be exact about its own uncertainty.

Give the system the wholesaler’s and manufacturer’s statements with their dates, what we hold, what we can obtain, and which services use the item.

Ask for the first date plainly and the second labelled as expected, with the number of times it has already moved written beside it.

Ask for two lists: the services affected, by name, and the services not affected, because the second list is what prevents the calls.

Ask for exactly one action, with a named person and a date, and ask it to refuse a second however reasonable the second looks.

What it must not do is propose a therapeutic alternative. What to do instead of an item that is unavailable is a decision for the prescriber, and a supply notice that suggests one has taken it.

> **DATA POINT · Shortage notices** — Supply guidance commonly recommends that shortage notices state the period affected, the services affected, the degree of confidence in any return date, and a single action for recipients. The content of any therapeutic alternative is a matter for the prescriber.  
> Source: general supply guidance, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Targeted notices** — Communication literature commonly reports that notices sent to wide distribution lists are acted on less often than notices addressed to the areas affected, and that multiple actions in one notice reduce completion of all of them. Distribution is set locally.  
> Source: general communication literature, 2025–2026.

## Unit 2 · An Alternative, and Who Decides It

*CEFR B1 · Grammar: options, and the decision that is not yours to take · AI: an options note drafted so that the decision stays with the prescriber*

**Figure 2.0** — A CHOICE MADE BECAUSE NOBODY ANSWERED IS STILL A CHOICE YOU MADE.: Two options, three differences, and a name at the bottom.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- When were you last asked to choose something that was not yours to choose?
- What do you write when a prescriber will not answer?
- What differences between two options can a pharmacist state?

When an item cannot be supplied, two kinds of decision follow, and they belong to different people.

The supply decision is the pharmacy’s: what can be obtained, from where, by when, and at what cost.

The therapeutic decision is the prescriber’s: whether any of it is suitable for this patient. Nothing a pharmacy writes can take that decision, though a badly written note can appear to.

So the options note sets out what is available and the differences between the options, and stops. No recommendation, and no sentence that functions as one.

And when nobody answers, you do not choose. You escalate, you record the attempts, and you tell the ward what has not yet been decided.

### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: receiving an enquiry: what is asked, who asks, by when**

- Use What was asked was for the recorded question. What was asked was whether the policy applies to day-case patients. Record it before you interpret it.
- Use Who is asking and on what number as one question. A name without a number is not an asker; it is a memory.

A medicines information service began research as soon as an enquiry arrived, on the view that asking a busy clinician four questions first was discourteous.

An answer leaves your desk and is forwarded. The person who forwards it did not read the question, and the person who receives it cannot see it.

So the answer carries its own scope. This answer covers the following question, asked on this date, about this situation.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A written answer drafted with its scope and its limits stated**

**Figure 4.5** — Six lines, and the second one is the one a stranger needs most.

A written answer drafted with its scope and its limits stated is written for the fourth person to read it, not the first.

Give the system the question as logged with its date and asker, the sources you used with their dates, what you could not obtain, and the review date of the main source.

Ask for a covers line naming the question, the asker and the date, because an answer detached from its question is a sentence anybody can use.

Ask for two or three named exclusions rather than a general disclaimer. Everything else is not covered is read as covering everything.

Ask for the sources with their dates in the body rather than in a footnote, so that a reader skimming for one sentence sees the age of the evidence.

What it must not do is add a caveat that protects the writer rather than informing the reader. A caveat that cannot be acted on is noise, and it teaches readers to skip the limits that matter.

> **DATA POINT · Written answers** — Medicines information guidance commonly recommends that written answers state the question addressed, the sources used with their dates, the limits of the answer, and a date for review. The therapeutic decision remains with the clinician who asked.  
> Source: general medicines information guidance, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Scope statements** — Information literature commonly reports that written answers are forwarded beyond their original recipient and applied to related but different questions, and that explicit scope statements reduce this. Local procedures set the format.  
> Source: general information literature, 2025–2026.
