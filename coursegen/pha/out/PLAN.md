# English for Pharmacists — International Edition

## The spine

In pharmacy a sentence is a safety device. One word, one number, one unit and one
missing line decide what somebody takes, and the sentence has to survive being
read by a person who was not there when it was written.

Everything else in these six books follows from that. An instruction a patient
cannot repeat back has not been given. A query that was never asked is a
discrepancy that nobody resolved. A strength written without its units is not a
strength. A label that records what the prescriber meant rather than what the
prescription says is a second error laid on top of a first. A near miss that
nobody wrote down will happen again next Thursday.

Sixty units, twelve parts each. Twenty vocabulary terms a unit, no term repeated
anywhere in the series: 1,200 in all. One grammar point and one AI capability per
unit, each taught with its boundary.

## Calibration

CEFR level is measured, not asserted. Mean sentence length must sit inside the
band for the book — A2 8.5–12.5, B1 11.5–16.5, B2 15.0–21.0 — with sentence caps
of 26, 34 and 44 words and 3,500–5,600 words of taught prose per unit.

## The signature

Part 10 of every unit is **The Question You Have to Ask**: the moment a pharmacist
has to put a query, a refusal or a correction in front of somebody who does not
want it — a prescriber, a patient, a manager, a wholesaler, a board — and stay
there while it is answered. Three turns, six holds, one redline.

## Four standing rules

1. **Never give a therapeutic instruction.** No dose, no strength, no regimen, no
   interaction, no indication and no named medicine appears anywhere in the
   series. Every therapeutic decision belongs to a prescriber who is named as
   owning it.
2. **Never write what the prescription does not say.** The pharmacist's writing
   records what is there and names what is absent; it does not supply the missing
   word from inference.
3. **Never leave a query unasked because asking is awkward.** The series signature
   exists because the awkward question is the job.
4. **Never state a legal or regulatory requirement as though this book set it.**
   Supply law, registers, unlicensed items, recalls, refusal, raising concerns,
   inspection and fitness to practise are governed by local law and by the
   employer's own standard operating procedures, which every unit says so.
