### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- Who is on in your pharmacy on Saturday afternoon?
- Which tasks wait until the pharmacist is back?
- What does a locum need in the first ten minutes?

A rota looks like a grid. It is really a list of hours, and every hour needs a name in it.

Three words do most of the work. Somebody is on. Somebody is off. Somebody is covering.

Covering is the dangerous one. It can mean standing at the counter, or it can mean being responsible.

So the rota says the hours a pharmacist must be present, and it says who that pharmacist is.

And it says what waits. A task that waits is a decision. A task that does not wait is a problem.

### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: arrangements in a rota: is on, is off, is covering**

- Use is on with the hours. Inês is on from nine until half past five. Give the hours, not the day alone.
- Use is off for absence. Agnieszka is off on Thursday. This is a fact, not an apology.
- Use is covering with the thing covered. Tomás is covering the counter at lunch. Covering the counter is not covering the dispensary.
- Say No pharmacist is present and then say what waits. The second half of that sentence is the useful half.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A rota checked for the hours a pharmacist must be present**

**Figure 1.5** — Six lines, and the fourth one is the only one anybody argues about.

A rota checked for the hours a pharmacist must be present turns a grid into a list of hours.

Give the system the opening hours, the published rota, the leave already agreed and the tasks that need a pharmacist.

Ask it to list every opening hour with the name of the pharmacist on, and to mark the hours where no name appears.

Ask it to treat covering as incomplete until the thing covered is named. Covering the counter is not covering the dispensary.

Ask for the tasks that wait in each gap, because a gap with three tasks in it is a different problem from an empty one.

What it must not do is decide which hours the law requires a pharmacist to be present for. That is in local law and in the procedures, and you read it yourself.

> **DATA POINT · Rotas** — Professional guidance commonly recommends recording, for each opening hour, which named person is responsible and which tasks may not be carried out in their absence. The hours a pharmacist must be present are set by local law and by the employer’s procedures.  
> Source: general professional guidance, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Named cover** — Operational literature commonly recommends naming the individual responsible for each hour rather than the team, and stating which tasks are suspended when nobody is present. Requirements differ between jurisdictions.  
> Source: general operational literature, 2025–2026.

### Part 8 · The Decision Room · Fifty-four hours and eleven dashes

**STAGE 1 · The Brief**

A pharmacy published its rota as a grid with dashes for hours nobody had been assigned. Staff read the dashes as quiet periods.

Over three months the rota carried 702 opening hours. Eleven hours a week, 143 in all, carried a dash. A review found that 96 of those 143 hours had a pharmacist present in fact, and 47 did not.

In the 47 hours, 61 tasks were attempted that required a pharmacist. 9 patients were told an item would be ready and found that it was not.

**STAGE 2 · The Evidence**

**Figure 1.8** — One hundred and forty-three dashes, and forty-seven of them real gaps.

Nobody had decided that those forty-seven hours would be uncovered. The grid simply had nothing written in them.

**STAGE 3 · The Voices**

**Jan Vermeer** · *the owner*  
“A rota with a name in every single hour is a document for an inspector, not for a working pharmacy.”

**Inês Carvalho** · *pharmacist*  
“It is a document for a Saturday. Nine patients were told an item was ready when nobody could finish it.”

**Agnieszka Zielińska** · *lead technician*  
“Write the gaps and the tasks that wait, and I shall stop promising patients half past one.”

**STAGE 4 · Your Analysis**

**Figure 1.9** — Your analysis, step by step.

- **Find it** — How many hours carried a dash?
- **Compare it** — Ninety-six covered against forty-seven not.
- **Infer it** — Why did the dashes mislead people?
- **Weigh it** — A fuller document against nine promises broken.
- **Decide** — What does every opening hour carry?

0. Find it. How many?  — *one hundred and forty-three of 702*
1. Compare it. What was the split?
2. Infer it. Why?
3. Weigh it. What was traded?
4. Decide. What does it carry?

**STAGE 5 · The Decision**

**Write the rule, in four lines.**

- Say what every opening hour carries.
- Say what covering must state.
- Say what is written where there is no pharmacist.
- Say when the rota is published and where.

*Language bank for the decision*
- Every opening hour on the rota carries the name of one pharmacist, and a dash is never used for any hour.
- Where a colleague is covering, the rota states what is being covered, and covering the counter is written as counter only.
- Where no pharmacist is present, the hour is marked as a gap and the tasks that wait in it are listed beneath the rota.
- The rota is published by Friday for the following week, placed where the whole team can see it, and a copy is kept in the day book.

> **WHAT HAPPENED** — The pharmacy adopts the four lines. Over the following three months, across 698 opening hours, hours carrying a dash fall from 143 to none, real gaps fall from 47 to 12 and are all marked in advance, tasks attempted without a pharmacist fall from 61 to 3. No patient is told an item is ready when nobody can finish it.

2. What happened to tasks attempted?
3. How many patients were told wrongly?
4. Over what period?

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A temperature excursion written as duration, range and action requested**

**Figure 5.5** — Six lines, and the fourth one is the one people are tempted to fill in.

A temperature excursion written as duration, range and action requested is a letter that must be exact about what it does not know.

Give the system the readings you have and the time of the last reading inside the range. Add the time of discovery and the stock list with batch numbers.

Ask for the highest and lowest readings, and for the longest possible period in hours rather than in words.

Ask it to write unknown where the start of the excursion cannot be established, and never to estimate it.

Ask for one clear request at the end: written advice on the batches listed, and nothing else.

What it must not do is advise. Whether the stock may be used is a decision for the manufacturer, and this letter exists to let them make it.
