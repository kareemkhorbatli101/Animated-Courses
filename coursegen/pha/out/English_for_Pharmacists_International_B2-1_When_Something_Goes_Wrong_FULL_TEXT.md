## Unit 1 · An Error That Reached a Patient

*CEFR B2 · Grammar: describing an error without evasion: I, we, and what happened · AI: an error account sorted into facts, actions and what is not yet known*

**Figure 1.0** — AN ACCOUNT WITH NOBODY IN IT IS AN ACCOUNT WRITTEN TO PROTECT SOMEBODY.: Three lists within the hour, and a person in every sentence.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- When did you last write an account of something that went wrong?
- Which of your facts had you verified yourself?
- What did you leave out because you did not yet know it?

An error has reached a patient. In the first hour the only useful thing anybody can produce is an accurate account, and accuracy here means a particular kind of honesty about sources.

So the account divides into three lists and not two. The facts, each marked as verified by you or reported to you; the actions already taken, with the hour each was taken; and what is not yet known.

The third list is the one that gets deleted, usually by somebody who thinks an incomplete account looks careless. An account that silently omits its unknowns is not more complete; it is less true.

And every sentence has a person in it. I typed the label; Agnieszka found it at the second check; the ward sister told me at ten past four. The passive voice at this stage is a request for somebody else to carry it.

A provisional cause is not a fact. It is a hypothesis with the authority of an incident form behind it, and the investigation will spend two weeks disproving the one you wrote in the first hour.

### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: describing an error without evasion: I, we, and what happened**

- Use I with the verb for your own acts. I typed the label at twenty past three. The passive — the label was typed — is the sentence that asks somebody else to own it.
- Mark every reported fact as reported, with its source. The ward sister told me at ten past four that the patient had taken it.
- Use What is not yet known is for the third list. What is not yet known is how long the item had been on the trolley.
- Use I shall not state a cause until, and give the condition. I shall not state a cause until the dispensing audit trail has been read by somebody other than me.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**An error account sorted into facts, actions and what is not yet known**

**Figure 1.5** — Six blocks, and the fifth one is the one somebody will ask you to delete.

An error account sorted into facts, actions and what is not yet known exists to be written in the hour when nobody yet knows what happened.

Give the system what you saw yourself with the times, what you were told and by whom, the actions already taken, and the questions you cannot answer.

Ask for every fact to carry its source, marked as verified by you or reported to you, because a reported fact that reads as verified is the commonest error in incident writing.

Ask for each sentence to carry a person. The passive voice in a first account is an invitation to four weeks of other people establishing who did what.

Ask for the third list in full and ask it to refuse to shorten it, since an account that quietly omits its unknowns is less true rather than more complete.

What it must not do is propose a cause, however obvious the cause appears. A provisional cause written in the first hour acquires the authority of the document it sits in, and the investigation will spend weeks discharging it.

> **DATA POINT · Incident accounts** — Safety guidance commonly recommends that initial accounts of an incident record verified facts separately from reported information, state the actions taken with their times, and identify what remains unknown, without proposing a cause. Local procedures set the form and the duties of disclosure.  
> Source: general safety guidance, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · First accounts** — Safety literature commonly reports that initial accounts written in the passive voice lengthen subsequent investigation, and that early statements of probable cause are frequently disproved. Reporting duties are set by local law and the employer.

**Model**

Scope: facts, actions and unknowns; it proposes no cause and makes no clinical assessment of harm, which belongs to the treating clinician. ### Part 8 · The Decision Room · Four weeks of establishing who

**STAGE 1 · The Brief**

A hospital pharmacy used an incident form that invited a narrative account and a probable cause, and most accounts were written in the passive voice two to three days after the event.

Over three years 184 incident accounts were reviewed. Of these 141 contained no named person as the subject of any sentence, and 118 stated a probable cause in the first account. Of those 118 causes, 71 were not supported by the completed investigation.

Investigations of accounts written within the hour took a mean of 9 days. Investigations of accounts written later took a mean of 27 days, of which a mean of 14 was spent establishing who did what and when.

**Write the rule, in four lines.**

*Language bank for the decision*
- A first account is written before the person who was present goes home, and is not revised afterwards except by addition with a date.
- Every fact is marked as verified by the writer or reported to the writer, with the name of whoever reported it and the time.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- What was the first sentence of the last difficult conversation you had?
- Which apology do you use: for what was done, or for how somebody feels?
- What promise can you make without asking anybody?

A patient has been affected by something the pharmacy did, and within the hour somebody has to sit down and tell them, which is a task that no amount of procedure makes easier.

The first sentence carries the act, in the first person and without the context that led to it, because context offered before the act reads as an excuse being assembled in advance.

The second sentence carries the effect, divided honestly into what is known and what is not yet known, since a patient who is told more certainty than exists will discover the difference later and will remember that rather than the apology.

The third carries the next step: one thing that will happen, with the name of the person who will do it and the day on which it will be done, because a promise without a name is a sentence that comforts the speaker.

And the apology is for what was done, not for how the patient feels. I am sorry that we gave you the wrong item is an apology; I am sorry you are upset is an observation about the patient.

### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: telling a patient: what happened, what it means, what now**

- Open with the act in the first person plural, with a date and no context. We gave you the wrong item on Tuesday afternoon.
- Divide the effect explicitly. What we know is that you took one of them; what we do not yet know is whether it has affected you.
- Give the next step with a name and a day. Dr Dubois will see you at ten tomorrow morning, and I shall telephone you myself on Friday.
- Apologise for the event. I am sorry that we gave you the wrong item is an apology; I am sorry you are upset says something about the patient rather than about us.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A disclosure conversation prepared as act, effect and next step**

**Figure 2.5** — Six lines, and the fourth one is the one that is usually left out.

A disclosure conversation prepared as act, effect and next step is twenty minutes of preparation for a conversation that a patient will remember for the rest of their life.

Give the system the act as it is known, the date and hour, what has been established about the effect, what has not, and the appointments and calls that have actually been arranged.

Ask for the act as the first sentence with no context in front of it, because any explanation offered before the act is heard as a defence rather than as information.

Ask for the effect split into two explicit halves, and ask it to refuse any sentence that reassures beyond the evidence, since reassurance that collapses later costs more than the original error.

Ask for a next step naming a person, a day and a number, and for the same three things written out on a single sheet the patient can take away and give to a relative.

What it must not do is assess the clinical consequences or offer comfort about them. What this means for the patient’s health is a matter for the treating clinician, and a pharmacist who guesses at it in that room has given a second piece of wrong information.

> **DATA POINT · Disclosure** — Professional guidance commonly recommends that patients be told promptly what has happened, what is known of the consequences, and what will be done next, with an apology for the event itself. The duties of disclosure, and who discharges them, are set by local law and by the employer’s procedures.  
> Source: general professional guidance, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Timing of disclosure** — Professional literature commonly reports that early disclosure with acknowledged uncertainty is associated with fewer formal complaints than delayed disclosure after investigation, and that written summaries given at the time are valued by patients. Duties are set by local law.  
> Source: general professional literature, 2025–2026.

### Part 8 · The Decision Room · Nineteen days and a solicitor

**STAGE 1 · The Brief**

A hospital told patients about dispensing errors after its investigation had concluded, on the view that a patient should be given accurate and complete information rather than partial information that might later change.

Over four years 96 errors reaching patients were recorded. In 24 cases the patient was told within twenty-four hours; in 72 the patient was told after the investigation, at a mean of 21 days. Formal complaints followed 4 of the 24 and 41 of the 72.

Of the 72, 29 patients said in their complaint that they had worked out that something had happened before anybody told them. Eleven mentioned legal advice; 9 of those 11 were in the group told after 21 days.

**STAGE 2 · The Evidence**

**Figure 2.8** — Four complaints from twenty-four early conversations, and forty-one from seventy-two late ones.

Twenty-nine patients told after the investigation said they had already worked out that something had happened.

**Write the rule, in four lines.**

- Say when a patient is told.
- Say what the first sentence is.
- Say how uncertainty is handled.
- Say what the patient takes away.

*Language bank for the decision*
- A patient affected by an error is told within the first hour in which they can be reached, and the investigation continues alongside that conversation rather than before it.
- The first sentence states what we did, with the date, in the first person and with no account of the circumstances in front of it.
- What is known and what is not yet known are stated as two separate things, and no reassurance is offered that the evidence does not support.
- The patient is given, in writing and at the time, the act, the two halves of the effect, and the next step with a name, a day and a direct telephone number.

A pharmacist’s refusal is a professional judgement, and the circumstances in which one may or must be made are set by local law and by the employer’s procedures rather than by anything in this book.

What the pharmacist controls is the sentence. I cannot supply this today. It comes first, in the first person, and it does not begin with an apology that invites negotiation.

The third sentence is the route that remains. What I shall do instead is telephone Dr Dubois at two, and you will hear from one of us before five, because a refusal with no route is a door rather than a decision.

And a refusal is recorded in writing the same day, in your own words, because a refusal that exists only in a conversation will be remembered by three people in four different ways.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A refusal drafted with its reason and the route that remains**

**Figure 3.5** — Six lines, and the third one is the one most people fudge.

A refusal drafted with its reason and the route that remains is a short document written for two readers who may not be given the same information.

Give the system the item, the day, the reason as you understand it, which parts of the reason may be given to the patient, and what you are able to do next with an hour against it.

Ask for the refusal as the first sentence in the first person, and ask it to remove any opening apology, because an apology at the front converts a decision into an opening position.

Ask for the reason in the version the patient may have, and for a separate line recording the part that goes to the prescriber alone, so that neither reader is given the other’s document by accident.

Ask for a route with an hour and a commitment naming who will telephone whom, since a refusal whose route is go back to your doctor leaves the patient to carry the consequence of your judgement.

What it must not do is decide whether to refuse. When a refusal may or must be made is a matter of law, of procedure and of your own professional judgement, and a drafting tool that appeared to decide it would be the most dangerous document in the dispensary.

> **DATA POINT · Refusal to supply** — Professional guidance commonly recognises that a pharmacist may decline to supply where professional judgement requires it, and recommends recording the decision, the reason and the action taken. When a refusal may or must be made is set by local law and by the employer’s procedures.  
> Source: general professional guidance, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Recording refusals** — Professional literature commonly reports that refusals recorded in writing on the day are upheld more consistently than those recorded later or not at all, and that recording pressure to withdraw a refusal assists subsequent review. Requirements are set locally.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A second written approach to a prescriber, with the escalation named**

**Figure 4.5** — Six lines, and the fourth one is the one people leave out of fear.

A second written approach to a prescriber, with the escalation named, is a letter that is easy to draft and difficult to send, and the difficulty is the point of it.

Give the system the question you asked and when, the reply you received, what remains unresolved, the escalation route your procedures set, and whether you intend to supply meanwhile.

Ask for the opening to concede the reply explicitly, because a second letter that does not acknowledge the first answer reads as though you had not understood it.

Ask it to state whether the disagreement concerns a fact or a judgement, since the first can be resolved by evidence and the second is a question about authority that evidence will not touch.

Ask for the escalation named in the body with a person and an hour, and ask it to refuse any formulation in which the escalation is implied, hinted at, or held in reserve as a threat.

What it must not do is characterise the prescriber. A letter that describes somebody as unwilling to engage has made an allegation about a person where a description of a position would have served, and the allegation is what will be remembered.

> **DATA POINT · Escalation** — Professional guidance commonly recommends that a pharmacist who remains concerned after a prescriber has responded should escalate through a route set by the employer, and should inform the prescriber that they are doing so. The routes and the obligations are set by local law and by the employer’s procedures.  
> Source: general professional guidance, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Informed escalation** — Professional literature commonly reports that escalation of which the original decision-maker was informed in advance produces less lasting damage to working relationships than escalation discovered afterwards, without reducing its effect. Routes are set locally.  
> Source: general professional literature, 2025–2026.

A recall notice is a short document that arrives at an inconvenient hour, and the hour it arrives decides most of what follows, because a notice read at four on a Friday is really a notice about a weekend.

The four actions go in a fixed order, and the order is not a matter of taste. Stop the stock going out, quarantine what you hold, arrange the return, and confirm to the supplier that you have done it.

Stopping comes first because it is the only one of the four that prevents anything; quarantining is housekeeping, returning is logistics, and confirming is paperwork, however urgently the notice asks for the last of them.

Then the harder half: who has already had it. A list of patients who have received the batch is useful only to the extent that it is complete, and the entries you cannot complete are the ones to name explicitly.

And every step carries the hour it was taken, because in March somebody will ask what this pharmacy did between four o’clock and six o’clock on the Friday, and a recall nobody can reconstruct is a recall that did not happen.

**Grammar: a recall: stop, quarantine, return, and by when**

- Use the four verbs as imperatives in a fixed order, each with an hour against it. Stop at 16:05. Quarantine by 16:20. Return arranged 16:40. Confirm sent 17:30.

A recall action list drafted as stop, quarantine, return and confirm is a short document whose whole value lies in the hours written against it.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A competence concern written as observed, measured and required**

**Figure 6.5** — Six columns, and the last one is the one that keeps the document professional.

A competence concern written as observed, measured and required is the document a pharmacist is most tempted to write badly, because the feeling that prompts it is stronger than any of the evidence for it.

Give the system what you saw with dates and hours, what others have told you, the rate you have measured with its denominator, any comparison you have, and the words of the standard itself.

Ask for the observed column to carry no inference at all, so that the person reading it can agree with every line of it before disagreeing with your conclusion.

Ask for the measured column to be a rate out of a denominator and to be left empty where you have not measured anything, because an empty column is a fact about your evidence and an asserted rate is not.

Ask for the standard quoted word for word from the procedure, since a paraphrase shifts the ground from what the organisation requires to what you believe it requires.

What it must not do is speculate about why. A health matter, a bereavement or a difficulty at home may lie behind everything in the first column, and that possibility belongs to the employer and to the person, not in a document written by a colleague.

> **DATA POINT · Competence concerns** — Employment and professional guidance commonly recommend that concerns about a colleague’s practice be based on observed behaviour, measured where measurement is possible, and set against the written standard, and that the individual be informed. The process, and any health or capability route, is set by the employer and by local law.  
> Source: general employment guidance, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Observed behaviour** — Employment literature commonly reports that concerns founded on observed and measured behaviour are more useful to both the individual and the employer than concerns expressed as general impressions, and that individuals informed by a colleague before a formal process engage with it more fully. Processes are set by the employer.  
> Source: general employment literature, 2025–2026.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A concern about practice recorded and routed without a verdict in it**

**Figure 7.5** — Six lines, and the fifth one is what makes the other five credible.

A concern about practice recorded and routed without a verdict in it is a document that has to be strong enough to act on and restrained enough to be believed.

Give the system what you saw with dates and hours, what you did at the time, the one thing you are asking for, the route your procedures set and the name it goes to.

Ask for the observation with no adjective in it, because an adjective is a conclusion travelling in disguise and the reader will answer the conclusion rather than the fact.

Ask for the action you took written honestly, including nothing where that is the truth, since a concern that improves the writer’s own conduct will be disbelieved in all its parts.

Ask for one request that somebody can grant or refuse, and for an acknowledgement with a date, because a concern raised into silence has been received by nobody.

What it must not do is reach the conclusion. Whether this is a competence matter, a health matter or a one-off is reserved to people appointed to decide it, and a concern that decides it has handed its reader a reason to set the whole document aside.

**An inspection answer drafted from the evidence that exists**

**Figure 8.5** — Six elements, and the fifth is the one that changes a finding.

An inspection answer drafted from the evidence that exists is a short written answer built only from documents that are in the building this morning.

Give the system the question as it was asked, a list of what exists and where, the day for anything held elsewhere, and the gaps with their reasons.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- How long does a complaint wait before it is acknowledged where you work?
- Who decides what a complaint is about?

Most complaints are answered. The ones that become cases are the ones where nobody wrote down, at the start, what the complaint was about.

So the first letter does three things. It acknowledges, within the number of days your procedures state. It sets out the scope, in the complainant’s own terms as far as possible. And it gives a date.

Scope is where it goes wrong in both directions. A scope you have widened turns one dispensing error into a review of the whole pharmacy. A scope you have narrowed answers the easy points and leaves the one that mattered.

And people want different things. An apology, a change, or somebody’s dismissal, and those three are not the same letter. You answer all three the same way, by saying what you can do and what you cannot.

The point you cannot answer is named, not skipped. We do not know why the telephone was not answered at ten past five on the eleventh, and we have not been able to establish it, is a sentence a complainant can accept. Silence on that point is not.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A complaint acknowledged with its scope and its timescale stated**

**Figure 9.5** — Six elements, and the third is the one that prevents a case.

A complaint acknowledged with its scope and its timescale stated is the first letter, and in most cases it decides whether a second letter is needed.

Give the system the complaint as written, the date it arrived, the acknowledgement period your procedures set and the day by which a full reply can realistically go.

Ask for the scope in the complainant’s own terms, with an explicit invitation to correct it, because a scope agreed in March cannot be disputed in November.

Ask for the timescale to be a day rather than a period, and for that day to be one you have reason to believe you can keep, since a missed date is a new complaint.

Ask for every point you cannot resolve to be named with what you tried, and for the escalation route to be stated plainly rather than buried in the last line.

What it must not do is narrow the scope to what is comfortable. A letter that answers the waiting time and says nothing about the telephone has told the complainant which of her points was inconvenient.

The periods, the escalation route and any statutory body are set by local law.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- What decision have you taken alone, with nobody to ask?
- What did you write down about it, and when?
- What would you write differently now that you know how it ended?

Sooner or later a pharmacist takes a decision alone, at an hour when there is nobody to ask, and has to write it down afterwards.

The record has four parts. What information you had at the time. What you decided. What you tried in order to reach somebody. And the basis, which is the reason you can state for the decision you took.

What does not belong in it is the outcome. A record written after you know how it ended is a different document, and an experienced reader can see the hindsight in it from across a room.

The attempts matter as much as the decision. Three telephone calls at twenty past two, twenty-five past and half past, to numbers you can name, is a record. I tried to get hold of somebody is not.

And sometimes you cannot state a basis. I took the view that waiting until morning carried less risk, and I cannot point to anything in writing that supports that, is an honest sentence, and far stronger than a reason invented later to fill the gap.
