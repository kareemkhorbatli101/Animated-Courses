# English for Pharmacists — International Edition

A six-book illustrated coursebook series for pharmacists working in English,
from CEFR A2.1 to B2.2. Six books, sixty units, 374,807 words, 1,200 pages,
918 original figures, 1,200 vocabulary terms.

In pharmacy a sentence is a safety device: one word, one number, one unit
decides what somebody takes. The series teaches the sentences, not the
pharmacology.

figure, two dialogues, three speaking tasks, an extended reading on using an
AI assistant for that unit's written product, a six-line writing tasking, a
tool-boundary page, a Decision Room case with its exhibit and its four-line
rule, a Brief Lab diagnosing six faults in a real-looking draft, and a
closing part called **The Question You Have to Ask**.

## Calibration

Levels are measured, not asserted. Every unit is checked against a mean
sentence length band, a sentence-length cap, and a prose-volume band for its
level.

| Level | Mean sentence length | Cap | Measured |
|-------|---------------------|-----|----------|
| A2 | 8.5 – 12.5 words | 26 | 10.20 / 10.78 |
| B1 | 11.5 – 16.5 words | 34 | 12.67 / 12.72 |
| B2 | 15.0 – 21.0 words | 44 | 16.89 / 17.26 |

Twenty-three per-book gates and two series-wide gates pass on all six books.
The series gates check that each of the eight fault types in the Brief Lab
ladder appears in at least twelve of the sixty labs, and that the Decision
Room clinics do not always vindicate the manager.

## Four standing rules

The series is about communication. It never gives a therapeutic instruction.

1. **No therapeutic content.** No dose, strength, regimen, interaction,
   indication or named medicine appears anywhere in the six books. Every
   therapeutic decision in every scenario is attributed to a named prescriber.
2. **Never write what the prescription does not say.** The discipline of the
   series is the difference between what is written, what is meant and what
   is missing.
3. **Never leave a query unasked because asking is awkward.** Part 10 of
   every unit — *The Question You Have to Ask* — is built on this.
4. **Law and procedure are local.** Supply law, registers, unlicensed items,
   recalls, refusal, raising concerns, inspection and fitness to practise are
   attributed throughout to local law and the employer's own standard
   operating procedures, never to a rule this book has invented.

## The AI thread

Each unit contains an extended reading on using an AI assistant for that
unit's specific written product, a six-line tasking (purpose, scope, inputs,
criteria, done, fallback) that the reader writes themselves, and a page
stating explicitly what the tool cannot do. Sixty distinct AI capabilities
across the series, each with its own boundary line.

The last unit of the last book, *What No System Can Do for a Pharmacist's
Judgement*, is about where all of it stops.

photographs, no stock art, no third-party material.
