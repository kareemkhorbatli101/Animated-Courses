# English for Commercial Correspondence — International Edition — plan

## What makes this edition different from the Syria Edition

The Syria Edition is written for a company rebuilding its export lines: reopening markets,
re-establishing bank relationships, writing to buyers who have never visited.

The International Edition is written for the condition that defines international
correspondence everywhere: **between a seller in one country and a buyer in another, English
is usually nobody's first language.** Everything in the edition follows from that.

Sixty units, none of them a rename of a Syria Edition unit. The new material is the material
only an international edition can carry:

* name order, address format and postcode position by destination country
* two systems of measurement, and the specification that must survive conversion
* which currency you quote in, the rate, the date, and who carries the difference
* working weeks and public holidays that are not yours
* time zones: when an answer can honestly be expected, and what a silence means
* reading an order written in imperfect English for what the writer meant
* bank charges, and the difference between the amount invoiced and the amount received
* four modes of transport, chosen and justified
* which documents each market demands, listed before the goods are made
* which law, which court, which language — and which text prevails
* a bilingual contract checked clause against clause
* force majeure along a chain, not at one point
* what you may not send abroad, and the question you must ask before attaching it
* one message segmented for forty markets
* writing that will be machine-translated, and the sentence shapes that survive it

## Architecture (unchanged, because it works)

Twelve parts per unit: Warm Up and Read · Vocabulary · Grammar and Dialogues · Speaking ·
Extended Reading · Guided Writing · Working with AI · The Decision Room · The Brief Lab ·
**The Money Conversation** · Six-Skill Review · Can-Do and Foundations.

Ten units per book, six books, A2.1 → B2.2. 153 generated figures per book. One grammar
point, twenty vocabulary terms and one AI capability per unit, each unique across the series:
60 grammar points, 1,200 terms, 60 capabilities.

## The cast

Aurora Instruments, Porto, makes measuring instruments and sells to thirty countries.
Meridian Trading in Rotterdam imports them; Kiyomizu Instruments in Osaka is a buyer, and
Hiroshi Tanabe writes the other half of the correspondence — so the learner sees both sides
of the same trade, which the Syria Edition could not show.

## The six books

1. **A2.1 · The Letter That Crosses a Border** — names, addresses, currencies, units, dates,
   time zones, and the first quotation to a buyer you cannot visit.
2. **A2.2 · Orders, Confirmations and Time Zones** — the order across two calendars, imperfect
   English, carton marks, bank charges, a silence, the second order.
3. **B1.1 · Documents, Carriers and Banks** — Incoterms, four modes, the transport documents,
   a credit line by line, a discrepancy priced by the day, insurance, customs.
4. **B1.2 · Claims, Credit and Currencies** — claims on evidence, credit you can verify from
   abroad, escalating reminders, exchange exposure, a price increase for forty markets.
5. **B2.1 · Contracts, Agents and Which Law Applies** — agency, territory, standard terms,
   the bilingual contract, jurisdiction, force majeure along a chain.
6. **B2.2 · One Company, Many Markets** — tenders, proposals, the profile, compliance
   questions, one message for forty markets, writing for translation, the handover.

## Rules the generator enforces

No forecasting. No live political reference. No vendor or product named. Register measured,
not asserted. Eight named precision faults on a ladder, each in ≥12 of the 60 Brief Labs.
A boundary line on all 60 AI capabilities. Four dialogues per book ending on an unanswered
objection. In the sixty Decision Rooms the senior person is wrong often enough to matter.
