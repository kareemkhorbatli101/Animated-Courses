### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- Has your company ever bid for public or institutional work abroad?
- Who reads the tender conditions before the technical work begins?
- What would disqualify your last quotation if it had been a tender?

An international tender is not a quotation with more paperwork; it is a procedure in which the first reading of your bid is a compliance check performed by somebody who is not permitted to overlook anything, however small.

That reader does not compare your price with anybody else’s. He establishes whether your submission may be opened at all, and a bid that fails on a formality is set aside before any technical merit is considered.

The dossier therefore divides into two kinds of requirement, and the division is rarely signalled as clearly as it should be. A mandatory requirement is one whose absence disqualifies you; a desirable requirement is one whose absence costs you points.

The sentences that disqualify are usually short, scattered and dull: the bid shall remain valid for ninety days; the tender guarantee shall be issued by a bank acceptable to the purchaser; alternative offers shall not be considered; all documents shall be in English.

Aurora bids for the supply of laboratory instruments to a university consortium in three countries, and the dossier runs to one hundred and forty pages. Four of its sentences decide whether six weeks of work will be read.

### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: impersonal and nominal style in formal submissions**

- A tender submission is written impersonally, because the committee is reading an offer from a company rather than a letter from a person: the goods offered comply with clause 4.2 of the specification, and delivery is offered within ninety days of order.
- The nominal style replaces verbs with nouns where the noun is the thing being evaluated: not “we will install the instruments and train your staff”, but “installation and training are included, as described at section 7”.
- Cross-reference is part of the register. Every claim points to where it is evidenced: as shown in annexe 3; the certificate is enclosed at tab 9. A claim with no reference costs the committee time and costs you points.
- Avoid the first person except where the dossier asks for an undertaking, where it is required and should be exact: the bidder undertakes to maintain the bid for ninety days from the date of opening.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**Read the dossier for exclusions before you read it for work**

**Figure 1.5** — Five exclusions and one disadvantage: only the last is about how good you are.

A tender dossier reduced to the lines that disqualify you is the most valuable half-hour of work in a bid, and it belongs at the beginning, before a single page of technical text has been drafted by anybody.

Give it the whole dossier, every addendum issued since, and the forms to be returned. Ask for one list only: the provisions whose breach excludes the bid, each quoted with its clause number and page.

Ask for each line to be classified as mandatory or desirable with the words that make it so, because “shall” and “must” carry a consequence that “should”, “preferably” and “it would be welcomed” do not.

Ask for every date to be extracted into one table: submission deadline in local time, bid validity, guarantee validity, delivery period, and the date by which clarification questions must be asked. Dates disqualify more bids than specifications do.

Ask it to flag the forms that require a signature by an authorised representative, and to say which documents must be originals rather than copies. Then have a second person check that list against the dossier by hand.

What no system can decide is whether a requirement you cannot meet is worth building a capability for. It can tell you that you would be excluded today, which is the fact you need, and the decision that follows is a commercial one.

> **DATA POINT · Compliance first** — Public procurement practice commonly provides for an administrative compliance check before technical and financial evaluation, with non-compliant bids excluded from further consideration.  
> Source: general public procurement guidance, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Formal exclusion** — Procurement guidance commonly treats late submission, missing guarantees, unsigned forms and prohibited variants as grounds for exclusion before evaluation, independently of the quality of the offer.  
> Source: general public procurement guidance, 2025–2026.

### Part 8 · The Decision Room · The lowest price, rejected unopened

**STAGE 5 · The Decision**

**Write the rule, in four lines.**

- Say what is done before any technical drafting begins.
- Say who checks the date table.
- Say how guarantee validity is calculated.
- Say what is done with a doubtful provision.

*Language bank for the decision*
- No technical text is drafted until the exclusion review and the date table are complete.
- The date table is checked by a second person against the dossier by hand.
- Guarantee and bid validity are calculated from the date of opening, never from the date of submission, with five days added.

*CEFR B2 · Grammar: structure signals in a long document · AI: a proposal structured for a reader with twenty minutes and a dictionary*

**Figure 2.0** — THE DECISION WAS TAKEN ON PAGE ONE: A proposal is read by somebody who will decide before reaching the detail.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- How long does a customer spend on your proposals before deciding?
- Where in your last proposal was the recommendation?
- Could your summary page be read on its own?

A proposal sent abroad is read by somebody with twenty minutes, a dictionary and four other proposals on the same desk, and the structure that suits him is not the structure in which the work was done.

The work runs from analysis to conclusion; the reading runs the other way. He wants the recommendation first, the price second, the three things that could go wrong third, and the reasoning only if the first three survive.

This is not a trick of presentation but a courtesy, since a reader in his second language spends part of his twenty minutes decoding rather than deciding, and every sentence he must read twice is a sentence taken from your argument.

A one-page summary that can be read entirely alone is therefore the most important page in the document. It states the recommendation, the price, the timetable, the assumptions and the name of the person who will answer questions.

Aurora proposes a laboratory refit to a buyer in Osaka, and the proposal runs to twenty-eight pages because the engineers were thorough. The buyer reads page one, the price table and the exclusion list, and decides.

### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: structure signals in a long document: first, accordingly, in conclusion**

- Signposting words are not decoration; they are the handles by which a reader in a hurry carries a document: first, second, third; accordingly; however; in conclusion.
- Use them in a fixed, visible pattern. First, the problem as the buyer stated it. Second, what we found. Accordingly, what we recommend. The reader who skims the first words of each paragraph should still arrive at the right answer.
- Signals of consequence must be honest. Accordingly and therefore announce that what follows is caused by what precedes, and a reader who checks one and finds it false stops trusting the rest.
- In conclusion is reserved for the end and used once, and a document with three conclusions has none. The summary page, which is read first, does not need it at all.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**Structure it for the reading, not for the work**

**Figure 2.5** — Six lines on one page, and the sixth saves the switchboard a call.

A proposal structured for a reader with twenty minutes and a dictionary is not a shorter proposal; it is the same proposal reordered, with a page at the front that can be read alone and decided from.

Give it the full draft, the buyer’s original enquiry and your price build-up. Ask first for the decision question in one sentence, because a proposal that cannot state the question it answers is not yet a proposal.

Ask for the summary page as six lines: recommendation, price with currency and exclusions, timetable, the three assumptions the price depends on, the three exclusions most likely to be queried, and a named contact with a telephone number and working hours.

Ask for the body to be reordered behind that page and signposted so that the first words of each section tell a skimming reader what it contains. Ask for nothing to be cut, because the evidence is what the buyer’s own engineer will read.

Ask for a sentence-length check on the summary page alone, since this is the page read by somebody working in his second language with a clock running, and a thirty-word sentence there costs more than it does on page twelve.

What no system can decide is which three exclusions your buyer will care about, because that depends on what he has been burnt by before. Ask him, and if he will not say, ask the engineer who installed the last one.

> **DATA POINT · Front-loading** — Technical-writing guidance commonly recommends placing recommendation and cost at the front of a long proposal, with a summary drafted to be read independently of the body.  
> Source: general technical and business writing guidance, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Reading behaviour** — Studies of professional reading commonly report that long documents are skimmed by heading and opening sentence rather than read sequentially, and that second-language readers skim more heavily under time pressure.  
> Source: general document design literature, 2025–2026.

### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: invitation and follow-up: we should be pleased to, may we suggest**

- The invitation formula is conditional and specific at the same time: we should be pleased to receive you at stand D24 in Hall 3, and may we suggest Wednesday 14 March at 11.00, local time in Hamburg.
- Offer an alternative in the same letter, because a reader who must decline your time and invent another will often simply not reply: should this be inconvenient, any slot on Thursday morning is free.
- Hold the slot with a date rather than indefinitely: we shall hold 11.00 on Wednesday until Friday 28 February, after which it will be offered elsewhere. That sentence is polite and it is also a deadline.
- Follow up once, and make the follow-up final without reproach: as we have not heard from you, we have released the slot; we shall be on the stand throughout and should be glad to see you at any time.

## Unit 5 · A Bank’s Compliance Questionnaire

*CEFR B2 · Grammar: answering a question exactly, with the evidence named · AI: a compliance questionnaire answered with evidence attached*

**Figure 5.0** — EIGHTEEN QUESTIONS, THIRTY-ONE DAYS: A bank reads an answer for exactness, not for willingness.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- Has a bank ever asked your company for documents before opening an account?
- Who in your company keeps the certificates and the ownership chart?
- How long did the last such request take to answer?

A bank that is asked to handle payments for a company it does not know sends a questionnaire, and the questionnaire is not a form to be filled in approximately. Each question has a correct answer and a document behind it.

The commonest reason for delay is not reluctance but imprecision: an answer that is broadly true, such as “we export to Europe, Asia and Africa”, where the question asked for the five largest counterparties by value in the last financial year.

The second commonest is an answer with no evidence named. “The company is owned by two families” is a statement; “the ownership chart at annexe 2, supported by the trade register extract at annexe 3” is an answer.

A question that does not apply must be answered rather than left blank, and answered with a reason: not applicable, because the company holds no accounts outside Portugal. A blank is read as an omission and returns the whole file.

Aurora opens an account with a bank in Rotterdam for its northern European trade, and the questionnaire runs to eighteen questions. Four are returned for imprecision and the account takes thirty-one days instead of ten.

- An answer repeats the shape of the question and then stops. Asked for the five largest counterparties by value in the last financial year, you give five names with values and the year, and nothing about the other forty.

## Unit 6 · One Message for Forty Markets

*CEFR B2 · Grammar: one text for many readers: conditional and segmented sentences · AI: one message segmented for forty markets without becoming forty messages*

**Figure 6.0** — ONE PRICE INCREASE, FORTY DIFFERENT TRUTHS: A circular is one text with named exceptions, not forty letters.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- How many markets does your company write to at once?
- What in your last circular was untrue in one market?
- Who signs off a circular before it goes out?

A company announcing something to all its markets at once faces a choice between forty letters, which nobody can keep consistent, and one letter, which will be untrue somewhere.

The working answer is one source text with named variants: a core message that holds everywhere, and a small number of sentences that change by condition rather than by country.

Segmenting by condition is what makes it manageable. “Where delivery is quoted ex works, the increase applies from 1 April; where delivery is quoted delivered, it applies from 1 May” covers forty markets in two clauses and needs no list of names.

A few statements cannot be made conditional, because they would be false or unlawful somewhere, and for those the only safe instrument is suppression: the paragraph is simply not sent to the markets where it does not hold.

Aurora raises prices by four per cent and writes to forty markets from one source text with three conditional sentences and one suppressed paragraph. Thirty-nine letters are right, and the fortieth is the one nobody checked.

### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: one text for many readers: conditional and segmented sentences**

- The conditional segment is the main tool: where, if and unless carry the variation inside one sentence, so that every reader finds his own case and ignores the rest.
- Write the condition first and the consequence second, because a reader scanning for his own case reads the opening of each sentence: where delivery is quoted delivered, the increase applies from 1 May.
- Keep the conditions mutually exclusive and jointly complete, since a reader who matches two clauses writes to ask which applies, and a reader who matches none assumes the letter is not for him.
- Unless carries exceptions economically: prices are quoted in euros unless otherwise agreed in writing. One word covers every market with a dollar contract without naming any of them.

A trade journal exists for its readers, who are engineers and buyers, and not for the companies that write to it. An editor therefore reads an announcement as raw material and keeps only what his readers can use.

What he keeps is the news: what the company has done, when, where, for whom, at what scale, and what it means for somebody buying this class of equipment. What he deletes is every adjective that the company awarded itself.

The register is news register and it is simpler than company prose: the active voice, the past tense for what has happened, and a named human source for anything that is an opinion rather than a fact.

An announcement that arrives already in that form is printed nearly as written, which is why the first paragraph should contain the whole story: company, action, object, place, date and scale, in about thirty words.

### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: news register for the trade press: the active voice and a named source**

- News register puts the actor first and the action next: Aurora Instruments launched the AX-40 analyser in Porto on 3 March. Not “the AX-40 was launched”, which hides who did it and reads as a company announcing itself.
- Facts are stated flatly in the past tense and opinions are quoted and attributed: “The instrument halves calibration time,” said Dr Anna Lindqvist, head of Aurora’s export desk. The claim belongs to her, and she must be willing to defend it.
- Figures carry their basis: four units were delivered to laboratories in January; calibration time fell from forty minutes to nineteen in Aurora’s own tests. “Our own tests” is three words and the difference between news and advertising.
- Where the company declines to say something, say that too, in the same register: Aurora did not disclose the development cost. An editor respects a stated refusal and distrusts a silence he discovers himself.

## Unit 8 · Writing That Will Be Machine-Translated

*CEFR B2 · Grammar: writing for translation: one clause, one subject, one verb · AI: a text written so that a translation engine cannot break it*

**Figure 8.0** — YOUR READER WILL NOT READ YOUR WORDS: Most commercial English is now read after passing through a translation engine.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- Do your customers read your letters in English or in translation?
- Which sentence in your last letter would a machine translate badly?
- Does your company use one word for one thing?

A commercial letter written in English today is frequently read in another language, because the reader pastes it into a translation engine before his coffee is cold, and nobody tells the writer this has happened.

A translation engine is good at plain sentences and bad at everything else, so the writer’s duty has changed: not to write more simply than his reader can manage, but to write in a way that survives being processed.

What breaks is predictable. Figurative expressions, phrasal verbs, long chains of nouns, pronouns whose antecedent is two sentences away, ellipsis, irony, and any sentence carrying three clauses and two negatives.

What survives is equally predictable: one clause, one subject, one verb; a term used consistently; a measurement written out; a date given in full; and an acronym expanded the first time it appears.

Aurora’s delivery clause reads “we will not hold off shipment pending your confirmation unless the goods have not cleared”. Translated into three languages and back, it means three different things, two of which would cost money.

**Grammar: writing for translation: one clause, one subject, one verb**

- The rule is one clause, one subject, one verb. “We shall ship the goods on 4 March” translates into every language without loss, and a sentence that needs two commas usually needs two sentences.
- Replace a phrasal verb with its plain equivalent: hold off becomes delay, bring forward becomes advance, look into becomes examine. A particle is where most engines lose the meaning, and a reader cannot tell that they have.
- Make every pronoun unnecessary. Repeat the noun even where repetition is inelegant, because “the analyser” cannot be mistaken and “it” can: the analyser ships with the calibration kit, and the analyser is tested before despatch.
- Use one negative at most, and write dates, measurements and acronyms in full. Never write 4/3/26, which is two dates, and never write kg where kilograms will travel.

**Written to survive a process the writer never sees**

**Figure 8.5** — Three habits that hold and three that break, in every language pair.

A text written so that a translation engine cannot break it is not a text written for machines; it is a text written for the reader on the other side of one, which is most of your readers.

**Grammar: handover register: as at, currently, outstanding, to be actioned by**

- The handover register fixes everything to a date and a person. The balance outstanding as at 31 March is nine thousand one hundred and forty euros; two claims are outstanding; the sample is to be actioned by the new correspondent before 10 April.

## Unit 10 · What No System Can Do for a Company’s Word

*CEFR B2 · Grammar: summary, judgement and what cannot be delegated · AI: what no system can do for a company’s written word*

**Figure 10.0** — SIX BOOKS, AND ONE SENTENCE THAT IS STILL YOURS: The last unit: what remains a person’s, in a trade conducted entirely in writing.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- What in your own job could not be delegated to anybody, person or system?
- Who reads your most important letters before they leave?
- When did your company last admit an error in writing?

Six books of this course have described work that a system does well: sorting, matching, checking, drafting, extracting and translating. The honest conclusion is that a great deal of commercial correspondence is now assisted, and nothing in that is shameful.

What has not moved is accountability. A letter commits a company, and a company is answerable through people, which means that somebody must read a letter before it leaves and be willing to be asked about it afterwards.

The distinction to hold on to is between a summary and a judgement. A summary says what the documents contain, and can be delegated to anything that reads accurately. A judgement says what should be done about it, and carries a name.

Four things in this trade cannot be delegated: a promise, a refusal, an admission of error, and the decision to send. Everything else in a letter may be drafted, checked and improved by any means available.

Aurora now drafts with assistance at every desk, and its last rule is one line long: no letter leaves this company that nobody has read. In three years that rule has stopped eleven letters, and two of the eleven would have been expensive.

### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: summary, judgement and what cannot be delegated**

- Summary and judgement are kept apart by their verbs. A summary reports: the documents show, the file contains, three of the four conditions are satisfied. A judgement decides: in my judgement, I recommend, we should, I accept.
- A judgement is written in the first person, because it belongs to somebody: I recommend that we pay this claim in full. “It is recommended” is a judgement hiding, and a hiding judgement is worth nothing to the person who must act.
- An admission of error takes the plainest form the language has, with no subordinate clause and no reason: the error was ours. Anything added to that sentence weakens it, including the explanation the writer is longing to give.
- A refusal is equally plain and equally owned: we shall not supply this market this year, and the decision is mine. The courtesies belong in the sentences around it, never inside it.

**Everything delegable, delegated; the rest named**

**Figure 10.5** — Three things to delegate without hesitation and three that carry a name.

What no system can do for a company’s written word is be answerable for it, and every other question in this course has turned out to be a version of that one.
