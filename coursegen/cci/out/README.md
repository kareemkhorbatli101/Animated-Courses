# English for Commercial Correspondence — International Edition

**LEDGER · ENGLISH FOR COMMERCIAL CORRESPONDENCE**
*The letters a company is judged by, in everybody’s second language*

Six illustrated coursebooks, A2.1 to B2.2, for people who write between a seller in one
country and a buyer in another — where English is usually nobody’s first language. The
series teaches the letters a business is judged by across borders: enquiries and
quotations in a second language, orders and confirmations across time zones, shipping
documents and documentary credits, claims and currencies, agency agreements and which law
applies, tenders, trade-press announcements and a company speaking to forty markets at
once. Every unit teaches one point of grammar, twenty terms of commercial vocabulary, and
one thing a system can do for a correspondent — together with the part of that work no
system can be given.

## What makes this the International Edition

The spine of the series is one fact: between a seller in one country and a buyer in
another, English is usually nobody’s first language, and almost every avoidable loss in
the trade comes from something that was clear to the writer. So the books teach, in order:
name order and address formats across markets; two measurement systems; which currency you
quote in and who carries the rate; working weeks and public holidays abroad; time zones and
when an answer can be expected; writing to a buyer whose English is better read than
spoken; bank charges and which side pays them; four transport modes and the documents each
market demands; which law, which court and which language a contract is in; a bilingual
contract and which text prevails; force majeure along a chain of suppliers; confidentiality
against a licensing requirement when a drawing is attached; one message segmented for forty
markets; and writing that will be machine-translated before it is read.

## The twelve parts of every unit

1. **Warm Up and Read** — a hero figure, three questions and a five-paragraph reading
2. **Vocabulary** — twenty terms, a figure of six, and two exercise types
3. **Grammar and Dialogues** — the rule, a loose-and-precise figure, nine gaps, six choices, two dialogues of twelve lines
4. **Speaking** — three paired tasks
5. **Extended Reading** — six paragraphs on the unit's AI capability, with a data figure
6. **Guided Writing** — a six-line tasking written out in full, as a specification card
7. **Working with AI** — what to say to a system, and the boundary of what it cannot know
8. **The Decision Room** — a case with an exhibit, three voices, five analytical steps and a recorded outcome
9. **The Brief Lab** — a six-line instruction, what came back, six diagnosed faults and a rewrite
10. **The Money Conversation** — the spoken round that happens when writing has failed: a disputed discount, a rejected document, a claim out of time, an unauthorised disclosure, a record somebody wants changed, a bid that was never opened
11. **Six-Skill Review** — reading, listening, speaking, writing, vocabulary and grammar
12. **Can-Do and Foundations** — five can-do statements and four rules to keep

## The world of the books

Invented firms in real trading cities: Aurora Instruments (Porto), Meridian Trading
(Rotterdam), Kiyomizu Instruments (Osaka), Hanseatic Logistics (Hamburg), Altiplano Coffee,
Sunda Rubber, Kestrel Components. Eleven recurring people on both sides of the deal — an
export correspondent in Porto, a documentary credits officer in Singapore, a forwarder in
Hamburg, a customs broker in Santos, a credit controller, an agent in Casablanca, a
purchasing manager in Osaka who is the buyer, a chamber of commerce, a distributor in
Rotterdam, an export manager and the head of the export desk. The ports and markets are the
real ones — Lisbon, Antwerp, Felixstowe, Gdańsk, Istanbul, Mumbai, Shanghai, Ningbo, Busan,
Surabaya, Santos, Valparaíso, Medellín, Casablanca, Durban, Mombasa, Houston, Veracruz —
and so are the documents: Incoterms with a named place, bills of lading and air waybills,
certificates of origin, documentary credits, agency agreements, tender dossiers, compliance
questionnaires.

## How to use the books

Each book is ten units of about five thousand words, which is a term's work at two hours a
week. A unit can be taught in sequence or on its own; the Brief Lab and the Decision Room
work as stand-alone workshop material for people already in the trade. Every exercise has
an answer in the key at the back of its own book.
