# English for the Oil and Gas Industries — Syrian Edition

Six coursebooks, sixty units, 380,660 words, 918 figures, 1,220 printed pages.

## What this course is

A course for the people who run, maintain and account for oil and gas
operations in Syria and who have to do it in English: process and panel
operators, shift supervisors, HSE officers, production and commissioning
engineers, maintenance and turnaround planners, contracts and procurement
staff, operations superintendents, and the managers who answer to a ministry,
a joint-venture partner, a board and the people who live beside the plant.

It is a Syrian edition because the work it describes is restart work. Fields
and plants are coming back after years of damage and deferred maintenance;
international operators, service companies and EPC contractors are returning;
and a Syrian operator and a contractor's engineer now stand at the same valve
holding two different procedures, in a second language, under schedule
pressure.

## The spine

**On a plant the words are part of the equipment.** A permit is not paperwork
about the work; it is the authority for the work. A handover is not a
courtesy; it is the transfer of custody of a hazard. A reading without its
unit is not a small error; it is a wrong number in a safe place.

And because the whole system rests on one authority that anybody may use and
nobody may be punished for using, the series signature is **Stop the Job**:
the sentence that halts work, said upwards, said to a client, said under
schedule pressure, by somebody who may turn out to have been wrong. Every one
of the sixty units ends with it.

## Four standing rules, held across all sixty units

1. **No engineering instruction.** The course teaches the language of the
   work, never how to do the work. No unit tells anybody how to isolate, test,
   commission or repair anything.

2. **The authority to stop the job is never undercut.** Every unit ends with a
   repair in which somebody stops work, or refuses to proceed, or declines to
   sign. The person who stops may turn out to have been wrong, and is still
   right to have stopped.

3. **No politics, no parties, no armed actors, no narration of conflict.**
   Where a unit needs an authority it is a ministry or directorate acting in
   its ordinary administrative capacity, and every operational decision is
   attributed to a named member of staff.

4. **Law and procedure are always attributed** — to local law, to the
   operator's own written system, to the joint operating agreement or to the
   contract. No requirement is presented as a fact about any jurisdiction. All
   volumes, counts, costs and percentages are illustrative.

## The two ladders

**The AI ladder.** Sixty capabilities, one per unit, each taught with an
explicit boundary line, running from *a hazard reported in one sentence* to
*what nothing can write for an operator's judgement*. The last unit makes the
division explicit: a page arranges what was written down; it cannot reach what
nobody wrote down, and it cannot be the person asked in two years why the job
was not stopped.

**The precision-fault ladder.** The eight faults — unscoped set, unbounded
quantity, unstated criterion, undefined done, ambiguous referent, missing
condition, unstated exception, hidden assumption — introduced progressively
across the levels and diagnosed in sixty Brief Labs, each on a real six-line
draft from the industry: a permit, a handover note, a spill notification, an
investigation report, a board paper, a notice to a village.

## The world

Invented companies and facilities: Mazyad Petroleum Company, Qasioun Energy,
Meridian Field Services, Rowen Engineering, Sarout Drilling Company, the
Marhan Refinery, the Tal Jurn Gas Plant, the Wadi Sahm field, the Arnab field,
the Harra Pumping Station, the Mirjan Terminal, the Petroleum Directorate.

Real Syrian cities appear as places: Damascus, Homs, Baniyas, Tartus, Latakia,
Deir ez-Zor, Al-Hasakah, Raqqa, Aleppo, Hama, Qamishli, Palmyra.

No real company, operator, contractor, government body or international
organisation is named anywhere in the series.
