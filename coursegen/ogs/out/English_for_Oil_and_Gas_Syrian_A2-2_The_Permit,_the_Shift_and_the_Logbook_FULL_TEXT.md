### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A permit read back as what you may and may not do**

**Figure 1.5** — One tag, two hours, two lists and one name.

A permit read back as what you may and may not do turns a page of boxes into two short lists. A man can hold two lists in his head.

Give the system the permit as it was signed and the tag numbers of the equipment in that area. Add the preconditions the written system sets.

Ask it to write the permitted work as verbs. Open, remove, drain. A verb is a thing a man either did or did not do.

Ask it to write the not-permitted list from what the permit leaves out. Lifting is not permitted because the permit says open.

Ask it to put the tag number at the top and the hours beside it. A permit for the wrong tag is the easiest mistake on a plant.

What it must not do is widen the permitted list. Only the person who signed the permit can add a verb to it.

> **DATA POINT · What a permit carries** — Many operators require a permit to name the equipment by tag, the hours it is valid and the work it allows. What a permit must carry is set by the operator’s own written system.  
> Source: general practice overviews, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · A permit for each piece of equipment** — Many operators issue a separate permit for each item of equipment and each kind of work. How work is divided between permits is set by the operator’s own written system.  
> Source: general practice overviews, 2025–2026.

**A defective tool report with the tool identified exactly**

**Figure 6.5** — Six lines, and a tool nobody else can take off the rack.

A defective tool report with the tool identified exactly is the page that stops a fault travelling through three men before anybody writes it down.

Give the system what the man said about the tool, the rack it came from, the serial numbers in the store, and the inspection dates recorded against them.

*Language bank for draft two*
- Purpose: so that the person who must decide something reads the page on Friday rather than on Monday
- Scope: one unit and one week; it closes nothing and it decides nothing
- Inputs: the week’s logbook entries, the permits issued and closed, the open items carried forward from last week with the dates they were raised, last week’s totals, and the events reported more than once in the seven days
