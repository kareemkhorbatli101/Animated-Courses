# English for the Oil and Gas Industries — Syrian Edition
## Series plan

### The spine

**On a plant the words are part of the equipment.**

A permit is not paperwork about the work; it is the authority for the work. A
handover is not a courtesy; it is the transfer of custody of a hazard. A
reading without its unit is not a small error; it is a wrong number sitting in
a safe place, waiting. In most industries bad writing wastes time. In this one
it is a mechanism of harm, and it is the mechanism nobody is taught to
recognise because it does not look like a mechanism.

That is why the series signature is not a sentence said when you are certain.
It is **Stop the Job**: the sentence that halts work, said upwards, said to a
client, said under schedule pressure, by somebody who may turn out to have been
wrong — and who must not be punished either way.

### Why a Syrian edition

The work these books describe is restart work. Fields and plants are coming
back after years of damage and deferred maintenance. International operators,
service companies and engineering contractors are returning. A Syrian operator
with twenty years on a unit and a contractor's commissioning engineer now stand
at the same valve holding two different procedures, in a second language, under
schedule pressure — and the senior knowledge in that pair is frequently the
Syrian one, while the document is frequently in English.

The language of that meeting is the language this course teaches. It is not the
English of a textbook plant. It is the English of a plant that has been
repaired, of drawings that no longer match, of a procedure written for
equipment that was replaced with something else, and of a workforce that knows
things no document records.

### Four standing rules, held across all sixty units

1. **No engineering instruction.** No pressure, temperature, flow rate, torque
   figure, chemical dose, well design, electrical rating or procedure step is
   given as an instruction anywhere in the series. Every technical decision in
   every scenario is attributed to a named competent person. The books teach
   the sentence, never the setting.

2. **Safety authority is never undercut.** Stop Work Authority is taught as
   something everybody holds and nobody is penalised for using. No unit
   resolves a safety concern by deference to rank, seniority, nationality or
   schedule, and no unit shows a person punished for stopping a job. Where a
   stop turns out to have been unnecessary, the unit says so and still endorses
   the stop.

3. **No politics, no parties, no armed actors, no narration of conflict.** The
   subject is the work: the permit, the handover, the reading, the report, the
   tender, the incident, the meeting. Where a unit needs an authority it is a
   ministry or a directorate acting in its ordinary administrative capacity.
   Damage to plant is described as damage and deferred maintenance, without
   cause or blame.

4. **Law, permit and procedure are always attributed** — to local law, to the
   contract, or to the operator's own written permit system and procedures. No
   requirement is presented as a fact about any jurisdiction. All volumes,
   rates, figures and sums are illustrative.

Services, Rowen Engineering, Sarout Drilling Company. Invented assets: the
Marhan Refinery, the Tal Jurn Gas Plant, the Wadi Sahm field, the Arnab field,

Real Syrian cities: Damascus, Homs, Baniyas, Tartus, Latakia, Deir ez-Zor,
Al-Hasakah, Raqqa, Aleppo, Hama, Qamishli, Palmyra. Real cities elsewhere for
the people who arrive and the places contracts are signed.

No real operator, service company, contractor, ministry, regulator or
international agency is named anywhere in the series.

### The two ladders

**The AI ladder.** Sixty capabilities, one per unit, each taught with an
explicit boundary line. It runs from *a map of who does what on site* to *what
nothing can write for an operator's judgement*. The last unit makes the
division explicit: a system arranges what is written down; it cannot smell gas,
cannot feel a pump running rough, cannot stop a job, and will not be the person
asked in two years why the work went ahead.

**The precision-fault ladder.** The eight faults — unscoped set, unbounded
quantity, unstated criterion, undefined done, ambiguous referent, missing
condition, unstated exception, hidden assumption — introduced progressively
across the levels and diagnosed in sixty Brief Labs, each on a real six-line
draft from the sector: a hazard report, a permit, a logbook entry, a method
statement, a shift handover, a first notification, a board paper.

### Calibration

Mean sentence length per book inside the band for its level: A2 8.5–12.5 words,
B1 11.5–16.5, B2 15.0–21.0. Sentence caps 26 / 34 / 44 words. 200 vocabulary
terms per book, 1,200 across the series, none repeated. No grammar point and no
AI capability repeats anywhere.
