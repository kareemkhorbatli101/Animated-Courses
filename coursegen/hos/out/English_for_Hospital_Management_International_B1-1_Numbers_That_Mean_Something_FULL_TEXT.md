Somebody says fourteen falls last month, and the room reacts. Fourteen out of what, over what period, counted how?

Fourteen falls among four hundred admissions is three and a half per cent; the same fourteen among eighty admissions is a different hospital altogether.

So every count you release carries three things: the number, the denominator, and the period. Fourteen falls per four hundred admissions in October.

### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: proportions and denominators: of, out of, per**

- Use out of for a plain proportion in speech, where both numbers are small enough to picture: fourteen out of four hundred admissions.
- Use per for a standardised rate, where the denominator is a round number chosen so that wards can be compared: thirty-five falls per thousand admissions.
- Use of for a share of a stated whole: fourteen of the four hundred admissions in October. The whole must have been named already.
- Attach the period to every one of them. Per thousand admissions in October is a figure; per thousand admissions is a claim without a date, and somebody will put a date on it for you.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A count turned into a proportion with its denominator**

**Figure 1.5** — Six lines, and the sixth is the one that keeps you out of trouble.

A count turned into a proportion with its denominator is the difference between a figure a committee can use and a figure a committee will misuse.

Give it the count, the candidate denominators available, the period, the case definition in force and any change of definition within the series.

Ask for the proportion to be expressed per a round denominator where wards are to be compared, and as a plain fraction where they are not.

Ask it to flag any denominator below about two hundred, since a percentage of eighty moves several points on a single event.

Ask for a line naming what the denominator cannot account for, such as the case mix of a rehabilitation ward where walking is the purpose of admission.

What it cannot do is decide whether a slip into a chair is a fall. That definition is a management decision, and once taken it must hold for years.

> **DATA POINT · Rates and denominators** — Health information guidance commonly recommends reporting counts with a denominator and a period, stating the case definition used, and avoiding comparison between populations of markedly different composition. Definitions are set nationally or by the organisation’s own policy.  
> Source: general health information guidance, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Small denominators** — Health information literature commonly reports that proportions calculated on small denominators are unstable between periods, and that undocumented changes of case definition are a frequent cause of apparent step changes in routine series.  
> Source: general health information literature, 2025–2026.

## Unit 2 · A Target and What It Distorts

*CEFR B1 · Grammar: purpose and side effect: in order to, with the result that · AI: a target examined for the behaviour it would produce*

**Figure 2.0** — EVERY TARGET IS ALSO AN INSTRUCTION.: One threshold, one cliff edge, and the behaviour nobody asked for out loud.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- Which target is quoted most often where you work?
- What do people do in the last week of a quarter?
- Who owns a target when nobody can affect it alone?

A target is set in order to improve something, with the result that people change what they do. Both halves of that sentence are certain; only the first is intended.

Set a four-hour threshold and watch what happens at three hours fifty. Patients are moved to decision areas, queues are reordered by clock rather than by need, and the figure improves.

So before you set a target, ask the question that nobody asks: what is the cheapest way somebody could meet this without doing the thing I actually want?

Then set a second measure that would catch that behaviour. A four-hour threshold with no measure of what happened after four hours is an invitation.

And give every target an owner who can affect it, a single definition, and a date on which it will be reviewed or retired.

**Grammar: purpose and side effect: in order to, with the result that**

### Part 8 · The Decision Room · The audit that was never re-audited

**STAGE 1 · The Brief**

A hospital’s internal audits of non-clinical processes produced recommendations that were implemented and recorded as complete, without re-measurement.
