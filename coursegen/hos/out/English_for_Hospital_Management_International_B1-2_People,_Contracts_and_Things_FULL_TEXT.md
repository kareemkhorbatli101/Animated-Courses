### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: duties and outcomes in a job description**

- Write duties as bare infinitives with objects: produce the weekly staffing report; chair the daily site meeting; authorise bank shifts up to two hundred pounds.
- Write outcomes as past participles with a measure: rotas published four days before the week begins; bank requests sent on the day a gap is identified.
- Write criteria with their evidence attached, using evidenced by: experience of holding a staffing budget, evidenced by the budget held and its annual value.
- Use is required rather than is desirable only where the work genuinely demands it, and be ready to say which duty requires it.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A job description drafted as duties, outcomes and evidence**

**Figure 1.5** — Six lines, and the last one widens the field at no cost.

A job description drafted as duties, outcomes and evidence produces a document a candidate can answer and a manager can later judge against.

Give it the work as it is actually done, the previous description, the grading framework, the financial authority of the post and its reporting line.

Ask for every duty as a verb with an object, and for every aspiration to be rewritten as an outcome carrying a figure or a date.

Ask it to attach evidence to each criterion in the specification, so that no criterion can be satisfied by assertion alone.

Ask it to list every requirement that no listed duty justifies, which is how degree requirements and vague qualities leave a description.

What it cannot do is decide whether nineteen duties are one job or two. That is a judgement about what a person can carry, and it belongs to the manager who will supervise them.

> **DATA POINT · Job descriptions** — Recruitment guidance commonly recommends duties expressed as actions, measurable outcomes, criteria capable of being evidenced, and the removal of requirements not justified by the work. Grading and equality duties are set by local law and the organisation’s own policy.  
> Source: general recruitment practice guidance, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Recruitment requirements** — Recruitment literature commonly reports that criteria lacking a stated evidence basis are applied inconsistently between panels, and that qualification requirements not derived from the duties reduce applicant numbers without improving appointments.  
> Source: general recruitment practice literature, 2025–2026.

### Part 8 · The Decision Room · The requirement with no duty behind it

**STAGE 1 · The Brief**

A hospital required a degree for a group of administrative posts. The requirement had been copied between descriptions since 2014 and appeared in posts whose duties did not reference it.

Over two years, 46 posts were advertised with the requirement. 9 were readvertised for lack of suitable applicants. A review of the duties found that 3 of the 46 contained a duty that required degree-level study.

The requirement was removed where no duty justified it. Applications per post rose from a mean of 7 to 19, readvertisements fell to 1, and twelve-month retention was unchanged.

**Write the rule, in four lines.**

- Say how a duty is written.
- Say what an outcome must carry.
- Say what a criterion must state.

*Language bank for the decision*
- Every duty in a job description is written as an action with an object, and no duty is expressed as a quality or an aspiration.

**Grammar: questions that produce evidence: tell me about a time when**

- Open with tell me about a time when and a concrete situation drawn from the duties: tell me about a time when you had to publish a rota with a gap in it.

**Grammar: receiving a grievance: acknowledgement, scope, timescale**

- Acknowledge in the first person and the present: I acknowledge receipt of your grievance dated the fourth of November, received on the sixth.

**Grammar: obligations in a contract: shall, is entitled to, is liable for**

- Shall creates an obligation. The trust shall provide access between six and eight. Read every shall whose subject is your own organisation before anything else.

**Grammar: a specification: must, should, may, and how it will be tested**

- Must is mandatory and exclusionary: the trolley must be height adjustable between fifty and ninety centimetres. Every must narrows the field, so each one needs a reason you could state aloud.
