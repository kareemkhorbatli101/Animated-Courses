### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: arrangements in a rota: is on, is off, is covering**

- Use is on for somebody working their own shift. Omar is on from eight on Tuesday. Present, not future, because the rota is an arrangement.
- Use is off for somebody not working. Grace is off on Wednesday. Say the day, because off on its own means nothing.
- Use is covering for somebody working another person’s shift, and name the person. Viktor is covering Nadia’s late shift on Friday.
- Use there is nobody for a gap, with the day, the shift and the hour. There is nobody on the late shift on Wednesday, from eight.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A rota checked for gaps before it is published**

**Figure 1.5** — Six lines, and the second one is the only one people remember.

A rota checked for gaps before it is published turns twenty-one lines into a short list of the ones with nobody against them.

Give it the draft rota, the leave already agreed, the grades each shift needs and anybody who must not be asked twice.

Ask for every gap as a day, a shift, a grade and an hour, because a gap without an hour cannot be filled.

Ask it to flag anybody put on two late shifts in a row, and anybody covering a shift they did not agree to.

Ask for the publication date against the check, so that a gap found on Thursday is never published on Sunday.

What it cannot do is decide whether to ask Nadia a second time this month. That is a judgement about a person, and it is yours.

> **DATA POINT · Unfilled shifts** — Workforce literature commonly reports that unfilled shifts identified several days ahead are filled by substantive or bank staff far more often than those identified within twenty-four hours, where agency cover is more frequently used.

### Part 8 · The Decision Room · The rota that was published late

**STAGE 1 · The Brief**

A hospital published its nursing rotas on the Sunday before the week began. Managers said they were waiting until the rota was complete.

Over three months, 41 shifts were unfilled at publication. Of those, 34 were known to be unfilled at least four days earlier. Agency cover was used on 29 of the 41.

Rotas were then published on the Thursday, with unfilled shifts marked as gaps and sent out as requests the same day. Unfilled shifts at the start of the week fell to 11, and agency use to 6.

**STAGE 2 · The Evidence**

**Figure 1.8** — The same gaps, found at the same time, asked four days earlier.

Publishing later did not fill the shifts. It only moved the asking from Thursday, when eight people could answer, to Sunday, when one could.

**Grammar: conditions for agreeing leave: if, provided that, as long as**

### Part 8 · The Decision Room · The leave nobody answered

**STAGE 1 · The Brief**

A hospital answered leave requests when the rota was written, often six weeks after the request arrived. Managers said they could not answer until they knew the rota.

Over six months, 212 requests were made. The mean wait for an answer was 31 days. 46 requests were refused, and 9 of those refusals offered an alternative date. 23 people had already booked travel before they were answered.

Answers were then required within five working days, with a condition or an alternative in every case. The mean wait fell to 4 days, refusals to 18, and bookings made before an answer to 3.

**Model**

Purpose: so that everybody I manage has twenty minutes a month in which something is agreed and dated.
