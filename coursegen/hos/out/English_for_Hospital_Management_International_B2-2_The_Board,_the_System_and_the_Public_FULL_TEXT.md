### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- How long is the longest board paper you have read?

The board meets for three hours and has nine papers. Yours is the fourth, and it will have about eleven minutes, of which the first two decide everything.

So the resolution goes in the first line, written as the minute should read. The board is asked to approve the closure of the Harbourview inpatient unit with effect from the thirtieth of June.

Assurance comes second, not instead. Three sentences on what has been done to test the recommendation: who has been consulted, what the clinical opinion is and who has checked the figures.

Every risk carries an owner. A risk belonging to a theme belongs to nobody, and a board that is given four themes and no names has been given a mood rather than a register.

And write the whole paper as though it will be published, because in a public board it will be. The sentence you would not want quoted is the sentence a journalist will quote, and writing it differently is not concealment but precision.

### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: a board paper: recommendation first, assurance second**

- Write the resolution as the minute should read, in the passive with the board as subject: the board is asked to approve the closure of the Harbourview inpatient unit with effect from the thirtieth of June.
- Write assurance in the present perfect, which shows completed work: the clinical director has reviewed the proposal; the finance director has verified the figures; the unions have been consulted on three occasions.
- Write each risk as a noun phrase with an owner: staff transfer risk, owned by the people director. Never as a theme, such as workforce risks, which no individual can be asked about.
- Write every sentence as if it will be read aloud at a public meeting, which in a public board is not a hypothesis but a description of what happens next.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A board paper drafted with the recommendation in the first line**

**Figure 1.5** — Six lines on two pages, and the last one changes every other.

A board paper drafted with the recommendation in the first line gives nine people eleven minutes of useful attention instead of four pages of context.

Give it the decision sought, the verification already obtained, the risks with their owners, the consultation including what was said against, and the evidence that belongs in appendices.

Ask for the resolution in the first line in the words the minute should use, so that the board can adopt it without redrafting.

Ask it to convert every themed risk into a risk with an owner, since a risk nobody owns cannot be reported on or closed.

Ask for what was said against the recommendation to appear in the body rather than only in an appendix, because a board that discovers opposition later stops trusting the papers.

What it cannot do is decide whether to seek a decision or invite a note. Asking to note is safer for the author and worse for the organisation, and choosing the harder route is a matter of character.

> **DATA POINT · Board papers** — Corporate governance guidance commonly recommends stating the decision sought at the head of a paper, limiting length, identifying risk owners, confirming financial verification, and drafting on the assumption of publication where the board meets in public. Standing orders are set by the organisation.  
> Source: general corporate governance guidance, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Papers and decisions** — Governance literature commonly reports that papers inviting a board to note consequential matters are a frequent source of later disagreement about what was approved, and that risks recorded without named owners are less often closed.  
> Source: general corporate governance literature, 2025–2026.

### Part 8 · The Decision Room · The direction of travel

**STAGE 1 · The Brief**

A hospital board received papers of which the majority invited it to note rather than to decide, on the view that noting preserved the board’s freedom.

Over three years, 214 papers were received. 161 invited the board to note. In 7 cases a matter noted by the board was subsequently treated by the executive as approved, including a commitment of about four million pounds. In 2 of those 7 the board minuted its disagreement a year later. A review found risks recorded without an owner in 38 papers, of which 31 remained open.

Standing orders were changed so that a paper seeking a decision states the resolution in its first line and every risk names an owner. Papers inviting a note fell to 71 of 198, and risks without owners to 2.

### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: chairing to a decision: the question, the options, the resolution**

- Write items as direct questions with shall we: shall we close the Harbourview unit on the thirtieth of June? The form makes the answer either yes or no, which is what a committee is for.
- Allocate time out loud with has: this item has twelve minutes. The verb is in the present and it is a statement rather than a hope.
- Bring somebody in by name and in the perfect: Dr Eze, you have not spoken on this. The perfect is a fact rather than a reproach.
- Read the resolution back with the resolution is that: the resolution is that the committee approves closure with effect from the thirtieth of June. Then ask for the vote, and not before.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A committee agenda built around decisions rather than updates**

**Figure 2.5** — Six lines per agenda, and the fifth one gives you back forty minutes.

A committee agenda built around decisions rather than updates turns nine speeches into four decisions and sends everybody away ten minutes early.

Give it the draft agenda, the papers, what each item is actually asking the committee to settle, the declared interests, and the actions outstanding from the last meeting.

Ask it to rewrite every item as a direct question and to move anything with no question in it into the pack as a document.

Ask for a draft resolution against every decision item, written in the words a minute would use, so the chair can read it aloud before the vote.

Ask for a time allocation against each item that adds to less than the meeting, and for the items most likely to be deferred to be placed first rather than last.

What it cannot do is notice that the person who runs the service has not spoken. Looking round a room and seeing who is silent is the whole art of chairing, and it cannot be delegated to an agenda.

> **DATA POINT · Chairing** — Governance guidance commonly recommends agendas framed as decisions, stated time allocations, declarations of interest at the outset, summary of the proposition before any vote, and contemporaneous minuting of resolutions. Procedures are set by the organisation’s standing orders.  
> Source: general corporate governance guidance, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Meeting effectiveness** — Governance literature commonly reports that agendas framed as questions with stated time allocations produce more recorded decisions per meeting, and that decisions taken without a stated resolution are more often disputed afterwards.  
> Source: general corporate governance literature, 2025–2026.

## Unit 3 · A Strategy That Says What You Will Stop

*CEFR B2 · Grammar: strategy as subtraction: we shall stop, we shall not start · AI: a strategy drafted as what will stop as well as what will start*

**Figure 3.0** — A STRATEGY WITH ELEVEN PRIORITIES HAS NONE.: Eleven priorities, three real ones, and four things that have to stop.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- What does your organisation’s strategy say it will stop?
- What has it actually stopped?
- Who would object if it stopped something?

The draft strategy has eleven priorities, four enabling themes and a diagram with nine boxes, and it does not say that the organisation will stop doing anything at all.

A strategy that only adds is a wish list, because the staff who must deliver it are already fully stretched and the money is already committed.

**A strategy drafted as what will stop as well as what will start**

**Figure 3.5** — Six lines, and the second one is the half that costs something.

A strategy drafted as what will stop as well as what will start produces a document that can be delivered by people who are already fully committed.

Give it the draft priorities, the current activity with the hours and money behind each, the dependencies between them, and the groups affected by any reduction.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A consultation document drafted with alternatives that are real**

**Figure 7.5** — Six lines, and the second one is where consultations are lost.

A consultation document drafted with alternatives that are real produces responses an organisation can act on rather than evenings of wasted effort.

Give it the decision already taken with its date, the options the organisation would genuinely accept, the cost of every option including doing nothing, the groups most affected, and the statutory period.

Ask it to remove every option the organisation would refuse, and to state the cost of each removed option with the date of the decision refusing it.

Ask for the proposal to be phrased as a question, because a proposal phrased as a conclusion produces objection rather than information.

Ask for the closing date and the decision date in the same sentence, so that nobody can take the decision before the responses are in.

What it cannot do is go and find the people who will not respond. The group most affected by a closure is usually the group least likely to write a letter, and somebody has to go to them.

> **DATA POINT · Consultation** — Consultation guidance commonly recommends presenting options the decision-maker would be prepared to adopt, stating what has already been decided, costing a do-nothing comparator, allowing an adequate period, and reporting reasons for rejecting suggestions. Statutory duties are set by law.  
> Source: general public consultation guidance, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Consultation practice** — Consultation literature commonly reports that options the decision-maker would not accept reduce confidence in the whole exercise, that decisions taken before a consultation closes are frequently successfully challenged, and that groups most affected respond least.

**A five-year plan drafted in conditionals with its assumptions named**

**Figure 8.5** — Six lines, and the second one is the page a successor will need.

A five-year plan drafted in conditionals with its assumptions named produces a document a successor can audit rather than inherit.

Give it the current establishment, the leaving and joining rates, the external numbers with their sources, the lead time for each role, and the decisions that cannot be deferred.

Ask for every projection as a conditional, with the conditions stated in the same sentence rather than in an appendix.

Ask it to separate roles with lead times over two years from the rest, because those are the only decisions a five-year plan genuinely has to take in its first year.

What it cannot do is decide what to do if two assumptions fail at once. That is judgement under uncertainty, and the plan exists to make it possible rather than to replace it.

Everything in these six books can be drafted by something other than you. The rota check, the leave answer, the business case, the board paper, the consultation, the five-year plan: all of them can arrive on your desk already written.

### Part 8 · The Decision Room · The eleven signatures

**STAGE 1 · The Brief**
