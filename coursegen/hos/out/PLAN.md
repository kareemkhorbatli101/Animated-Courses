# English for Hospital Management — International Edition

## The spine

A hospital runs on sentences that say **who will do what, by when, and what
will not be done at all**. The third of those is the one that is almost never
written, and it is the one that decides what actually happens at two in the
morning.

Managers who work in English without having grown up in it carry a double load:
the operational judgement, and the sentence that has to carry it to a ward, a
supplier, a committee, a regulator or a journalist. This series teaches the
sentence.

The failures the books are built around are all failures of language with an
operational cost:

- a bed state nobody could act on;
- a request for staff with no number, grade or hour in it;
- a decision taken in a corridor and recorded nowhere;
- a target that bought one figure by wrecking another;
- a paper that asked for a discussion rather than a decision;
- a specification whose one loose word cost four months;
- a no that was never actually said, so everybody assumed yes.

## Calibration

CEFR level is measured, not asserted. Mean sentence length, sentence cap and
word count per unit are checked against bands for A2, B1 and B2, unit by unit,
before any book is built.

## The series signature

Part 10 of every unit is **The Unwelcome Number**: the moment a manager has to
put a figure or a fact in front of somebody who does not want it, and stay in
the room. A ladder, three turns, six holds, a redline and six questions —
sixty times, escalating from a ward sister who disputes a bed figure in Book 1
to a board that does not want the workforce projection in Book 6.

## The editorial line

This series teaches **management communication and management writing**.

- No clinical practice, clinical guidance or treatment appears anywhere: no
  dose, no regimen, no diagnostic rule. Every clinical decision in these pages
  is attributed to a named clinician who owns it.
- No legal, financial, employment or regulatory advice is given. Every unit
  touching employment process, procurement, disclosure, consultation or a
  regulator's powers says that local law and the organisation's own policy
  govern it, and that the manager's job is to use the route rather than invent
  one.
- No vendor, product, platform, living official or political claim appears.
- Hospitals, clinics, suppliers and committees are invented; cities are real.
  All figures are illustrative and belong to invented organisations.

## What a manager is never allowed to do in these books

Four rules hold across all sixty units, and the Brief Labs and Decision Rooms
exist mostly to teach them:

1. **Never give a clinical instruction.** A manager asks a clinician for a
   decision and records whose it was.
2. **Never present a number without its denominator** and the period it covers.
3. **Never leave a decision unowned.** Every action has a name and a date, and
   a risk nobody has accepted by name has been accepted by whoever was on duty.
4. **Never say the thing that is easier than the true thing** to a ward, a
   family, a committee, a regulator or a journalist.
