# English for Hospital Management — International Edition

to say who will do what, by when, and what will not be done at all.

## What is in each unit

Twelve parts, identical in every one of the sixty units:

1. **Hero spread** — the unit's claim in one sentence, with a figure.
2. **Warm-up** — three questions about the reader's own hospital.
3. **Reading** — five paragraphs, with a sourced data point.
4. **Comprehension** — six open questions, six true/false, a gap-fill, a choice
   exercise and a four-way sorting task.
5. **Vocabulary** — twenty terms with glosses, a figure, and two exercises.
6. **Grammar** — the unit's point, four rules, a loose/precise comparison figure,
   a gap-fill and six discriminations.
7. **Dialogues** — two, the second deliberately unresolved.
8. **Speaking** — three paired tasks.
9. **The AI capability** — what a drafting tool can assemble, what it must not
   supply, a model six-line tasking, and the boundary.
10. **The Unwelcome Number** — the series signature: a scene in which a manager
    has to say a figure, a cost or a refusal that the room does not want, with a
    ladder, three turns, six holds and a redline.
11. **The Decision Room** — a clinic with figures, three voices, five analytical
    steps, a four-line rule to write, and a measured outcome.
12. **Brief Lab** — a real six-line draft, diagnosed against exactly three fault
    types, then rewritten; plus review, four foundations and a closing line.

## The spine of the series

Four rules run through all 371,461 words:

- never give a clinical instruction;
- never present a number without its denominator and its period;
- never leave a decision unowned;
- never say the thing that is easier than the true thing.

## The editorial line

This series teaches **management communication and writing, not clinical
practice and not law**. There is no dose, no regimen and no diagnostic rule
anywhere in it. Every clinical consequence is attributed to a named clinician.
Every employment, procurement, disclosure, consultation and regulatory matter is
attributed to local law and to the organisation's own policy, which the books say
govern it rather than describing what it requires.

Hospitals, trusts, suppliers and committees are invented. Cities are real. All
data points are described as general guidance or general literature of 2025–2026,
without attributing figures to named studies.
