# English for Management with AI — International Edition
## Build record

Six books, sixty units, one invented world. Series one of a field-by-field
franchise: the harness that produced it is parameterised by field, so the next
series (medicine, law, logistics, education) reuses the machinery and replaces
the field content.

---

## 1. What the series is for

The reader is one of two people, and often both at once.

- **Somebody setting up a business fast**, who needs the language to direct
  systems precisely and the technical understanding to know what to direct them
  to do.
- **A manager who does not yet understand what these systems can do**, or
  doubts it, or has no vocabulary for it — and who therefore cannot evaluate
  what is being proposed to them.

Language is the enabler and the book teaches it. But the book is also an
honest, substantial account of how these systems actually help a business: where
the leverage is, what a falling unit cost does to a business model, what to
build and what to buy, where bias enters, what must never be delegated. The
technical thread is taught almost as hard as the language, because a
non-native-speaking manager who has the words but not the understanding is no
better placed than before.

Everything is invented. No real company, product, model or platform is named
anywhere in 355,713 words, and two automated gates enforce it. The reason is
liability and shelf life: a named tool dates a book in eighteen months, and a
named company invites a letter.

## 3. The three locked ladders

Every unit is the intersection of three sequences, and each one explains the
other two. This is the structural claim of the series.

1. **An AI capability** — sixty of them, in order of abstraction. Book 1 is
   what the thing does; Book 6 is accountability, dependence and limits.
2. **A grammar point** — sixty, following the CEFR progression of the earlier
   series, unchanged. Talking to a system is about precise language, so the
   grammar syllabus is not decoration.
3. **A precision fault** — eight recurring faults in instructions, each
   repaired by a specific grammar point. The Brief Lab diagnoses three per unit.

The eight faults and the grammar that repairs each:

| Fault | Repaired with |
|---|---|
| unbounded quantity | quantifiers and exact measures |
| unscoped set | defining relative clauses and articles |
| unstated criterion | comparatives and superlatives with a stated basis |
| undefined done | modality of requirement, purpose and result |
| ambiguous referent | pronoun reference, cohesion and substitution |
| missing condition | conditionals; unless, provided that, failing that |
| unstated exception | concession: although, except, even if |
| hidden assumption | articles for given and new information |

The full sixty-row table is in `ALIGNMENT.md`.

## 4. The eleven parts of a unit

Each unit runs to about 5,400 words and fourteen figures.

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| 1 | Warm-up reading, five kinds of comprehension |
| 2 | Twenty key terms, gap and matching practice |
| 3 | The grammar point, a loose/precise contrast, two dialogues |
| 4 | Speaking tasks, including a spoken specification |
| 5 | Extended reading with a sourced data point |
| 6 | Guided writing from a model specification |
| 7 | **Working with AI** — one capability, taught with its boundary |
| 8 | **Decision Clinic** — a five-stage thinking scaffold |
| 9 | **The Brief Lab** — write it, watch it fail, name the fault, repair it |
| 10 | Six-skill review |
| 11 | Can-Do check |

Parts 7, 8 and 9 are new to this series. Part 9 is the one that makes the
series teachable rather than merely informative: the reader sees a real brief,
sees what came back, sees the gap named in the eight-fault vocabulary, and
rewrites it against a six-line scaffold (Purpose, Scope, Inputs, Criteria,
Done, Fallback). Every lab ends with what draft one cost.

## 5. What makes it hard to copy

Four editorial commitments, each of which is enforced by a gate rather than by
good intentions.

**Every capability is taught with its boundary.** Part 7 of every unit ends
with a "what this does not do" box, and gate 21 fails the build if any of the
sixty is missing or vacuous. A book that only says what works is a brochure.

**The sceptics win often enough to be worth reading.** Across sixty Decision
Clinics, the manager or founder turns out to be wrong 24 times and the sceptic
right 18 times, and twenty-four dialogues end on an objection nobody answers.
Gate 22 enforces floors on all three. A reader who doubts the premise has
somebody to identify with.

**No forecasts.** Gate 17 rejects any sentence of the form "within two years
systems will be able to". Capability claims tied to a date are what date a book.

**The model answers are held to the standard the book teaches.** Gate 15 scans
every model specification in the series for the vague words the book itself
forbids — *soon*, *better*, *several*, *improve* — and fails the build if one
appears without a number attached.

## 9. Reusing the harness for the next field

The generator is parameterised. To produce *English for Medicine with AI*,
*English for Law with AI* or any other field:

1. Replace `series.py` — the field name, the six book titles, the cast, the
   sixty-row AI ladder and the sixty-row grammar ladder.
2. Keep the eight faults, the six-line scaffold and all twenty-five gates
   unchanged; they are field-independent.
3. Write sixty unit specifications against the same dictionary shape.
4. Start the new field's vocabulary ledger empty, so the new series may reuse
   general terms while never repeating within itself.

The render layer, the figure archetypes, the answer-key generation, the register
measurement and the twenty-five gates are reused without modification.
