### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A referral drafted around the one question it asks**

**Figure 1.5** — Six lines, and the first one decides whether the rest is read.

A referral drafted around the one question it asks is one page read in ninety seconds by somebody with thirty-nine others.

Give the system the question you want answered, the urgency as a number of days, the images and notes you are enclosing and what you have already tried.

Ask it to refuse a second question. A referral with two questions in it will be answered on whichever one is easier, and the other will come back to you in a letter.

Ask it to convert every adjective of urgency into days, because urgent means a different interval in every department and a referral marked urgent is triaged by guesswork.

Ask it to list the attachments by name and date, so that a missing one is obvious to the person opening the envelope rather than three weeks later.

What it must not do is ask the clinical question for you. Which question matters here is a judgement, and a referral that asks the wrong question clearly is worse than one that asks the right question badly.

> **DATA POINT · Referrals** — Referral guidance commonly recommends that a referral state the specific question asked, the clinical information and images relied on, and the urgency with a timescale, and that the referrer retain responsibility until the referral is accepted. Referral pathways and their requirements are set locally.  
> Source: general professional guidance, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Triage** — Referral literature commonly reports that referrals stating a specific question and a timescale are triaged faster and returned less often, and that referrals relying on images not enclosed are a frequent cause of delay.  
> Source: general professional literature, 2025–2026.

### Part 10 · The Sentence You Must Say Before You Start — The Sentence You Must Say Before You Start · I am asking you to decide, not to advise

Grace wants Dr Rahman to make the decision rather than to advise on it. Asking for advice is easier and leaves the decision with Grace.

Asking somebody to decide is asking them to take responsibility for a patient they have not met.

**Figure 1.12** — One question, two verbs, and the difference between them.

*Work in pairs. Student B reads each line as it was said; Student A repairs it out loud.*

**Turn 1 · Grace:** I am asking you to decide whether to extract, not to advise me about it.

**Turn 2 · Aisha:** That is a bigger request. Most referrals ask for an opinion.

**Turn 3 · Grace:** Most referrals come back as an opinion I then have to act on with less information than you had.

*Repair moves you can use without correcting anybody*
- If I ask for advice, I shall have an opinion and still have the decision.
- If I ask you to decide, I am asking you to see the patient and own it.
- You may refuse, and then I shall have an opinion and know where I stand.
- What I will not do is write advise and mean decide, which is how patients wait twice.
- I will say what I have tried, so that your decision is not my March suggestion.
- And I will say in days when I need it, because you cannot triage a worry.

> **WHAT DOES NOT SURVIVE TRANSLATION** — Never write advise when you mean decide. Ask for the thing you actually want.

## Unit 2 · A Treatment Plan in Writing

*CEFR B1 · Grammar: a treatment plan: stages, dates, costs and review points · AI: a treatment plan drafted as stages, dates, costs and reviews*

**Figure 2.0** — A PLAN IN ONE BLOCK IS A PLAN THE PATIENT CANNOT GET OUT OF.: Four rows on a page, and the last column is where somebody can stop.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- How do your patients receive a treatment plan?
- How many stages can they stop after?
- Which stage commits the next one?

A course of treatment written as one paragraph is a single decision the patient takes once, at the beginning, with the least information they will ever have.

Written as four numbered stages with four dates and four costs, it becomes four decisions, three of which the patient takes knowing more than they knew at the first one.

So each stage carries a date, a cost and a review point. The review point is the sentence that says: we shall look at this before going on to stage three.

And you mark the stages somebody can stop after. Most of them. Then you mark the one that commits the next one, because that is the honest exception and it has to be said in advance.

Then the patient takes a copy away. A plan that exists only in the notes is a plan the patient has not agreed to; they have agreed to a conversation about one.

**A treatment plan drafted as stages, dates, costs and reviews**

**Figure 2.5** — Six columns, four rows, and the last column keeps it honest.

A treatment plan drafted as stages, dates, costs and reviews is one page with four rows that a patient can hold in their hand.

Give the system the stages in order, the dates you can offer, the cost of each stage, the review points and which stages commit the next one.

Ask it to put a cost against each stage rather than one figure at the bottom, because four figures can be weighed and one figure can only be accepted or refused.

What it must not do is sequence the treatment. Which stage comes first and which commits which is a clinical judgement, and a plan in the wrong order is worse than no plan at all.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A report drafted with its scope and its limits stated**

**Figure 4.5** — Six paragraphs, and the second one is the one that survives.

A report drafted with its scope and its limits stated is a document that will outlive the question it was written to answer.

Give the system the instructions you received, the date and nature of your examination, the records you have seen, the facts you found and the inferences you draw.

Ask it to put the limits in the second paragraph rather than the last, because a reader who has reached a conclusion by page three will not revise it on page four.

Ask it to mark every inference as an inference. An inference that reads as a fact is the sentence somebody who was not in the room will quote back at you.

Ask it to leave unanswered every question you were not asked, and to decline in one line every question outside your competence.

What it must not do is offer an opinion. What your findings mean is your judgement, and a report that supplies one you did not reach has put your name to somebody else’s conclusion.

> Source: general professional guidance, 2025–2026.

A request for records is the one piece of correspondence where being helpful quickly is the most expensive thing you can do.

Three questions first. What exactly is being asked for. For whom. And under what authority, named rather than assumed.

A specialist’s reply does three things at once, and most of the harm comes from reading it as though it did one.

## Unit 8 · A Claim and Its Supporting Evidence

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- Who makes claims in your practice?
- What evidence is on the file when a claim goes out?
- What happens when a claim is refused?

A claim is a statement about something that happened in the past, made to somebody who was not there.

So it has three parts. What was done. On what date. And what on the file supports it.

The third part is the only one that matters when the claim is checked, and it is written long before anybody thinks about claiming.

A note written on the day is evidence. A note written three weeks later, from memory, is a reconstruction, and it is worth very much less.

Which means the honest answer is sometimes that a claim cannot be made, because what the record shows is thinner than what you remember doing.

**A claim drafted as what was done, when, and what supports it**

**Figure 8.5** — Three columns, and a fourth that stops a claim going out.

A claim drafted as what was done, when, and what supports it is the half-hour that keeps an audit uneventful.

Give the system the note for the appointment, the documents on the file with their dates, and the scheme’s own list of codes.

Ask it to set out each claimable item as three columns: what was done with the site named, the date it was done, and the document on the file that supports it.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A record audit written up as method, finding and change**

**Figure 9.5** — Five parts, in this order, or it is a morning of reading.

A record audit written up as method, finding and change is the document that lets somebody repeat your work next year and get a comparable number.

Give the system the standard you wrote down beforehand, how the sample was chosen, and the counts you took against each item of the standard.

Ask it to write the method so that a stranger could draw the same kind of sample, because an audit nobody can repeat cannot show an improvement.
