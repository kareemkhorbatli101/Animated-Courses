# English for Dentists — International Edition

A six-book illustrated coursebook series for dentists and dental teams working
in English, from CEFR A2.1 to B2.2. Sixty units, each with its own twelve-part
structure, 918 original figures and a vocabulary of 1,200 distinct terms, no
term taught twice across the series.

## What the series is for

It teaches the English a dentist actually writes and says: seating a patient,
taking a history, agreeing a stop signal, explaining options so that a choice
is possible, writing a referral that asks one question, answering a complaint
about your own work, telling a patient that the wrong tooth has been removed,
raising a concern about a colleague, putting a case to the partners, and
answering a regulator within the exact scope of the question.

It is not a clinical text. There is no diagnosis, no technique, no named
material, instrument setting, drug, dose or radiographic interpretation
anywhere in the six books. Every clinical decision in every scenario is
attributed to a named clinician. All sums of money are illustrative. Consent,
records, complaints, raising concerns, inspection and fitness to practise are
attributed throughout to local law and to the practice's own written procedures.

## The twelve parts of every unit

1. **Hero spread** — one proposition, drawn.
2. **Warm-up** — three questions before any new language.
3. **Read** — five sentences of argument, within the level's sentence cap.
4. **Comprehension** — five exercise types: short answer, true/false, gap-fill, multiple choice, and a four-way classification task.
5. **Vocabulary** — twenty terms with glosses, a six-word figure, and two exercise types.
6. **Grammar** — a rule in four parts, a loose/precise comparison figure, a gap-fill and a discrimination exercise.
7. **Two dialogues** — twelve turns each, the second ending on an unresolved objection.
8. **Speaking** — three paired tasks.
9. **The AI unit** — what the system does, what it must not do, a six-line written specification, and a boundary line.
10. **The Sentence You Must Say Before You Start** — the series signature: a scenario in which the professionally correct sentence is the uncomfortable one, with a ladder, three turns, six holds, a redline and a score sheet.
11. **Brief Lab** — a real six-line draft, diagnosed against a ladder of eight specification faults, then rewritten.
12. **The Decision Room** — a case with an exhibit, three voices, a five-step analysis and a four-line rule to write; in every one of the sixty, a manager or principal is wrong on the figures and a sceptic turns out to be right.

Each unit closes with a review spread and a Foundations list.

## Calibration

Levels are measured rather than asserted. Every unit is built against a mean
sentence length band and a hard sentence cap for its level, and a book is only
accepted when its measured mean falls inside the band:

| Level | Mean sentence length | Sentence cap | Words per unit |
|-------|---------------------|--------------|----------------|
| A2 | 8.5 – 12.5 | 26 | 3,500 – 4,700 |
| B1 | 11.5 – 16.5 | 34 | 4,000 – 5,200 |
| B2 | 15.0 – 21.0 | 44 | 4,400 – 5,600 |

## Quality gates

Every book passes twenty-three automated gates and the series passes two more.
Among them: no vocabulary term repeated anywhere in 1,200; every gap-fill
answer unique within its exercise; every figure numbered and captioned; no
product, model or platform named anywhere; no forecast about future machine
capability; a boundary line on every one of the sixty AI units; every Brief Lab
containing exactly three fault types twice each, all permitted at that level;
and, across the sixty labs, each of the eight specification faults appearing in
at least twelve of them.
