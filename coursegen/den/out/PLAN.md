# English for Dentists — International Edition · the spine

## Why this series exists

A dentist works on somebody who is awake, who is paying, and who cannot answer
once the work has started. That single fact reorganises everything about the
language of the job.

Everything that matters has to be said **before** the first instrument touches
the tooth: what is wrong, what you propose, what it will cost, what could go
wrong, what happens if nothing is done at all, and how the patient makes you
stop. After that the patient has a raised hand and nothing else.

So the series is built around sentences that must be said in advance and
written down afterwards:

- a consent the patient could repeat back a week later;
- an estimate that states what it does not cover;
- a laboratory instruction that cannot be read two ways by somebody at eight
  in the evening with nobody to ask;
- a referral that asks one question;
- a record written on the day rather than after the complaint arrives;
- a refusal that leaves the patient a route;
- and, at the end, the judgement no checklist contains.

## The series signature

Part 10 of every unit is **The Sentence You Must Say Before You Start**: the
sentence that is awkward, necessary, and only possible while the patient can
still answer. One per unit, sixty in the series.

## Four standing rules

1. **No clinical content.** No diagnosis, no technique, no named material, no
   instrument setting, no drug, no dose and no radiographic interpretation
   appears anywhere in the six books. Every clinical decision in every
   scenario is attributed to a named clinician who owns it.
2. **No money as law.** Every sum is illustrative and belongs to an invented
   practice. Fee scales, insurance rules and what a patient is entitled to are
   attributed throughout to local arrangements, never to a rule this book has
   set.
3. **Never take a consent the patient could not describe afterwards.** A
   signature is evidence of a conversation, not a substitute for one.
4. **Law and procedure are local.** Consent, records and their retention,
   complaints, raising concerns, inspection and fitness to practise are
   attributed to local law and the practice's own written procedures. The
   dentist's job is to use the route, not to invent one.
