# English for College Students — International Edition
## Series plan

### The spine

**The rules you are graded on are rarely the rules you were told.**

A student arriving on an English-medium degree is handed a timetable, a reading
list and a module handbook. What they are not handed is the hidden curriculum:
how much disagreement a seminar expects, where their own words end and a
source's begin, how long an email to a lecturer should be before it stops being
read, which university office stops a problem and which one only records it,
and what the instruction verb in an essay question is actually asking for.

They are assessed on all of it from week one.

This course teaches that hidden curriculum explicitly, in English, at six
levels, because nobody else is going to. It is not a study-skills book with
vocabulary attached, and it is not an English book with campus scenery. Every
unit is one professional move a student has to make — asking, disagreeing,
citing, conceding, appealing, defending — taught as language and as judgement at
the same time.

### Why an international edition

The institution is invented and so is the town it stands in. Calderbrook
University, the Marden Institute of Technology, Alverrey College and Thornfield
Polytechnic are not anywhere, which means a reader in Lagos, Lima, Almaty or
Lisbon can put their own university in the place of this one without first
having to translate somebody else's system.

The students' home cities are real, because that part is. Eleven people from
eleven places sit in the same seminar room, and English is a first language for
none of them. The series never treats that as a deficit to be corrected. It
treats it as the ordinary condition of higher education, and it teaches the
conventions that are genuinely institutional — citation, hedging, concession,
appeal — rather than the ones that are only somebody's accent.

### The twelve-part unit

Every one of the sixty units has the same shape: hero statement, warm-up,
reading, six kinds of comprehension, twenty vocabulary terms, a grammar point
with a loose-and-precise contrast, two dialogues of twelve lines, three
speaking tasks, the AI capability with its boundary, the series signature, a
five-stage Decision Room, and a Brief Lab.

### The series signature

Part 10 of every unit is **the sentence you must say when you do not
understand.** It is the hardest sentence in the building, and the cost of not
saying it compounds: a lecture not understood in week two is a module failed in
week eleven, and an essay question not understood is a misconduct allegation.
The series teaches it in escalating conditions — to a lecturer in a corridor, to
a seminar group, to a supervisor, to a panel, to an examiner, and finally to
yourself about your own work.

### The two ladders

**The AI ladder.** Sixty capabilities, one per unit, each taught with an
explicit boundary line. It runs from *a list of who to ask for what, checked
against your own timetable* to *what no tool can do for your own thinking*. The
series is honest about the thing every student is actually doing, and draws the
line where the institution draws it: a tool can split a question, map a
literature or check a reference list; it cannot hold the position you will be
asked to defend in a viva, and it cannot be the author.

**The precision-fault ladder.** The eight faults — unscoped set, unbounded
quantity, unstated criterion, undefined done, ambiguous referent, missing
condition, unstated exception, hidden assumption — introduced progressively and
diagnosed in sixty Brief Labs, each on a real six-line draft from student life:
an email to a lecturer, a group work plan, an extension request, a mark query,
a mitigating-circumstances claim, a research proposal.

### Four standing rules

1. **No real institution, board, academic, vendor or product.** The university,
   the college, the language centre, the scholarship trust, the library and the
   examination board are all invented. Home cities are real; nobody's national
   system is described as the system.
2. **No deficit framing.** Nobody in these books is a weak speaker who needs
   fixing. The problems are institutional conventions nobody stated, and they
   are solved by learning the convention and saying so out loud.
3. **No procedure presented as universal.** Every data point panel attributes a
   rule to the institution's own regulations, the module handbook or the
   examination board's published procedure. No deadline, penalty, fee or appeal
   ground is stated as a fact about any real country's higher education system.
   All figures are illustrative.
4. **Academic honesty is taught, not policed.** The books teach what counts as
   your own work and why, including the AI question, by showing the student what
   they will be asked to defend and when. No unit instructs a reader in how to
   avoid detection.

### Calibration

Mean sentence length per unit inside the band for its level: A2 8.5–12.5 words,
B1 11.5–16.5, B2 15.0–21.0. Sentence caps 26 / 34 / 44 words, over-cap held low.
200 vocabulary terms per book, 1,200 across the series, none repeated. No
grammar point and no AI capability repeats across the sixty units.
