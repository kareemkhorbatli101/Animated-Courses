### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- What is the verb in your current essay question?
- What does it require?
- How do you know?

At this level the instruction verb carries most of the marks, and the three commonest ones require three entirely different pieces of work.

Evaluate means weigh something against stated criteria and reach a judgement. A judgement is required; a balanced survey is not an evaluation.

Account for means explain why something happened, which requires causes and not a description of what happened.

To what extent means say how far a claim holds, which requires a quantity: largely, in two of three cases, only where the record survives.

And about half of all questions at this level have a second part hidden after a comma, which is where the remaining marks usually sit.

### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: instruction verbs: evaluate, account for, to what extent**

- Evaluate takes an object and a stated basis. Evaluate the two methods against the reliability criteria in the brief.
- Account for takes a phenomenon and produces causes. Account for the rise, and rank the causes you give.
- To what extent takes a claim and produces a quantity. To what extent does the comparison hold? It holds for two of the three districts.
- A second part begins after a comma and usually begins with and. And consider what follows for the appendix figures.

## Unit 2 · A Plan Before a Draft

*CEFR B1.2 · Grammar: an essay plan: the claim, the sections, the evidence · AI: an essay plan with one claim per section*

**Figure 2.0** — A PLAN OF HEADINGS IS A TABLE OF CONTENTS. A PLAN OF CLAIMS IS AN ARGUMENT.: Diego planned seven headings and wrote three thousand words that never made a single claim.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- What does your current plan look like?
- Is there a claim in it?
- How many sections?

A plan made of headings tells you what each section is about. A plan made of claims tells you what each section argues.

So write each section as one sentence that could be denied. Not "the two methods" but "the second method cannot produce a comparable figure".

If you cannot write that sentence for a section, the section does not exist yet, and writing it will produce three hundred words of description.

Then order the sections by what the reader needs rather than by what you found first, and put the word count beside each one.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- How do you paraphrase?
- Do you look at the original while you write?
- What would a marker see?

Most students learn that a paraphrase means changing the words, and that is the half of the rule that causes the trouble.

A sentence whose every word has been swapped but whose clauses arrive in the same order, with the same joins, is still that author’s sentence.

A defensible paraphrase is written with the original closed. You read it, you understand it, you shut the book, and you write what you now know.

Then you open the original and check two things: that no phrase of three or more words survives, and that your sentence is not shaped like theirs.

And you attribute it anyway, because the idea is still theirs however you have written it, which is the part that has nothing to do with words at all.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- What is the strongest objection to your current claim?
- Is it in the essay?
- Can you answer it?

Every essay at this level is expected to contain the case against itself, and most contain a version of it chosen for being easy.

The test is simple. The counter-argument you include should be the one you would least like an examiner to raise.

Write it at its strongest, in the words somebody who held it would use, and with its own evidence attached.

Then do one of two things, and say which you are doing. Answer it, or concede that it stands and narrow your claim accordingly.

Conceding is not losing. A claim narrowed to the ground it can actually hold is worth more than a wide claim with a hole in the middle of it.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A counter-argument drafted at its strongest**

**Figure 5.5** — Four tests, and the first one disqualifies most counter-arguments.

Markers find the straw man in about forty seconds, because the weak version is always phrased in the same way and the strong one never is.

Give the system your claim, the evidence you have, and the sources that take a different view with their page numbers.

**A query about a mark drafted against the marking criteria**

Almost every mark query fails for the same reason, which is that it quotes the student’s experience instead of the institution’s criteria.

Give the system the mark, the published band descriptors, the marker’s comments, and the relevant parts of your own essay.

Ask it to find any place where a comment and the band awarded appear not to match, because that is the strongest query there is.

Ask it to check the arithmetic: section marks against the total, and the total against the band boundaries.
