## Unit 1 · Setting Out Options So a Manager Can Choose

*CEFR B1 · Grammar: options a manager can choose between: either, or, and the difference · AI: options laid out side by side with their differences*

**Figure 1.0** — A MANAGER GIVEN FOUR OPTIONS WILL CHOOSE NONE OF THEM.: Two options, one difference, and a recommendation with your name on it.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- How do you present a choice to your manager?
- How many options do you give her?
- Which one do you recommend?

A manager asked to choose between four options will ask for more information, which is a polite way of choosing none of them.

So give her two. If there are really four, you have already discarded two of them and you should say so.

Then say in one sentence what separates the two, because that sentence is the whole of her decision.

And say what each option does not fix. An option presented as solving everything is an option she will not believe.

Then recommend one, in your own name. A note with no recommendation is a note that comes back next month.

### Part 8 · The Decision Room · Four options and no decision

## Unit 2 · What Happens If Nothing Is Done

*CEFR B1 · Grammar: doing nothing: if you leave it, it may, it will probably · AI: the do-nothing option written out as a real option*

**Figure 2.0** — THE OPTION NOBODY COSTS IS THE ONE THAT HAPPENS.: Doing nothing is a decision. Price it like one.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- What is your organisation leaving undone this year?
- Who decided that?
- Is it written down anywhere?

Doing nothing is always available, always free at the moment of choosing, and almost never written down as an option.

So it happens by default, and in six months nobody can say who chose it.

Write it out properly. If you leave it, this may happen, and this will probably happen by about March.

Keep may and will probably apart. A thing that may happen once in a decade is not the same as a thing that will happen by spring.

And if somebody decides to accept the risk, write down who accepted it and when it will be looked at again.

**The do-nothing option written out as a real option**

**Figure 2.5** — Six lines. The third one is where most notes stop short.

The do-nothing option written out as a real option is the paragraph that stops an organisation drifting into a decision nobody took.
