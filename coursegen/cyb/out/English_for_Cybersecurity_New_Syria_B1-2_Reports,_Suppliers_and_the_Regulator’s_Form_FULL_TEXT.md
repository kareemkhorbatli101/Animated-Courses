### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**An escalation drafted around the one question it asks**

**Figure 1.5** — Six lines. Two questions in the first one ruins all six.

An escalation drafted around the one question it asks is the message that gets answered inside the hour instead of read the following Monday.

Give the system everything you know about the matter, the decision you need, who can take it, when the window closes, and what you will do if nothing comes back.

Ask it to reduce the matter to exactly one question phrased as a choice between two courses, because a question with no alternative in it reads as a request for agreement.

Ask it to name only the evidence needed to answer that question, since an escalation carrying everything you have signals that you have not yet decided what matters.

Ask it to state the hour you need an answer by together with the reason for that hour, because an urgency with no mechanism behind it is read as pressure rather than a deadline.

What it must not do is take the decision. The default on silence is yours to propose and theirs to overrule, and a draft that answers its own question is a report pretending to be a question.

> **DATA POINT · Escalation routes** — Many organisations set out who may be escalated to, in what form, and what happens where no answer is received inside a stated period. Escalation routes are set by the organisation’s own arrangements.  
> Source: general practice overviews, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Defaults on silence** — Many organisations require an escalation to state the action that will be taken where no answer is received, so that silence does not become an unrecorded decision. What is required is set by the organisation’s own arrangements.  
> Source: general practice overviews, 2025–2026.

### Part 8 · The Decision Room · Ninety escalations and the hour they were read

**STAGE 1 · The Brief**

A ministry examined 90 security escalations sent over two years and compared how each was written with how quickly it was answered.

Of the 90, 23 asked exactly one question. Those 23 were answered in a median of 40 minutes. The other 67 asked between two and six questions and were answered in a median of 19 hours, with 14 never answered at all.

**An access audit written up as method, finding and change**
