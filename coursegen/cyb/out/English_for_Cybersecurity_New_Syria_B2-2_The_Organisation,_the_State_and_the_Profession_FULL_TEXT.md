### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- What is the largest thing you have ever asked a board to fund?
- Where was the recommendation in your paper?

A business case is a document in which the author’s enthusiasm and the reader’s patience run in opposite directions, and the reader’s patience is the shorter of the two.

So put the recommendation in the first line, with its shape and its price. We recommend establishing a twelve-hour security operations centre at a first-year cost of about four hundred thousand, rising to six hundred thousand in the third year.

Then say what the thing would and would not do, because a board that is not told the limits will assume twenty-four-hour cover and will say so in a year when something happens at three in the morning.

Separate the benefit you can measure from the benefit you cannot, and label each as such, since a case that presents an unmeasurable benefit as a measured one is a case that will be audited against a number you invented.

And name what would be given up, because every organisation funds this out of something else and the paper that does not say what is a paper that has concealed its real cost.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A business case drafted with the recommendation in the first line**

**Figure 1.5** — Six lines. The sixth one almost never appears in a real case.

A business case drafted with the recommendation in the first line is the paper that is judged on what it asks for rather than abandoned on page three of its own enthusiasm.

Give the system the thing you are recommending, its cost in the first and third years, what it would and would not do, the benefits you can measure with their baselines, and what the organisation would give up to fund it.

Ask it to open with the recommendation and its price, because a board reads the recommendation and then goes looking for whichever part of the supporting case it doubts, in whatever order suits it.

Ask it to state what the thing would not do immediately afterwards, since a board that is not told the limits will remember having bought full cover and will say so in a year when something happens outside the hours you funded.

Ask it to give exactly one measurable benefit, with its baseline and the month you will report against it, and to label every other benefit as unmeasurable, which costs less than being found to have overstated one.

What it must not do is omit the sacrifice. Every organisation funds a capability of this kind out of something it is currently doing, and a case that does not name what stops has concealed the larger half of its cost.

> **DATA POINT · Business cases** — Many organisations require a business case to state the recommendation and its cost on the first page, with the benefits separated into those that can be measured and those that cannot. What is required is set by the organisation’s own arrangements.  
> Source: general practice overviews, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Stop conditions** — Many organisations ask a business case to state the circumstances in which the author would recommend stopping or reversing the investment. What is required is set by the organisation’s own arrangements.  
> Source: general practice overviews, 2025–2026.

**Model**

Scope: one business case for one capability; it claims no benefit it cannot either measure or label as unmeasurable. ### Part 8 · The Decision Room · Thirty-one cases and where the ask was

**Figure 2.0** — AN UPDATE IS NOT AN ITEM. AN ITEM HAS A QUESTION IN IT.: A committee that only receives updates has stopped being a committee.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- How many items on your last agenda contained a question?
- How many decisions were minuted?
- Which item has been carried over most often?

A committee that spends its time receiving updates is a committee that has quietly become an audience, and the person who allowed that to happen is the chair.

So build the agenda out of decisions. Every item carries a question in its first line, and any paper without one is circulated for information and not discussed at all.

Allot time to each item and protect the later items from the earlier ones, because the first item expands to fill whatever it is given and the last item is always the one that needed the committee most.

Close discussions explicitly and read the resolution aloud before anybody leaves, since a decision that everybody believes was taken and nobody can quote is a decision that will be taken again in March.

And draw out the member who has said nothing, particularly the one without a technical background, because the question they ask is usually the question the paper was written to avoid.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**An agenda built around decisions rather than updates**

**Figure 2.5** — Six lines. The fourth is the one a chair can control absolutely.

An agenda built around decisions rather than updates is the document that determines, before anybody arrives, whether the committee will decide anything at all.

Give the system the matters before the committee, which of them genuinely require a decision, the options available on each, and the time the committee actually has.

Ask it to require a question in the first line of every item, because an item with no question in it will produce a discussion, and a discussion is what a committee does instead of deciding.

Ask it to place the hardest item second rather than first or last, since the first item absorbs whatever time it is given and the last is decided by people who want to go home.

Ask it to separate papers circulated for information, which are not discussed at all, from items for decision, because a committee that debates its information papers has no time left for its decisions.

What it must not do is draft the resolution in advance. The words of a resolution are agreed in the room, read aloud before anybody leaves, and a resolution drafted beforehand turns a committee into an audience for a decision already taken.

> **DATA POINT · Committee agendas** — Many organisations require each agenda item to state the decision sought, and circulate information items separately without discussion. How a committee is run is set by its own terms of reference.  
> Source: general practice overviews, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Resolutions** — Many organisations require the words of a resolution to be read to the meeting and agreed before the item closes. What is required is set by the committee’s own terms of reference.  
> Source: general practice overviews, 2025–2026.

A water authority examined 11 security committee meetings over two years, comprising 94 agenda items, and recorded what each produced.

## Unit 3 · A Policy That Says What Will Not Be Permitted

*CEFR B2 · Grammar: a policy of refusal: will not be permitted, except where · AI: a policy drafted as what will not be permitted as well as what will*

**Figure 3.0** — A POLICY THAT FORBIDS NOTHING PERMITS EVERYTHING.: Write the refusal, then write who may lift it and for how long.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- What does your security policy forbid, in words you can quote?
- Who may grant an exception?
- How long does an exception last?

Most security policies describe aspirations, and an aspiration cannot be breached, which is why most security policies have never been breached by anybody.

So write what will not be permitted, in the plainest available words. Shared administrator accounts will not be permitted on any system holding payment or clinical records.

Then write the exception properly, with the person who may grant it and the period it lasts. Except where the chief information security officer grants an exception in writing, for a period not exceeding ninety days.

Find the workaround your refusal creates before your staff do, because a policy that makes legitimate work impossible produces an undocumented practice that is worse than what you forbade.

And keep it to one page in sentences people can quote, since a forty-page policy is a policy whose only reader is the person who wrote it and the auditor who finds it two years later.

**A. Comprehension.**
0. Why have most security policies never been breached?  — *because an aspiration cannot be breached*

**A joint problem stated so that neither body owns it alone**

**Figure 4.5** — Six lines. The sixth is the one that costs eleven days.

A joint problem stated so that neither body owns it alone is the opening exchange that keeps both organisations in a problem that neither can see by itself.

Give the system what your own records show, what you believe the other body can see, what you are prohibited from sharing, and which parts of the response each of you can actually carry out.

Ask it to state what each side can see as separate sentences, because a problem described in general terms invites the larger body to take it over entirely or to hand it back entirely, and both outcomes lose information.

Ask it to state at the outset what you may not share and why the restriction exists, since a restriction that emerges in the third exchange is read as reluctance while one stated first is a fact both of you can plan around.

Ask it to assign leadership on each part of the response in the first exchange, and to name explicitly any part that neither body is willing to lead on, because that part is the one that will be unattended for a fortnight.

## Unit 5 · A Regulator’s Question, Answered Exactly

*CEFR B2 · Grammar: answering a regulator within the exact scope of the question · AI: a regulator’s question answered within its exact scope*

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- When did you last answer a formal question in writing?
- Did you quote it back?
- What did you add that nobody had asked for?

A regulator’s question is drafted carefully, often by somebody who has read your previous answers, and it means precisely what it says and nothing wider.

So quote the question back in your own letter before you answer it, because an answer that does not restate the question can be read against a different question in a year, usually a wider one.

Then answer within its exact scope. If the question asks about the four systems named in its annexe, the answer concerns those four systems, and the other eighty-seven do not appear in the letter at all.

Answer a two-part question in two numbered parts, since a single flowing paragraph covering both parts will be read as having answered one of them and that one will not be the part you thought.

And where you believe the question is the wrong question, answer the question asked and say separately, in its own paragraph, what you think the question that would serve would have been.

A regulator’s question answered within its exact scope is the letter that answers one question rather than inviting four, which is the only arithmetic that matters in a regulatory correspondence.

Give the system the question in its own words, the systems and the period it names, the records that bear on exactly that, and any wider observation you wish to make.

Ask it to quote the question in full at the top of the answer, because a letter that does not restate the question can be read in a year against a wider question that was never asked.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A consultation document drafted with alternatives that are real**

**Figure 7.5** — Six lines. The third one is the one that costs courage.

A consultation document drafted with alternatives that are real is the exercise that finds out who is actually using a system, which is the only reliable way to discover it.

Give the system the proposal and its date, the alternatives you would genuinely accept, what has already been settled and by whom, and the groups you believe use the system.

Ask it to offer only alternatives you would accept, because a respondent who identifies a formality in your document stops answering consultations and takes four colleagues with them.

Ask it to state what is already settled and what remains open, since a consultation presented as open on a matter decided in March solicits afternoons of work that you will read and cannot act on.

Ask it to put the question that actually matters, which is not whether the respondent agrees but what they will do on the day after the system goes, because that answer is the one nobody in your organisation can supply.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- Does your organisation have a multi-year security plan?

A five-year security plan written in the indicative mood is a work of fiction, because nobody writing it knows what the third year will contain, and everybody reading it knows that.

So write it in conditionals and name the condition. If the funding holds at its current level, the second centre opens in year three; if it is reduced by a fifth, the second centre does not open at all.

Name the assumptions explicitly, in a list, because the assumption nobody wrote down is the one that fails and the failure then looks like incompetence rather than a change in the world.

Say what you would stop if the money were cut, and what you would do first if more arrived, since those two answers are the only part of a five-year plan that anybody ever actually uses.

And write it for your successor rather than for the board, because the board will read it once and she will work from it for three years without being able to ask you what you meant.

Every unit of this series has set out a thing a system can draft and a thing only a person can decide, and the division has been the same one each time.

A tool can arrange what you give it. It cannot know what your organisation will do with a figure, which of your colleagues is exhausted rather than careless, or whether a regulator’s letter is a direction in courteous clothing.

It cannot be accountable. Accountability is the state of being the person who will be asked, in two years, why a particular sentence was written, and no drafting system can occupy that position on your behalf.

So the division is not between the easy work and the hard work. It is between the work that can be arranged from what is written down and the work that depends on what nobody has written down.

And the second kind is almost always the work that matters. Declaring at four in the morning, refusing to restore at six, telling a woman that a fact about her cannot be unmade, and putting a figure to a board that will not like it.
