### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- How many tickets are in your queue this morning?
- Which is the oldest?
- When did anybody last touch it?

A queue looks like one list, and it is really three. Some tickets are open, some are closed, and some are waiting.

Waiting is the word that hides things, because a ticket can wait for a user, for a supplier, or for nobody at all.

So every waiting ticket needs two more words: what it is waiting for, and since when.

Then look for the tickets nobody has touched for a fortnight. There is always at least one.

And be honest about why. Some tickets are old because they are hard, and some are old because you do not want to open them.

## Unit 2 · An Asset List That Is Out of Date

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- Does your organisation have an asset list?
- When was it last updated?
- Who owns the machine under your desk?

An asset list is a list of every machine the organisation has. It is almost always wrong.

It is wrong in three ways, and all three matter. Things on the list that we no longer have.

Things we have that were never on the list. And things on the list that nobody owns.

The second kind is the dangerous one, because nobody patches a server nobody knows about.

So compare the list with what is actually on the network, and write the date you did it.
