### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A list checked for the names that should be on it**

**Figure 1.5** — Five fields. A record without the last two cannot be explained.

A list check is not reading a list. It is asking which names should be on it and are not.

Give the system the office file, the wall list, and the names people asked about at the desk this week.

Ask it to mark every difference between the two lists, and to say which list each name is missing from.

Ask it to flag names written two ways, since a spelling that does not match is the commonest cause of a family being told no.

Ask it to list every removal with no reason beside it. A removal nobody can explain is an error until somebody explains it.

What it must not do is decide who should be registered. It finds differences; the criteria and the decision belong to the project and the officer.

> **DATA POINT · Lists and their states** — Many organisations hold a registration list in which every record carries a status and a date, so that a removal can be explained afterwards. What is held is set by the organisation’s own written procedures.  
> Source: general practice overviews, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Removals and reasons** — Many organisations require a reason and a date for every removal from a registration list, and treat an unexplained removal as an error to be reviewed. What is required is set by the organisation’s own written procedures.  
> Source: general practice overviews, 2025–2026.

### Part 8 · The Decision Room · Three hundred and eleven, or two hundred and seventy-one

**STAGE 1 · The Brief**

An organisation compared the wall list and the office file at four distribution sites on one day.

The four wall lists held 1,106 names. The four office files held 1,019. Of the 87 differences, 61 were names in the office file and not on the wall. 26 were on the wall and not in the file.

The organisation then looked at the 26. Nineteen were the same family written with a second spelling, five were removals with no reason recorded, and two were households that had moved. In the four weeks before the comparison, 48 families had been told at a desk that they were not on the list. Of those 48, 31 were in the office file.

**A shortfall explained in the order people are standing in**

**Figure 2.5** — Five lines. The fourth is the one a queue remembers.

A shortfall is not bad news to be softened. It is four numbers and a date, said while people are still in order.

Give the system the opening stock, the stock now, the number of families waiting, and the position of the last family who can be served.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**An induction list written for a volunteer at a distribution point**

**Figure 7.5** — Five lines, read aloud in ten minutes before the gate opens.

An induction is not a policy. It is the ten minutes that decide whether a volunteer is useful or exposed.

Give the system the tasks a volunteer will do, the numbers they will see, the three things they must never do, and the role they ask.

Ask it to keep the prohibitions to three and to give a reason for each, since a limit with no reason behind it is heard as distrust.

Ask it to include the two sentences word for word, because a volunteer under pressure needs a sentence and not a principle.
