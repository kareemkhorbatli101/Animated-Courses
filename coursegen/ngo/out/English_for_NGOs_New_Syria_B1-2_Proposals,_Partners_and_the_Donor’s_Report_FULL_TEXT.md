A concept note is read in six minutes by somebody holding forty others. It must ask for one thing.

Write the problem as a measured condition. Of every hundred households in the three villages, thirty-one draw water more than a kilometre away.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A concept note drafted around the single change it buys**

**Figure 1.5** — Five lines on one page. Everything else is a full proposal.

A concept note is not a short proposal. It is the single change a project buys, priced.

Give the system the measured problem with its base and date, the one response that follows from it, the total cost, the number of households reached, and who will do the work.

Ask it to state the change in one clause: what will be different for which households, by when.

Ask it to divide the cost by the households reached and to print the division, since a funder comparing ten notes compares that figure first.

Ask it to flag any sentence that introduces a second problem or a second response, so that it can be moved to a separate file before the note is sent.

What it must not do is add what it thinks a funder wants to see. Gender, environment and sustainability clauses pasted into a note about four water points make it read as a template, and a template is the one thing a funder has seen forty times this month.

> **DATA POINT · Concept notes** — Many funders invite a short concept note before a full proposal and assess it on the clarity of a single problem, a single response and a cost per beneficiary. What is required is set by the funder’s own published guidance.

### Part 8 · The Decision Room · Forty notes and six minutes each

**STAGE 1 · The Brief**

A funder analysed 212 concept notes received over two rounds, against its own assessment decisions.

Of the 212, 79 proposed a single response to a single stated problem. The other 133 proposed two or more. Ninety-one notes printed a cost per household reached; 121 did not.

Invited to full proposal: 41 of the 79 single-response notes and 22 of the 133 multiple-response notes. Of the 91 notes printing a cost per household, 48 were invited; of the 121 that did not, 15. Assessors recorded a mean reading time of 5.8 minutes per note. In 34 cases an assessor recorded that the note appeared to be a template, and 2 of those 34 were invited. The funder also noted that 29 declined notes contained a second idea that would have been competitive on its own.

A results chain has four columns, not three. Activity, output, outcome, and the assumption between each pair.

An activity is what you do: build four water points. An output is what exists afterwards: four working water points.

An outcome is what changes for households: the time spent fetching water falls from fifty minutes to twelve.

Between each pair there is an assumption. Between output and outcome: that households will use the new points rather than the old spring.

Write every assumption as a sentence beginning with we assume that, and test the weakest one in the first month, not the last.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A results chain drafted with its assumptions written beside it**

**Figure 2.5** — Five columns. The fourth is the one that will be quoted in the review.

A results chain is a set of claims about cause. Every arrow in it is a sentence somebody has chosen not to write.

Give the system the activities with their quantities, what will exist afterwards, the change expected for households, and the data you can actually collect.

Ask it to write one assumption for every arrow, beginning with we assume that, and to mark the one it judges weakest.

Ask it to distinguish an output from an outcome by asking whether the statement would still be true if nobody used what was built.

Ask it to give every indicator a collector, a frequency and a cost, and to flag any indicator whose cost is not in the monitoring budget.

What it must not do is invent an outcome you have no way of observing. An outcome you cannot measure is a hope, and a hope in a results framework is a finding waiting two years to be written.

> **DATA POINT · Results chains** — Many funders require a results framework showing activities, outputs and outcomes with the assumptions between them, and an indicator for each level. What is required is set by the funder’s own published guidance.

### Part 8 · The Decision Room · Four working taps and nine minutes

**STAGE 1 · The Brief**

A funder reviewed 94 completed projects against the results frameworks submitted at proposal stage.

Of the 94, 36 had written an assumption beside every link in the chain. The other 58 had arrows with nothing beside them.

Projects meeting their outcome targets: 24 of 36 and 19 of 58. Where an outcome target was missed, the funder asked whether the cause had been foreseeable. In 31 of the 39 misses among the 58, the cause was an assumption that had not been written down. In 7 of the 12 misses among the 36, the failing assumption had been written and tested, and the project had reported the shortfall early. Projects reporting a shortfall before the mid-term review: 11 of 36 and 2 of 58. Thirteen frameworks contained an outcome indicator with no collection cost in the budget; 11 of those reported quarterly estimates rather than measurements.

### Part 3 · Grammar and Dialogues

**Grammar: a subaward: shall, should, may, and what to query**

- Use shall for a binding obligation, with the party named. The partner shall submit a financial report within fifteen days of each quarter end.
- Use should only where you accept that it is advisory. The partner should hold a community meeting before works begin.

- Query any clause containing applicable, appropriate, reasonable, timely or as required. Each of them postpones a dispute instead of preventing one.

**A donor report drafted with its scope and its limits stated**

**Figure 4.5** — Five lines in the first paragraph. Everything else is detail.

A donor report is the sentence your organisation will be quoted on for years, usually by somebody who read only its first paragraph.

Give the system the period, the sites visited, the sites not visited, every figure with its source, and every variance against the plan.

Ask it to open with what the report covers and what it does not, with the reason for each exclusion, and never to put a limit in an annex.

Ask it to attribute every figure to the person or organisation that collected it, and to mark as unverified anything nobody from your organisation has seen.

Ask it to put each shortfall on the first page with its figure, its cause and one sentence on what you would do differently, since a shortfall a funder finds costs many times one you report.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A data request to a partner drafted with its basis stated**

**Figure 6.5** — Five lines before the attachment. The third is the one usually missing.

A data request reaches somebody whose job is to protect the people in the spreadsheet. Write it so they can say yes without breaching anything.

Give the system the question you must answer, the fields available from the partner, the relevant clause of the agreement, and your organisation’s retention period.

Ask it to reduce the request to the smallest set that answers the question, and to list every field it has removed so that you can see what you nearly asked for.

Ask it to quote the permitting clause by number, since a request with no basis obliges the partner either to refuse a funder or to breach their own consent forms.

Ask it for a deletion date rather than a condition, because when no longer needed is a phrase that leaves four hundred names on a shared drive for four years.

What it must not do is ask for personal data to satisfy a template. A template written three years ago is not a lawful basis, and the person compiling the spreadsheet is the one who carries the risk.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A verification visit written up as method, finding and change**

**Figure 9.5** — Five parts, one week, one text shared with the partner.

A verification visit is only as good as the sentence describing how you chose what to look at.

Give the system the sampling rule you used, the size of the population, who drew the sample, what you examined, what you could not, and the counts you found.

Ask it to put the method before the findings, because a finding without a method is an impression and cannot be acted on or checked by anybody.

Ask it to express every finding as a count against a count rather than as an adjective, and to record the partner’s position beside any finding they dispute, in their own words.

Ask it to carry exactly one agreed change with a date and an owner, since a visit producing nine changes produces none.

What it must not do is resolve a dispute. A report that decides between your count and the partner’s explanation has stopped being a record and become a judgement, and the reader loses the ability to weigh it.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**An extension request drafted with what you are not asking for**

**Figure 10.5** — Five lines. The fourth is why it is approved in a week.

An extension request is a short letter that costs a funder an open file and some staff time. Make it cheap to approve.

Give the system the activities still outstanding with their quantities, the time each needs, the funder’s own period end, the cause of the delay, and what you are not asking for.

Ask it to put the period requested and the words no additional funds in the first line, because that is the sentence that decides which approval route the request takes.

Ask it to list what you are not asking for in full: no additional funds, no change to outputs, no change to reporting dates, no second request.

Ask it to classify the cause as inside or outside your control and to say so plainly, since a funder left to work it out will assume the answer that reflects worse on you.

What it must not do is request a period beyond the funder’s own period end. An extension nobody can grant wastes the only letter you get, and the honest alternative is to report the activity as not delivered.

> **DATA POINT · Extension requests** — Many funders distinguish a no-cost extension, which changes only the period, from a variation that changes funds or outputs, and require the request to state which is sought. What is required is set by the funding agreement.  
> Source: general practice overviews, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Period limits** — Many funders cannot extend a grant beyond the end of their own funding period, whatever the merits of a request. What is possible is set by the funder’s own arrangements and the agreement.
