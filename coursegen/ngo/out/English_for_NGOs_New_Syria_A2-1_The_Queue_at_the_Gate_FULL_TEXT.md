### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A list of who does what, checked against the day at the gate**

**Figure 1.5** — Three jobs. The fourth row is the one nobody wrote down.

A list of who does what, checked against the day at the gate, is the page that stops a family being told they are selected by somebody who does not decide.

Give the system the jobs your team does, the names of the people doing them today, and the things families actually asked about yesterday.

Ask it to keep registering, deciding and delivering as separate rows. The whole point of the list is that the person writing the record is not the person choosing.

Ask it to mark what a volunteer may and may not do, since a volunteer who answers a question about selection has made a decision the organisation did not authorise.

Ask it to name the duty officer for the day. A family who leaves knowing one name can come back and ask somebody rather than shouting at the gate.

What it must not do is decide who is selected. That belongs to the person the procedure names, on the criteria the procedure sets, and no list can move it.

> **DATA POINT · Volunteers** — Many organisations set out in writing which tasks a volunteer may carry out and which are reserved to staff. What a volunteer may do is set by the organisation’s own written procedures.

**Form questions rewritten so an interpreter can read them aloud**

**Figure 3.5** — Six lines. The fifth one stops thirty-one guesses.

Form questions rewritten so an interpreter can read them aloud are the difference between a record of answers and a record of explanations.

Give the system the questions as the form prints them, what each question is actually trying to find out. The words the interpreter uses when asked to explain them.

Ask it to write each question as how many for a number, since when for a date, or do you have for a thing. Those three forms can be read out without being explained.

Ask it to split any question with two parts into two questions, since a double question produces one answer and nobody can tell afterwards which part it answered.

Ask it to make unknown an allowed answer on the page. A box that cannot be left empty and cannot be marked unknown will be filled with a guess that reaches a report.

What it must not do is decide what the question is for. Only the office that uses the answer knows what it needs. A question rewritten to be readable but asking the wrong thing is worse than the original.
