# English for Doctors — International Edition

Six illustrated coursebooks, A2.1 to B2.2, for doctors who work in English with
patients, families and colleagues who are also working in a second language.

**367,065 words · 918 figures · 1,183 PDF pages · 60 units · 1,200 field terms**

## The books

1. **A2.1 · The Patient in Front of You** — identification, the first four questions of a history, pain in the patient's own words, duration, the story in order, what he has already taken, allergies, explaining a procedure, instructions he can repeat back, saying the consultation back.
2. **A2.2 · Ward, Notes and Team** — the ward round written up, documenting a patient, observations and what they mean, drug charts, asking a colleague to look, the shift's priorities, requesting an investigation, handing a ward over, speaking to a family, writing what you think.
3. **B1.1 · Handover, Referral and the Record** — handing a patient over, the referral letter, the urgent request, reading a report and acting on it, the discharge summary, writing to a family doctor, recording a telephone conversation, the account of an incident, correcting an entry, the Friday list.
4. **B1.2 · Explaining, Consent and the Family** — explaining a diagnosis without a technical word, risk in usable numbers, consent as a conversation, working with an interpreter, a patient who does not want to be told, a family that wants to decide for him, discharge instructions that are followed, a leaflet rewritten, recording a refusal, answering a complaint.
5. **B2.1 · When Things Go Wrong** — escalation that is heard, graded assertiveness, disagreeing with a senior colleague, disclosing an error, the incident report written the same shift, breaking bad news, what treatment can and cannot achieve, telling a family of a death, asking for a second opinion, writing about another team's record.
6. **B2.2 · The Doctor and the System** — the one-minute case, the written case report, research consent in plain language, transfer into a different health system, a report for a third party, a guideline made local, feedback to a junior, an audit that changes something, an application and interview, and what no system can do for a clinician's word.

## What is in every unit

A hero spread and can-do list; a reading passage with data panels; five kinds of
comprehension exercise; twenty field terms with a six-word figure; one grammar
point with a loose-versus-precise comparison; two dialogues, the second ending on
an objection nobody has answered; speaking tasks; an extended reading on one AI
capability with its stated limits; a six-line writing tasking with model and
checklist; **The Difficult Conversation**, a three-turn escalation with holding
phrases and a redline; a case clinic; a Brief Lab that diagnoses a badly written
instruction; and a review.

## What these books do not do

They teach communication and writing, not medicine. There is no dose, no regimen
and no diagnostic rule anywhere in the series. Every unit that touches a clinical
decision says that the decision belongs to the treating clinician, and the data
panels state that legal and professional requirements differ between countries.

## Files

Each book is supplied as a `.docx`, a PDF and a complete `_FULL_TEXT.md`.
`ALIGNMENT.md` is the sixty-unit grid; `BUILD_RECORD.md` has the figures and the
gates; `vocab_ledger.json` lists all 1,200 terms and where each is introduced.
