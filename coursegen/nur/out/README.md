## What is in each unit

Twelve parts, identical in every one of the sixty units:

1. **Hero spread** — the unit's claim in one sentence, with a figure.
2. **Warm-up** — three questions about the reader's own ward.
3. **Reading** — five paragraphs, with a sourced data point.
4. **Comprehension** — six open questions, six true/false, a gap-fill, a choice
   exercise and a four-way sorting task.
5. **Vocabulary** — twenty terms with glosses, a figure, and two exercises.
6. **Grammar** — the unit's point, four rules, a loose/precise comparison figure,
   a gap-fill and six discriminations.
7. **Dialogues** — two, the second deliberately unresolved.
8. **Speaking** — three paired tasks.
9. **The AI capability** — what a drafting tool can assemble, what it must not
   supply, a model six-line tasking, and the boundary.
10. **Speaking Up** — the series signature: a scene in which a nurse has to say
    something difficult, with a ladder, three turns, six holds and a redline.
11. **The Decision Room** — a clinic with figures, three voices, five analytical
    steps, a four-line rule to write, and a measured outcome.
12. **Brief Lab** — a real six-line draft, diagnosed against exactly three fault
    types, then rewritten; plus review, four foundations and a closing line.

## The editorial line

This series teaches **communication and writing, not nursing practice**. There is
no dose, no regimen and no diagnostic rule anywhere in 384,446 words. Every unit
that touches a clinical decision says that the decision belongs to the treating
clinician. Every unit that touches a professional duty — witnessing, consent,
delegation, raising a concern, scope of practice, disclosure — says that local
law and the employer's policy govern it.

Hospitals and clinics are invented. Cities are real. All data points are
described as general guidance or general literature of 2025–2026, without
attributing figures to named studies.
