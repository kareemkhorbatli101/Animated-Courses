### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- When did you last travel for work outside a capital city?
- What do you need to know before you arrive somewhere you have never been?

A mission that never leaves the capital knows one city and calls it a country. Ziad Mikhail had been at the post for fourteen months before he made his first field visit, and by then he had formed several opinions that the visit corrected in two days.

A field visit is approved, not simply taken. Somebody writes a visit objective, somebody checks the cost, somebody looks at the roads and the season, and somebody senior signs. The Mission has a duty of care that does not stop at the city boundary.

Before he left, Ziad asked a system for a background pack on the province. It came back in four minutes: fourteen pages, well organised, with the main industries, the recent public investments, the names of the regional authorities and a short history.

It was accurate. Every figure checked out. The organisation was better than anything he would have assembled by Thursday evening. He read it twice on the road and it was worth the four minutes many times over.

It was also entirely made of things that somebody had chosen to publish. There was nothing in it about who actually decides a licence in that province, which families do not speak to each other, or why the new road stops four kilometres short of the border.

- Use it with before, after, by the time, already, never … until.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**Open sources give you the structure of a place; the road gives you the people**

**Figure 1.5** — Three questions answered in four minutes, three in two days, and nobody needed to choose.

A background pack built from open sources is one of the best uses of a system in this course. Fourteen pages in four minutes, organised, sourced and accurate, is better preparation than most officers assemble in an evening, and it costs almost nothing.

It gives you structure. Who holds which office, what has been built, which industries matter, what the population is, what happened in the last five years. All of this is published, stable and checkable, and knowing it stops you wasting a first meeting.

What it cannot give you is influence. Open sources describe formal authority. They do not describe who is listened to, which official has been quietly moved aside, or which family controls the only working port on that coast.

There is a second limit, and it is the one officers forget. Everything in an open-source pack has been published by somebody with a reason to publish it. A regional investment office does not publish the dispute that is holding up the road.

So read the pack as the map and the visit as the territory. Use the four minutes to learn the structure, then spend the two days on the three questions no document will ever answer: who decides, who is listened to, and what nobody will say first.

> **DATA POINT · What open sources can and cannot give** — Analytical practice commonly distinguishes open-source background, which establishes structure and chronology, from field reporting, which establishes influence and intent, and treats the first as preparation for the second rather than a substitute.  
> Source: reported analytical practice, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Why publication is a filter** — Open-source analysis commonly treats the motive for publication as part of the evidence, since material reaches the public domain because somebody chose to place it there, and silence on a subject is not an absence of fact.  
> Source: reported open-source practice, 2025–2026.

### Part 7 · Real-World Skills — A briefing pack for a visit, from open sources

**Figure 1.7** — The left column is four minutes. The right column is the reason you are driving.

Ask for structure and for sources. Who holds which office and since when, what has been announced and by whom, which industries employ people, what the population is. Then ask for a source beside every figure, because a pack without sources cannot be argued with.

Ask also for the publication question, which most officers never put: who published each of these, and what would they have had a reason not to publish? That single question turns a pack from a summary into evidence with a known shape.

Then leave the pack in the car. The three things you are driving for are who actually decides, who is actually listened to, and what nobody mentions first. None of them is published anywhere, and all three are learned by sitting in rooms.

*Say it like this*
- Ask for structure: **Who holds which office in this province, and since when?**
- Ask for sources: **Give the source and the date beside every figure.**
- Ask the filter question: **Who published these, and what would they omit?**

> **WHAT THIS DOES NOT DO** — An open-source pack contains only what somebody chose to publish, so it describes formal authority and not influence. It cannot tell you who is listened to, and it will not mention the dispute that nobody has written down.

### Part 8 · The Decision Room · The road that stopped four kilometres short

**STAGE 1 · The Brief**

The Mission is asked whether to support a transport project in the eastern province. The open-source pack shows a new road, announced two years ago, with the budget, the contractor and the completion date, all accurate.

On the second day of a field visit Ziad sees that the road stops four kilometres short of the border crossing it was built to serve. Nothing published mentions this. Three separate people mention it within an hour of being asked.

The reason is a land dispute between two families, one of which supplies the contractor. It has been unresolved for eleven months. The Mission has to decide what to report, what to recommend, and how to describe a fact that cannot be sourced to any document.

## Unit 2 · Meeting a Regional Authority

*CEFR B1 · Grammar: non-defining relative clauses · AI: who you are about to meet, and what is actually known*

**Figure 2.0** — A NOTE WITH ONE SENTENCE THAT CHANGED HOW AN OFFICER SAT: A waiting room in a provincial headquarters, with a four-line note folded in a pocket.

### Part 1 · Warm Up and Read

- What do you want to know about somebody before you meet them officially?
- What would you not want a stranger to have read about you?
- How do you tell a fact from an allegation?

Regional authority is real and is usually underestimated by diplomats. A governor, who may be appointed or elected depending on the country, can hold powers over land, licensing, public works and policing that the capital cannot easily override.

Missions therefore prepare a note before meeting one. A biographical note is four or five lines: the office, the date of appointment, the previous post, the areas the person actually decides, and anything the Mission has promised or asked before.

Ziad asked for a note on Governor Hald, who had taken office nineteen months earlier. The note came back with three accurate facts, two correct dates and one further sentence, which reported an allegation about a construction contract.

The allegation came from a local site which is openly partisan and which has published similar claims about four other officials. No court, no inquiry and no mainstream outlet has ever reported it. The note gave it one line, like the others.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A note about a person is read by people who will never meet them**

**Figure 2.5** — Four sourced facts and one unverified line, given equal space on one page.

A biographical note is a small document with a long life. It is copied into visit packs, pasted into later notes and read by officers who will never meet the person described. Whatever is in it tends to stay in it, and to acquire authority by being repeated.

Building one is work a system does well. The office, the date of appointment, the previous post, the formal powers, the public statements: these are published, checkable and dull to assemble by hand, and four minutes produces a better note than an hour would.

The difficulty is that a search finds everything, and everything includes claims. A partisan site, a disgruntled former contractor and a court judgment all appear as text, and a note built from text gives each of them a line.

An allegation with a line of its own, in the same typeface as the facts, is not neutral. It changes how an officer sits in a chair. The person opposite has usually spent years reading faces, and they will see it even if nothing is said.

So split the document. Facts, with sources, in the note. Allegations, with their source and their status, in a separate paragraph that is read deliberately. And nothing at all unless two independent sources have published it, because one site is not a fact.

> **DATA POINT · Why allegations are handled separately** — Reporting guidance commonly requires that unverified allegations about named individuals be recorded separately from biographical fact, with their source and status, because an unmarked allegation is read by later users as part of the record.  
> Source: reported reporting practice, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Two sources, or a separate paragraph** — Practice in preparing briefing material on individuals commonly requires either corroboration by two independent sources or explicit separation of allegation from fact, to prevent unverified claims acquiring standing through repetition.  
> Source: reported briefing practice, 2025–2026.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A screen measures the enquiry; nobody has yet built one that measures the opportunity**

**Figure 3.5** — The screen was right about eight and wrong about the one the Mission most needed.

Screening is the most honest answer to a real constraint. A commercial officer receives far more approaches than can be pursued, and pursuing them all equally means doing all of them badly while appearing conscientious.

Four marks do most of the work. A named person at a named company. A product or service that exists and can be described. Some evidence of trading before. A specific question rather than a general hope. Three of the four is usually enough.

A scoring system applies those marks consistently in four seconds and never gets tired on a Friday afternoon. It also removes a quiet unfairness: approaches written in fluent English used to do better than approaches written in difficult English.

What it cannot do is know what the Mission is trying to achieve this year. A badly written letter from a new company in a province where the Mission has no contacts at all may be worth more than three polished approaches from the capital.

So keep the screen and keep a named exception. One enquiry a month may be pursued against its score, by a decision of the head of section, with the reason written down. If the reason cannot be written in one line, it was not a reason.

> **DATA POINT · Screening and its exceptions** — Commercial practice commonly combines a scoring screen for incoming enquiries with a standing exception for cases that meet a strategic objective, on the basis that a score measures the enquiry and not the opportunity.  
> Source: reported commercial diplomacy practice, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Why a written exception matters** — Decision practice commonly requires that an override of a scoring rule be recorded with its reason, so that the pattern of exceptions can be examined later and either absorbed into the rule or stopped.  
> Source: reported decision practice, 2025–2026.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A community is not an organisation, and two fluent versions are not one announcement**

**Figure 4.5** — Six channels, none of which reaches everybody, and one group that no channel reaches at all.

A diaspora is not an organisation. It is forty thousand people who have in common a country and very little else. The associations, the hometown societies and the mailing lists are real and useful, and none of them speaks for everybody.

Reaching people therefore means several channels at once, and accepting that the people hardest to reach are often the ones with most need of a consulate: the isolated, the elderly, the ones who never joined anything and the ones who stopped coming.

It also means two languages. The first generation reads one; their children often read the other far better. A single-language notice is not a notice to half the community; it is an invitation to be told later that nobody was informed.

Producing both versions is now quick and the quality is high. The risk is not bad translation. It is two good translations that do not say the same thing, because obligation, permission and time are carried differently in different languages.

The check is not reading each version; both will read well. The check is comparing them clause by clause, with somebody who reads both, looking specifically for must, may, by and from. Twenty minutes, against three hundred telephone calls.

> Source: reported public communication practice, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Compare, do not just read** — Translation quality practice commonly distinguishes reading a target text for fluency from comparing source and target for equivalence, and treats modality and time expressions as the categories most often shifted.  
> Source: reported translation practice, 2025–2026.

### Part 7 · Real-World Skills — Reaching a community in two languages

**Figure 4.7** — Producing is four minutes. Comparing is twenty, and it is the only part that protects you.

Produce both versions together and keep the clause order identical. A notice written in the same order in both languages can be compared line by line in twenty minutes by somebody who reads both, which is the only check that works.

Ask specifically for the modality to be preserved, and say which word you mean. If the notice says available, it must not become required. If it says may, it must not become must. State this in the instruction, because it is the thing most often lost.

Then have a person compare the clauses, not read the translation. A good translation reads perfectly and may still say something else, and a reader of one version alone will never see it. Twenty minutes, every time, before anything is posted.

*Say it like this*
- Fix the order: **Keep the clause order identical in both versions.**
- Protect the modality: **Available must not become required anywhere.**
- Compare, do not read: **Rim, compare clause 3 in both and tell me if the obligation matches.**

> **WHAT THIS DOES NOT DO** — A translation system produces a fluent target text, not a guarantee of equivalence, and it will not flag that an obligation has shifted. A reader of only one version has no way of noticing, because what they are reading is correct.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A confident wrong answer is produced by agreement, and agreement is produced by old pages**

**Figure 5.5** — One page is right and four are old, so the usual answer is the wrong one.

Scholarship administration is mostly answering the same five questions. Who may apply, what qualifications count, what the language requirement is, what the deadline is, and what happens after an award. Everything else is paperwork.

Those answers exist on a Mission page and are correct. The trouble is that the page is written as prose for a reader who starts at the beginning, and almost nobody reads it that way. They ask somebody, or they ask a system.

A general system answers by weighing what it has read, and what it has read includes last year’s version of your page, a university listing from two years ago, two agency sites and a forum. If four of those say April, April is what comes back.

It is not inventing and it is not careless. It is reporting the balance of what exists, and the balance of what exists is almost always out of date, because nobody ever removes anything from the internet, including missions.

So the remedy is unglamorous. Put the date of the last revision at the top. Answer the five questions in five short paragraphs with headings. Take down every superseded page you control. Then repeat the dates in every message you send to anybody.

> **DATA POINT · Why authoritative pages are dated** — Public information practice commonly requires a visible date of last revision and a single authoritative page for each scheme, because secondary descriptions and cached copies continue to circulate long after a change.  
> Source: reported public information practice, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Remove as well as publish** — Information management practice commonly treats removal of superseded material as part of publication, since outdated pages under an organisation’s own control are a frequent source of incorrect public answers.  
> Source: reported information management practice, 2025–2026.

### Part 7 · Real-World Skills — The scholarship question a general system answers wrongly

**Monitoring counts mentions; only a person weighs what a mention is worth**

**Figure 6.5** — Eleven flags, two large audiences, one false claim, and nothing that needed a reply.

Media monitoring is one of the clearest gains available to a press office. A system reads four times as many sources as two officers could, every morning, and produces an ordered summary before anybody arrives. Nothing is missed because somebody was ill.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A good summary of a law is accurate about what it read, and schedules are at the back**

**Figure 7.5** — Four provisions found in the chapters, and the fifth in a schedule that amends another act.

Nobody at a mission reads a ninety-one-page act. There is no time, it is in the host language, and ninety of the pages are irrelevant. Before, the Mission learned what a law required when a national was refused something at a counter.

Asking a system a specific question changes that completely. Which provisions apply to foreign nationals resident here? Eleven minutes, with section numbers, usually right. That is a transformation in a part of the work that was simply not being done.

The weakness is structural and worth understanding. A long act has chapters, which are where the subject matter lives, and schedules at the back, which are where consequential amendments to other laws are put.

A question about this act may therefore be answered accurately and still miss a provision that changes a different act. Governments do not do this to hide anything. It is simply how legislation is assembled, in every country.

So ask the question three times. Once about the chapters, once naming the schedules, and once about the explanatory memorandum, which usually lists every other law amended. Then remember that all of this is reporting, and that advice about a person’s case is not something a mission gives at all.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**Provenance is a list of what is documented; the finding is usually what is not**

**Figure 9.5** — Four documented periods, one claim, and seventeen years about which nothing is said.

Objects reach a mission through a dozen doors: a loan proposal, a family letter, a customs telephone call, a dealer with a certificate. In every case the question is the same. What is the recorded history of this thing, and where does the record stop?

Provenance is that history. A strong one names owners, dates, sales and exhibitions, and supports each with a document that can be examined. A weak one asserts the same facts with nothing behind them, and reads exactly as well.

The finding is almost always a gap. Documented periods are rarely controversial; it is the undocumented years that matter, especially where they coincide with a war, an occupation, a forced sale or a period of unregulated excavation.

A summarising system reports what its sources contain. It will give you eight owners and four dates, accurately, in seconds, and it will not tell you that nothing covers 1961 to 1978, because none of its sources says that nothing covers it.

So ask the other question. Ask for every year in the history for which nothing is cited. That is answerable, it takes seconds, and it turns a reassuring narrative into a research task with a shape. Then a person asks what was happening in those years, and why.

> **DATA POINT · Gaps are the finding** — Provenance practice commonly treats an unexplained gap in an ownership history as the principal matter to be reported, since documented periods rarely raise questions and undocumented periods frequently do.  
> Source: reported provenance research practice, 2025–2026.

> **DATA POINT · Ask for the absence** — Research practice commonly recommends requesting an explicit list of undocumented periods when using automated summarisation of ownership histories, since a summary reports what sources contain and not what they omit.

### Part 5 · Extended Reading

**A report says what happened; a costing says what it took, and nobody writes the second**

**Figure 10.5** — Everything the visit produced is in the report. Everything it consumed is in two other systems.

A visit report is the normal product of travelling. It names who was met, what was said and what should happen next. It is read by three or four people, filed, and occasionally rediscovered two years later by somebody preparing to go to the same place.

What it almost never contains is what the visit cost. The fuel and the hotel are in a travel claim, in another system, read by an accountant. The staff time is in nobody’s system at all, because officers are salaried and their days feel free.

They are not free. Five working days of two officers is the largest single item in most visits, and it is the one item that never appears. A mission that does not count it cannot compare a visit with a video call, or with not going.

Producing the figure is now easy. The travel claims exist, the diary exists and the staff grades exist. Put the three together and a cost per meeting and per day comes back in a minute and a half, with a comparison.

What the figure cannot do is tell you whether the visit was worth making. A contact made in a province may be worth nothing for three years and then worth a great deal. The costing tells you what you spent; the judgement remains entirely yours.

> **DATA POINT · Costing the routine** — Administrative practice commonly recommends costing recurring activities including staff time, on the basis that activities whose cost is never calculated are neither defended when challenged nor compared with alternatives.
