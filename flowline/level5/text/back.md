# Grammar Reference

Everything in this book that is a structure rather than a fact. Each unit's two structures are set
out with what they do, and with the mistake each one produces when it is left out.

## Starting Point · Naming a shape of failure

A bad day has a shape, and naming the shape is the first analytical act.

| | |
|---|---|
| **a violation** | somebody did something they knew was not allowed |
| **a drift** | nothing was decided; everything moved slowly, for good reasons |
| **a stale assumption** | the hardware was sound; the paper it was sized from was old |
| **two at once** | each failure was on somebody's list, and never on one list |

**The mistake:** reporting every event as the first shape, because the first shape has somebody to
blame and the others do not.

## Unit 1 · Generalizing carefully; attributing a claim

**Generalizing.** *In most cases* · *it is generally accepted that* · *the evidence suggests* ·
*there is no published dataset behind* · *widely repeated rather than reproduced*.

**Attributing.** *according to* · *is attributed to* · *it is often claimed that* · *the figure
originates in* · *estimated by* · *on the basis of*.

> *Is attributed to* lets you report a classification without endorsing it. *Shows that* endorses it.
> The two are one word apart and a long way apart.

**The mistake:** *our safety is excellent because we have had no injuries*. The count is low; that is
a different sentence.

## Unit 2 · Reporting an investigation; contrast

**Reporting.** *the investigation found that* · *it was found that* · *the report states that* · *the
panel concluded that* · *the report does not address* · *the findings as published include*.

**Contrast.** *although* + clause · *despite* + noun · *even though* + clause · *at the same time* ·
*whereas* · *nevertheless*.

> Two facts in one sentence force the reader to hold both. Two sentences let the reader keep the first
> and forget the second, which is why reports are written in two sentences.

**The mistake:** *the safety figures were wrong*. They were right, and about something else.

## Unit 3 · Guide words; hypothesizing

**Guide words.** *no* · *more* · *less* · *as well as* · *part of* · *reverse* · *other than*, applied
to flow, pressure, temperature, level and composition.

**Hypothesizing.** *if* + past simple · *suppose* · *in the event that* · *would be expected to* ·
*could conceivably* · *it is not credible that* — **with a reason**.

> A guide word makes a team **search** instead of **recall**. That is the whole invention.

**The mistake:** *it cannot happen*. Every dismissal needs a reason on the same line, and an action
closed as *no action required* without one has been deleted rather than answered.

## Unit 4 · Selecting a method; what a figure decides

**Selecting.** *we selected* · *on the basis that* · *in preference to* · *was considered and rejected
on the grounds that* · *is appropriate where* · *is not warranted where*.

**What a figure decides.** *supports a comparison between* · *does not establish that* · *is sensitive
to* · *if that rate were twice as high* · *changes no decision currently open to us*.

> Recording what you **rejected**, and why, is the only part of a method selection that is auditable
> in five years.

**The mistake:** *the risk is acceptable because the figure is low*. A figure is compared against a
criterion that comes from somewhere else, and that somewhere must be named.

## Unit 5 · Reporting an inquiry; responsibility

**Reporting.** *the inquiry found that* · *it was found that* · *the inquiry concluded that* · *the
report does not establish individual liability* · *among the findings as published*.

**Responsibility.** *was responsible for* · *no one had* · *it was nobody's job to* · *rested on
informal communication* · *the conclusion existed in nobody's hands*.

> *It was nobody's job to* is more useful than a name, because a name is replaced and a gap is not.

**The mistake:** *somebody made a mistake*. Each person may have done exactly what their own form
required.

## Unit 6 · Gradual change; the absence of consequences

**Gradual change.** *gradually* · *increasingly* · *over time* · *little by little* · *each one on its
own* · *cumulatively*.

**Absence of consequences.** *the plant continues to operate normally* · *no consequence has been
observed* · *that is what defense in depth does* · *the absence of a consequence is not evidence of a
margin* · *the margin has to be measured directly, not inferred*.

> You need *each one on its own* **and** *cumulatively* in the same paragraph. The first concedes, the
> second argues, and without the first nobody listens to the second.

**The mistake:** *we have had no incidents, so the change was fine*. That is the exact inference the
1997 paper exists to warn about.

## Unit 7 · Conditions; authorizing

**Conditions.** *may not be entered unless* · *only if* · *provided that* · *no entry is permitted
until* · *shall not* · *is authorized to*.

**What an arrangement is.** *the permit records* · *it does not establish that* · *has not been asked
whether* · *nobody has attempted* · *a telephone number is not an arrangement*.

> *Shall not* is absolute; *should not* is advice. The attendant's duty takes the first.

**The mistake:** *rescue arrangements are in place*. *In place* says nothing about whether anybody can
perform one.

## Unit 8 · Proportionality; proving

**Proportionality.** *is proportionate to* · *is adequate for* · *is required where* · *is warranted
by* · *was selected on the basis of* · *habit is not a justification*.

**Proving.** *has been proved by* · *was verified by* · *was demonstrated by* — each one with a
**duration** and a **witness** · *it is not sufficient to* · *no reliance is placed on*.

> Closed is a **position**. Proved is a **measurement**. And *what did you expect out of the drain* is
> the question that catches a blocked drain, because *clear* is only good news if clear was expected.

**The mistake:** *the line was isolated*. By what arrangement, proved how, and what came out?

## Unit 9 · Tiers; escalation

**Tiers.** Defined by **resources and distance**, never by volume. *tier one* — your own site, now ·
*tier two* — regional · *tier three* — national or international.

**Escalation.** *in the first instance* · *activate* · *escalate to* · *beyond the capability of* ·
*on the assumption that* · *stand down*.

> Escalation is a decision made early or not at all, which is why *stand down* has to be a respectable
> sentence in an organization before early escalation is possible.

**The mistake:** *we have a response plan*. Say what it assumes, when it was last exercised, and what
you have measured.

## Unit 10 · Reporting findings; hedging a conclusion

**Findings.** *was found to* · *was detected in* · *exceeded the guideline value at* · *in a number of
locations* · *is compared with a published value, named* · *no sample was taken below*.

**Hedging.** *no conclusion can be drawn about* · *the assessment did not cover* · *in the absence of
baseline data* · *further sampling would be required to* · *this is not a clean site and not a
contaminated site; it is an unmeasured site*.

> *Was detected in* reports a presence. *Exceeded the guideline value at* reports a level against a
> named figure. A reader is entitled to very different conclusions from the two.

**The mistake:** *the site is clean*. Sampled where, to what depth, compared with what, and what was
not sampled?

## Unit 11 · Cause and inference

**Marking an inference.** *which suggests that* · *the reason appears to be* · *a more plausible
explanation is* · *the evidence is consistent with both* · *I cannot establish which*.

**Blocking a bad one.** *it does not follow that* · *is inconsistent with* · *attributing this to X
explains nothing, because it would explain any outcome* · *this is a design problem, not a character
problem*.

> A useful explanation says what somebody would do differently. An explanation that fits every
> outcome has never produced an action.

**The mistake:** *it is a cultural problem*. Keep the number in the sentence instead.

## Unit 12 · Argument and concession

**Conceding, then arguing.** *it is true that … but* · *while accepting that* · *granted* · *even
allowing for* · *the force of this objection is* · *this does not displace*.

**Proposing a measure.** *we currently count* · *I would also count* · *each of these still means
something in a year with no incidents* · *I do not have a single number and I am not going to invent
one*.

> Every strong argument in this book concedes first. State the objection at its **strongest**, not at
> its weakest, and then answer it.

**The mistake:** *we should stop counting incidents*. The argument says **complement**, not replace.

# The British Layer

This book is written in American English, and the switch to British English is a layer rather than a
correction. The patterns are printed in full in Book 1. The endings this book touches most often are
the **-ize** and **-yze** verbs and their nouns, the **-se** and **-ce** pairs in *license*,
*defense* and *offense*, the **-er** ending in measuring words, the doubling of a single **l** in
*labeled*, *traveled* and *canceled*, and **gray**, **skeptical** and **program**.

**Three things never change, in either layer.**

**Standards, regulations and document titles.** IEC 61882 is IEC 61882. ISO 17776 is ISO 17776. A
regulation cited by its number keeps its number, and where a quoted instrument spells a word
differently from this book's base, the instrument's spelling stands inside its own title. This matters
in this book more than in any other, because several of the documents quoted here were written in
British English and one of them has a word in its title that this layer would otherwise change.

**Names of people, inquiries, journals, agencies and United Nations bodies.** Heinrich, Kletz,
Cullen, Rasmussen, Hopkins, Hollnagel, Linnhoff. The publishers and the bodies, including those whose
own names contain endings this layer would otherwise change, and including the word **Programme**
where it is part of the name of an organization.

**Quoted figures and their units.** A guideline value quoted in milligrams per liter stays as the
source gives it. A measured response time stays as it was measured, with the conditions of the
measurement beside it.

**One deliberate choice, which is not a spelling layer.** Money is written with no currency symbol and
no currency name anywhere in this series. Where a source states a sum — and one document in this book
recommends a large initial capitalization for a restoration fund — this book says that a figure was
stated and does not print it in any currency, because the series teaches the reasoning rather than a
market.

# Sources

One real document for each unit, named with its author or publisher and its date, and with the limits
its own authors state. Nothing here is official and nothing here is advice.

| Unit | The document | What it says it does not cover |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | H. W. Heinrich, *Industrial Accident Prevention: A Scientific Approach*, McGraw-Hill, **1931** | The data came from insurers' records and employers' accident reports of the 1920s and **was never published in a re-examinable form**; the proportion attributed to unsafe acts was taken from a **cause box on a form** completed in an era with an expected answer. The ratio concerns **injuries**, not fires, releases or structural failures |
| **2** | The US Chemical Safety and Hazard Investigation Board report on the **BP Texas City** explosion and fire of 23 March 2005, published **2007**, with *The Report of the BP U.S. Refineries Independent Safety Review Panel*, **January 2007** | Findings and **recommendations**, not law. One event, one site, one company's refineries. **The panel's remit was management systems and culture, not the cause of the accident.** Neither document establishes individual liability; **the mechanism travels, the conclusion does not** |
| **3** | Trevor Kletz, *HAZOP and HAZAN*, Institution of Chemical Engineers, **1983** and later editions, with **IEC 61882**, *Hazard and operability studies — Application guide* | It cannot find a hazard **nobody in the room knows about**, and team composition is the largest determinant of quality. It is performed **on a drawing**, which may not be the plant. It is a **photograph** of a plant on a day. And the most common failure of the method is in **follow-up**, not in the study room |
| **4** | **ISO 17776:2016**, *Petroleum and natural gas industries — Offshore production installations — Major accident hazard management during the design of new installations* | A standard about **choosing** methods, not a method. It gives **no threshold** for tolerable risk — those come from national regulators. Its scope is the **design of new offshore installations**. And it states honestly that the decisions that matter most are available only when the least is known |
| **5** | *The Public Inquiry into the Piper Alpha Disaster*, report by the Hon. Lord Cullen, HMSO, **1990** | One event, one installation, **the regulatory regime of that time**. Not a criminal proceeding: it **does not establish individual liability**. Its 106 recommendations are addressed to one industry in one country. **It produced better permit forms everywhere and could not produce the conversation they are meant to prompt** |
| **6** | Jens Rasmussen, "Risk management in a dynamic society: a modeling problem", *Safety Science*, **1997** | A **framework**, not a method: no procedure and no forms. It **does not tell you where the boundary is** — only that it is unmarked and must be marked. It **predicts nothing** about any particular plant. And it does not excuse anybody from noticing |
| **7** | **29 CFR 1910.146**, *Permit-Required Confined Spaces*, Occupational Safety and Health Administration, United States | **One country's law; it does not apply here.** It sets **minimums**, not good practice, and has a separate standard for construction. It cannot make a rescue arrangement real, and it cannot stop a person going in to help somebody they can see — which is the pattern in a very high proportion of these deaths |
| **8** | **HSG253**, *The safe isolation of plant and equipment*, Health and Safety Executive of Great Britain, second edition **2006** | **Guidance from another country; it does not apply here** and there it supports legal duties rather than replacing them. It **cannot tell you what is in the line** — a dead leg is exactly where nobody has looked. It cannot detect a passing valve for you, and it cannot make the proving step happen on a Friday afternoon |
| **9** | *Tiered preparedness and response*, an **IPIECA–IOGP** Good Practice Guide, **2015**, with the **International Convention on Oil Pollution Preparedness, Response and Co-operation, 1990** | The guide is **good practice and binds nobody**. And neither changes the arithmetic of the first hour |
| **10** | *Environmental Assessment of Ogoniland*, **United Nations Environment Programme**, **2011** | **One region**, not the whole Niger Delta; its findings describe what its samples found, where they were taken. The health observations are **not an epidemiological study**. | **11** | Andrew Hopkins, *Failure to Learn: The BP Texas City Refinery Disaster*, CCH Australia, **2008** | **One author's analysis, not an official finding**, and he says so. **Other analysts emphasize different factors** and the disagreement is part of the literature. It concerns one company and one refinery. And it **cannot be implemented**: three of its five arguments are about how attention and information behave in large organizations |
| **12** | Erik Hollnagel, *Safety-I and Safety-II: The Past and Future of Safety Management*, Ashgate, **2014** | It **does not say stop investigating accidents** — the second view **complements** the first. And it is an **argument**, widely contested, which has not displaced the older view in practice |

**Three of these twelve disagree with each other on purpose.** The 1931 book attributes most accidents
to the unsafe acts of individuals. The 1997 paper says organizations migrate towards their boundaries
through ordinary, legitimate pressures that nobody decides on. The 2014 book says counting what goes
wrong tells you very little about safety at all. All three have been enormously influential. A reader
who finishes this book should be able to say what each one is good for and where each one misleads, and
that is the real examination.

# A Note on Everything Else in This Book

**Jazira Petroleum Operations does not exist.** Neither do the Mureij Training Center, the Hasakah
injection system, the Deir flowline corridor, the C-301 column, V-204, P-212, PSV-411, the permits,
the certificates, the audits, the drift list, the readiness review, the finding history, or any of the
fourteen people. All of it was written for this book, and all of it was written to be **recognizable**
rather than true.

**The real places are named as public record only.** Deir ez-Zor, Hasakah, Homs, Banias, the Syrian
Petroleum Company, the Arab Gas Pipeline, the Kirkuk–Banias line. Nothing in this book describes an
event at any of them, and nothing in it describes the condition of any real installation.

**One real incident is described in this series and it is in Book 2.** The accidents referred to in
this book — a refinery explosion in 2005, a platform destroyed in 1988, and sixty years of releases in
a region of the Niger Delta — are the subjects of the published investigations, inquiries and
assessments named in the reading texts, and **nothing is attributed to them that those documents do
not say**. Where a document is careful, this book is careful in the same place. Where the proportions
are contested, this book says so and does not settle them.

**The documents themselves are real, and so are their limits.** Each reading text names its source
with its author or publisher and its date, prints what that source says it does not cover, and is
written in our own words rather than quoted at length. Two of the twelve are from countries whose law
does not apply here, and both say so on the page. Two of the twelve are arguments rather than
findings, and both say so on the page.

**And the standing sentence of the series applies most of all to this book.** Safety information in a
language course is not a substitute for a competent person, a procedure and a permit. Nothing here
authorizes anybody to issue a permit, isolate a line, enter a confined space, classify a hazard,
assess a risk, or sign anything at all.

# At the End of the Book

**Five books of plant and one book of organizations.** You have the wellhead, the reservoir, the
pipeline, the refinery, and now the twelve documents that the industry wrote after it had hurt
somebody.

**What this book was actually teaching** was a register: the English of **saying something is probably
true, for these reasons, within these limits**, without either overclaiming or going quiet. That is
the hardest thing to do in a second language and one of the hardest in a first, and it is the only
language in which any of this can be raised at all. Nine of the twelve units asked you to write *I am
not claiming that*, in one form or another, and the reason is simple: a careful sentence survives a
meeting and an exaggerated one does not.

**The four signatures.** A permit, an isolation certificate, an entry authorization, a handover. In
all four, the paper is not the protection; the conversation it forces is the protection, and the paper
is the evidence that the conversation happened. A system in which the signing continues and the
conversation has stopped looks identical from the outside, and that is the whole of this book in two
sentences.

**And the figures.** A pyramid whose data was never published. An excellent injury year beside a
deteriorating plant. A safeguard that existed only on a drawing. A target copied into a budget. Two
permits in two rooms. Twelve approvals and no decision. Three gas readings and a two-hour gap. A valve
closed and not proved. Four hours assumed and seven measured. A certificate that certified a surface.
The same finding, three times, getting softer on the way up. None of those figures was false. Every
single one of them was read as evidence of something it did not measure.

**Book 6 — *Contracts, Capacity and the Long Horizon*** leaves the plant altogether. It is about the
agreements under which this industry operates, where the revenue goes, what it does to the country it
happens in, who is allowed to see the numbers, and what you leave behind when the field is finished. It
is the hardest English in the series and the only book in it with no equipment in it at all.
