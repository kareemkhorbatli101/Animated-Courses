# Unit 11 · Why There Is a Flame
%%SUB Three reasons a flare burns, only one of which anybody is trying to stop%%
%%GOALS

The flame on the stack is the first thing anybody sees from the road, and most people have one
explanation for it. There are three, they are completely different, and an initiative launched in
2015 is trying to eliminate exactly one of them.

**2A  Read the note. Then answer the questions in section 3.**

> **JAZIRA PETROLEUM OPERATIONS — Mureij Training Center**
> **Note for new operators: why the stack is lit, and who is counting**
> **Source: the World Bank's *Zero Routine Flaring by 2030* initiative, launched in 2015, and the annual *Global Gas Flaring Tracker Report*. Printed here in our own words.**
>
> **1** The initiative asks governments and companies to end **routine** flaring by 2030. The word
> *routine* does a lot of work, so the initiative defines it.
>
> | The kind | What it is |
> |---|---|
> | **safety flaring** | gas sent to the flare to protect the plant: a relief valve lifting, an emergency blowdown |
> | **non-routine flaring** | flaring during start-up, shutdown, maintenance or an upset |
> | **routine flaring** | flaring of associated gas in normal production, because there are no facilities to do anything else |
>
> **2** Only the third is the target. Nobody asks for safety flaring to stop. A flare is a safety
> device, and taking it away makes a plant more dangerous, not less.
>
> **3** Gas comes up with the oil, whether anybody wants it or not. Every other use costs money.
> Where the oil is worth more than the gas, the gas is burned.
>
> | What else you could do with the gas | What that needs |
> |---|---|
> | send it down a pipeline | a pipeline, and somebody at the other end to buy it |
> | process it at a gas plant | a plant, and a market for the products |
> | burn it in a power station | a station near enough to reach |
> | compress it and put it back underground | compressors, and the power to run them |
>
> **4** The reported world volume has been about **140 to 150 billion cubic meters a year** for over
> ten years, and it has not fallen much. For a country that burns gas to make electricity, the
> arithmetic is hard. Gas burned at a wellhead is gas not making power.
>
> **5** The initiative is voluntary. Governments and companies endorse it. There is no treaty, no
> penalty and nobody to enforce it. An endorsement is an intention.
>
> **6** The volumes are satellite estimates, not meter readings. An instrument in orbit sees the heat
> and light of a flare. So the figures carry an uncertainty.
> Small flares can be missed. Giving a flare to one operator is a judgment about place. Burning is not
> complete either: a bad flare sends out methane and soot.
>
> *Our summary of a published initiative and its annual report, named with its sponsor and its launch year. Read the published Tracker, and write down the year you read.*

### 1 · *Because* and *so*

| | |
|---|---|
| The gas is flared **because** there is no pipeline. | result first |
| There is no pipeline, **so** the gas is flared. | reason first |
| **Since** the compressor is down, we are flaring more. | reason first, formal |
| The compressor is down. **As a result**, flaring is up. | reason, then result, separately |
| That is **why** the stack is brighter today. | the reason, pointed at |

**1A  Join each pair twice, once with *because* and once with *so*. Then read both aloud with a
partner.**

### 2 · Saying which kind it is

| | |
|---|---|

### 📝 ON THE JOB

> **Write the reason, not the size.** *The flare was bigger* tells the next shift nothing. *The flare
> was bigger because the second-stage compressor tripped at 09:40 and the gas went to the header* tells
> them what to look at, and it is the difference between a log and a diary. And when you genuinely do
> not know why the flare is lit, write that — a reason code of *unknown* is information, and a blank is
> not.
