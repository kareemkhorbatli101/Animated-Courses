# Grammar Reference

This is the whole grammar of the book on a few pages. Use it when you are writing and cannot remember
the shape of something. The unit number tells you where it was taught.

## Starting Point · Saying where something is

| | |
|---|---|
| The field is **in** the northeast. | a region |
| The refinery is **at** Banias. | a point |
| The crude comes **from** Deir ez-Zor. | an origin |
| The oil goes **to** Homs. | a destination |
| The station is **between** the field and the plant. | a position between two things |

## Unit 1 · *Be* and *have*; one and many

| | |
|---|---|
| The crude **is** heavy. | *be* + adjective |
| The crude **is** 24 degrees API. | *be* + a figure |
| The well **has** a high water cut. | *have* + a noun |
| The wells **have** high water cut. | plural subject, plural verb |
| There **is** one separator. There **are** three separators. | *there is* / *there are* |
| **Some** gas is dissolved. **A little** sand comes with it. | uncountable quantity |
| **A few** wells are shut in. **Several** are producing. | countable quantity |

> **Careful:** *water*, *gas*, *sand*, *crude* and *equipment* are uncountable in English. You cannot
> say *three equipments*. Say *three items of equipment*, or name them.

## Unit 2 · Numbers and quantities

| | |
|---|---|
| **about** 159 liters | an approximation |
| **just under** 0.16 cubic meters | slightly less |
| **just over** six barrels | slightly more |
| **roughly** 42 gallons | a rounded figure |
| **exactly** 42 US gallons | a definition, not an estimate |
| **at** 60 degrees Fahrenheit | a reference condition |
| 24 degrees API, **which is** heavy | a figure, then what it means |

> **Careful:** *about*, *roughly* and *approximately* mean the figure is estimated. Do not put them in
> front of a figure that is defined. A barrel is **exactly** 42 US gallons, by definition.

## Unit 3 · Part and whole; position in a line

| | |
|---|---|
| The wellhead **consists of** a casing head, a tubing head and a tree. | whole, then parts |
| The tree **includes** a master valve and a wing valve. | some of the parts |
| The gasket is **part of** the flange connection. | one part |
| The choke is **downstream of** the wing valve. | after, in the direction of flow |
| The master valve is **upstream of** the choke. | before, in the direction of flow |
| The valve is **on** the flowline. | on a line |

> **Careful:** *upstream* and *downstream* are about the direction of flow, not about height or
> distance. Something downstream can be physically higher and nearer.

## Unit 4 · Present simple at work; roles

| | |
|---|---|
| Samira **reports to** the maintenance supervisor. | a reporting line |
| She **is responsible for** the instruments. | a responsibility |
| The superintendent **authorizes** the permit. | an authority |
| The contractor **works** on the compressor. | a routine activity |
| **Who** does the gas test? | a question about a role |
| **Everybody** may stop a job. | a right, not a rank |

> **Careful:** *report to* means a reporting line. *Report* on its own means to tell somebody
> something. *I report to Samir* and *I reported the leak* are different verbs doing different work.

## Unit 5 · Imperatives; *must not*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Check** the isolation before you break the joint. | an instruction |
| **Do not** stand under a suspended load. | a prohibition |
| You **must not** bypass a safety control without authorization. | a strong prohibition |
| **Never** enter a confined space without a permit. | an absolute prohibition |
| **Always** wear your detector in that area. | an absolute instruction |
| You **may** stop any job. | a permission |

> **Careful:** *must not* is a prohibition. *Do not have to* is the absence of an obligation. *You must
> not do it* and *you do not have to do it* are opposites.

## Unit 6 · Obligation: *have to*, *need to*, *should*

| | |
|---|---|
| You **have to** wear a detector in that area. | an external requirement |
| You **need to** change the cartridge every month. | a practical necessity |
| You **should** stand upwind of the vent. | advice, strongly meant |
| Hearing protection **is required** above the pump house. | the passive, for a rule |
| The hazard **has to be** eliminated **if** it can be. | the order of the hierarchy |

> **Careful:** personal protective equipment is the **last** control, not the first. In English as in
> engineering, put *eliminate* and *substitute* before *wear*.

## Unit 7 · Ability and warning: *can* and *cannot*

| | |
|---|---|
| You **can** smell it at a fraction of a part per million. | an ability |
| You **cannot** smell it at a hundred parts per million. | the absence of ability |
| It **can** kill you in one breath at high concentration. | a possibility |
| If the alarm sounds, **leave** and **go** upwind. | a conditional instruction |
| **Do not** go in after somebody who has collapsed. | the instruction people break |

> **Careful:** *can* has two meanings here — *is able to* and *is possible*. Both are correct, and in a
> warning, say which one you mean.

## Unit 8 · *Before*, *after*, *until*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Before** you break the joint, check the isolation. | sequence |
| **After** the gas test, sign the permit. | sequence |
| Do not start **until** the permit is signed. | a limit in time |
| The permit is valid **for** eight hours. | a duration |
| The permit is valid **until** 18:00. | an end point |
| **Once** the work is finished, hand it back. | a trigger |

> **Careful:** *for* takes a length of time; *until* takes a point in time. *Valid for eight hours*,
> *valid until 18:00*.

## Unit 9 · Instructions and comprehension checks

| | |
|---|---|
| **Tell me** what you will do first. | a check that works |
| **Any questions?** | a check that does not |
| **Who** is isolating this line? | a check with a name in the answer |
| **Say it back to me.** | a check with a voice in the answer |
| **What will you do if** the alarm sounds? | a check with a plan in the answer |

> **Careful:** a question answered with *yes* or a nod has checked nothing. Ask questions whose answers
> contain information.

## Unit 10 · *Rose to*, *rose by*, *fell to*, *fell by*

| | |
|---|---|
| The level **rose to** 4.4 meters. | the new value |
| The level **rose by** 1.3 meters. | the change |
| The pressure **fell to** 12 bar. | the new value |
| The pressure **fell by** 4 bar. | the change |
| The level **rose from** 3.1 **to** 4.4. | both values |
| The reading **has not changed** since 06:00. | no change, with a time |

> **Careful:** *rose by* and *rose to* are one letter apart and a long way apart. Say which one you
> mean, and say the time with it.

## Unit 11 · *Because* and *so*

| | |
|---|---|
| The gas is flared **because** there is no pipeline. | result, then reason |
| There is no pipeline, **so** the gas is flared. | reason, then result |
| **Since** the compressor is down, flaring is up. | reason first, formal |
| The compressor is down. **As a result**, flaring is up. | two sentences |
| That is **why** the stack is brighter. | the reason, pointed at |

> **Careful:** *because* is followed by a clause — *because there is no pipeline*. *Because of* is
> followed by a noun — *because of the lack of a pipeline*. Not *because of there is*.

## Unit 12 · The handover

| | |
|---|---|
| **As of now,** we are at reduced rate. | the present state |
| **Changed since 06:00:** tank 2 rose by 1.3 meters, to 4.4. | movement, with figures and a time |
| **Still open:** permit 4471, performing authority Ahmad. | unfinished items, with names |
| **Left for you:** the second lab sample. | deliberate handover |
| **Watch** the discharge temperature. | the thing to look at |
| **I do not know** why the flare was brighter at 03:00. | the honest gap |

> **Careful:** *normal* is not a handover. It is the word people use instead of giving one.

# The British Layer

This book is written in American English. Everything in it can be switched to British English, and
the switch is a layer, not a correction. The list below gives the **patterns** that change, rather
than a column of respelled words, because a pattern is portable and a word list is not.

| What changes | How it changes | Words in this book it touches |
|---|---|---|
| **-er** at the end of a measuring word | becomes **-re** | meter, liter, kilometer, center |
| **-or** in a small set of nouns | gains a **u** | color, behavior, labor, favor, neighbor |
| **-ize** and **-ization** | become **-ise** and **-isation** | authorize, organize, realize, recognize, apologize |
| **-yze** | becomes **-yse** | analyze, analyzed, analyzing |
| **sulf-** | becomes **sulph-** | sulfur, sulfide |
| **-se** in a noun after a verb of the same sound | becomes **-ce** | license, defense, offense |
| **-ce** in a verb | becomes **-se** | practice (as a verb) |
| a single **l** before a vowel ending | doubles | traveling, canceled, labeled, modeling |
| **-ed** in four irregular verbs | becomes **-t** | learned, spelled, burned, dreamed |
| **gray**, **mold**, **tire**, **curb**, **skeptical**, **aluminum**, **math**, **program** | each has a different British form | the words themselves |

**Three things never change, in either layer.**

**Standards numbers and titles.** API Spec 6A is API Spec 6A. ISO 10423 is ISO 10423. The title of a
published document is printed as its publisher printed it, including its publisher's own spelling.
Where a cited document spells a word the British way in its own title, that spelling stays, and this
book does not correct it.

**Names of people and organizations.** A person's name is spelled the way they spell it. So is an
organization's. Several bodies cited in this series spell their own names with endings that the
patterns above would otherwise change, and renaming a source is a worse error than a spelling.

**Units in a quoted figure.** A figure quoted from a document keeps the unit the document used. If a
standard gives 60 degrees Fahrenheit, this book gives 60 degrees Fahrenheit, and gives the Celsius
equivalent beside it rather than instead of it.

**One deliberate choice, which is not a spelling layer at all.** Money in this book is written with
no currency symbol: *twelve thousand*, and nothing in front of it. A currency symbol in a teaching
book tells the reader where the book thinks they are. This book does not know.

# Sources

Each unit of this book is built on one real published document. The table gives the document, the
unit, and the limit the document itself states. Nothing in this book is quoted at length; the reading
texts are our own summaries, written for this course, and each one names its document in its own first
lines. **Read the published documents.** They are better than any summary of them, including this one,
and every one of them is clearer about its own limits than people who cite it tend to be.

| Unit | The document | What it says about its own limits |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | American Petroleum Institute, *Manual of Petroleum Measurement Standards*, Chapter 1, *Vocabulary* | It defines terms for measurement purposes. It is not a dictionary of the industry, and a term defined for custody transfer may be used differently in a drilling office. |
| 2 | API, *Manual of Petroleum Measurement Standards*, Chapter 11.1, published jointly as ASTM D1250 and IP 200 | It gives correction factors for stated ranges of density and temperature, and states that it does not apply outside them. The 60 degree Fahrenheit and 15 degree Celsius bases are different bases, not a rounding. |
| 3 | API Specification 6A, identical to ISO 10423, *Wellhead and christmas tree equipment* | It specifies what equipment must be capable of. It does not say any particular wellhead is fit for service today, and it is not a maintenance or operating document. |
| 4 | International Association of Oil and Gas Producers, Report 423, *HSE management — guidelines for working together in a contract environment* | It is guidance for members, not a standard, and it states that the arrangements it describes have to be adapted to the contract and the jurisdiction. |
| 5 | IOGP Report 459, *Life-Saving Rules* — the 2013 original set of eighteen, revised in 2018 to nine | The rules address personal safety: activities that have killed individuals. They are explicitly not a process safety management system, and the set is not a list of all the ways a person can be killed. |
| 6 | ISO 45001:2018, *Occupational health and safety management systems — Requirements with guidance for use* | It specifies requirements for a management system. Certification states that the system meets those requirements. It does not state that a workplace is safe, and the standard says so. |
| 7 | API Recommended Practice 49, *Recommended Practice for Drilling and Well Servicing Operations Involving Hydrogen Sulfide* | It is a recommended practice for particular operations. Exposure limits are set by regulators and differ between jurisdictions; the document does not set them. |
| 8 | Health and Safety Executive (United Kingdom), HSG250, *Guidance on permit-to-work systems*, 2005 | It is guidance, not law, written for a particular jurisdiction. It states that a permit system is only as good as the discipline applied to it, and describes how such systems degrade. |
| 9 | IOGP Report 510, *Operating Management System Framework for controlling risk and delivering high performance in the oil and gas industry*, 2014 | It is a framework of elements, not a procedure. It contains no instructions for a shift briefing, and this unit says so rather than pretending otherwise. |
| 10 | API, *Manual of Petroleum Measurement Standards*, Chapter 3, *Tank Gauging* | It assumes a current capacity table, a sound datum plate, an intact reference mark and calibrated equipment. It is a measurement standard for custody transfer, not an operating instruction, and it cannot help with a dead instrument. |
| 11 | The World Bank's *Zero Routine Flaring by 2030* initiative, launched 2015, and the annual *Global Gas Flaring Tracker Report* | The initiative is voluntary, with endorsers rather than obligations, and it addresses routine flaring only. The volumes are satellite-derived estimates with a stated uncertainty, not meter readings, and they say nothing about how completely the gas burned. |
| 12 | IOGP Report 456, *Process safety — recommended practice on key performance indicators*, revised 2018 | It is a recommended practice. The tier thresholds are definitions, and a count depends on them. Reporting is by member companies, so rates between companies are only comparable if both are counting the same events over comparable hours. |

# A Note on Everything Else in This Book

**The documents above are real.** Every other named thing in this book was written for it.

**Jazira Petroleum Operations** does not exist. Neither does the **Tell Sahra** field, the **Mureij**
gathering station, the **Sahra** central processing facility or the **Mureij Training Center**. **Nour
Al-Masri, Samir Haddad, Yusuf Khalaf, Samira Qassab, Ahmad Sweid, Nadia Shahin, Adeel Rahman, Yara
Barakat, Mariama Bah, Omar Dabbagh, Lars Eriksen, Ida Lindqvist, Wei Zhao** and **Tomas Varga** are
invented people. Every permit number, tank level, pressure reading, flaring rate, production figure
attributed to the company, log entry, incident and conversation in this book was written as teaching
material. None of it is a record of anything that happened anywhere.

**The real places are named as matters of public record only.** Deir ez-Zor, Hasakah, Homs, Banias,
the Syrian Petroleum Company, the Arab Gas Pipeline and the Kirkuk–Banias line appear in this book
because they are facts about the country and a book about this industry in this country that did not
name them would be strange. Nothing in this book describes their current condition, ownership,
operation or capacity, and nothing in it should be read as a statement about any of those things.

**The published production figures disagree.** The Starting Point unit is built on that fact rather
than around it. Where this book gives a figure for Syrian production or refining capacity, it gives
the figure, the year and the kind of source, and where sources differ it says so. That is the honest
position, and it is also the useful one: an operator who can say *the figures disagree and here is
why* is more use than one who has memorized the wrong one.

**This is a first-edition teaching draft.** It is not published, endorsed or approved by the Syrian
Ministry of Petroleum and Mineral Resources, by the Syrian Petroleum Company, by any other Syrian
state body, by any standards organization named in it, or by any company. It carries no official
status, and nothing in it is engineering, legal, safety, commercial or regulatory advice.

**And the standing line, once more, because it matters most in the book where the English is
simplest.** Safety information in a language course is not a substitute for a competent person, a
procedure and a permit. This book will teach you to say *the isolation is not confirmed*. It will not
tell you whether it is.

# At the End of the Book

You started twelve units ago with the words for what comes out of the ground. You can now say what
grade a crude is and why the grade matters; convert a barrel to a cubic meter and explain why two
careful people can disagree about the same volume; name the parts of a wellhead and say which one is
upstream of which; say who is on shift, who they report to and who may stop a job; recite nine rules
and say what they are not; put personal protective equipment last in a list of five controls; describe
a gas you must not trust your nose about; ask the four questions a permit answers; brief a work party
in five minutes and check that they heard you; read a gauge and say what a zero might mean; look at a
flame and say which of three reasons it is burning for; and hand a shift over in six parts, one of
which is *I do not know*.

That is not a small amount of English. It is also, deliberately, not a qualification in anything.

**Flowline 1 — *From Reservoir to Tank*** starts where the oil does: in the rock, under pressure,
nineteen hundred meters down, and works forward to the tank you learned to gauge in Unit 10. It begins
with a French engineer who in 1856 worked out how water moves through sand, and it ends with the
question of how much oil a company is allowed to say it has.

The flow does not stop at the end of a book. Neither does this.
