# Unit 1 · What Comes Out of the Ground
%%SUB Three things arrive at the same time in the same pipe, and only one of them is the product%%
%%GOALS
Name the three fluids in a well stream
Tell crude oil from condensate
Say what associated gas is
Use *be* and *have* to describe a fluid
Say what a standard's vocabulary is for
%%

A well does not produce oil. It produces a **stream**: oil, gas and water together, in one pipe, at
the same time. Everything in this book happens to that stream. The first job is to name its parts.

### 2 · Read

%%FIG ra01-f5 | A one-page internal note on headed paper: a title, a line giving the standard, its chapter and its publisher, a four-row table of defined terms with what each one means, a short note on standard conditions, a boxed paragraph headed a measurement vocabulary, not a dictionary, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

**2A  Read the note. Then answer the questions in section 3.**

> **JAZIRA PETROLEUM OPERATIONS — Mureij Training Center**
> **Note for new operators: what the words mean, and who says so**
> **Source: American Petroleum Institute, *Manual of Petroleum Measurement Standards*, Chapter 1,
> *Vocabulary*. Printed here in our own words.**
>
> The industry has a dictionary, and it is not a dictionary. It is a chapter of a measurement
> standard, and it exists because two companies buying and selling oil must mean the same thing by
> the same word.
>
> | The term | What the chapter defines it as |
> |---|---|
> | **crude oil** | the liquid hydrocarbon mixture as it comes from the reservoir, after water and sediment are removed |
> | **condensate** | a liquid at surface conditions that was in the gas phase in the reservoir |
> | **sediment and water** | the material in a crude sample that is not oil, reported as a percentage |
> | **custody transfer** | the point at which ownership of the quantity passes from one party to another |
>
> The chapter also fixes something that sounds small and is not. A volume of oil **changes with
> temperature**, so a barrel measured on a hot afternoon is not the same quantity as a barrel measured
> at dawn. The standard therefore defines **standard conditions** — a reference temperature and
> pressure — and every reported volume is corrected to them.
>
> That is why the lab report has two numbers for the same tank.
>
> **A MEASUREMENT VOCABULARY, NOT A DICTIONARY**
> This chapter defines terms **for the purposes of measurement**. It is written so that a quantity can
> be bought, sold and disputed, and the definitions are shaped by that job.
>
> It does not define everything people say on site. There is no entry for the word operators use for a
> leaking gland, and the standard is not trying to provide one.
>
> It is also an **American standard**, written in American units and for American practice. Much of
> the world uses it, and much of the world also uses standards from elsewhere; a definition that is
> authoritative in a contract is not automatically the law in a country.
>
> And a **definition is not a measurement**. The chapter says what *sediment and water* means. Whether
> last night's figure was right is a question about the laboratory, the sample and the person who took
> it — and that is Unit 10 and Book 2.
>
> *Our summary of a published standard, named with its chapter. Read the published chapter, and write
> down the edition you read.*

### 2 · One and many

| | |
|---|---|
