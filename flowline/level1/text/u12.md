# Unit 12 · One Shift, Start to Finish
%%SUB What a shift hands over, and what gets counted when it is finished%%
%%GOALS

A shift ends the way it began: one person telling another what is true right now. Then somebody
counts what happened. This unit is about both, and about why the counting is harder than it looks.

### 2 · Read

%%FIG ra12-f5 | A one-page internal note on headed paper: a title, a line giving the report, its publisher and its revision year, a four-row table of tiers, a two-row table contrasting leading and lagging, a boxed paragraph headed what a low number does not prove, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

**2A  Read the note. Then answer the questions in section 3.**

> **JAZIRA PETROLEUM OPERATIONS — Mureij Training Center**
> **Note for new operators: what gets counted, and what counting cannot do**
> **Source: International Association of Oil and Gas Producers, *Process safety — recommended practice on key performance indicators*, Report 456, revised 2018, aligned with the American Petroleum Institute's practice on the same subject. Printed here in our own words.**
>
> **1** The report sets out four **tiers** of process safety indicator. The first two count events. The
> other two count the state of the system meant to stop them.
>
> | Tier | What it counts |
> |---|---|
> | **Tier 1** | a bigger loss of containment: a release above a set quantity, or one causing injury, fire or serious damage |
> | **Tier 2** | a smaller loss of containment, above a lower set quantity |
> | **Tier 3** | a challenge to a safety system: a relief valve lifting, an alarm on a critical reading, a trip |
> | **Tier 4** | how well the system is run: overdue inspections, failed tests, permit quality, training |
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | **Lagging** | Tiers 1 and 2. Certain, comparable, and about the past. |
> | **Leading** | Tiers 3 and 4. Uncertain, local, and about what is coming. |
>
> **2** Tier 1 and 2 counts come as a rate: events per million hours worked. The hours are the
> denominator. A rate with no hours is not a number. It is a mood.
>
> **3** A plant can report no Tier 1 events for years and still be in trouble. This really happened. A refinery that killed fifteen people in 2005 had good injury figures before it. Personal
> safety and process safety are different things, and one was read as proof about the other.
>
> **4** The tiers are set by thresholds. That makes them comparable. It also makes them easy to game. An event one kilogram under the Tier 1 quantity is a Tier 2 event. The person who
> estimates the quantity knows that.
>
> **5** Counting is voluntary, and the hours differ. The association collects figures from its members.
> Compare two companies and you assume both count the same events over the same hours.
>
> **6** The leading indicators are the useful ones and the unpopular ones. Tier 4 counts the paperwork
> nobody enjoys: inspections overdue, tests failed, permits with no hand-back. They are
> unpopular because they are always bad, everywhere. The honest answer gets worse the moment you look
> properly.
>
> *Our summary of a published recommended practice, named with its publisher and its revision year. Read the published report, and write down the edition you read.*

### 1 · The grammar of the whole book

| From | | |
|---|---|---|
| Unit 1 | The crude **is** heavy. The wells **have** high water cut. | *be* and *have* |

| Unit 4 | Samira **reports to** the maintenance supervisor. | present simple at work |

### 📝 ON THE JOB

> **Say the number and the time together.** *The level is up* is a feeling. *Tank 2 rose by 1.3 meters,
> to 4.4, between 06:00 and 14:00* is a handover, and the next operator can act on it before they have
> finished their tea. And keep the last box. A handover with an *I do not know* in it is worth more
> than a tidy one, because the tidy one has the same gap in it and has not told you where.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

> A handover is a small ceremony, and every site does it differently: standing or sitting, written or
> spoken, two minutes or twenty, with or without the supervisor there. None of those is the right way.
> What matters is whether the person coming on can act without guessing, and whether the person going
> off can admit a gap without it costing them anything. The second part is cultural, not procedural,
> and no form can create it. **Ask your group: at your site, is it safe to say *I do not know*?**
