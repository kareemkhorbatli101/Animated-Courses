# Unit 6 · What You Wear, and Why
%%SUB Protective equipment is the last line of defense, and the reason it is last is written in a standard%%
%%GOALS
Name the items of personal protective equipment
Say the order of controls, from first to last
Say why protective equipment is last
Use *have to* and *need to*
Say what a management system standard does
%%

You put on coveralls, boots, a hard hat, glasses, gloves and a gas monitor. All of it is between you
and the hazard, and all of it is the **last** thing anybody should be relying on. A standard published
in 2018 puts the order in writing, and the order is the point of this unit.

### 2 · Read

%%FIG ra06-f5 | A one-page internal note on headed paper: a title, a line giving the standard, its number, its title and its year, a five-row table of the hierarchy in order, a short note on what a management system standard requires, a boxed paragraph headed a standard for a system, not for a hazard, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

**2A  Read the note. Then answer the questions in section 3.**

> **JAZIRA PETROLEUM OPERATIONS — Mureij Training Center**
> **Note for new operators: why your hard hat comes last**
> **Source: ISO 45001:2018, *Occupational health and safety management systems — Requirements with guidance for use*. Printed here in our own words.**
>
> **1** This standard says what an organization must have in place to manage health and safety. One
> clause is worth learning by heart. It sets the order for every hazard on this site.
>
> | Order | The control | Why it is where it is |
> |---|---|---|
> | **1** | **eliminate the hazard** | if the hazard is not there, nothing else is needed |
> | **2** | **substitute** a less hazardous process, material or piece of equipment | a smaller hazard needs smaller controls |
> | **3** | **engineering controls**, and changing how the work is organized | built in, and it works whether or not anybody remembers |
> | **4** | **administrative controls**, including training | works only when people follow it |
> | **5** | **personal protective equipment** | works only when it is worn, fitted and in date |
>
> **2** The order is the content. Anybody can write that list. The standard says to work **down** it,
> and not to stop at the first step that is easy.
>
> **3** Look at what happens as you go down. An engineering control still works at four in the
> morning, when the person who knew about it has gone home. A procedure works if somebody reads it.
> Gloves work if they are worn, and the right size.
>
> **Reader's Tip** — So the line to take from this unit is not *wear your equipment*. It is this: every
> time you rely on equipment, somebody above you decided against four better things, and it is fair to
> ask why.
>
> **4** The standard is for a system, not for a hazard. It does not say which gloves to use, or where
> to set a gas detector. That is in other documents, in the maker's data, and in a competent person's
> judgment. It is generic on purpose: the same standard is written for a refinery, a school and a bank.
>
> **5** It can be certified, and that means less than it sounds. A certificate says the system meets
> the standard. It does not say the site is safe, and the standard says so itself. And the order is a
> priority, not a ban. Equipment is not bad. It is the weakest of the five answers.
>
> *Our summary of a published standard, named with its number, its title and its year. Read the published standard, and write down the clause you read.*

### 📝 ON THE JOB

> **Check the date before you check the fit.** A monitor out of calibration, a harness past its
> inspection and an escape set with a low cylinder are all equipment that looks right and is not. The
> date is on the item, it takes four seconds, and it is the only part of your equipment that cannot
> be judged by looking at it.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

> Protective equipment is the most visible part of safety and the least effective, and that
> combination makes it politically useful. A site that issues everybody new coveralls looks safer than
> one that quietly replaced a coupling guard, and photographs better. The standard puts equipment last
> for a reason, and it is worth noticing when attention goes to the bottom of the staircase. **Ask
> your group: what was the last safety improvement you saw, and which level of the hierarchy was it
