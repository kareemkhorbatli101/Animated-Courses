# Unit 2 · The Barrel and the Cubic Meter
%%SUB Two units, two reference temperatures, and a correction factor nobody thinks about%%
%%GOALS
Convert between barrels and cubic meters
Say why a tonne needs a density
Say what a volume correction factor does
Read a figure aloud with its unit
Say what a convention is, as against a law of nature
%%

A barrel is forty-two gallons. A cubic meter is a cubic meter. The first is a convention from the
1860s, the second is a definition — and the industry uses both, corrects both to a reference
temperature, and disagrees about which temperature. None of this is difficult. All of it is on your
report.

### 2 · Read

%%FIG ra02-f5 | A one-page internal note on headed paper: a title, a line giving the standard, its chapter and the two bodies that publish it jointly, a worked conversion with four lines of arithmetic, a two-row table of the two reference temperatures, a boxed paragraph headed a table of factors, not a law of nature, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

**2A  Read the note. Then answer the questions in section 3.**

> **JAZIRA PETROLEUM OPERATIONS — Mureij Training Center**
> **Note for new operators: the two units, and the number in between**
> **Source: American Petroleum Institute, *Manual of Petroleum Measurement Standards*, Chapter 11.1, *Temperature and Pressure Volume Correction Factors*, published jointly with the ASTM and the Energy Institute as ASTM D1250 and IP 200. Printed here in our own words.**
>
> **1** A barrel is **forty-two US gallons**, about **159 liters** or **0.159 cubic meters**. The number comes from the oil trade of the 1860s. It was a decision, not a law.
>
> **2** One cubic meter is about **6.29 barrels**. So 900 cubic meters is about 5,660 barrels.
>
> **3** A tonne is a mass, not a volume. You cannot get from one to the other without a **density**.
> Light crude gives more barrels per tonne than heavy crude. So 100,000 tonnes is not one number
> of barrels.
>
> **4** Oil expands when it is warm. A tank read at thirty degrees gives a bigger number than the same
> oil at dawn. Both are right. So we correct every volume to a **reference temperature**, with a
> factor from this chapter's tables.
>
> | The tradition | Its reference temperature |
> |---|---|
> | the American tables | **60 degrees Fahrenheit** |
> | metric practice | **15 degrees Celsius** |
>
> **5** Those two are not the same temperature. Sixty degrees Fahrenheit is about 15.56 Celsius. Two
> parties can correct to their own base. Both are right, and the numbers differ. The contract says
> which one counts.
>
> **6** The tables give factors, not laws of nature. Each factor has an uncertainty, and it is not
> zero. Each one needs a density, and somebody measured that too. A correction on a bad
> density is a neat number that is wrong. The tables are for ordinary crudes. A very waxy or very heavy crude is a
> special case, and the chapter says so.
>
> *Our summary of a published standard, named with its chapter and the bodies that publish it. Read the published chapter, and write down the edition you read.*

### 📝 ON THE JOB

> **Never write a figure without its unit, and never write a converted figure without saying so.**
> Those two habits cost four words each. Between them they prevent the two arguments that eat the most
> time on a production site: a number nobody can place, and a number two people computed differently.
> If you converted it, write *converted from* and the original.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

> The barrel has outlived every reason for existing. No oil has been shipped in a forty-two-gallon
> wooden barrel for well over a century, the unit is not metric, the world's largest producers report

### 🧩 THINK IT THROUGH

%%FIG ra02-f16 | A desk at seven in the evening. **Two tickets for Tank 4 gave net volumes four cubic meters apart.** Neither was wrong. One corrected
to fifteen degrees Celsius and one to sixty degrees Fahrenheit, and the two reference temperatures are
about half a degree apart.

Four cubic meters in nine hundred is under half a percent. On a cargo of a hundred thousand tonnes it
is not.

**Discuss in your group.**

2 Both tickets are correct. Who decides which one is used?
3 The barrel has no technical justification left. Should the industry drop it?
