# Flowline Foundation
%%SUB English for the Oil and Gas Industries · Syrian Edition · CEFR A2%%

## How to use this book

This is the first book of six. It teaches the English of **the plant and the people**: the words for
what comes out of the ground, the parts of a wellhead, the units, the shift, the permit and the first
safety rules. Every unit is built on one real published document, named with its edition and its
date.

**Twelve units**, sixteen pages each, with the same six lessons in the same order every time.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Sixteen words, a second set of six, and a team research task |
| **2 · Reading** | One real standard, guide or report, with its date and its limits |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures, with the mistake each one causes |
| **4 · On Site** | Clear English, a dialogue, a culture check and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A model, then your own |
| **Team Up & Review** | A picture, a team project, a survey, and one problem with no easy answer |

**The audio.** 🔊 marks a track. 195 tracks, numbered `FL1-unit-track`.

## What is different about this book

**Every unit has one real source.** Not a summary of good practice: one document — an industry
standard, a safety guide, an inquiry report — named, dated, with the sentence in which its own
authors say what it does not cover. Eleven of this book's twelve units carry a published document;
the twelfth is a review.

**The sequence follows the oil, not the syllabus.** This book is the plant and the people, because
you cannot read a production report before you can name a separator. Book 2 goes down the hole, Book
3 into the pipe, Book 4 into the refinery, Book 5 into risk and integrity, and Book 6 into contracts
and institutions. Each book assumes the one before it.

**The English is simple and the engineering is not simplified.** At this level the sentences are
short. What they say is exact: which standard, which edition, what it covers, and what it leaves to a
competent person on site.

**A language course is not a safety course.** This book names hazards and quotes the documents that
govern them, so that you can read a permit and understand a briefing. It does not authorize you to do
anything. On site, the procedure, the permit and the competent person decide.

**Money has no symbol in this series.** Every figure is written without a currency sign, because the
unit depends on where you are and what year it is.

## The people

One operating company and one training center, in every unit of all six books.

**Jazira Petroleum Operations** is an operating company of about nineteen hundred people. It runs a
field cluster in the northeast — the **Tell Sahra** field, the **Mureij** gathering station and the
**Sahra** central processing facility — and it is **invented**, on purpose. So are the field, the
station and the facility.

**Nour Al-Masri** is a process engineer and runs the **Mureij Training Center**. She is the voice you
will hear most. **Samir Haddad** is the field operations superintendent, and in this book he is the
one who keeps asking *where does that number come from?* **Yusuf Khalaf** is the HSE officer.
**Samira Qassab** is an instrument technician. **Ahmad Sweid** is a mechanic. **Nadia Shahin** works
on metering and allocation. **Adeel Rahman** works on pipeline integrity. **Yara Barakat** is a
laboratory chemist. **Mariama Bah** is a reservoir engineer.

Four of the fourteen work for contractors, because reconstruction brings contractors: **Lars
Eriksen** leads a contractor's HSE team, **Ida Lindqvist** is a commissioning engineer, **Wei Zhao**
is a rotating-equipment specialist, and **Tomas Varga** is a corrosion specialist.

**Omar Dabbagh** joined four weeks ago. He is the newest operator at Tell Sahra, he has never worked
on a production site before, and he asks the questions this book is an answer to.

## Nothing here is official

This is an independent teaching draft. It is **not published or endorsed by the Syrian Ministry of
Petroleum and Mineral Resources, by the Syrian Petroleum Company, by any other state body, by any
standards organization named in it, or by any company**, and it carries no official status.
**Nothing in it is engineering, legal, safety, commercial or regulatory advice.**

The real places, companies and infrastructure named — **Deir ez-Zor**, **Hasakah**, **Homs**,
**Banias**, the **Arab Gas Pipeline**, the **Kirkuk–Banias pipeline**, the **Syrian Petroleum
Company** — are named as matters of public record only. Every person, field, station, figure and
incident attached to Jazira Petroleum Operations was written for this book.

The standards, guides and reports quoted in the reading texts are real, and each text names the
document, its edition or date, and the limits its own authors state.
