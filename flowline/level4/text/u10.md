### 2 · Read

%%FIG rd10-f5 | A one-page internal note on headed paper: a title, a line giving the guidelines, the three bodies that publish them and the edition year, a diagram of two boundaries round one group of operations with two totals, a table of source categories with the usual quantification method against each, a boxed paragraph headed almost nothing is weighed, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

**2A  Read the note. Then answer the questions in section 3.**

> **JAZIRA PETROLEUM OPERATIONS — Mureij Training Center**
> **Note for operators: the number that is almost never weighed**
> **Source: ***Petroleum industry guidelines for reporting greenhouse gas emissions***, **second
> edition, 2011**, published jointly by IPIECA, the American Petroleum Institute and the
> International Association of Oil and Gas Producers. Printed here in our own words.**
>
> **Two questions have to be answered before any arithmetic.** Which emissions are **ours**, and
> **how** will each kind be quantified.
>
> **THE BOUNDARY**
> A company is rarely a single thing. It operates some assets, part-owns others, and buys services
> from more. So it has to decide which emissions to count as its own, and there are recognized ways
> of doing it.
>
> | The basis | What you count |
> |---|---|
> | **operational control** | everything from operations you operate, at one hundred percent |
> | **equity share** | your percentage share of every operation you have an interest in, whether you operate it or not |
>
> A company that operates a plant it owns half of counts **all** of it under the first basis and
> **half** of it under the second. A company that owns half of a plant somebody else operates counts
> **none** of it under the first and **half** under the second.
>
> **The totals differ, often by a large fraction, and nothing physical has changed.** Both bases are
> legitimate. The guidelines are clear that a company must **state which one it has used**, because a
> total without a basis cannot be compared with anything.
>
> **THE SOURCE CATEGORIES**
>
> | Category | What it is | Usually quantified by |
> |---|---|---|
> | **combustion** | fuel burned on purpose | the **metered fuel** and its composition |
> | **flaring** | gas burned at a flare | a measured or estimated flare rate |
> | **venting** | gas released unburned | engineering calculation, often from design data |
> | **fugitives** | leaks from flanges, valves and seals | **emission factors** and component counts |
> | **mobile** | vehicles and equipment | fuel purchased |
> | **indirect** | energy bought in | the supplier's figures and a grid factor |
>
> **ALMOST NOTHING IS WEIGHED**
> This is the sentence to take away. The guidelines set out a preference order for quantification:
> **direct measurement** where it is practical, then **mass balance or engineering calculation**, then
> **published emission factors**.
>
> In practice, the great majority of reported emissions come from the **second and third** of those.
> And the quality is extremely uneven:
>
> **Combustion is good.** Fuel to a furnace is metered and its composition is known, so the carbon
> dioxide figure is close to arithmetic.
>
> **Flaring is moderate.** If the flare gas is metered it is reasonable; if the rate is estimated, less
> so — and whether it burned completely is another question again.
>
> **Venting is poor to moderate.** Vents are rarely metered. The figure is usually calculated from
> design data and operating assumptions.
>
> **Fugitives are the worst.** Nobody can weigh a leak from a flange. The figure comes from counting
> components and multiplying by published factors, which were derived from surveys of other plants,
> and the uncertainty on that is large.
>
> So a single company total is an aggregate in which one part is nearly arithmetic and another part is
> a factor from somebody else's survey. **Reporting one total with no indication of which parts are
> which is technically complete and practically misleading**, and the guidelines ask for uncertainty
> to be addressed for that reason.
>
> **CONSISTENCY, AND THE RESTATEMENT**
> The number's real value is comparing a company **with itself**, year to year. That only works if the
> boundary and the methods stay the same. When they change — an asset is sold, a boundary basis moves,
> a better factor is adopted — the earlier years have to be **restated** and the change explained.
>
> That paragraph is the most-skipped in corporate reporting, and a fall in a reported total that is
> entirely explained by a restatement is one of the commonest misreadings of this kind of number.
>
> **And its own status.** These are **voluntary industry guidelines**, not a regulation. Where a
> regulator requires reporting, the regulator's rules govern, and they may define boundaries, factors
> and categories differently. A company can therefore honestly publish **two different totals** in two
> documents in the same year, and both can be right.
>
> *Our summary of published industry guidelines, named with their publishers and their edition. Read
> the published guidelines, and read the section on uncertainty.*

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

> Emissions reporting is where engineering meets public argument, and the argument is almost never
> about what engineers think it is about. Most of the suspicion directed at these numbers is not
> suspicion of the arithmetic; it is suspicion of the boundary, and it is well founded, because the
> boundary is a choice and the choice affects the answer. The defense is not to insist the numbers are
> accurate. It is to publish the basis, the methods, the uncertainty and the split between real change
> and boundary change — at which point an honest company has nothing left to be suspected of, and the
> conversation can be about emissions.

properly, on a stated basis, and the restatement note was in the back of the report where the
guidelines ask for it to be.

The press release quoted fourteen percent. Twelve of those points were the sale of an asset whose
emissions continue under a new owner. Two points were a real reduction that somebody had worked hard
for and nobody has mentioned since.

**Discuss in your group.**

1 Did the company mislead anybody?
2 The restatement note was in the report. Why did it not travel?
3 Two points were real and are now invisible. Who lost by that?
4 What would you put in the press release?
