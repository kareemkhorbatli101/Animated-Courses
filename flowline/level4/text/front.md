## How to use this book

This is the fourth book of six. Book 3 ended at the refinery gate. This one goes inside: the test
that decides what a barrel is made of, the textbook that taught the industry how to design a plant,
the chemistry that doubled what a barrel is worth, the law that fixed what may be sold, the two
numbers on a fuel pump, the specification a ship's engine depends on, the difference between what a
plant was built to do and what it does, the heat you already have, the system that catches everything
when it goes wrong, the emissions nobody weighs, the label on the tanker, and the number traders look
at every morning that almost nobody on the plant has ever seen.

**Twelve units**, twenty-two pages each, with the same six lessons in the same order every time.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Sixteen words, a second set of six, and a team research task |
| **2 · Reading** | One real test method, textbook, law, specification or guideline |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures, with the mistake each one causes |
| **4 · On Site** | Clear English, a dialogue, a culture check and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A model, then your own |
| **Team Up & Review** | A picture, a team project, a survey, and one problem with no easy answer |

**The audio.** 🔊 marks a track. 195 tracks, numbered `FL4-unit-track`.

## What is different about this book

**This is the book where money appears.** Everything up to here could be judged by whether it worked.
A refinery is judged by whether the products are worth more than the crude, and the last unit of this
book is the number that judgment is made with. The engineering does not change when money enters the
room; the conversations do, and this book teaches the English of both.

**Two of the twelve sources are not technical documents at all.** Unit 3 is a professional society's
commemoration of an invention, which is a different kind of writing from a standard and has to be
read differently. Unit 12 is a government statistical agency explaining a market indicator to the
public, which is the plainest writing in the whole series.

**One of them is a law, and it is somebody else's law.** Unit 4 is a European directive, which does
not apply here. It is in the book because a refinery that wants to export has to meet the
specification of the place it is exporting to, which is the single most important commercial fact
about product quality anywhere in the world.

**And the thread running through the book is measurement.** Almost every number in a refinery is
defined by the test that produces it. An octane number is what a particular engine from the 1930s
says it is. A boiling range is what a particular glass apparatus says it is. A reported emission is
almost always calculated rather than weighed. None of that makes the numbers worthless. All of it
means the test belongs beside the figure.

**A language course is not a safety course.** This book quotes a relief system standard and a
dangerous goods classification. It does not authorize you to design, classify, label or ship
anything.

**Money has no symbol in this series** — including in the unit that is entirely about money.

## The people

The same company and the same fourteen people. Jazira Petroleum Operations does not own a refinery,
and that is deliberate: the people in this book are learning the downstream from outside it, which is
the position almost everybody in this industry is actually in.

**Yara Barakat** carries this book. She is the laboratory chemist, she spends four units explaining
that a number is what a method says it is, and her line is *tell me which test*.

**Nour Al-Masri** runs the **Mureij Training Center**. **Samir Haddad** is the field operations
superintendent, and in this book he is the one who asks what things are worth. **Yusuf Khalaf** is the
HSE officer. **Samira Qassab** is an instrument technician. **Ahmad Sweid** is a mechanic. **Nadia
Shahin** works on metering and allocation and in this book on product accounting. **Adeel Rahman**
works on integrity. **Mariama Bah** is the reservoir engineer. **Lars Eriksen**, **Ida Lindqvist**,
**Wei Zhao** and **Tomas Varga** work for contractors. **Omar Dabbagh** is now sixteen months in.

This is an independent teaching draft. It is **not published or endorsed by the Syrian Ministry of
Petroleum and Mineral Resources, by the Syrian Petroleum Company, by any other state body, by any
standards organization, professional society, statistical agency, regulator or legislature named in
it, or by any company**, and it carries no official status. **Nothing in it is engineering, legal,

The real places and infrastructure named — **Homs**, **Banias**, **Deir ez-Zor**, **Hasakah**, the
**Syrian Petroleum Company** — are named as matters of public record only. Where this book gives a
figure for a refinery's capacity it gives the figure, the year and the kind of source, and says that
published figures differ.

The test methods, textbooks, laws, specifications, papers and guidelines quoted in the reading texts
are real, and each text names the document, its edition or date, and the limits its own authors
state.
