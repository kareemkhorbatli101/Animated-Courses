# Grammar Reference

Everything in this book that is a structure rather than a fact. Each unit's two structures are set
out with what they do, and with the mistake each one produces when it is left out.

## Starting Point · Naming the four claims on a barrel

Four claims, and none of them is natural: whoever spent the money, the state, the companies, and
everybody else. **The terms are not natural facts. They were negotiated** — by whom, when, under what
conditions, and with what information.

**The mistake:** treating a term as a given, when somebody argued for it on a particular afternoon in
a particular year of the price cycle.

## Unit 1 · Shares and conditions; denominators

**Shares and conditions.** *up to a maximum of* · *whichever is the lower* · *after deduction of* ·
*net of* · *in the event that production exceeds* · *on a sliding scale from X to Y*.

**Denominators.** *the take is X percent of profits* · *Y percent of gross revenue* · *entitlement
is Z percent of volume* · *most of which is cost oil* · *all four figures are correct*.

> One page of terms produces four correct percentages with four different denominators. A government
> announces one, a company announces another, and a newspaper prints a third.

**The mistake:** *government take is sixty-two percent*. Of what, at what rate, at what cost level?
**A take figure with no denominator is not a figure.**

## Unit 2 · Reporting what an agreement provides; obligation and permission

**Reporting.** *the agreement provides that* · *under clause N* · *clause N limits* · *clause N is
silent on* · *I am reading clause N as*.

**Obligation and permission.** *shall* · *may* · *subject to* · *is entitled to* · *notwithstanding* ·
*for the avoidance of doubt*.

> *For the avoidance of doubt* exists because somebody once argued the opposite. Read every one of
> them as the scar of an old dispute.

**The mistake:** *the contract says we have to*. Which clause, and is it *shall* or *may*?

## Unit 3 · Rights and obligations; escalation

**Rights and obligations.** *shall* within a stated period · *may* · *is entitled to* · *is obliged
to* · *unless otherwise agreed* · *at its sole risk and expense*.

**Escalation.** *in the first instance* · *if the default continues* · *thereafter* · *at that point*
· *ultimately* · *the provisions are severe on purpose, because*.

> Do the pass mark arithmetic before any meeting and write it on one line. It tells you who you are
> actually negotiating with.

**The mistake:** writing *in default* with nothing beside it. Some readers hear *a few days late* and
others hear *about to lose a field*. **Name the step.**

## Unit 4 · Prohibition; reporting a demand

**Prohibition.** *it is an offense to* · *no payment shall be made* · *under no circumstances* ·
*irrespective of whether* · *it is not a defense that* · *this applies to payments made through
agents*.

**Reporting a demand.** the **time and date** · what was asked, **in the words used** · what you said,
**in the words used** · *I made no payment and I promised nothing* · when the note was written · to
whom it was reported.

> Two sentences, learned by heart, in the languages you work in: *I am not able to do that* and *I
> would have to ask*. Neither accuses anybody and both move the decision somewhere it can be made.

**The mistake:** *somebody tried to extort money from us*. Quote what was said and what you said, and
interpret nothing.

## Unit 5 · Defining; stating a basis

**Defining.** *for the purposes of this report, X means* · *this definition excludes* · *it includes*
· *a locally registered company is not necessarily* · *where a definition is not stated, a figure
cannot be compared*.

**Stating a basis.** *on a spend basis* · *on a headcount basis* · *on a value retained basis* · *all
three figures describe the same operation* · *the gap between the first and the third is*.

> Three clauses beside every local content figure: the **definition**, the **basis**, and what it
> **excludes**. They turn a slogan into a number.

**The mistake:** *local content is sixty-eight percent*. On which basis, under which definition,
including what?

## Unit 6 · Disclosure; explaining a difference

**Disclosure.** *is disclosed* · *is reported as* · *was reconciled against* · *remains unreconciled*
· *falls below the materiality threshold* · *is outside the agreed scope*.

**Explaining a difference.** *is consistent with* · the two **dates** · *no conclusion is drawn as to*
· *a reconciliation compares two reported figures and verifies neither* · *further work would be
required to* · *I am offering an explanation, not a finding*.

> Give the two dates. *Paid 27 December, received 4 January* is eight words and it closes the question
> before anybody opens it.

**The mistake:** *money is missing*. A difference between two reported figures is a difference.

## Unit 7 · Correlation and causation; objecting fairly

**Correlation and causation.** *is associated with* · *is correlated with* · *controlling for* · *does
not establish that* · *the direction of causation* · *a plausible mechanism is*.

**Objecting fairly.** *the objection is that* · *if that is right* · *several later studies found* ·
*that does not make the original result worthless* · *it makes it a result in an argument* · *on the
evidence I have read, probably*.

> Separate the **contested** part from the **arithmetic** part. Whether resources cause slower growth
> is argued about. Whether resource revenue is volatile is not.

**The mistake:** *research proves* in one direction, *it has been disproved* in the other. **Contested
and disproved are different words.**

## Unit 8 · Attributing an argument; naming a kind of claim

**Attributing.** *the argument runs that* · *on this account* · *the author contends that* · *at its
strongest, the argument is that* · *the claim is not that X but that Y* · *critics object that*.

**Naming the kind.** *this is an argument rather than a measurement* · *it is tested by whether the
steps follow* · *there is no coefficient to check* · *it is not predictive* · *a counterexample is
relevant but not decisive*.

> Four moves, in order: state it at its strongest, name the kind of claim, name one published
> objection, then say what you think. The first move is the one everybody skips.

**The mistake:** *this book proves*. It is an argument, and that is usually not even its claim.

## Unit 9 · Assumptions and models; now against later

**Assumptions.** *assuming* · *on the assumption that* · *the model abstracts from* · *in the absence
of* · *the result is sensitive to* · *the model is not intended to*.

**Now against later.** *a barrel produced this year cannot be produced in twenty years* · *the
opportunity cost of producing now is* · *accelerating raises X and reduces Y* · *the gain is this
decade's and the cost is the next one's* · *lost, not deferred*.

> Two figures, two periods, on the same page. With both, it is a decision. With one, it is an
> announcement.

**The mistake:** *accelerating increases revenue*. It increases **this year's** revenue, and some of
the barrels are **lost** rather than deferred.

## Unit 10 · Obligation and derogation; who bears a cost

**Obligation and derogation.** *is prohibited* · *shall not be* · *by way of derogation* · *may be
permitted where* · *subject to an assessment and consultation* · *there is a presumption against*.

**Who bears a cost.** *is recoverable* · *recoverable costs come out before the split* · *therefore
the fiscal split determines who bears it* · *the obligation falls due when there is little left to
recover it from* · *a cost recoverable in principle and unrecoverable in practice lands somewhere*.

> Publish a bad estimate. A wide number becomes an argument; a missing number stays missing for twenty
> years.

**The mistake:** *the company pays for decommissioning*. The company pays the invoices. **The fiscal
system decides who bears it.**

## Unit 11 · Recommending; sequencing

**Recommending.** *should* · *it is recommended that* · *good practice is to* · *the charter
recommends* · *this is a recommendation, not an obligation*.

**Sequencing.** *before X can be done, Y must* · *the precondition for* · *in sequence* · *each
decision constrains the next* · *where capacity is limited, begin with* · *read the chain backwards
from what is going wrong*.

> Color your own chain on one page: what can start this year, what has no owner, what is political.
> Then find the link that belongs to you.

**The mistake:** *we should implement all twelve*. With what capacity, in what order, owned by whom?

## Unit 12 · A long argument; a provisional position

**A long argument, in five moves.** *the question is* · *at its strongest, the argument against me is*
· *I accept that* · *three things follow* · *on the evidence I have seen, and provisionally* · *what I
would do first is*.

**A provisional position.** *probably, and here is why* · *I would change my mind if* · *I am less
sure about X than Y* · *I have no view I am entitled to express on* · *but the figure in front of me
has no denominator, and that is mine*.

> *I would change my mind if* is the hardest sentence in the book. It tells the room you are arguing
> rather than announcing, and it forces you to find out whether you have a position or a preference.

**The mistake:** *I am just a technical person, so I have no view*. You have no standing on some of it
and **complete** standing on the rest.

# The British Layer

This book is written in American English, and the switch to British English is a layer rather than a
correction. The patterns are printed in full in Book 1. The endings this book touches most often are
the **-ize** and **-yze** verbs and their nouns, the **-se** and **-ce** pairs in *license*,
*defense*, *offense* and *practice*, the **-or** and **-our** pairs in *labor*, *behavior* and
*favor*, the doubling of a single **l**, and **program**, **gray** and **skeptical**.

**Four things never change, in either layer.**

**Names of organizations, treaties, standards and publications.** An organization's own legal name
carries its own spelling, whichever layer the surrounding text is in. That rule matters more in this
book than anywhere else in the series, because several bodies cited here spell their own names with
endings this layer would otherwise change, and renaming a cited international organization is a far
worse error than a spelling inconsistency.

**Defined terms in an agreement.** Where a contract or a model form defines a term, the defined term
keeps the agreement's spelling inside quotation and inside any reference to that agreement. A model
operating agreement's standard of liability is the standard that document uses, spelled as that
document spells it, and this book does not restyle a term of art.

**Names of people, journals, inquiries, institutes and universities.** Auty. Hotelling. Humphreys,
Sachs and Stiglitz. The journals and the publishing bodies.

**Quoted figures and their units.** A percentage, a ratio, a share or a period is reproduced as its
source gives it, with the denominator this book insists on written beside it.

**One deliberate choice, which is not a spelling layer.** Money is written with **no currency symbol
and no currency name** anywhere in this series, including in this book, which is entirely about money.
Every figure here is a percentage, a share, a ratio, a multiple or a period. Where a source states a
sum, this book says that a sum was stated. The reasoning is what transfers between countries; a
currency is not.

# Sources

One real document for each unit, named with its author or publisher and its date, and with the limits
its own authors state. Nothing here is official and nothing here is legal, fiscal, commercial or
investment advice.

| Unit | The document | What it says it does not cover |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Daniel Johnston, *International Petroleum Fiscal Systems and Production Sharing Contracts*, PennWell, **1994** | It explains **mechanics**, not any particular agreement. A published **take** figure is almost never comparable with another: it does not state its **denominator**, its **production rate**, its **cost level**, or whether bonuses, rentals and obligations are included — and it contains **no time**, so a high take with fast cost recovery can be worth more than a low take with slow recovery. The book's illustrative country data is **thirty years old** and terms are renegotiated |
| **2** | Jenik Radon, "The ABCs of Petroleum Contracts", in *Covering Oil: A Reporter's Guide to Energy and Development*, Open Society Institute and Revenue Watch Institute, **2005** | A **primer**, not a legal text: it explains categories, and the operative words in any agreement are that agreement's. It describes practice **as at 2005**, and practice has moved. It is written from a **stated position** — that these documents should be readable by the public — which is legitimate and is not neutrality. **And no agreement is typical** |
| **3** | The **Model International Operating Agreement**, Association of International Petroleum Negotiators, **2012** form | **Not a contract**: a starting point for negotiation, published with **bracketed alternatives** because the parties are expected to choose between them. **It does not bind the state** — it governs the relationship among contractor parties. It is a practitioners' publication, so its defaults reflect the concerns of the parties who were in the room. **And it cannot make a venture work**: everything in it is a procedure for a disagreement |
| **4** | The **Convention on Combating Bribery of Foreign Public Officials in International Business Transactions**, adopted under the OECD, **1997** | **It does not itself create an offense**: it obliges each Party to criminalize the conduct under its own law, so what can prosecute anybody is a **national statute**. It addresses the **supply side**, not the demand for a bribe, and **foreign** public officials, not domestic or private-sector bribery. **Enforcement varies enormously between Parties**, and the Convention's own peer reviews say so by name. **And it cannot help anybody at a barrier** — that is what the four steps are for |
| **5** | *Local content: a guidance document for the oil and gas industry*, **IPIECA**, second edition **2016** | **Not a standard and not law**: each country's own regulations define *local* for legal purposes and they differ, so a company in six countries is measuring six things. **It cannot make a target meaningful** — a spend-basis target can be met by routing purchases through a locally registered intermediary, which builds nothing and is the target working exactly as written. **It cannot make figures comparable.** And it cannot create demand that **outlasts the field** |
| **6** | The **EITI Standard**, Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative, **2023** edition | **Voluntary at country level**, and it covers what the multi-stakeholder group agrees is **in scope**, above an agreed **materiality threshold** — so an absent payment is not necessarily a payment not made. **A reconciliation is not an audit**: it compares two reported figures and verifies neither. The data is **a year or two old**. It cannot create the **third chair** where civil space is constrained. **And disclosure is not accountability**: it is a precondition, and it quietly depends on audit institutions, a parliament, courts and a press that it does not provide |
| **7** | Richard M. Auty, *Sustaining Development in Mineral Economies: The Resource Curse Thesis*, Routledge, **1993**, with Jeffrey D. Sachs and Andrew M. Warner, NBER Working Paper **5398**, **1995** | The measure is **resource exports as a share of GDP** — a ratio that falls when other sectors grow, so it may partly measure **failure elsewhere** rather than endowment; several later studies using **subsoil wealth** measures found the relationship weakened or reversed. Results move with the **period**. A large later literature argues **institutions decide the sign**. The **counterexamples are not marginal**. And cross-country regression establishes **association, not direction**. **The curse is contested; the volatility is arithmetic** |
| **8** | Timothy Mitchell, *Carbon Democracy: Political Power in the Age of Oil*, Verso, **2011** | **An argument, not a measurement**: there is no coefficient to check, and it is tested by whether the steps follow and the cases fit. It is **contested in print** by historians and political scientists — that it weights material form over ideas and institutions, that the history is more varied, and that oil workers have exercised considerable leverage in some countries and periods. **It is not the claim it is usually reported as making**: not that oil causes dictatorship, but a claim about **capacities**. And it is **not predictive** |
| **9** | Harold Hotelling, "The Economics of Exhaustible Resources", *Journal of Political Economy*, **1931** | It assumes a **known, fixed stock**, no new discoveries, no technical change, constant extraction cost, perfect capital markets, a known demand curve, no uncertainty and no political risk — and **six of those eight are false for any real field**. Reserves are an **economic** category, not a geological one. Real resource prices **have not followed the rule**. **The model has no reservoir in it**, so it cannot see that producing too fast **loses** barrels rather than deferring them. And it is **silent on who owns it**. It is a **benchmark**, never a prediction — and step one survives everything: a barrel produced now cannot be produced later |
| **10** | **OSPAR Decision 98/3** on the Disposal of Disused Offshore Installations, OSPAR Commission, **1998** | **Regional: it binds its Contracting Parties in the North-East Atlantic maritime area and does not apply here**; onshore obligations come from national law and from contracts. **It does not settle the environmental comparison** — it adopts a **presumption against** leaving in place as policy, and does not claim to have resolved every case scientifically. **And it says nothing about who pays**, which is the question that matters most in a producing country: where decommissioning is a **recoverable cost**, the **fiscal split** decides who bears it, and the obligation falls due when there is almost nothing left to recover it from |
| **11** | The ***Natural Resource Charter***, second edition **2014**, Natural Resource Governance Institute | **Not law and not a standard**: nobody is assessed against it. **It cannot sequence itself for you** — twelve recommendations, and a country has capacity for a few. It is written from a **stated position** about transparency, competition and accountability, argued for rather than assumed. **And it assumes you can start at the beginning**, which almost nobody can: for a country decades into production it is not a plan but a **map of where the constraints came from** |
| **12** | *Escaping the Resource Curse*, edited by Macartan Humphreys, Jeffrey D. Sachs and Joseph E. Stiglitz, Columbia University Press, **2007** | **Not a single argument**: an edited collection whose chapters do not fully agree, and reading it as one position is a misreading. **It does not settle the empirical question** of Unit 7. Its authors hold a **stated position** about institutions, competition and transparency, which is contested by others. **And it cannot make the sequence available to you**: it is clearest about decisions taken before production starts, and for a reader thirty years in, the honest advice is smaller — fix what is still open, document what is not, and do not pretend the earlier decisions can be unmade |

**Three of these twelve are economics or political argument rather than technical documents**, and
each unit prints the objections. Unit 7 is a measured result with a serious measurement objection
against it. Unit 8 is an argument about mechanism, contested in print. Unit 9 is a model whose
assumptions are almost never met and whose question cannot be avoided. A reader who finishes this book
should be able to state each one at its strongest, say what kind of claim it is, name one published
objection, and only then say what they think.

# A Note on Everything Else in This Book

**Jazira Petroleum Operations does not exist**, and in this book that matters more than in any other.
Every fiscal term, share, rate, ceiling, split, pass mark, threshold, percentage, interest, payment,
estimate, obligation and license condition in it was **written for this book**. They were constructed
to be arithmetically consistent with each other so that the exercises work, and they are **not the
terms of any real agreement anywhere**. The waterfall in Unit 1, the clause numbers in Unit 2, the
three interests in Unit 3, the sixty-eight percent in Unit 5, the reconciliation in Unit 6 and the
abandonment estimate in Unit 10 are all invented.

**The real places and institutions are named as public record only.** Deir ez-Zor, Hasakah, Homs,
Banias, the Syrian Petroleum Company, the Arab Gas Pipeline, the Kirkuk–Banias line. Nothing in this
book describes the terms on which anybody operates anywhere, and nothing in it describes any real
negotiation, payment, dispute or liability.

**The documents are real and so are their limits.** Each reading text names its source with its author
or publisher and its date, prints what that source says it does not cover, and is written in our own
words rather than quoted at length. Two of the twelve do not apply in this country and say so on the
page. Three are arguments rather than findings and say so on the page.

**Unit 4 is written carefully and deliberately.** It describes a public international convention, what
it obliges states to do, and what to do when somebody asks you for a payment. It is **not legal
advice**, it does not state the law of any country, and the only operational guidance in it is the one
thing every compliance function agrees on: **do not decide alone, and write down what happened.**

**And nothing in this book is advice.** Not legal, not contractual, not fiscal, not tax, not
commercial, not financial, not investment, not regulatory. It is a language course about how to read
documents of this kind, how to check an arithmetic claim, and how to write carefully about something
that is not settled.

# At the End of the Series

**Six books.** The wellhead and the words. From reservoir to tank. Pipes, pumps and pressure. The
refinery and the barrel. Risk, integrity and the permit. And this one.

**What the series was actually teaching** was never the plant. It was a habit of language with a habit
of mind inside it: **say what you know, say how you know it, say what you do not know, and say it in
that order**. Seventy-two real documents in six books, and every single one of them was introduced the
same way — named, dated, and printed with the limits its own authors state. That was not politeness.
It was the method.

**The six books in one line each.**

**Book 1:** a correct number can be useless. **Book 2:** five weak inputs stand behind one honest
reserves figure. **Book 3:** eleven failures, and not one of them was steel. **Book 4:** the
definition never travels with the number. **Book 5:** the paper is not the protection; the
conversation it forces is. **Book 6:** the terms were negotiated, and a percentage without its
denominator is not a figure.

**What you have, that you did not have.** A hundred and ninety-two units of plant and process English,
and underneath it a register: *approximately*, *as reported by*, *is attributed to*, *within about*,
*it was nobody's job to*, *has been proved by*, *no conclusion can be drawn about*, *whichever is the
lower*, *on the assumption that*, *at its strongest the argument is*, *provisionally*, and *I would
change my mind if*. Twelve phrases. Between them they are the whole difference between being believed
for thirty years and being believed until somebody checks.

**And the last thing.** Six books have been careful to say that nothing in them is advice, that every
document has limits, and that almost every important question in this industry is contested. All of
that is true. But there is a short list of things in these six books that are **not** contested by
anybody — a figure needs its definition, an obligation needs a date, a demand needs a record, an
isolation needs proving, a plan needs exercising, a liability needs an estimate — and every one of them
is available to you, in your own job, without anybody's permission, on **Monday**.

There is no Book 7. There is a Monday.
