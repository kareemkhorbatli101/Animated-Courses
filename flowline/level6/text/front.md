# Flowline 5
%%SUB English for the Oil and Gas Industries · Syrian Edition · CEFR B2+ to C1%%

## How to use this book

This is the sixth and last book, and it is the only one with no equipment in it at all.

Books 1 to 4 built the physical chain, from the rock to the barrel. Book 5 asked how organizations
that know all of that still have accidents. This book asks the question underneath all of them:
**what is this industry for, who gets what from it, and what happens to a country that has it**.

Twelve units. A book that explains what a fiscal system actually is. A primer written to let
journalists read a contract. A model operating agreement that governs how companies share a field. A
convention about bribery. A guide to local content and why it is so hard to measure. A transparency
standard. The economics literature on whether resource wealth helps or harms. A work of political
history about energy and democracy. A paper from 1931 on how fast a finite resource should be used
up. A decision about what you do with a platform when the field is finished. A charter of twelve
precepts. And a book of essays on whether any of it can be escaped.

**Twelve units**, twenty-two pages each, with the same six lessons in the same order every time.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Sixteen words, a second set of six, and a team research task |
| **2 · Reading** | One real book, paper, model agreement, convention, standard or charter |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures, with the mistake each one causes |
| **4 · On Site** | Clear English, a dialogue, a culture check and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A model, then your own |
| **Team Up & Review** | A picture, a team project, a survey, and one problem with no easy answer |

**The audio.** 🔊 marks a track. 195 tracks, numbered `FL6-unit-track`.

## What is different about this book

**The English is the hardest in the series, and the reason is the subject.** Everything here is
contested. A technical standard tells you what to do. A fiscal system tells you what somebody
negotiated, and every number in it was argued over by people with opposing interests, all of whom
were acting reasonably. So the register of this book is **argument with attribution**: this is what
the document says, this is who wrote it and why, this is what is disputed, and this is what I think,
marked as what I think.

**Money appears throughout, and it still has no symbol.** The series rule holds to the last page.
Figures are percentages, ratios, shares and multiples, and where a source states a sum this book says
that a sum was stated. The reasoning is what transfers between countries; a currency is not.

**Two units are economics, and the book does not pretend they are settled.** Unit 7 is the resource
curse literature, where the original cross-country results are real, influential and heavily
contested, and where later work finds that institutions change the answer. Unit 9 is a paper from
1931 whose assumptions are almost never met and whose idea you cannot think without. Both units print
the objections.

**One unit is about bribery**, and it is written carefully. It describes a public international
convention, what it obliges states to do, and the practical consequences for a company and for a
person asked for a payment. It is not legal advice, it does not tell you what the law is in any
country, and the only safe operational advice in it is the one line every compliance function in the
world agrees on: **do not decide alone, and write down what happened.**

**And one unit is about the end.** Unit 10 is what you do with the steel when the field is finished,
which is the only subject in the series where the cost is certain, the benefit is invisible and the
decision is taken by people who will never see the consequence.

**Nothing in this book is legal, commercial, fiscal, financial or investment advice.** It is a
language course about how to read documents of this kind and how to write carefully about them.

## The people

The same fourteen people, five years on, and in this book most of them are out of their depth, which
is the point.

**Nadia Shahin** carries this book. She came into the series doing metering and allocation, which is
the arithmetic of whose barrel it is, and in this book she does the same arithmetic on revenue. Her
line is *whose barrel is it, and whose was it before?*

**Mariama Bah**, the reservoir engineer, carries the other half. She is the only person in the series
who thinks in decades by profession, and her question is *over what horizon?*

**Nour Al-Masri** runs the **Mureij Training Center**. **Samir Haddad** is the field operations
superintendent. **Yusuf Khalaf** is the HSE officer. **Yara Barakat** is the laboratory chemist.
**Samira Qassab** is an instrument technician. **Ahmad Sweid** is a mechanic. **Adeel Rahman** works
on integrity. **Lars Eriksen**, **Ida Lindqvist**, **Wei Zhao** and **Tomas Varga** work for
contractors. **Omar Dabbagh** is five years in, and in the last unit of the last book he gets the
final word.

## Nothing here is official

This is an independent teaching draft. It is **not published or endorsed by the Syrian Ministry of
Petroleum and Mineral Resources, by the Syrian Petroleum Company, by any other state body, by any
standards organization, industry association, international organization, United Nations body, OECD
body, publisher, university or non-governmental organization named in it, or by any company**, and it
carries no official status.

**Nothing in it is legal, contractual, fiscal, commercial, financial, investment, tax or regulatory
advice.** No figure in it describes the terms of any real agreement, and nothing in it should be used
to negotiate, interpret, audit or comply with anything.

The real places and institutions named — **Deir ez-Zor**, **Hasakah**, **Homs**, **Banias**, the
**Syrian Petroleum Company**, the **Arab Gas Pipeline**, the **Kirkuk–Banias line** — are named as
matters of public record only. **Jazira Petroleum Operations and every field, station, person,
contract, figure, license round and negotiation attached to it were written for this book.** Every
fiscal term, share, rate and percentage in it was invented to be arithmetically consistent and is not
the term of any real agreement anywhere.

The books, papers, model agreements, conventions, standards, charters and assessments quoted in the
reading texts are real, and each text names the document, its edition or date, its author or
publisher, and the limits its own authors state. Two of the twelve are economics whose conclusions
are contested; both units print the objections. One is a work of political argument; the unit says
so.
