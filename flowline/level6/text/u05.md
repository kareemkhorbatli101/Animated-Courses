# Unit 5 · Local, and What That Means
%%SUB Sixty-eight percent, and almost all of it is a margin%%
%%GOALS

A figure is on the front of the annual report: **local content sixty-eight percent**. It is true, it
was calculated carefully, and nobody has misstated anything. When you take it apart you find that
most of it is money passing through a company that imports everything, and the part that builds
anything is about nine points.

%%FIG rf05-f5 | A one-page internal note on headed paper: a title, a line giving the guidance document, the association that publishes it, its edition and its year, a three-column table of the measurement bases, a diagram of one large contract unbundled into four, a boxed paragraph headed what good practice guidance cannot do, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

**2A  Read the note. Then answer the questions in section 3.**

> **JAZIRA PETROLEUM OPERATIONS — Mureij Training Center**
> **Note for operators: the word *local*, and what it is doing**
> **Source: *Local content: a guidance document for the oil and gas industry*, published by **IPIECA**,
> second edition **2016**. Printed here in our own words.**
>
> **What the idea is.** This industry spends very large sums in places that often have little other
> industry. **Local content** is the attempt to make that spending build something durable: employment
> for people who live there, suppliers that can still trade when the field is finished, and skills
> that stay in the country.
>
> **Why it is harder than it sounds.** Because almost every word in it has to be defined before a
> figure means anything, and the definitions are choices.
>
> **What does *local* mean geographically?** The country? The province? The district the wells are in?
> A company from the capital, employing people from the capital, is national but is not local to the
> community that sees the flare.
>
> **What makes a company local?** Registration in the country is the easiest test and the weakest one.
> A locally registered company may be wholly foreign owned, may employ three people, and may exist to
> import goods and add a margin. Ownership, where the value is added, and who the employees are, all
> give different answers.
>
> **What is being measured?** There are three usual bases and they give very different numbers for the
> same operation.
>
> | Basis | What it counts |
> |---|---|
> | **headcount** | the proportion of the workforce who are nationals |
> | **spend** | the proportion of procurement spend paid to local companies |
> | **value retained** | the value actually added within the country, net of imported content |
>
> **A spend basis counts the whole invoice.** If a locally registered company imports a pump for a
> hundred and sells it to you for a hundred and ten, a spend basis records a hundred and ten of local
> content. The value added in the country was ten. **Both figures are correct, and they differ by a
> factor of eleven.**
>
> **What the guidance recommends doing about it.** Practical things, and they are mostly about
> procurement rather than about targets.
>
> **Plan early.** Capability takes years to build, and a decision made at the design stage about how a
> package is specified determines whether a local supplier can bid at all.
> **Unbundle.** One enormous contract can only be won by an international contractor. Four smaller
> packages may each be winnable locally. The administrative cost of four contracts is the price of the
> policy.
> **Help suppliers pre-qualify.** Most local suppliers fail on paperwork, certification, safety
> systems and payment terms, not on capability. Those are all fixable, and fixing them is cheaper than
> importing.
> **Pay on time.** A small supplier that waits ninety days for payment cannot grow, and may not
> survive. This is the single most effective local content measure available to any operator and it
> costs nothing but working capital.
> **Measure something meaningful, and say which.** A figure with its basis stated is useful. A figure
> without one is a slogan.
>
> **WHAT GOOD PRACTICE GUIDANCE CANNOT DO**
> **It is not a standard and not law.** It describes what has worked. Each country's own regulations
> define *local* for legal purposes, and they differ, so a company operating in six countries is
> measuring six different things.
>
> **It cannot make a target meaningful.** A percentage target set on a spend basis can be met by
> routing purchases through a locally registered intermediary, which builds nothing. That is not fraud;
> it is the target working exactly as written.
>
> **It cannot make figures comparable.** Two published local content percentages are almost never
> comparable, for the same reason two government take figures are not.
>
> **And it cannot create demand that outlasts the field.** A supplier that exists only to serve one
> operator has not been developed; it has been employed. Whether anything durable was built is a
> question answered twenty years later, by which time nobody who set the target is still there.
>
> *Our summary of a published guidance document, named with its publisher, edition and year. Read the
> guidance, and then go and take your own local content figure apart.*

### 📝 ON THE JOB

> **Never publish a local content figure without three things beside it:** the **definition** of
> *local* you used, the **basis** you measured on, and what the figure **excludes**. Three short
> clauses. They turn a slogan into a number. **And if you only ever change one thing in your
> organization's local content performance, change the payment terms.** Ninety days is why small
> suppliers stay small, and thirty days is a decision somebody can make in a meeting. It is worth more
> than any target, it builds actual companies, and nobody will ever put it on the front of an annual
> report.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

> Local content is where this industry's relationship with the place it operates in becomes concrete,
> and it is almost always measured in a way that flatters everybody. A government can announce a
> percentage; a company can report one; a local intermediary can earn a margin; and nothing durable
> has to happen for all three to be satisfied. The people who know this best are the local suppliers
> themselves, who can usually tell you in one sentence what is stopping them — payment terms,
> certification, package size — and who are almost never in the room where the target is set. The most
> useful thing anybody in an operating company can do about local content is unglamorous and
> procedural: make it possible to bid, and pay on time.

### 🧩 THINK IT THROUGH

%%FIG rf05-f16 | A desk in the evening. A local content figure of sixty-eight percent lies open on an annual report page, beside a four-part breakdown with nine circled, a pre-qualification failure list with five of six reasons being paperwork, a payment terms page reading ninety days, a photograph of a workshop with one machine in it, and a glass of tea. A clock shows seven.%%

**A company has exceeded its local content target.** The figure is correct, it was calculated
carefully, the target was set in good faith, and the people who met it worked hard at it. Local
employment is high and the catering, security and transport contracts all go to companies in the
region.

Thirty-four points of the figure is margin on imported goods. Five of six local bidders failed
pre-qualification on documentation. The payment terms are ninety days, and the one workshop in the
region that could fabricate structures has one machine.

**Discuss in your group.**

1 Has local content been built?
2 The target was met exactly as written. What was wrong with the target?
3 Nobody misled anybody. What has never been written down?
4 You can change one thing tomorrow. Which, and what would it cost?

**There is no answer printed at the back of this book.** Unit 6 is what may be published, who decides,
and what a reconciled figure can and cannot tell you.
