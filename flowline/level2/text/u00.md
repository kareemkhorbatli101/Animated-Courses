## 2 · The one idea

%%FIG rb00-f2 | Four icons: a block of sandstone with visible pore spaces, two blocks side by side one with connected pores and one with isolated pores, a core sample the size of a thumb, and a map of a field several kilometers across.%%

**2A  Look at the four pictures. Say which two are the same size and which two are not.**

Oil is not in a hole. It is in the **spaces between grains of rock**, the way water is in a sponge.

Two different words describe those spaces, and people mix them up constantly.

| The word | What it means | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **porosity** | how much space there is | it tells you how much oil could be there |
| **permeability** | how well the spaces connect | it tells you whether the oil can get out |

A rock can have plenty of space and almost no connection between the spaces. Then the oil is there,
it is real, and you cannot produce it. **Porosity is the volume. Permeability is the argument.**

**2B  Say both words aloud with a partner. Then say which one you would ask about first, and why.
Take turns giving an example of each.**

## 3 · Where the numbers come from

%%FIG rb00-f3 | A thumb-sized core plug beside a scaled outline of a field twelve kilometers long, with a caption reading the measurement is this one, and the answer is about that one.%%

**3A  Look at the picture with a partner. Say how many times bigger the field is than the core.**

A permeability is measured in a laboratory, on a cylinder of rock about the size of your thumb, cut
out of a core that came up from one point in one well.

The number is then used to say something about a reservoir several kilometers across.

That is not a mistake. It is the only thing anybody can do. But it is the reason this book keeps
printing the same sentence: **a model is not a measurement**, and a number with no idea of its own
uncertainty attached is the most dangerous kind of number on a site.

**3B  Discuss in your group.** If a core from one well says one thing and a core from another well
says something else, what do you think an engineer does?
