### 2 · Read

%%FIG rb04-f5 | A one-page internal note on headed paper: a title, a line giving the report, its commission, the president it reported to and its month and year, a nine-row table of decisions, a boxed paragraph headed what the report says about itself, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

**2A  Read the note. Then answer the questions in section 3.**

> **JAZIRA PETROLEUM OPERATIONS — Mureij Training Center**
> **Note for operators: nine decisions, and what an inquiry concluded about them**
> **Source: National Commission on the BP Deepwater Horizon Oil Spill and Offshore Drilling,
> *Deep Water: The Gulf Oil Disaster and the Future of Offshore Drilling*, Report to the President,
> **January 2011**. Printed here in our own words.**
>
> On 20 April 2010 a well in deep water blew out. **Eleven people were killed.** A commission was
> set up and reported the following January.
>
> Nothing about that well is like our wells. It was offshore, it was in nearly fifteen hundred meters
> of water, it was being temporarily abandoned at the end of drilling, and the pressure margins were
> narrow. We are not reading it for the engineering. We are reading it for the shape of the thing.
>
> **The report traced the blowout to a series of identifiable decisions** by the operator, the
> cementing contractor and the drilling contractor. Among them were decisions about the casing design
> for the final string, the number of centralizers, how much the well was circulated before
> cementing, the kind of cement used, whether a log was run afterwards to check it, **how a negative
> pressure test was interpreted**, how deep the heavy fluid was displaced with seawater before the
> final plug was set, how closely the well was watched during that displacement, and where the flow
> was sent when it reached the rig floor.
>
> Two things about that list matter more than the list.
>
> **The first is that almost every decision had an argument behind it.** The report observes that many
> of the choices which increased risk also saved the companies significant time, and therefore money.
> Not one of them required anybody to intend harm, or even to be careless in the ordinary sense. They
> were trade-offs, made on ordinary afternoons, by people who expected to go home.
>
> **The second is that no single one of them explains the outcome.** Remove any one and the others
> remain. The report's conclusion is not that somebody made a mistake; it is that the mistakes
> revealed **systematic failures in risk management**, and that most of them traced back to a single
> overarching failure — a **failure of management**.
>
> **The sixth decision is the one to learn.** A negative pressure test removes the pressure holding
> the well down and watches to see whether anything comes back. If the cement and the plugs are
> sealing, nothing does. On that evening the test gave readings that did not fit, the readings were
> discussed, an explanation was adopted, and the operation continued. The well had already told them.
> The information existed, in the room, on a gauge, and it was interpreted away.
>
> **WHAT THE REPORT SAYS ABOUT ITSELF**
> It is a **presidential commission's report**, written to a deadline, with a mandate that was partly
> about the future of offshore drilling and not only about one well. It is **not a finding of legal
> liability**, and it says so: establishing fault in law is a court's work, not an inquiry's.
>
> The commission said publicly that it **had no power to compel testimony**. It worked from documents
> and voluntary interviews. Other investigations of the same event — by the regulator, by the coast
> guard, by a separate safety board and by the companies themselves — reached conclusions that
> overlap but are not identical, and a reader who has only read one of them has read one of them.
>
> It is about **deepwater drilling in one jurisdiction** at one time. Its recommendations are written
> for that regulatory system. The part that travels is not the recommendations. It is the sentence
> about nine defensible decisions.
>
> *Our summary of a published inquiry report, named with its commission, its title, its month and its
> year. Read the published report. It is freely available, it is written for the public rather than
> for engineers, and the first chapter is the best thing in this course.*

### 1 · The past simple for a sequence

| | |
|---|---|
| The casing **was run** on the 19th. | past passive, for a step in a process |
| They **pumped** the cement that evening. | past simple active, with a time |
| They **did not run** a log afterwards. | a negative: something that did not happen |

### 📝 ON THE JOB

> **Write the reading, then the explanation, then who was not satisfied.** A test record that gives
> only the conclusion has thrown away the only thing that would have been useful later: the number
> that did not fit. And write the dissent. *Two were satisfied, one was not* is one of the most
> valuable sentences in any record, because it tells the next reader that the question was live and
> was closed by people, not by evidence.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

> Every organization says it wants people to speak up, and almost every organization is hard to speak
> up in — not because of cruelty but because of ordinary things: rank, lateness, tiredness, the cost
