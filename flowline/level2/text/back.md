# Grammar Reference

The whole grammar of the book on a few pages. The unit number tells you where it was taught.

## Starting Point · Describing a measurement and what it stands for

| | |
|---|---|
| The permeability **is measured** on a core plug. | the method, stated impersonally |
| The plug is **about the size of** a thumb. | a comparison of scale |
| The field is **twelve kilometers** across. | a dimension |
| The figure **stands for** the whole interval. | what a number is being used to represent |
| **A model is not a measurement.** | the sentence the book is built on |

## Unit 1 · The passive; comparatives

| | |
|---|---|
| The permeability **is measured** on a core plug. | present passive: a standard method |
| The plug **was cut** from the core in March. | past passive: one event |
| The tube **has been packed** with uniform sand. | present perfect passive |
| The result **cannot be applied** outside that range. | a limit, stated impersonally |
| Rock A is **more permeable than** rock B. | long adjective |
| Rock B is **tighter than** rock A. | short adjective |
| Rock A is **ten times** more permeable **than** rock B. | a factor |

> **Careful:** one comparative at a time. *Tighter*, not *more tighter*. And the passive hides who did
> something, which is honest in a method and dishonest in an account of a decision.

## Unit 2 · The present perfect; *since*, *for* and *by*

| | |
|---|---|
| The field **has produced** 41 million barrels. | a total, up to now |
| Pressure **has fallen** by 40 bar **since** 2015. | a change, with a starting point |
| The ratio **has risen** every year **for** six years. | a trend still running |
| We **have not measured** the pressure **this year**. | not yet, and the period is open |
| Pressure fell 40 bar **between 2019 and 2022**. | past simple: finished, at a named time |

> **Careful:** *for* takes a length of time, *since* takes a starting point. Not *since eleven years*.

## Unit 3 · The first conditional; warnings

| | |
|---|---|
| **If** the well flows, the stack **will be** shut in. | a real possibility and its result |
| **If** you see gas at the shaker, **tell** the driller. | condition plus instruction |
| **Unless** the pressure is bled off, the valve **will not** open. | the exception, first |
| **Never** open it before the pressure is bled off. | an absolute prohibition |
| **Do not assume** the rams will shear that pipe. | a prohibition about a belief |

> **Careful:** the condition stays in the present. Not *if the well will flow*.

## Unit 4 · Past simple; sequencers

| | |
|---|---|
| They **pumped** the cement that evening. | a finished event, at a time |
| They **did not run** a log afterwards. | something that did not happen |
| **First** the casing was run. **Then** the cement was pumped. | ordering |
| **After that**, no log was run. | a step, with a pause |
| **Meanwhile**, the displacement was being prepared. | at the same time |
| **By the time** the plug was set, the well **had started** to flow. | past perfect for the earlier event |

> **Careful:** a finished event at a named time takes the past simple, not the present perfect.

## Unit 5 · Proportion; *the more … the less*

| | |
|---|---|
| The rate is **proportional to** the drawdown. | a fixed ratio |
| Flow is **inversely proportional to** viscosity. | one up, the other down |
| **Twice as much** drawdown gives **twice as much** oil. | a factor, both sides |
| 40 barrels a day **for every** bar of drawdown. | a rate per unit |
| **The more** you pull, **the less** each extra bar gives you. | linked comparatives |

> **Careful:** the second half of *the more … the less* is a comparative phrase, not a sentence: *the
> less oil you get*.

## Unit 6 · Purpose

| | |
|---|---|
| We inject gas **to** lighten the column. | *to* plus a verb |
| Gas is injected **in order to** reduce the pressure. | formal |
| We inject gas **so that** the reservoir can lift the fluid. | a clause with its own subject |
| The compressor is there **for** the injection gas. | *for* plus a noun |
| We lowered the rate **so as not to** waste compression. | a negative purpose |

> **Careful:** *to lighten* with a verb. Never *for lighten*.

## Unit 7 · *Will* and *going to*; hedging

| | |
|---|---|
| Every well **will** decline. | a general truth |
| At this rate it **is going to** reach the limit in 2031. | present evidence, future event |
| We **are going to** do a workover in May. | a plan already made |
| **On current trend**, the rate **should** be about 240. | a hedged forecast |
| It is **likely to** flatten. · It **may** flatten. | probable · possible |
| **Assuming no intervention**, the total is 1.6 million barrels. | the assumption, named |

> **Careful:** one hedge is enough. Not *it may possibly perhaps flatten*.

## Unit 8 · Relative clauses

| | |
|---|---|
| The pad **that** catches the droplets is fouled. | defining: which pad |
| The vessel **which** we installed in 1986 is overloaded. | defining |
| The operator **who** lowered the level is on nights. | defining, a person |
| The section **where** the droplets fall out is this one. | defining, a place |
| The separator**, which we installed in 1986,** is overloaded. | non-defining: extra information |
| The water cut is 34 percent**, which is four times the design figure.** | commenting on the sentence |

> **Careful:** a clause with commas takes *which*, never *that*.

## Unit 9 · *Shall*, *must*, *may not*

| | |
|---|---|
| The owner **shall** demonstrate mechanical integrity. | obligation, older drafting |
| The well **must** be cased and cemented. | obligation, plainer |
| Injection pressure **may not** exceed the maximum. | prohibition |
| The operator **is required to** report monthly. | obligation, passive |
| The agency **may** require additional testing. | a power, not an obligation |
| **Where** a state has authority, its own rules **apply**. | *where* for a situation |

> **Careful:** *may* and *may not* are not simple opposites. *May* grants a power or a permission.

## Unit 10 · *Unless* and other conditions

| | |
|---|---|
| Do not issue a factor **unless** the runs agree. | the one exception |
| The factor may be used **provided that** the rate is in range. | permission with a condition |
| It holds **as long as** the flow stays near the proving rate. | a continuing condition |
| Accept the run **only if** it is consecutive. | a single strong condition |
| Prove it again, **otherwise** the ticket is not defensible. | the consequence of not doing it |

> **Careful:** the negative is already inside *unless*. Not *unless the runs do not agree*.

## Unit 11 · Defining

| | |
|---|---|
| Failure **is defined as** the loss of a required function. | the formal definition |
| A pump at half flow **counts as** a failure here. | a local decision, stated |
| The motor **is treated as** part of the pump. | a convention, stated |
| **By** *critical* **we mean** that it stopped. | explaining your own word |
| **For the purposes of** this report, downtime starts at the alarm. | one document only |

> **Careful:** a cause answers *why*. *The pump stopped* is a mode, not a cause.

## Unit 12 · Degrees of certainty

| | |
|---|---|
| **At least** 41 million barrels, with **90 percent** probability. | the conservative statement |
| **Between** 41 **and** 79 million barrels. | a range |
| 58 million, **as a central estimate**. | the middle, labeled |
| 41 million **as of** 31 December, **at** the stated price assumption. | date and assumption attached |
| This **assumes** the infill wells are drilled. | the condition, named |
| I **cannot** give you one number. | the honest refusal |

> **Careful:** the probability is about the quantity being **equaled or exceeded**, not about the
> estimate being exactly right.

# The British Layer

This book is written in American English, and the switch to British English is a layer rather than a
correction. The patterns are the same as in Book 1 and are printed there in full. The ones this book
touches most are the **-er** ending in measuring words, the **-ize** and **-yze** endings, **sulf-**,
the **-se** and **-ce** pairs in nouns and verbs, and the doubling of a single **l** before a vowel
ending.

**Three things never change, in either layer.**

**Standards numbers and titles.** API Standard 53 is API Standard 53. ISO 14224:2016 is
ISO 14224:2016. A published title is printed as its publisher printed it, including its publisher's
own spelling.

**Names of people, journals and societies.** Darcy, Arps, Vogel and Gilbert are spelled as they
spelled themselves. So are the journals and the societies, several of which spell their own names with
endings this book would otherwise change.

**Units in a quoted figure.** A figure quoted from a document keeps the unit the document used. Where
Gilbert's correlation is in pounds per square inch, barrels a day and sixty-fourths of an inch, this
book says so rather than converting it quietly.

**One deliberate choice, which is not a spelling layer.** Money is written with no currency symbol
anywhere in this series. A symbol tells the reader where the book thinks they are, and this book does
not know.

# Sources

Each unit of this book is built on one real published document. The table gives the document, the
unit, and the limit the document itself states. Nothing is quoted at length; the reading texts are
our own summaries, written for this course, and each one names its document in its own first lines.
**Read the published documents.** Four of them are short papers by single authors, and those four are
the clearest writing about uncertainty you will find anywhere in this industry.

| Unit | The document | What it says about its own limits |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Henry Darcy, *Les Fontaines Publiques de la Ville de Dijon*, Paris, 1856 | The experiments used water, one fluid, at low velocity, through loose uniform sand, on one sample. The relationship is empirical, holds while the flow is laminar, and says nothing about oil, gas and water moving together. |
| 2 | L. P. Dake, *Fundamentals of Reservoir Engineering*, Elsevier, 1978 | A textbook, not a standard; nobody is certified against it. The material balance contains no geometry at all, so it cannot say where to drill. It needs an average reservoir pressure and accurate cumulative production, and the aquifer estimate is drawn from the same pressure history it is used to explain. |
| 3 | API Standard 53, *Blowout Prevention Equipment Systems for Drilling Wells*, 2012 edition | An equipment standard. It is not a well control procedure and not a well design document. A passed test shows that equipment held that pressure, from that direction, in that configuration, on that day; shear rams are qualified against particular pipe sizes and grades. |
| 4 | National Commission on the BP Deepwater Horizon Oil Spill and Offshore Drilling, *Deep Water*, Report to the President, January 2011 | A presidential commission's report, written to a deadline, about deepwater drilling in one jurisdiction. It is not a finding of legal liability. The commission said publicly that it had no power to compel testimony, and other investigations of the same event reached conclusions that overlap but are not identical. |
| 5 | J. V. Vogel, "Inflow Performance Relationships for Solution-Gas Drive Wells", *Journal of Petroleum Technology*, 1968 | Built from twenty-one computed hypothetical cases, not from field measurements, and the paper says so. It is for solution-gas drive, assumes no significant well damage, and its maximum rate is at zero bottomhole pressure — a construct, not an operating point. |
| 6 | W. E. Gilbert, "Flowing and Gas-lift Well Performance", in *Drilling and Production Practice*, API, 1954 | Curves constructed by hand from measurements on particular wells, with particular tubing sizes and fluids, in field units. Empirical, not a theory of multiphase flow. The choke relationship holds only in critical flow, and the curves say nothing about whether a lift system is actually working. |
| 7 | J. J. Arps, "Analysis of Decline Curves", *Transactions of the AIME*, 1945 | Empirical equations for fitting observed data, not derived from reservoir physics. They assume the well is produced the same way throughout, that nothing is done to it, and that nothing changes in the reservoir around it — three assumptions an operating company violates deliberately and regularly. |
| 8 | API Specification 12J, *Specification for Oil and Gas Separators* | A specification for a vessel: design, fabrication, marking and testing. Not a process design for a facility. Its sizing assumes known inlet conditions and a fluid that separates; it says nothing about how the vessel is operated; and a nameplate records what the vessel was built to handle, not what it will do with today's stream. |
| 9 | The Underground Injection Control program of the US Environmental Protection Agency, Class II rules at 40 CFR Parts 144 and 146 | Applies in one jurisdiction only and is **not the law here**; within that jurisdiction the detail differs by state. Its purpose is protecting underground sources of drinking water, not the environment generally, and it does not cover the surface. Mechanical integrity is a defined term: no significant leak, by the test applied, on the day of the test. |
| 10 | API, *Manual of Petroleum Measurement Standards*, Chapter 4, *Proving Systems*, and Chapter 12, *Calculation of Petroleum Quantities* | Chapter 12 makes two parties agree on one answer; it does not improve a measurement, and a precisely computed figure from a badly proved meter is a precise wrong number. A meter factor belongs to the conditions it was found at. Neither chapter says how often to prove, and the prover has its own calibration and uncertainty. |
| 11 | ISO 14224:2016, *Petroleum, petrochemical and natural gas industries — Collection and exchange of reliability and maintenance data for equipment* | A standard about how to write things down. It sets no target, no benchmark and no acceptable failure rate, and it does not do the analysis. It states that data quality depends on the collection process and on the competence of the people recording. It makes comparison possible, not automatic: two organizations following it can still be uncomparable if they drew their boundaries differently. |
| 12 | The *Petroleum Resources Management System*, SPE, WPC, AAPG and SPEE, 2018 revision | A set of guidelines for classification, not a reporting regulation; securities regulators have their own differing definitions, so one field can honestly carry two numbers. Reserves are a property of a project, not of a reservoir, and the certainty percentages describe the estimate rather than the rock. |

# A Note on Everything Else in This Book

**The documents above are real.** Every other named thing in this book was written for it.

**Jazira Petroleum Operations** does not exist. Neither does the **Tell Sahra** field, the **Mureij**
gathering station, the **Sahra** central processing facility or the **Mureij Training Center**. Every
person named in this book is invented, and so is every well number, permeability, pressure history,
test result, proving run, failure record, flaring rate, water volume and reserves figure attributed to
the company. None of it is a record of anything that happened anywhere.

**One event in this book really happened**, and it is the only one. Unit 4 is about a blowout on 20
April 2010 in which eleven people were killed. Everything the unit says about it comes from the
published inquiry report it names, and the unit says plainly that other investigations reached
conclusions that overlap and are not identical. The invented company's own conversations about that
report are, like everything else here, teaching material.

**The real places are named as matters of public record only.** Deir ez-Zor, Hasakah, Homs, Banias,
the Syrian Petroleum Company, the Arab Gas Pipeline and the Kirkuk–Banias line appear because they
are facts about the country. Nothing in this book describes their current condition, ownership,
operation or capacity.

**This is a first-edition teaching draft.** It is not published, endorsed or approved by the Syrian
Ministry of Petroleum and Mineral Resources, by the Syrian Petroleum Company, by any other Syrian
state body, by any standards organization, professional society or regulator named in it, or by any
company. It carries no official status, and nothing in it is engineering, legal, safety, commercial or
regulatory advice.

**And the standing line.** Safety information in a language course is not a substitute for a competent
person, a procedure and a permit. This book will teach you to say *the shear rams are qualified for a
smaller pipe than the one in the hole*. It will not tell you what to do about it.

# At the End of the Book

Twelve units ago you could name what comes out of the ground. You can now say where it was before
that, and — more usefully — you can say how far to trust each number between the rock and the ticket.

You can explain why a thumb-sized core cannot describe a field; name five things that push oil out of
a reservoir and say which one is doing the work; say which barrier is holding a well in and which one
is merely famous; tell the story of nine defensible decisions; say why pulling harder stops working;
find the point past which more gas gives you less oil; extend a decline curve and name the three
things that make it worthless; read a separator nameplate against today's stream; say what a
mechanical integrity test demonstrates and on what day; find the cause of a metering difference before
anybody is accused of anything; say where a pump ends; and say what a company is allowed to claim it
has.

That is the whole upstream, in a language that keeps its assumptions attached to its numbers.

**Flowline 2 — *Pipes, Pumps and Pressure*** begins where the tank ends. It opens with a code for
liquid pipelines, goes through the two experiments that explain why anything flows at all — Reynolds
in 1883 and Moody in 1944 — and ends with a management system standard written after a pipeline
failure that a published inquiry traced to an organization rather than to a weld.

The oil does not stop at the tank. Neither does this.
