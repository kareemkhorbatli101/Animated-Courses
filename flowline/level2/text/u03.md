%%SUB The equipment you use when the thing that was supposed to work has already failed%%

There is a stack of steel on the wellhead when a rig is over it, and it is the most photographed
equipment in the industry. It is also the second line of defense, not the first, and almost every
public conversation about it forgets that.

### 2 · Read

%%FIG rb03-f5 | A one-page internal note on headed paper: a title, a line giving the standard, its number, its publisher and its edition year, a cross-section of a stack with six labeled parts, a two-row table of barriers, a boxed paragraph headed what a passed test proves, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

**2A  Read the note. Then answer the questions in section 3.**

> **JAZIRA PETROLEUM OPERATIONS — Mureij Training Center**
> **Note for operators: the stack, and what it is for**
> **Source: American Petroleum Institute, **Standard 53**, *Blowout Prevention Equipment Systems for
> Drilling Wells*, **2012** edition. Printed here in our own words.**
>
> When a rig is over one of our wells, the rig is run by a drilling contractor and the stack belongs
> to them. You will still read their daily report and their test records, so you need the words.
>
> **Two barriers, and they are not equal.**
>
> | | What it is | When it works |
> |---|---|---|
> | **primary** | the column of drilling fluid in the hole, and its weight | all the time, quietly, while nothing is happening |
> | **secondary** | the blowout preventer stack | only once the primary barrier has already failed |
>
> That table is the whole philosophy. The stack is not the plan. The stack is what is left of the
> plan.
>
> **The parts.** From the bottom up, a stack carries **ram preventers** and usually an **annular
> preventer**. Pipe rams close around one particular pipe size. Blind rams close on an open hole.
> **Blind-shear rams** cut whatever is across them and then seal. The annular preventer closes around
> almost anything, including irregular shapes, and can close on open hole as well. Out of the side go
> a **choke line**, which routes flow to a manifold where it can be let out under control, and a
> **kill line**, which lets heavy fluid be pumped back in.
>
> **The control system** closes all of this. An **accumulator** stores pressurized fluid so that the
> stack can be operated without waiting for a pump, and the standard sets out how much stored capacity
> is required: enough to operate the functions and still have a reserve, with the charge pumps off.
> There are remote control panels, in more than one place, because the one nearest the well is the one
> you may not be able to reach.
>
> **Testing.** The standard sets out two different things that both get called testing. A **function
> test** operates the equipment to confirm it moves. A **pressure test** applies pressure to confirm
> it seals. They are not interchangeable, they are done at different intervals, and the standard
> specifies both.
>
> **WHAT A PASSED TEST PROVES**
> A passed pressure test proves that **that equipment held that pressure, from that direction, in
> that configuration, on that day**. That is a real and valuable thing to know, and it is narrower
> than people hear.
>
> It does not prove the rams will shear **the pipe that happens to be across them** at the moment
> something goes wrong. Shear rams are qualified against particular pipe sizes and grades. A tool
> joint, a wireline, a thick-walled drill collar or a dropped object across the rams is a different
> problem, and the standard is an equipment standard: it does not promise an outcome in a situation
> nobody chose.
>
> **This standard is about equipment systems.** It is **not** a well control procedure. It does not
> tell anybody how to recognize a well flowing, when to shut in, which method to use to circulate out
> an influx, or who decides. Those are separate documents, separate training and separate competence.
>
> It is also **not** about well design. Casing depths, cement, barrier design and the decisions made
> before anybody needs a preventer are elsewhere. The next unit is about exactly those decisions.
>
> *Our summary of a published standard, named with its number, its publisher and its edition year.
> Read the published standard, and write down the edition you read.*

### 1 · The first conditional

| | |
|---|---|
| **If** the well flows, the stack **will be** shut in. | a real possibility and its result |
| **If** the pipe is not the right size, the rams **will not** seal. | a negative result |

> **Say which test, every time.** *Tested* is not a word. *Function tested on the 4th, pressure tested

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

> The blowout preventer is the most famous object in this industry and the least representative one.
> It is in the news, in the photographs and in the public imagination, and the reason is simple: it is
> the thing that was there when something went wrong. The barrier that works is a column of gray
> fluid that nobody photographs, held at a weight somebody calculated, topped up by somebody who is
> not on anybody's list of heroes. Every industry has this problem — the thing that saves you is
> boring and the thing that fails is dramatic — and it shapes where the money goes.
> **Ask your group: at your site, which safety equipment gets attention, and which gets maintained?**
