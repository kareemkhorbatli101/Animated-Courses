You have followed the oil from the rock to the tank. Now it has to go somewhere, and getting it there
costs something that nobody outside the industry ever thinks about.

## 2 · The one thing a pipe takes

%%FIG rc00-f2 | Four icons: a pipe with a pressure gauge at each end reading two different numbers, a pump station, a rough internal pipe wall magnified, and a sheet of paper headed the power bill.%%

**2A  Look at the four pictures. Say what is different about the two gauges.**

Push a fluid along a pipe and it arrives at a **lower pressure** than it started at. Every meter of
pipe takes a little. The fluid rubs against the wall, and the fluid rubs against itself.

That loss is **friction**, and you pay for it twice: once in the pump that has to make up the
difference, and once in the power that pump consumes for as long as the line is running.

**Almost everything in this book is about that loss** — how to calculate it, what makes it bigger,
what pays for it, and what it does to the steel over thirty years.

**2B  Say the two words aloud with a partner.** *Friction* and *pressure drop*. Then say which one is
the cause and which is the effect, and say why you pay for it twice.

## 3 · The two real lines

%%FIG rc00-f3 | A simple map outline with two lines drawn across it: one running from the northeast to the coast, labeled the Kirkuk–Banias pipeline, and one running from the southwest northward, labeled the Arab Gas Pipeline, with a coastal point labeled Banias and an inland point labeled Homs.%%

**3A  Look at the map with a partner. Say which line goes to the coast.**

Two pipelines in this country are matters of public record, and anybody who works in this industry
here will be asked about them.

| The line | What it is, as a matter of record |
|---|---|
| the **Kirkuk–Banias pipeline** | a crude oil line running from northern Iraq across Syria to the Mediterranean coast at Banias, built in the 1950s |
| the **Arab Gas Pipeline** | a regional natural gas pipeline system connecting several countries, with a section crossing Syria |

**This book says nothing about the current condition, ownership, capacity or operation of either of
them.** They are named because a course about this industry in this country that did not name them
would be strange, and because every engineering idea in the next twelve units applies to a line like
them.

**3B  Discuss in your group.** Both lines cross a border. Say three things that become more
complicated when a pipe crosses a border, and say which of the three is not an engineering problem.
Why would that one be the hardest to fix?

## 4 · What this book will do

The order is the order a pipeline is actually built and then lived with: first the code, then the
physics, then the machinery, then the slow problems, then the places where the pipe meets somebody
else, and finally the organization that is supposed to be holding all of it together.
