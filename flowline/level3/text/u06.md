# Unit 6 · The Thing That Runs Inside the Pipe
%%SUB A number with a plus and a minus on it, and the four words missing from most reports%%
%%GOALS
Say what an in-line inspection tool does
Say what a reported depth actually means
Say what a verification dig is for
Report a figure with its tolerance
Say why an empty report is not good news
%%

Nobody can see inside a pipeline. So something is sent through it that measures the wall as it goes,
and comes out with a list of features and depths. Every number on that list has a plus and a minus
attached to it, and the plus and the minus are usually left off.

### 2 · Read

%%FIG rc06-f5 | A one-page internal note on headed paper: a title, a line giving the two documents, their numbers and their publisher, a drawing of a tool in a pipe with sensors against the wall, a four-row table of what the tool can and cannot promise, a boxed paragraph headed the three numbers behind every depth, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

**2A  Read the note. Then answer the questions in section 3.**

> **JAZIRA PETROLEUM OPERATIONS — Mureij Training Center**
> **Note for operators: the machine inside the pipe, and what its report says**
> **Source: **API Standard 1163**, *In-line Inspection Systems Qualification*, read alongside **API
> Recommended Practice 1160**, *Managing System Integrity for Hazardous Liquid Pipelines*. Printed
> here in our own words.**
>
> An **in-line inspection tool** is a machine that travels along the inside of a pipeline, carried by
> the product, with sensors pressed against the wall. Different tools measure different things: metal
> loss, the shape of the pipe, or cracks. It is launched into a barrel at one end and caught in a
> barrel at the other, and it comes out with a recording.
>
> Weeks later you receive a **feature list**: for each thing the tool found, a position along the line,
> a position around the circumference, a type, and a depth.
>
> **The standard is about the whole system, not the machine.** It treats the tool, the procedures, the
> software, the people doing the analysis and the way the results are validated as **one system to be
> qualified**. The machine is the least interesting part of it. A perfect tool with a poor analyst
> produces a poor report, and the standard says so by refusing to separate them.
>
> | What the report gives you | What it cannot promise |
> |---|---|
> | a feature was found | that **every** feature was found |
> | what kind of feature it is | that the kind was identified correctly |
> | a depth | that the depth is exact |
> | nothing at a location | that there is **nothing** there |
>
> **THE THREE NUMBERS BEHIND EVERY DEPTH**
> A depth on a feature list is never a single number, and a report that prints it as one is incomplete.
> Behind it there are three things.
>
> **A tolerance.** The vendor's performance specification states how close the reported depth is
> expected to be — for example within a stated percentage of the wall thickness.
>
> **A confidence.** That tolerance applies with a stated probability, commonly eighty percent. That
> means **about one feature in five may fall outside the band**, and when one does, the tool has not
> failed; it has performed to specification.
>
> **A detection threshold.** Below a certain size the tool does not reliably report a feature at all.
> The specification states the size and the probability of detection.
>
> So *thirty-two percent* is really *an estimate of thirty-two, expected to lie between about
> twenty-two and forty-two, four times out of five*. Which number do you use for a decision? **The top
> of the range.** The cost of being wrong is not symmetrical.
>
> **The verification dig.** The only way to know whether a tool performed to its specification **on
> your pipe** is to dig some of the features up and measure them by hand, then compare. The standard
> expects that comparison. It is the step most often reduced in scope when a schedule tightens, and it
> is the step that tells you whether the whole report can be trusted.
>
> **AND THE EMPTY PART OF THE REPORT**
> Here is the sentence to carry out of this unit. **The absence of a feature from a report is not
> evidence of the absence of a feature from the pipe.**
>
> Features below the detection threshold are not reported. Features the analysis did not identify are
> not reported. Sections where the sensors lifted off the wall, where the tool ran too fast, or where
> the data was poor are, in a good report, **listed as such** — and in a bad one they are simply
> blank.
>
> A clean report is good news. It is not the same as a clean pipe, and the difference has killed
> people.
>
> *Our summary of two published documents, named with their numbers and their publisher. Read the
> published standard, and then read the performance specification that came with your last inspection.*

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

> Inspection creates a strange incentive in every organization that uses it: the report that says
> nothing is the cheapest report to receive. Nobody has to decide anything, no budget moves, and the
> line keeps running. So the pressure, which is never spoken and is often not even conscious, is
> toward reading an incomplete result as a clean one. The defense is not suspicion of inspection
> companies, most of whom write careful appendices nobody reads. It is a rule: **every blank gets a
> length and a location**, and every summary carries them on the front page.
> **Ask your group: when a test at your site comes back clear, does anybody ask what it could not

specification, attached. The 1.9 kilometers of poor data were listed in an appendix, correctly, by a
vendor who had done nothing wrong.

The summary that went to the committee had one number on it. The feature was never dug up. The wadi
crossing was never re-run. Four years later there is still no information about that 1.9 kilometers.

**Discuss in your group.**

1 Who decided that the appendix did not go to the committee?
2 The vendor's report was honest. Where was the honesty lost?
3 The feature's upper bound crossed the limit. Would one extra column have changed anything?
4 What should be written down now, for somebody to find in ten years?
