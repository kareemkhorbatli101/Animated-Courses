# The Foundation books — agreed architecture

*Supersedes §1–2 of `TOEFL_Foundation2_Plan.md`. Everything else in that plan
stands.*

---

## The decision

Do both, and do them as one spiral rather than two separate fixes.

* **Foundation 1 keeps everything it has** — the ten campus and personal units,
  all three existing cycles in every skill, every word of them — and **gains two
  academic cycles per skill**, so five cycles a skill.
* **Foundation 2 is a parallel book of five cycles a skill**, academic
  throughout, which completes the climb to Volume 1.

## Cycles per skill, across the whole course

| Book | Level | Cycles a skill | Of which academic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Foundation 1** | A1–A2 | **5** | 2 |
| **Foundation 2** | A2–B1 | **5** | 3 |
| Volumes 1–2 | B1 | 3 | 1 |
| Volumes 3–4 | B2 | 3 | 1 |

The foundation books carry more cycles than the volumes they feed, which is the
right way round: a learner at A1 needs more repetitions of a smaller thing, and
a learner at B2 needs fewer repetitions of a larger one.

## The field spiral

Each of Volume 1's ten fields is met **three times before Volume 1 teaches it**:
twice in Foundation 1, five times in Foundation 2, three times in Volume 1.

| Foundation unit | Its campus topic (unchanged) | The field it now also carries | Prepares | Grammar it can be written in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | People, Names and the First Week | **People and Their Ways** | V1 U9 Anthropology | be, present simple |
| 2 | The Campus and How to Find It | **Making and Building** | V1 U6 Art and Design | there is/are, prepositions |
| 3 | Days, Times and the Timetable | **Space and the Sky** | V1 U2 Astronomy | present simple |
| 4 | Family, Home and Where You Live | **Living Things** | V1 U3 Cells and Genetics | have got |
| 5 | A Day in Your Life | **People and the Mind** | V1 U5 Psychology | present continuous |
| 6 | Food, Prices and the Cafeteria | **Work, Trade and Money** | V1 U8 Economics | much, many, some, any |
| 7 | Weather, Seasons and the Year | **Water, Air and Weather** | V1 U4 Climate | past of be, regular -ed |
| 8 | Getting There: Travel and Transport | **History and Change** | V1 U1 American History | irregular past |
| 9 | The Body, Health and the Clinic | **Rocks, Land and Time** | V1 U7 Geology | can, should, imperatives |
| 10 | Study, Work and Your Plans | **Stories and Writing** | V1 U10 Literature | will, going to, comparatives |

The pairing is constrained twice over, and the second constraint is the one
that decides it. A field has to be a neighbour of the unit's campus topic, and
it has to be **writable in the grammar that unit has reached**. History is the
clearest case: *Why Cities Grew Where They Did* is a past-tense text about
transport, so it can only sit in Unit 8, where the irregular past arrives — and
Unit 8 is Travel and Transport, which is the natural neighbour anyway. Days and
the calendar take the moon and the month; the campus and its buildings take how
a building is designed; family takes why children resemble their parents, on
the unit that teaches *have got*.

Foundation 2 uses the same ten fields in the same order, at A2–B1, on five new
sub-topics each.

## What this does to the numbers

| | Foundation 1 now | Foundation 1 after | Foundation 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Words | 119,000 | ~155,000 | ~200,000 |
| Figures | 348 | ~430 | ~520 |
| Academic passages | 0 | **20** | **30** |
| Academic talks | 0 | **20** | **30** |
| Pages a unit | 20 | ~28 | ~32 |

A student arriving at Volume 1 Unit 1 will have read **five** passages about
history and heard **five** talks about it, instead of none.

## The gate that decides whether it worked

Unchanged from the Foundation 2 plan, and now run after each book:

> **Gate 7 — readiness.** Count the word types a graduate has never met, across
> all ten real Volume 1 passages.

> | | unknown types per passage |

> | Target after Foundation 2 | **≤ 5** |
