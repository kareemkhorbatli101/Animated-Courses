## How to use this book

This is the third book of six. It is about **one paper**: Academic Reading. Three passages, forty
questions, sixty minutes, and **no extra time at the end**. Nobody finishes it by reading carefully
from the first word, and this book is about what competent readers do instead. Every unit is built on
one real published document, named with its edition and its date.

**Twelve units**, sixteen pages each, with the same six lessons in the same order every time.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Sixteen words, a second set of six, and a team research task |
| **2 · Reading** | One real published document, with its date and its limits |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures, with the mistake each one causes |
| **4 · In the Test** | Clear English, a dialogue, a culture check and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A model, then your own |
| **Team Up & Review** | A picture, a team project, a survey, and one problem with no easy answer |

**The audio.** 🔊 marks a track. 195 tracks, numbered `PL3-unit-track`.

## What is different about this book

**The paper is a search task with a reading problem inside it.** Sixty minutes, three passages and
forty questions means about ninety seconds a question including the reading. The skill is not reading
every word more quickly. It is knowing which words you must read, which you may skip, and how to tell
them apart — and then being able to read the ones that matter with real precision.

**Twelve real sources, and twelve limits.** A book-length review that concludes we know less about
reading than we would like. A twenty-year summary of eye-tracking done mostly with skilled readers of
their own language. A coverage threshold from one study with one text. A word list built on the
academic writing of one period. A famous reasoning task whose results change when you change the
content. A readability formula that counts syllables and knows nothing about meaning.

**The hardest question type in the paper is a logic question.** *Not given* is not a reading
difficulty, it is a reasoning difficulty, and Unit 7 is built on the experiment that shows what
people do instead of looking for the case that would prove them wrong.

**Nothing here is a past paper.** Every passage, question, heading and option in this book was
written for it. For material that looks like the examination there is one source: the test owners'
own published sample material.

**Money has no symbol in this series.** Every fee and figure is written without a currency sign,
because this book is used in many countries and the unit is whatever currency you pay in.

## The people

The same preparation center and the same class as Books 1 and 2.

**Meridian Language Center** is an independent preparation center of about forty teachers. **Priya
Raman** teaches the class. **Grace Mwangi** is the academic manager. **Lars Eriksen** is the
assessment coordinator.

The class: **Junseo Park**, **Nour Khalil**, **Marek Nowak**, **Mei Lin**, **Yusuf Demir**, **Ines
