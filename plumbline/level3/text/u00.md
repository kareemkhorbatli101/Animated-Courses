# Starting Point · Sixty Minutes
%%SUB Three passages, forty questions, no extra time, and the arithmetic nobody does%%
%%GOALS
Review the English you need to search a passage
Say what each of the three passages is likely to be
Work out how long you actually have for one question
%%

Sixty minutes. Three passages. Forty questions. The answers have to be on the answer sheet when the
hour ends, because there is no extra time. Almost nobody does that arithmetic before the morning.

### 1 · Six things you already have

%%FIG qc00-f1 | A single review sheet with six boxes. Each box has a heading — nouns that carry a question, synonyms, numbers and dates in a text, reference words, negatives, and question stems — and one short example from an academic passage.%%

**1A  Read the six examples aloud with a partner. Which two do you use when you search?**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Nouns that carry a question** | *What was the **purpose** of the **second trial**?* |
| **Synonyms** | *rose* · *increased* · *grew* · *climbed* |
| **Numbers and dates** | *in 1948* · *about 12 per cent* · *one in fifty* |
| **Reference words** | *this* · *these* · *such* · *the latter* |
| **Negatives** | *not all* · *few* · *hardly* · *unlikely* |
| **Question stems** | *According to the passage…* · *The writer suggests that…* |

**1B  Listen and repeat.** %%AUD PL3-00-01%%

### 2 · The three passages, and what the published description says

%%FIG qc00-f2 | Three panels in a row, each headed with a passage number and showing one object: a magazine page with a photograph, a textbook page with a diagram, and a journal page dense with text and a small glossary at the foot.%%

**2A  Read the three rows. Then say, for each one, what you would expect it to be about.**

| Passage | What the published description says |
|---|---|
| **1** | A text on a topic of general interest, from a book, journal, magazine or newspaper |
| **2** | The same kinds of source, usually a little denser |
| **3** | The same kinds of source, and **at least one** of the three contains detailed logical argument |

The passages may contain diagrams, graphs or illustrations, and where a text uses technical terms a
short glossary may be given. **The published description does not say what the topics will be, and
nothing in this book can tell you.**

**2B  Listen.** %%AUD PL3-00-02%%

### 3 · The arithmetic nobody does

%%FIG qc00-f3 | A card taped inside a notebook, printed with four lines of arithmetic in bold, each with one line of smaller type beneath it.%%

**3A  Read the four lines. Then say the last one aloud to your partner.**

> **60 minutes for 3 passages = 20 minutes each.**
> *Including reading the passage, finding every answer and writing it down.*
>
> **40 questions in 60 minutes = 90 seconds a question.**
> *Including the reading.*
>
> **A passage of 900 words read at 200 words a minute = 4½ minutes.**
> *And that is one careful reading, with no searching.*
>
> **So: about 15 minutes to answer 13 questions, after you have read once.**
> *Which is why nobody reads three times, and why the reading has to be a different kind of
> reading.*

**There is no extra time at the end.** Unlike Listening, the hour includes writing your answers onto
the sheet. **If you are still reading at minute fifty-eight, the answers you have in your head are
worth nothing.**

### 4 · Two ways of reading one paragraph

%%FIG qc00-f4 | Two copies of the same short paragraph, one above the other. The first is covered evenly with underlining. The second has one noun phrase circled, one date circled and one reference word boxed.%%

**4A  Look at the two paragraphs. Then discuss: which one belongs to somebody who finished the
paper?**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Reader 1** | Underlines nearly every line. Understands the paragraph. Has used four minutes. |
| **Reader 2** | Circles one noun phrase, one date and one *such*. Understands less. Has used forty seconds and knows where to come back. |

Reader 1 is doing something admirable and expensive. Reader 2 is doing the paper.

**4B  Listen and read.** %%AUD PL3-00-03%%

leave blank because the hour ended?
