# Plumbline · sources

**Academic English for IELTS · International Edition.** Six books, seventy-one units carrying a
source and one review unit without one; seventy-one separate source bodies. Nothing in this series
reuses a figure or a source from Wayfinder English, from the Clearline business series, from Foreign
Service English, from Keystone or from Throughline.

## Nothing in this series is official

**This is an independent teaching draft.** It is **not published, endorsed or approved by the British
Council, by IDP: IELTS Australia, by Cambridge University Press & Assessment, or by any other
examination body**, and it carries no official status. IELTS is the trademark of its owners and is
named in these books only to say which examination they prepare for.

**No question, passage, recording or prompt from any past examination is reproduced anywhere in the
series.** Every practice item in every book was written for it. Where the series describes what the
examination asks, it describes a published document — the test format, the public band descriptors,
the published scale, the notice to candidates — names it, dates the day it was read, and prints what
that document says it does not cover.

**Nothing in these books is advice about anybody's admission, registration, visa or employment.** The
band a reader needs is the band the organization asking for it says it needs, on the day they apply.
Every book says so, in the back matter, in those words.

**And nothing in these books is a prediction.** A practice score is what a person did on one set of
questions on one morning. The series says this in Book 1 Unit 2, repeats it whenever a figure
appears, and comes back to it in the last unit of Book 6.

## The rule this series is built on

Every reading text in every book quotes something real: a published document from the test owners, a
framework published by an intergovernmental body, a journal paper, a book, a word list. Three rules
apply without exception.

1. **The text names its document, its edition or its date, and its publisher or author.** Not "the
   examiners want" but "the public band descriptors for Writing Task 2, as published, read on this
   date". Not "research shows" but "a review published in *Psychological Science in the Public
   Interest* in 2013, which rated ten techniques and said what *low* meant". An edition and a date
   are part of a citation, and a student who cannot name one has not finished the sentence.
2. **The text prints what the document does not cover.** In this series that is the point. A test
   format gives the shape of the papers and not their contents. A band scale publishes an indicative
   raw-score guide and says it is indicative. A framework written to compare courses states that it
   is not prescriptive and is not a test. A table of means describes a self-selected crowd and
   cannot predict one person. A 1885 experiment had one subject and used nonsense on purpose. A 1990
   paper is a contested hypothesis and says so. Every reading text ends with that line, and every
   unit's honesty note repeats it.
3. **Every person, class, center, university, employer, figure and conversation is invented.**
   Meridian Language Center does not exist. Neither do its teachers, its students, its clients or
   the universities and hospitals that appear in the dialogues, and **the band each invented
   organization asks for is invented too**. Where a unit needs a failure — a memorized answer, a
   missed group of answers, a notebook full of corrections that changed nothing — that failure is
   written from the shape of the document being taught, not from any real candidate at any real
   center.

**The series is provider-neutral.** No preparation course, publisher, app, teacher-training scheme or
software product is recommended anywhere in the six books. Where sample material is needed, the books
say to use the test owners' own published sample material and nothing else.

**Money has no symbol anywhere in this series.** A fee is written as a number and the unit is
whatever currency the reader pays in. A number without a symbol is readable in every country; a
number with the wrong symbol is readable in none.

## Eleven admissions, one per unit

- **Unit 1.** The published format is a description of a shape. Every student who has ever been
  surprised by a question type was surprised by something the document never claimed to tell them.
- **Unit 2.** The number printed largest on the result form is the one that is least use to anybody
  who needs a band in **each** paper. The large print is for the people who want one number.
- **Unit 3.** A framework written to help compare courses across a continent is used daily as a
  gate. The Council of Europe has said more than once that it is not for that.
- **Unit 4.** Everybody in the published tables chose to be there. A mean of about two million
  strangers is a planning figure, and it has been quoted at individual students as a verdict for
  years.
- **Unit 5.** Most wrong answers in a Listening paper are not vocabulary. They are the question the
  candidate stayed with after the recording had left it, and almost no lesson in the world diagnoses
  that.
- **Unit 6.** The rule students break most often is the watch, and nobody who breaks it means to.
  The rule students most want explained — what counts as a memorized answer — is the one the notice
  does not define.
- **Unit 7.** Four thousand extra word families buy the last three per cent of coverage. Coverage of
  96 per cent still loses the question if the missing word is the one the question turns on.
- **Unit 8.** Somebody chose two thousand words by hand, using judgement as well as counting, and
  much of the world learned those two thousand first. It was a good list. It was still a choice.
- **Unit 9.** The three most popular study techniques in the world came out at the bottom of the
  review, and the technique that feels like failing came out at the top.
- **Unit 10.** One man, one metronome and a list of nonsense, and every review schedule since has
  been drawn on his curve. The schedules are not in his book.
- **Unit 11.** Six corrections, six notebook entries, no change — and then one lecture, and the
  thing stopped. One student in one term is not evidence, and it is still the most useful thing in
  the unit.

## Figures and audio

196 figures, prefix `qa`, every one generated from a spec in `plumbline/_build/spec_qa1.py` to
`spec_qa4.py` and rendered at twice print size. No figure in this book appears in any other book of
this series or in any of its five sister series. Every figure carries alt text written for a reader
who cannot see it.

195 audio tracks, prefix `PL1`, three in the Starting Point and sixteen in each of the twelve units,
in the same sixteen positions every time. **The audio is a teaching recording made for this book.**
It is not a sample of the examination, the speakers are not examiners, and nothing in it tells a
student how fast the real recordings are played. For that there is only the test owners' own
published sample material, and the back matter says so.

Where a figure prints figures that look like published data but are not — the illustrative
first-language table in Unit 4, the sense shares in Unit 8, the class curves in Units 9 and 10 — the
figure says on its face that the numbers are the book's own illustration or the invented class's own
count, and tells the reader to go and look up the published ones.
