Four criteria, equally weighted, and the first one is called Task Response. It asks whether you
answered the question. Most of the essays that stop at band 6 are well written, well organized, and
answers to a question nobody asked.

### 2 · Read

%%FIG qe01-f5 | A one-page internal note on headed paper: a title, a line giving the source and the date it was downloaded, a four-row table of criteria with what each asks, a two-row comparison of band 6 and band 7 in the first criterion, a boxed paragraph headed a scale, not a method, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

**2A  Read the note. Then answer the questions in section 3.**

> **MERIDIAN LANGUAGE CENTER — note to the Writing class**
> **Subject: what the first criterion asks, and what it does not**
> **Source: the published Writing Task 2 band descriptors and the public statement of how Task 2 is
> assessed, from the test owners' own pages. Downloaded 19 May. Printed here in our own words.**
>
> Four criteria, **equally weighted**. Each is worth a quarter of the mark for this task, and Task 2
> itself counts for twice as much as Task 1 in the Writing score.
>
> | The criterion | What it asks of you |
> |---|---|
> | **Task Response** | that you address the question, present a clear position and develop it with support |
> | **Coherence and Cohesion** | that the essay is organized, paragraphed and logically sequenced |
> | **Lexical Resource** | range and accuracy of vocabulary, including spelling |
> | **Grammatical Range and Accuracy** | range and accuracy of structures, including punctuation |
>
> The published wording of the first criterion is worth reading slowly. Around band 6 it describes a
> response that **addresses** the task, with a position that **may become unclear** in places and
> support that may be **insufficiently developed**. Around band 7 it describes a response that
> **addresses all parts** of the task, with a **clear position throughout** and **extended, supported**
> ideas.
>
> Three differences, and none of them is about English.
>
> **All parts.** A question with two halves — *do you agree, and what should be done?* — is two
> tasks. An essay that answers the first and forgets the second is incomplete whatever its
> vocabulary.
>
> **Throughout.** A position stated in the introduction and abandoned in paragraph three is not a
> clear position throughout. Nor is one that changes.
>
> **Extended and supported.** An idea mentioned is not an idea developed. The difference is usually
> two sentences: the claim, and the reason somebody who disagreed would have to answer.
>
> **The length requirement.** At least 250 words. Writing fewer is penalized under Task Response.
> Writing many more is not penalized directly, and it is where most of Samira's problem lives: a long
> essay is not a developed one.
>
> **A SCALE, NOT A METHOD**
> The descriptors describe **what each band looks like**; they do not say how to get there. They name
> no vocabulary, prescribe no essay shape, give no model answer, and say nothing about which statement
> you will be given.
>
> They are also the **public** versions. Fuller descriptors are used in examiner training and are not
> published; this note does not claim to know what is in them, and neither does this book.
>
> A scale is a description of an outcome. A method is somebody else's job — which is why a book like
> this one exists, and why no book like this one is official.
>
> *Our summary of a published scale. Read the owners' own pages, and write down the date.*

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

> What counts as a good argument differs by education system. In some, a strong essay states a
> position in the first line and defends it; in others, stating your own view early looks blunt or
> arrogant, and the expected shape is to lay out the positions and let the reader conclude. The
> descriptors for this examination ask for a clear position throughout, which is a choice of
> convention, not a universal truth about good writing. **Ask your group: in your first language,
> where in an essay does the writer's own view belong?**
