## How to use this book

This is the fifth book of six. It is about **one task**: Writing Task 2, in which you are given a
statement or a question and asked, in at least two hundred and fifty words in about forty minutes, to
take a position and defend it. Every unit is built on one real published document, named with its
edition and its date.

**Twelve units**, sixteen pages each, with the same six lessons in the same order every time.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Sixteen words, a second set of six, and a team research task |
| **2 · Reading** | One real published document, with its date and its limits |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures, with the mistake each one causes |
| **4 · In the Test** | Clear English, a dialogue, a culture check and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A model, then your own |
| **Team Up & Review** | A picture, a team project, a survey, and one problem with no easy answer |

**The audio.** 🔊 marks a track. 195 tracks, numbered `PL5-unit-track`.

## What is different about this book

**It is built on the study of argument, not on essay templates.** Most preparation material for this
task gives you a four-paragraph shape and a list of linking words. This book gives you the 1958 book
that took arguments apart into claim, grounds and warrant; the 1975 lecture on what a reader assumes
you meant without your saying it; the 1965 paper on what the sentence after a topic sentence is
actually doing; the 1991 study that asked ordinary people to argue and found most of them could not
say what would change their mind; and the 1996 paper on what happens to teaching when a test matters.

**A position is not a topic.** Everything in this book turns on one distinction: the question you
were asked, and the question you can answer. An essay that is well written, well organized and about
something slightly different is the commonest band-6 answer in the world, and it is not a language
problem.

**Twelve real sources, and twelve limits.** A model of argument its own author called a sketch. A
lecture published after it was given, whose categories nobody has agreed since. A rhetoric built from
published prose, not from learners. A corpus study of English in one decade. A framework for research
article introductions, which your essay is not. And a paper about validity that says a test's
consequences are part of whether it is any good.

**Nothing here is a past paper.** Every statement, question, model answer and prompt in this book was
written for it.

**Money has no symbol in this series.**

## The people

The same preparation center and the same class as Books 1 to 4.

**Meridian Language Center** is an independent preparation center of about forty teachers. **Priya
Raman** teaches the class. **Grace Mwangi** is the academic manager. **Lars Eriksen** is the
assessment coordinator, and in this book he is the one who keeps asking *which question is this essay
an answer to?*

The class: **Junseo Park**, **Nour Khalil**, **Marek Nowak**, **Mei Lin**, **Yusuf Demir**, **Ines
Oliveira**, **Oksana Hryn**, **Samira Aziz**, **Tomas Varga**, **Hana Sato** and **Chiamaka Obi**. In
this book the person you will hear most is **Samira**, who writes three hundred and twenty words in
