# Starting Point · Fourteen Minutes
%%SUB Three parts, one examiner, and the one criterion that decides the last half band%%
%%GOALS
Say what the three parts of the Speaking test are
Say what range means in each of the four criteria
Say why accuracy is not your problem
Say where the fourteen minutes go
%%

Eleven to fourteen minutes, three parts, one examiner and a recorder. You have done this. If you are
reading this book you have probably done it more than once and been given the same figure each time.
This book is about why.

### 1 · Six things you already have

%%FIG qf00-f1 | A single review sheet with six boxes. Each box has a heading — fluency fillers, paraphrase, opinion language, comparison, the past, and hypothesis — and one short example in each.%%

**1A  Read the six examples aloud with a partner. Which two do you use most?**

| What you have | An example |
|---|---|
| Fluency fillers | *Well, I suppose* · *That's an interesting question* |
| Paraphrase | *or rather* · *what I mean is* |
| Opinion language | *Personally, I'd say* · *I'd argue that* |
| Comparison | *far more than* · *nothing like as* |
| The past | *I used to* · *I'd never actually* |
| Hypothesis | *If I had to choose* · *Supposing* |

**1B  Listen and repeat.** %%AUD PL6-00-01%%

### 2 · The three parts

%%FIG qf00-f2 | Four panels in a row: an examiner and a candidate at a table with a recorder, a card being handed over, a minute of silence with a pencil moving, and two people in a longer exchange.%%

**2A  Read the three rows. Then say which part you find hardest, and why.**

| Part | What happens | How long |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Introduction and familiar topics: where you live, work, study, free time | 4 to 5 minutes |
| **2** | A card with a topic and three or four prompts; one minute to prepare, one to two minutes to speak | 3 to 4 minutes |
| **3** | A discussion of abstract questions connected to the Part 2 topic | 4 to 5 minutes |

**2B  Listen.** %%AUD PL6-00-02%%

### 3 · Two answers to the same question

%%FIG qf00-f3 | A card taped inside a notebook, printed with two answers to one question, one above the other, with a tick beside the second and a cross beside the first.%%

**3A  Read the two answers. Then say which one you would give, and why.**

> **✗** *I think reading is very good. It is good for your mind and it is good for your knowledge. I
> think everyone should read because it is good for them.*

> **✓** *I read less than I used to, which I regret rather than excuse. What I notice is that I read
> differently — in ten-minute pieces, on a screen, and almost never anything long enough to change my
> mind about something.*

Both are accurate. One of them uses eleven different content words and one uses four.

think*? Count them. If the answer is one, that is the half band.
