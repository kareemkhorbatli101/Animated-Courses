# Grammar reference

Every structure in this book, in unit order. The second column is the mistake it prevents, which is
the column worth reading twice.

## Starting Point · Fourteen minutes

| | |
|---|---|
| **three parts, 11 to 14 minutes, four criteria** | the fourteen minutes include the minute with the card |
| **Part 1 familiar · Part 2 planned · Part 3 abstract** | three different conditions, three different jobs |
| **at band 7 the problem is range, not accuracy** | nobody has ever been told their English was too correct |
| **count the ways you said *I think*** | if the answer is one, that is the half band |

## Unit 1 · Self-correction

| | |
|---|---|
| **range appears in three of the four criteria** | only one is about correctness, and correctness is half of it |
| ***or rather* · *what I mean is* · *to be more precise*** | a mistake made visible is rewarded, not penalized |
| **hesitation is distinguished by its cause** | pausing for content is not pausing for language |
| **six ways to open an opinion, two or three per answer** | six in two minutes sounds like a list of phrases |

## Unit 2 · Families and lemmas

| | |
|---|---|
| **analyze · analysis · analytical · analytically** | a family in your head, four items in speech |
| ***figure* · *process* · *value* · *rate* · *significant*** | words from your first year with academic senses |
| **the wrong part of speech is the band-7 error** | made by somebody with a band-8 vocabulary |
| **a coverage figure needs its corpus** | fourteen per cent of what? |

## Unit 3 · Collocation

| | |
|---|---|
| **heavy rain · strong wind · a big mistake** | grammatical and not said, which is a different kind of wrong |
| **raise a question · draw a distinction · make a case** | the verb is not deducible from the meaning |
| ***the question is whether* · *it is not so much X as Y*** | frames retrieved whole buy you a clause |
| **collect pairs, not words** | single words produce *strong rain* |

## Unit 4 · General and specific

| | |
|---|---|
| **the speaker's economy ↔ the auditor's economy** | everything that makes speech easy for you costs the listener |
| ***thing* → factor · *good* → defensible · *do* → conduct** | three replacements an answer, not thirty |
| **a general word, then its specification** | *a problem — a problem of enforcement* |
| **keep one general word on purpose** | a thesaurus answer is worse than a plain one |

## Unit 5 · Formulaic sequences

| | |
|---|---|
| **a run is about nine words; hesitation falls between runs** | hesitation inside a clause is the kind that is heard |
| **a lexicalized sentence stem has a slot in it** | retrieved whole, not assembled |
| **five stems in the Part 2 minute, not five ideas** | you cannot plan two minutes of sentences |
| **fluency and novelty pull against each other** | everything new costs planning time |

## Unit 6 · Repair and hesitation

| | |
|---|---|
| **original → interruption → editing term → repair** | the third element is the one candidates leave out |
| ***sorry* · *or rather* · *I mean* · *well*** | one syllable turns a stumble into editing |
| **a lemma without its word form** | you have the meaning, so paraphrase is available |
| **describe the word and keep going** | four seconds of silence costs more than any paraphrase |

## Unit 7 · Complexity and accuracy

| | |
|---|---|
| **complexity · accuracy · fluency, one pool of attention** | you cannot push all three in one sentence |
| **planning time raises complexity** | which is Part 2 and nowhere else |
| **cheap: relative clause, participle, appositive, cleft** | usable in Part 3 under pressure |
| **expensive: inversion, long cleft** | half-formed under pressure, and worth nothing |

## Unit 8 · Stress and meaning

| | |
|---|---|
| **nuclear stress placement changes the meaning** | nine words, six placements, six meanings |
| **core: clusters, vowel length, nuclear stress** | the features whose absence caused breakdowns |
| **not core: the *th* sounds, weak forms, word stress** | and the ones everybody is corrected on |
| **answer the question the stress asked** | the words were identical; the stress was the question |

## Unit 9 · Reporting a distinction

| | |
|---|---|
| **accentedness · comprehensibility · intelligibility** | partially independent, and routinely confused |
| ***is related to but distinct from* · *partially independent of*** | more than *different*, less than *unrelated* |
| **the criterion asks about effort, not accent** | the word *accent* is not in it |
| **comprehensibility is a property of an encounter** | the listener is half of it |

## Unit 10 · What an error is evidence of

| | |
|---|---|
| **an interlanguage is a system with its own rules** | errors are systematic, so they are diagnosable |
| **transfer · transfer of training · overgeneralization** | five sources, one system |
| **three rules account for three quarters of your errors** | a smaller job than *be more accurate* |
| ***resistant* rather than *permanent*** | nobody can tell you which of yours is which |

## Unit 11 · Evidence and its limits

| | |
|---|---|
| **validity is a property of an interpretation for a use** | a test is not valid or invalid |
| **content · response processes · internal structure · relations · consequences** | five sources of evidence |
| ***for this use* · *on the available evidence* · *this does not license*** | attach the limit to the claim |
| **name the interpretation and the use, or say nothing** | *my English is a 7.0* is a sentence about a number |

## Unit 12 · What a test is for

| | |
|---|---|
| **administrative pressure shaped the design** | fourteen minutes because forty was not affordable |
| **psychometric ↔ communicative** | consistency against resemblance to real use |
| **a history explains how, not whether** | the argument from origin to judgment is a bad step |
| **four things: replacements · stems · runs and stress · an editing term** | the half band, and the rest was so you would know why |

# The British layer

This book is written in American English, and the series carries a switchable British layer: a fixed
list of words is respelled at build time. The list lives with the series rather than in this book.

**In Speaking, spelling does not exist and accent is not assessed.** The Pronunciation criterion asks
whether you are understood with little effort and whether you use features of pronunciation to convey
meaning. Unit 9 is about why those are not the same as sounding native, and Unit 8 is about which
features carry the meaning.

What the layer does not touch:

1. **A quoted document keeps its own spelling.** Most of this book's sources are published outside
   the United States, and their titles and quoted sentences are printed as published.
2. **A journal, publisher or organization name keeps its published form.** *Applied Linguistics*,
   *TESOL Quarterly*, *IRAL*, Oxford University Press, Cambridge University Press, MIT Press,
   Longman, Addison-Wesley, the American Educational Research Association, the American Psychological
   Association and the National Council on Measurement in Education are citations.
3. **A technical term keeps the spelling its field uses.** *Interlanguage*, *lemma*, *lexeme*,
   *accentedness* and *fossilization* are the sources' own words.
4. **Phonetic description is not respelled.** Where the book refers to *the two th sounds*, *the dark
   l* or *vowel length*, it is describing sounds and not spellings.
5. **Money has no symbol anywhere in this series.**
6. **A name is never respelled.** Gardner, Davies, Sinclair, Zipf, Pawley, Syder, Levelt, Skehan,
   Jenkins, Derwing, Munro, Selinker and Spolsky are spelled as they are published.

# A note on everything else in this book

Meridian Language Center does not exist. Priya Raman, Grace Mwangi, Lars Eriksen and the eleven
students in the class were written for this book, and so was every band figure any of them is given.
Ashworth University, Calderbrook Institute of Technology, Northvale College, Harrowgate School of
Nursing and Pennine Regional Hospital are invented, and so is the band each of them is said to ask
for.

Every question, card, transcript, model answer, rating sheet, admissions page and realia document in
this book was written for it. No question, prompt, recording or transcript from any past examination
is reproduced anywhere. The repair transcripts in Unit 6, the run-length transcript in Unit 5, the
rating matrix in Unit 9, the error tally in Unit 10 and the threshold pages in Unit 11 are all the
book's own.

**Every figure attributed to "our own class" is invented** — the repeated-phrase count in Unit 1, the
four-form survey in Unit 2, the collocation errors in Unit 3, the ranked word list in Unit 4, the run
lengths in Unit 5, the repair tally in Unit 6, the three task measures in Unit 7, the stress survey in
Unit 8, the five rated speakers in Unit 9, the twelve errors in Unit 10, the five score uses in Unit
11 and the five summary sheets in Unit 12. Each says on its face that it is an invented class's own
count, with eleven students, and several units stop to say why eleven students cannot establish
anything.

**Two tables in this book are our own reading of a document rather than anything the document says.**
The matrix of core and non-core features against what classrooms correct, in Unit 8, and the table of
the test's features against purposes and constraints, in Unit 12, are both ours. Each says so, and
Unit 12 invites the reader to say which row we have got wrong.

**Unit 2 says that Book 3 of this series taught a method its own source argues against.** That is
true. The earlier word list was worth teaching, because it is the list most published material still
uses and because its own author stated its limits — and a reader of this series is entitled to be
told that part of their course rests on a position the field has moved past.

**Unit 12 turns the discipline on the whole series.** It states that the examination is a compromise
arrived at under administrative constraint, that these six books exist because that examination
exists, and that if it had been designed differently the units would be different units. What it
offers in the series' defense is what the series has done throughout: every unit built on a real
published document, named with its date, with its own authors' stated limits printed beside it, and
most of those documents not about examinations at all.

Nothing in this book is advice about your admission, registration or employment, and nothing in it is
a prediction. A practice band is what one listener thought of one performance on one afternoon.

# Closing

You began these six books counting the minutes and you end them able to say what range is and where
yours is narrow; to produce four forms of a word and the pair that goes with it; to spend a syllable
where a general word would have been cheaper; to fill a stem instead of building a clause; to repair
yourself in a way that is heard as precision; to put your hardest structure where the planning time
is; to move the nuclear stress and hear it moved; to tell an accent from an effort; to state the rule
behind your own error; to say what a score is evidence for; and to refuse the step from a thing's
origin to a judgment about it.

Twelve sources in this book, and one for every unit of all six, every one of them named, dated and
printed with what it cannot establish.

One habit, and it is the one the whole series was built to leave you with: **before you believe
something, find out who said it, when, and what they said it did not cover.**

Five sentences to take with you, one from each part of this book.

From Unit 1: **how many ways can I say that?**

From Unit 4: **whose convenience is this?**

From Unit 6: **one syllable in the middle.**

From Unit 9: **effort, not accent.**

From Unit 12: **a history explains how, not whether.**

And one from the first book of the six, which is where this ends.

**There is no answer printed at the back of this book.**
