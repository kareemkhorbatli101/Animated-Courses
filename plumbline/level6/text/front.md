## How to use this book

This is the sixth and last book of six. It is about **the Speaking test** and about the thing that
decides the last half band in every part of the examination: **range**. Every unit is built on one
real published document, named with its edition and its date.

**Twelve units**, sixteen pages each, with the same six lessons in the same order every time.

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1 · Vocabulary** | Sixteen words, a second set of six, and a team research task |
| **2 · Reading** | One real published document, with its date and its limits |
| **3 · Grammar** | Two structures, with the mistake each one causes |
| **4 · In the Test** | Clear English, a dialogue, a culture check and a role-play |
| **5 · Writing** | A model, then your own |
| **Team Up & Review** | A picture, a team project, a survey, and one problem with no easy answer |

**The audio.** 🔊 marks a track. 195 tracks, numbered `PL6-unit-track`.

## What is different about this book

**It is built on corpus linguistics, psycholinguistics and measurement, not on topic vocabulary.**
Most material at this level gives you lists by subject — *environment, education, technology*. This
book gives you the 2014 word list that measured what academic English actually uses; the 1991 book
that argued most of a fluent speaker's output is not built word by word; the 1949 observation that
the words you use most are the shortest; the 1983 paper on what fluent speakers have stored whole;
the 1998 account of why you cannot be complex, accurate and fluent at the same moment; and the 2000
proposal about which English sounds actually matter between two non-native speakers.

**At 7.0 the problem is almost never accuracy.** It is range: the same six structures, the same
thirty words, used correctly. This book is about the structures and words you do not reach for, and
about why you do not.

**Twelve real sources, and twelve limits.** A word list whose authors say it is not a syllabus. A
corpus argument from one lexicographer's reading of concordance lines. A frequency regularity with no
agreed explanation. A pair of puzzles rather than a theory. A model of speaking in a first language. A
proposal about pronunciation that is not what you are assessed against. And a set of professional
standards that is not law.

**Nothing here is a past paper.** Every question, answer, transcript and prompt in this book was
written for it.

**Money has no symbol in this series.**

## The people

The same preparation center and the same class as Books 1 to 5, for the last time.

**Meridian Language Center** is an independent preparation center of about forty teachers. **Priya
Raman** teaches the class. **Grace Mwangi** is the academic manager. **Lars Eriksen** is the
assessment coordinator, and in this book he is the one who keeps asking *how many different ways can
you say that?*

The class: **Junseo Park**, **Nour Khalil**, **Marek Nowak**, **Mei Lin**, **Yusuf Demir**, **Ines
Oliveira**, **Oksana Hryn**, **Samira Aziz**, **Tomas Varga**, **Hana Sato** and **Chiamaka Obi**. In
this book the person you will hear most is **Nour**, who speaks fluently, makes almost no mistakes,
