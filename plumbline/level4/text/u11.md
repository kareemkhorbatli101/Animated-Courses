# Unit 11 · Where the Figures Came From
%%SUB Ten principles adopted in 1994 about who produces official figures, and what that means for a chart with a source line%%
%%GOALS
Attribute a figure to its source
Say what a chart's source line tells you and what it does not
Use *according to*, *as reported by*, *figures from*
Distinguish a figure from a claim about a figure
Say why principles are not a guarantee
%%

At the foot of every published chart, in small type, is a line nobody reads. It says who produced the
figures. That line is the difference between a number and a number you can say something about — and
there is an international document, adopted in 1994, about what it ought to mean.

### 2 · Read

%%FIG qd11-f5 | A one-page internal note on headed paper: a title, a line giving the document, the body that adopted it and the years, a numbered list of five of the ten principles in short form, a boxed paragraph headed principles, not a guarantee, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

**2A  Read the note. Then answer the questions in section 3.**

> **MERIDIAN LANGUAGE CENTER — note to the Writing class**
> **Subject: who produces official figures, and what they undertake**
> **Source: *Fundamental Principles of Official Statistics*, adopted by the United Nations Statistical
> Commission in **1994** and endorsed by the General Assembly in **2014**. Printed here in our own
> words.**
>
> There are **ten** principles. They are short, and they are addressed to the national offices that
> produce a country's official figures. These five matter most to a reader of charts.
>
> | | The principle, in short |
> |---|---|
> | **1** | Official statistics are a **public entitlement**: they serve government, business and the public, and are to be made available impartially. |
> | **2** | The office decides, **according to professional considerations**, the methods and procedures it uses. |
> | **3** | To help correct interpretation, the office **presents information on the sources, methods and procedures** it used. |
> | **4** | The office is **entitled to comment publicly** on misinterpretation and misuse of its figures. |
> | **6** | Individual data are **strictly confidential** and used only for statistical purposes. |
>
> Principle **3** is the one that puts the line at the foot of a chart. The undertaking is that the
> figures come with an account of **where they came from and how**, so a reader can interpret them
> correctly. The source line, the footnote about a change of definition, the word *provisional*: these
> exist because of a commitment of this kind.
>
> For a writer, the use is narrow and real. If the figure's origin is **printed on the task**, you may
> attribute it: *according to the chart*, *as reported by*. If it is **not printed**, you may not
> invent it, and you should not write *according to government figures* about a chart that says no such
> thing.
>
> And attribution is not the same as endorsement. *According to the chart, the figure fell by twelve
> percent* describes the chart. It does not claim the fall happened, and that modesty is exactly right
> in a task where you have been given a figure and no way to check it.
>
> **PRINCIPLES, NOT A GUARANTEE**
> The principles are a **statement of what official statistics should be**. They are **not
> enforceable**: there is no inspector, no sanction and no certificate. A country can endorse them and
> publish figures that meet none of them, and the document provides no mechanism for stopping that.
>
> They also apply to **official** statistics — the output of national statistical offices. They say
> nothing about figures produced by a company, a campaign, a newspaper or a private survey, which is
> most of what you will meet.
>
> And they are about **production**, not about **reading**. Nothing in the ten principles tells you how
> to describe a chart, and nothing in them is about English. What they give you is a reason why that
> line of small type is there, and a reason to read it.
>
> *Our summary of a published document, named with the body that adopted it and both of its dates, with
> its enforceability stated.*

### 📝 ON THE DAY

> **One clause, early, costs you nothing.** *According to the chart* at the start of your overview, or
> *the figures for the final year are described as provisional* in the body, shows the examiner that
> you read the whole figure and not just the bars. It takes six words. But do not invent: if the task
> gives no producer, write about the figures and attribute nothing. An invented source is worse than no
> source.
