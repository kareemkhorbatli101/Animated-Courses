# Grammar reference

Every structure in this book, in unit order. The second column is the mistake it prevents, which is
the column worth reading twice.

## Starting Point · Twenty minutes

| | |
|---|---|
| **150 words, 20 minutes, four criteria** | the twenty minutes include reading the figure |
| **describe what is there, not why it is there** | an explanation of a cause is outside the task |
| **an overview is one of the four criteria** | an answer without one cannot reach the top bands |
| **four minutes reading, fourteen writing, two checking** | the two at the end are the cheapest marks in the paper |

## Unit 1 · The language of an overview

| | |
|---|---|
| ***Overall*, *in general*, *the most striking feature*** | an overview is a sentence, not a summary of everything |
| **the four criteria are equally weighted** | your best criterion cannot carry the other three |
| **an overview states the pattern, not the first figure** | the first figure is not a pattern |
| **the overview can be the first sentence or the last** | but it has to be somewhere |

## Unit 2 · Verbs and adverbs of change

| | |
|---|---|
| **rose · fell · climbed · declined · leveled off · fluctuated** | one verb for each shape, not one verb for all |
| ***rose sharply* · *rose steadily* · *rose slightly*** | the adverb has to match the size of the change |
| **a rise *of* four · a rise *to* 86 · a rise *from* 71 *to* 86** | three prepositions, three different facts |
| **a peak, a trough, a plateau** | name the shape and you have described it |

## Unit 3 · Proportions, fractions, percentages

| | |
|---|---|
| **a third of · one in four · 25 % · a quarter** | four ways to write one proportion |
| **a proportion is always a proportion *of* something** | name the base or the figure means nothing |
| **the majority · a minority · just over half** | plainer than a decimal, and usually enough |
| **a share rose; the total may have fallen** | a proportion and a quantity move independently |

## Unit 4 · Comparison; *twice as many as*

| | |
|---|---|
| **twice as many as · three times the figure for · half the number of** | a multiple says more than a difference |
| **ten percentage points higher than** | a difference between two percentages |
| ***whereas* · *while* · *by contrast* · *unlike*** | four contrasts, not four *buts* |
| **position on a common scale is read most accurately** | which is why a bar chart is easier than a pie chart |

## Unit 5 · What an average conceals

| | |
|---|---|
| **the average *of* the four cities · an average *across* the period** | an average is always an average of something |
| **the mean conceals a range from ___ to ___** | the sentence that earns the average its place |
| ***although* + clause; *despite* + noun phrase** | the commonest concession mistake in the class |
| **four data sets, identical statistics, four shapes** | the correlation says nothing about the shape |

## Unit 6 · Packing data into one sentence

| | |
|---|---|
| **480 households, of which 120 had no car** | one sentence, two figures, one relation |
| **six categories, with transport the largest** | a figure hung on a noun |
| **rose to 86, reaching a peak in May** | a participle, same subject |
| **three facts in a sentence is plenty** | four is a sentence nobody can follow |

## Unit 7 · The passive; sequence

| | |
|---|---|
| **the mixture is heated · the waste is sorted** | a process diagram shows no people |
| ***first* · *once* · *after which* · *at this stage* · *finally*** | and never *then* five times |
| **once it has been dried, it is packed** | the present perfect passive for a finished stage |
| **a loop arrow means a stage repeats** | eight stages in a straight line is a different process |

## Unit 8 · Place, and change over time

| | |
|---|---|
| **to the north of · adjacent to · surrounded by · running along** | a feature has a position, not just a name |
| **the farmland had been built on by 2025** | the past perfect passive for change between two maps |
| **was demolished · was replaced by · was converted into** | three different disappearances |
| **the woodland remained unchanged** | the sentence that gives the change a size |

## Unit 9 · Rounding and approximation

| | |
|---|---|
| **approximately · just over · just under · nearly · around** | five hedges on a figure, not one repeated |
| **two effective digits, and the unit at the top** | the digits that vary, not the first two on the page |
| **from 9.8 to 61 million · between 9.8 and 61 million** | never *ranged between 9.8 to 61* |
| **rounding and saying you have rounded is accurate** | copying nine digits and running out of words is not |

## Unit 10 · Hedging a comparison; axes

| | |
|---|---|
| **shows · appears to show · suggests · may indicate** | four strengths, and *shows* is for printed figures |
| **the vertical axis begins at 94 rather than zero** | one clause, and the examiner knows you read the figure |
| **although the line is steep, the rise is four percent** | the concession that saves the verb |
| **never *dramatically* on a four percent change** | the single commonest lost mark in Task 1 |

## Unit 11 · Attribution; *according to*

| | |
|---|---|
| **according to the chart · as reported by · figures from** | attribute what is printed, invent nothing |
| **the figures are described as provisional** | the chart's own word, quoted |
| **attribution is not endorsement** | *according to the chart* does not claim the fall happened |
| **it is not stated whether** | an absence is a thing you can report |

## Unit 12 · Numbers, units and ranges

| | |
|---|---|
| **25 kg, 40 km/h, 15 %** | a space before the unit; a symbol takes no plural |
| **from 10 % to 20 %** | a dash can be read as a subtraction; *to* cannot |
| **a rise of 10 percentage points is not a rise of 10 %** | the one point in Unit 12 that is meaning, not convention |
| **five checks, two minutes** | overview · nine figures · verbs · percentage points · attribution |

# The British layer

This book is written in American English, and the series carries a switchable British layer: a fixed
list of words is respelled at build time. The list lives with the series rather than in this book.

**In Task 1, spelling is yours and it is marked.** Lexical Resource counts spelling, and either
system is accepted — but **not both in one answer**. Choose one and hold it for 150 words. The
commonest mixed pair in this class is *analyze* beside *organization*, which is two systems in one
sentence.

What the layer does not touch:

1. **A quoted document keeps its own spelling.** Several sources in this book are published outside
   the United States, and their titles and quoted sentences are printed as published.
2. **A journal or publisher name keeps its published form.** *The American Statistician*, *Journal of
   the American Statistical Association*, *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society*, Graphics Press,
   the University of Chicago Press and W. W. Norton are citations.
3. **A standard's number and title.** ISO 5807 and ISO 80000-1 are printed as published, with their
   editions and their dates.
4. **The date.** The month in letters in prose; the international format in data. In an answer, *3
   May* rather than *3/5*.
5. **Money has no symbol anywhere in this series.** Every figure is written without a currency sign,
   because this book is used in many countries and the unit is whatever the figure in front of you
   uses.
6. **A name is never respelled.** Cleveland, McGill, Anscombe, Tufte, Monmonier, Ehrenberg, Huff and
   Nightingale are spelled as they are published.

# Sources

Twelve source bodies carry the twelve reading texts, one per unit, and the Starting Point carries
none. Every one is a published document, paper, book or standard, cited with its edition or its date,
and every reading text prints what its own source says it does not establish.

| Unit | The source |
|---|---|
| **1** | The published Writing Task 1 band descriptors, and the public statement that the four criteria are equally weighted |
| **2** | The published Academic Writing task description, with the word count and the time |
| **3** | Florence Nightingale's 1858 diagram of mortality in the Crimea, as published in her own report |
| **4** | William S. Cleveland and Robert McGill, *Graphical Perception: Theory, Experimentation, and Application to the Development of Graphical Methods*, Journal of the American Statistical Association, 1984 |
| **5** | F. J. Anscombe, *Graphs in Statistical Analysis*, The American Statistician, 1973 |
| **6** | Edward R. Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, Graphics Press, 1983 |
| **7** | ISO 5807, *Documentation symbols and conventions for data, program and system flowcharts*, first edition 1985, since withdrawn |
| **8** | Mark Monmonier, *How to Lie with Maps*, University of Chicago Press, 1991 |
| **9** | A. S. C. Ehrenberg, *Rudiments of numeracy*, Journal of the Royal Statistical Society, Series A, 1977 |
| **10** | Darrell Huff, *How to Lie with Statistics*, W. W. Norton, 1954 |
| **11** | *Fundamental Principles of Official Statistics*, adopted by the United Nations Statistical Commission in 1994 and endorsed by the General Assembly in 2014 |
| **12** | ISO 80000-1, *Quantities and units — Part 1: General*, second edition 2022 |

And the admission each text carries, in one line:

| Unit | What the source does not establish |
|---|---|
| **1** | the descriptors say what a band looks like, not how to get there |
| **2** | the task description fixes the length and the time and nothing else |
| **3** | one diagram, one war, one campaigner: it changed a policy and proves nothing about charts in general |
| **4** | specific stimuli, specific participants; a guide to designing graphs, not a law of perception |
| **5** | four constructed data sets in a short teaching paper; no experiment, and no claim about real data |
| **6** | a design argument with no experiment; the strong form of the rule is a position, not a finding |
| **7** | withdrawn as a standard, and no examination diagram is obliged to follow it |
| **8** | an argument illustrated with examples; it does not say map makers are liars |
| **9** | recommendations from 1977, not a controlled experiment, and wrong wherever every digit is required |
| **10** | a popular polemic with no original data, and *always start at zero* has been argued against |
| **11** | principles with no inspector and no sanction, covering official figures only |
| **12** | a measurement standard, not a style guide; most published English does not follow it |

# A note on everything else in this book

Meridian Language Center does not exist. Priya Raman, Grace Mwangi, Lars Eriksen and the eleven
students in the class were written for this book, and so was every band figure any of them is given.
Ashworth University, Calderbrook Institute of Technology, Northvale College, Harrowgate School of
Nursing and Pennine Regional Hospital are invented, and so is the band each of them is said to ask
for.

Every chart, table, process diagram, map and realia document in this book was drawn for it. Where a
figure is described as our own class's count, it is an invented class's own count, with eleven
students, and several of the units stop to say why eleven students cannot establish anything.

The six proportions of data ink in Unit 6 are our own illustration of the 1983 ratio: nobody counted
them and nowhere published them. The five source lines in Unit 11 are invented in the same way. Both
charts say so on their faces.

The two line graphs in Unit 10 plot the same five real-looking figures twice, once from zero and once
from 94. The figures are invented; the effect is not.

Nothing in this book is advice about your admission, registration or employment, and nothing in it is
a prediction. A practice band is what you did on one figure on one morning.

# Closing

You began this book able to describe a chart and unable to say which parts of it were worth
describing. You end it able to write an overview, match a verb to the size of a change, say what an
average conceals, pack three figures into a sentence, describe a process in order, place a feature on
a map, round a number honestly, read an axis before reading a line, attribute a figure to what the
chart actually says, and write a quantity correctly — and able to say, of every one of those, where
the idea came from and what its source does not cover.

Twelve sources, and one habit: read the figure before writing about it.

Five sentences to take with you, one from each part of the book.

From Unit 1: **the four criteria are equally weighted.**

From Unit 5: **what is this an average of?**

From Unit 7: **does it loop?**

From Unit 10: **read the axis before you choose the verb.**

From Unit 12: **five checks, two minutes.**
