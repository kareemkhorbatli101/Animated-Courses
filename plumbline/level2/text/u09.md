larger than the evidence. One study looked, found something small and uneven, and told its readers
not to make it bigger.

### 2 · Read

%%FIG qb09-f5 | A one-page internal note on headed paper: a title, a line giving four authors, the paper, the journal and the year, a description of the design with listener groups and speaker accents, a small matrix with close figures, a boxed paragraph headed small, uneven, and not to be stretched, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

**2A  Read the note. Then answer the questions in section 3.**

> **MERIDIAN LANGUAGE CENTER — note to the Listening class**
> **Subject: what one study found about accents**
> **Source: R. C. Major, S. F. Fitzmaurice, F. Bunta and C. Balasubramanian, *The effects of
> nonnative accents on listening comprehension: implications for ESL assessment*, TESOL Quarterly,
> **2002**. Printed here in our own words.**
>
> First, a separate fact. **The published material for this examination states that the recordings
> use a range of accents of English, from more than one country.** That is a statement by the test
> owners about the test. It is not a research finding and this note does not treat it as one.
>
> Now the study. The researchers recorded the same lecture material spoken by speakers of English
> from several backgrounds — some native speakers, some speakers of English as a second language from
> different first languages. They then gave listening comprehension tests on those recordings to
> several groups of listeners, themselves from different first languages.
>
> The design lets you ask the question everybody in a preparation class asks: **does a listener
> understand a speaker better when the speaker shares their first language?**
>
> The answer is not the clean one anybody wanted. Overall, changing the speaker's accent made
> **small** differences to comprehension scores. For some combinations of listener group and speaker
> there was an advantage; for others there was none; for some there was a disadvantage. **The pattern
> was not consistent across groups.**
>
> That is a genuinely useful result, because it removes two beliefs at once. It removes *I will always
> do better with a speaker like me*, and it removes *an unfamiliar accent will ruin my score*. Both
> were larger than the evidence.
>
> **SMALL, UNEVEN, AND NOT TO BE STRETCHED**
> The study used **particular accents**, **particular listener groups** and **particular test
> material**. The authors are explicit that the results should not be generalized to other accents or
> other populations, and a reader who takes one combination from the paper and applies it to their own
> case has done exactly what they asked people not to do.
>
> The effects were **small**. Small effects in one study are a reason to stop repeating a rumor, not a
> reason to start a new one.
>
> It is about the comprehension of **lecture material in a research setting**. It is not a study of
> this examination, it makes no claim about anybody's band, and it does not say which accents appear
> on any paper.
>
> *Our summary of a published paper. Read it if your center has access; the matrix is the part that
> changes minds.*
