# Unit 12 · Forty Marks, One Band
%%SUB Review — the eleven sources, the indicative table, and the numbers, dates and spellings that decide the rest%%
%%GOALS
Review the eleven sources of this book and what each does not cover

Read an indicative conversion table and say what it is not

Build your own listening routine for the next twelve weeks

Forty answers become one band through a table that is published as a guide. This unit adds no new
idea. It asks whether the eleven in this book have changed anything you do — and it drills the small
mechanical things that quietly decide four or five marks a paper.

### 2 · Read

%%FIG qb12-f5 | A one-page summary sheet on headed paper: a title, a line saying it is a review of eleven sources with the dates they were consulted, an eleven-row table of the source and the one thing it does not cover, a small indicative conversion table, a boxed paragraph headed what the table is not, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

**2A  Read the page. Then answer the questions in section 3.**

> **MERIDIAN LANGUAGE CENTER — one page for a new Listening student**
> **Subject: everything in Book 2, and the line under each of it**
> **Sources: eleven published documents, papers and books, consulted between 11 February and 22
> April. Each is named in full in the back of this book. The indicative table below is published by
> the test owners. Printed here in our own words.**
>
> **The paper.** Four parts, ten questions each, played once. Parts 1 and 2 everyday, Parts 3 and 4
> academic. *The published guide says which question types may appear, not which will.*
>
> **The sound.** Speech is a stream, not a row of words. There are three kinds: single careful words,
> textbook connected speech, and real spontaneous speech — and you were taught the first two. *The
> three names are a teaching device, not a finding.*
>
> **The small words.** Function words lose their full vowel; sounds are dropped and changed. You often
> cannot hear which small word it was, and you recover it from the sentence. *How much disappears
> depends on the speaker, the speed and the style, so no rule is absolute.*
>
> **Mishearing.** What you hear instead is nearly always real English with the same rhythm, and the
> commonest error is the right sounds cut in the wrong place. *A collection of noticed errors cannot
> say how often they happen.*
>
> **Recognition.** You narrow the possible words from the first sounds, and usually know the word
> before it ends. *A laboratory model, revised by its own first author in 1987.*
>
> **Writing it down.** Speech-based material fades in a few seconds, and writing is itself a
> speech-based task, so a long answer costs you the next one. *A model revised for twenty-six years,
> with a fourth part added in 2000.*
>
> **The shape of a lecture.** Sentences that announce a section help; small joining words do not. *One
> study, modest effects, and it cannot tell you what your lecturer will do.*
>
> **Stress.** Most content words begin loud, so a loud syllable is probably a word beginning — except
> in words like *result*. *A count of a vocabulary is not a fact about an ear.*
>
> **Accents.** The published material says a range of accents is used. One study found accent effects
> small and uneven. *Its authors ask readers not to generalize.*
>
> **Being taught to listen.** Predict, listen, compare before you check, find the cause, listen again
> and reflect. *One term, intact classes, one teacher, and a subgroup result that has yet to be
> repeated.*
>
> **The number.** Every score has a width. *The coefficient behind that is a property of items and a
> group, not of a test, and its own author wrote critically about its use.*
>
> **FORTY MARKS, ONE BAND**
> Guidance published alongside the band scale gives, as a rough guide, the raw score out of forty that
> is near each band. About 30 of 40 is near band 7. About 23 is near band 6. About 16 is near band 5.
>
> **WHAT THE TABLE IS NOT**
> It is described as **indicative**. The number of correct answers that reaches a band is worked out
> **for each version of the test**, so this is not the table used on your paper. It tells you nothing
> about the width round your own score. And it cannot be used backwards: a practice score of 23 is not
> a band 6, it is 23 answers on one set of questions on one morning.
>
> *A review page. Nothing here replaces the eleven sources; read them, and write down the dates.*

> This book has asked, eleven times, what a source does not cover. Applied to listening that habit
