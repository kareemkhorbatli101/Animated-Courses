# Starting Point · Thirty Minutes
%%SUB What the Listening paper contains, and the four reasons a mark goes%%
%%GOALS
Review the English you need for the first part of the paper
Say what each of the four parts contains
Name the reason a mark went, not just the number
%%

Thirty minutes, forty questions, one playing. Almost nobody loses marks evenly across the paper, and
almost nobody loses them for the reason they think.

### 1 · Six things you already have

%%FIG qb00-f1 | A single review sheet with six boxes. Each box has a heading — numbers, spelling aloud, prepositions of place, times and dates, plurals, and question words — and one short example from a recorded conversation.%%

**1A  Read the six examples aloud with a partner. Which two cost you most marks?**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Numbers** | Thirteen, thirty. Fifteen, fifty. Double four. Oh seven. |
| **Spelling aloud** | H-A-double L, then I-D-A-Y. |
| **Prepositions of place** | **Opposite** the library, **next to** the gate. |
| **Times and dates** | Half past two. The third of May. |
| **Plurals** | Two **forms**. Three **weeks**. One **pass**. |
| **Question words** | **How many? How much? Which one? Where exactly?** |

### 2 · The four parts, and what each one is

%%FIG qb00-f2 | Four panels in a row, each headed with a part number and showing one object: two people at a desk with a form between them, one speaker alone beside a small map, three students and a tutor at a table, and a lecture theater with one speaker at a podium.%%

**2A  Read the four rows. Then say, for each one, how it is different from the one before.**

| Part | What you hear | What it is about |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | A conversation between two people | An everyday matter: booking, joining, asking |
| **2** | One speaker alone | An everyday subject: a place, a plan, an arrangement |
| **3** | A conversation, up to four people | Study or training: a project, an assignment, a course |
| **4** | One speaker alone | An academic subject: a short lecture |

Parts 1 and 2 are everyday. Parts 3 and 4 are academic. **The English gets harder as you go, and the
clock does not slow down.**

**2B  Listen to thirty seconds of each part.** %%AUD PL2-00-02%%

### 3 · Four reasons a mark goes

%%FIG qb00-f3 | A card taped inside a notebook, printed with four short lines in bold, each with one line of smaller type under it saying what to do about it.%%

**3A  Read the four reasons. Then say which one is yours.**

> **1  I did not know the word.**
> *The only one a vocabulary list helps.*
>
> **2  I knew it and did not hear it in the stream.**
> *Units 2, 3 and 8 are about nothing else.*
>
> **3  I heard it and was too slow to write it.**
> *Unit 6.*
>
> **4  I was still on the question before.**
> *The most expensive habit in the paper, and the cheapest to change.*

Only the first of those four is a vocabulary problem. **Three out of four are about sound, speed and
habit, and that is why this book is not a word list.**

### 4 · Two ways of reporting a wrong answer

%%FIG qb00-f4 | Two speech bubbles, one above the other. The first says I got 19. The second gives a question number, a reason and a place in the recording, with three short lines beneath it.%%

**4A  Read the two reports. Then discuss: which one could your teacher act on tomorrow?**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Report 1** | "I got 19 out of 40. Listening is my worst paper." |
| **Report 2** | "I got 19. Eleven of my wrong answers were in Parts 3 and 4, in three groups. In every group I knew the first word and was still writing it when the next answer came." |

Both are true. Only the second one names something that can be changed this week.

**4B  Listen and read.** %%AUD PL2-00-03%%

**4C  One question to finish.** When you last got a Listening score, did anybody ask you **why**?
