%%FIG qa06-f5 | A one-page internal note on headed paper: a title, a line giving the source and the date it was downloaded, four short headed lists of what must, may, must not and need not be brought, a two-row table of what can happen when a rule is broken, a boxed paragraph headed the question this notice does not answer, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

**2A  Read the note. Then answer the questions in section 3.**

> **MERIDIAN LANGUAGE CENTER — note to students sitting in March**
> **Subject: the rules for the morning**
> **Source: the notice to candidates and information for candidates published by the test owners,
> together with our own test center's page. Downloaded 4 February. Printed here in our own words.
> Rules differ a little between countries: read your own center's page.**
>
> **Identification.** You must bring the **same** identification document you used when you booked —
> for most people a passport. Your photograph is taken at the center on the day, and in some
> countries a finger scan is taken as well. If the document does not match the booking, you may not
> be allowed to sit.
>
> **What you may bring into the room.** Water, in a **clear bottle with the label removed**. A pencil
> and an eraser. That is close to the whole list.
>
> **What you must not bring.** A telephone. A watch of any kind — there is a clock in the room. A
> dictionary. Notes of any kind. Any electronic device. Personal belongings are left outside the room,
> usually in a locker. You must not take any test material, or any note you made, out of the room.
>
> **What happens when a rule is broken.** The published notice is plain. A result may be **withheld**
> or **canceled**, and in some cases a candidate may be required to sit the test again. The owners
> state that they may withhold or cancel a result where they have reason to doubt that it is the
> candidate's own work.
>
> **Speaking.** The notice also says that an answer learned by heart will be penalized, and that
> examiners report it when they hear it.
>
> **THE QUESTION THIS NOTICE DOES NOT ANSWER**
> It lists the rules; it does not explain them, and it does not say how a decision about misconduct is
> made, or by whom, or whether anybody can argue with it. It does not define what makes an answer a
> **memorized** one — the line between a phrase you have practiced and a paragraph you have learned
> is nowhere in the document, and it is the question students ask most. It cannot tell you what your
> own center does at the door, which is why it tells you to ask them.
>
> *Our summary of a published notice. Read your own center's page, and read it before the week of the
> test.*

### 1 · *Must*, *must not*, *may* and *do not have to*

| | |
|---|---|
| You **must** bring your passport. | necessary |
| You **must not** bring a watch. | forbidden — not *you must not to* |
| You **may** bring water. | permitted |
| You **do not have to** bring a pen. | not necessary — and not forbidden |
| **May I** leave the room? | asking permission |

**Careful.** *Must not* and *do not have to* are not the same. One forbids; the other releases you.

### 2 · *Allowed* and *not allowed*

A notice often says the same thing in the passive, with **allowed**.

| | |
|---|---|
| Telephones **are not allowed** in the room. | not *are not allow* |
| Water **is allowed** in a clear bottle. | |
| You **are not allowed to** take notes out. | **allowed to** + verb |
| A result **may be withheld**. | *may be* + past participle |
| Candidates **are photographed** on the day. | |

### 📝 ON THE DAY

> **The door is not the test.** Everything in this unit is settled before you sit down, and all of it
> can be settled the week before: read your center's page, put your watch in a drawer at home, take
> the label off the bottle the night before, and check that the passport in your bag is the one in the
> booking. Students lose mornings to objects far more often than to misconduct. Nobody has ever got a
> better band by thinking about a locker at nine o'clock.

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

> What happens at the door of an examination differs sharply from place to place. In some countries
> the invigilator is a neighbor and the rules bend; in others there is a photograph, a scan and a
> locked bag, and nothing bends at all. This test belongs to the second kind, and students from the
> first kind are the ones most often surprised. The notice does not explain this, because a notice
> cannot explain a culture. **Ask your group: at your school examinations, what could you take into
> the room? Was the rule the same for everybody?**
