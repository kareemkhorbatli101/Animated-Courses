# Starting Point · What the Test Measures
%%SUB Four papers, four bands, and one sentence about what a score is not%%
%%GOALS
Review the English you already have
Say what each of the four papers asks you to do
Ask four questions about any score
%%

The test gives you four numbers and then one more. Almost everything in this book follows from
knowing what those numbers are numbers of.

### 1 · Six things you already have

%%FIG qa00-f1 | A single review sheet with six boxes. Each box has a heading — the present simple, the past simple, numbers, dates, there is and there are, and questions — and one short example sentence from a language class.%%

**1A  Read the six examples aloud with a partner. Which two do you use most?**

| | |
|---|---|
| **The present simple** | The Listening paper **has** four parts. |
| **The past simple** | I **took** the test in March. |
| **Numbers** | Forty questions. Sixty minutes. A band of 6.5. |
| **Dates** | **On** 9 March. **In** April. **By** Friday. |
| **There is / there are** | **There are** three passages in the Reading paper. |
| **Questions** | **How** long is the Speaking test? **Who** marks it? |

### 2 · Four papers, and what each one asks

%%FIG qa00-f2 | Four panels in a row, each headed with a paper name and showing one object: a pair of headphones with a numbered answer sheet, three stapled passages, a lined sheet with a small bar chart above it, and two chairs facing each other with a small recorder on a table between them.%%

**2A  Read the four rows. Then say, for each one, whether you have ever done it in English.**

| Paper | What you do | How long |
|---|---|---|
| **Listening** | Answer forty questions while you listen | about 30 minutes |
| **Reading** | Answer forty questions on three passages | 60 minutes |
| **Writing** | Describe some information, then write an essay | 60 minutes |
| **Speaking** | Talk to one person in three parts | 11 to 14 minutes |

The Listening and Speaking papers are the same for everybody. The Reading and Writing papers are
different in the Academic test and the General Training test. **This book is about the Academic
test.**

**2B  Listen.** %%AUD PL1-00-02%%

### 3 · Four questions about any score

%%FIG qa00-f3 | A card taped inside a notebook, printed with four questions in bold, each with one line of smaller type under it saying who asks it.%%

**3A  Read the four questions. Then ask your partner all four about a test they have taken.**

> **1  What is this a score of?**
> *Almost nobody asks this, and it is the first question.*
>
> **2  Who decided the number I need?**
> *Not the test. A university, an employer or a government decided it.*
>
> **3  Would I get the same number again next week?**
> *Probably close. Probably not the same.*
>
> **4  What does this score not tell anybody?**
> *This is the question the published documents answer best.*

The fourth question is the one this book returns to. **A score with no answer to question four gets
used for things it was never evidence for.**

### 4 · Two ways of answering question one

%%FIG qa00-f4 | Two speech bubbles, one above the other. The first says my English is 6.5. The second names a paper, a date and a task type, with three short lines beneath it.%%

**4A  Read the two answers. Then discuss: which one do you hear more often?**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Answer 1** | "My English is 6.5." |
| **Answer 2** | "In February I answered 23 of the 40 Reading questions on that paper, and that was reported as a 6." |

Both answers come from the same morning. Only the second one says what happened. The first one turns
a number about four papers into a number about a person. **Nobody who writes the published
documents talks like Answer 1, and Unit 2 shows you why.**

**4B  Listen and read.** %%AUD PL1-00-03%%

**4C  One question to finish.** Who told you which band you need, and have you read it yourself?
