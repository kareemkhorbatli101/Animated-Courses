means takes the rest of this book.

### 2 · Read

%%FIG qa02-f5 | A one-page internal note on headed paper: a title, a line giving the source and the date it was downloaded, a nine-row table of bands and their published names, a three-row worked example of the rounding rule, a small table of raw scores against bands marked as a guide, a boxed paragraph headed what this scale does not claim, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

**2A  Read the note. Then answer the questions in section 3.**

> **MERIDIAN LANGUAGE CENTER — note to new students**
> **Subject: the band scale, and how an overall band is worked out**
> **Source: the band scale and scores pages published by the test owners. Downloaded 4 February.
> Printed here in our own words.**
>
> There is no pass mark. The test reports a **band** from 1 to 9, and 0 if you did not attempt it.
> Each band has a published name: 9 is *expert user*, 6 is *competent user*, 4 is *limited user*.
>
> You get **five** numbers: one for each of the four papers, and an **overall band**. The overall band
> is the average of the four, rounded. The published rule is this. If the average ends in **.25**, it
> is rounded **up to the next half band**. If it ends in **.75**, it is rounded **up to the next whole
> band**. Anything else goes to the nearest whole or half band.
>
> Three worked examples are published. Scores of 6.5, 6.5, 5.0 and 7.0 average 6.25, and are reported
> as **6.5**. Scores of 4.0, 3.5, 4.0 and 4.0 average 3.875, and are reported as **4.0**. Scores of
> 6.5, 6.5, 5.5 and 6.0 average 6.125, and are reported as **6.0**.
>
> For Listening and Academic Reading there are forty questions. Nothing is taken away for a wrong
> answer. Guidance published alongside the scale says that, as a rough guide, about 30 correct out of
> 40 is near band 7 and about 23 out of 40 is near band 6. **That guidance is described as indicative
> only.**
>
> **WHAT THIS SCALE DOES NOT CLAIM**
> The published guide to raw scores is **not** the table used on your paper. Every version of the test
> is set against the same scale, and the number of correct answers that reaches a band is worked out
> for that version. The scale does not tell you how far your own band might move if you sat a second
> version next week. It does not say that two people with the same band speak the same English. It
> does not say what a band is worth in a job, a seminar or a hospital ward. Somebody else decided
> that, and the scale is silent about it.
>
> *Our summary of a published scale. Read the owners' own pages before you book.*

### 1 · Numbers, *out of*, and half bands

| | |
|---|---|

### 📝 ON THE DAY

> **Nobody in the room is working out your average.** On the morning of the test the only number that
> exists is the one you are making, question by question. The arithmetic in this unit happens

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

> In many countries an examination result is a percentage, and everybody knows what 72 percent
> means. A band is not a percentage. It is a point on a described scale, and the published
> description of band 6 is a paragraph about what a person can do, not a count of marks. This is why
> two students with 6.0 can be unlike each other, and why a university that asks for 6.0 is asking a
> question about description, not about arithmetic. **Ask your group: in your country, is a public
> result a percentage, a grade or a description? Which do you trust more, and why?**
