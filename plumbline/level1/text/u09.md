### 2 · Read

%%FIG qa09-f5 | A one-page internal note on headed paper: a title, a line giving five authors, the article, the journal and the year, a three-row table of high, moderate and low with the techniques listed in each, a boxed paragraph headed what a low rating means, a short list of four things the review does not claim, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

**2A  Read the note. Then answer the questions in section 3.**

> **MERIDIAN LANGUAGE CENTER — note to students**
> **Subject: which study techniques the evidence supports**
> **Source: J. Dunlosky, K. A. Rawson, E. J. Marsh, M. J. Nathan and D. T. Willingham, *Improving
> Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques*, Psychological Science in the Public
> Interest, 2013. Printed here in our own words.**
>
> Five researchers took **ten** study techniques that students actually use, read the published
> evidence on each one, and gave each a rating: **high**, **moderate** or **low** utility.
>
> Two were rated **high**. **Practice testing** — closing the book and trying to produce the answer —
> and **distributed practice** — spreading study across days instead of massing it in one session.
>
> Three were rated **moderate**: asking yourself *why* a fact is true, explaining a page to yourself
> as you read it, and mixing different kinds of problem in one session.
>
> Five were rated **low**: writing summaries, **highlighting and underlining**, the keyword mnemonic,
> drawing pictures for text, and **rereading**. Three of those five are the most common study
> behaviors in the world.
>
> The authors are clear about why rereading does so badly. It produces a strong feeling of knowing,
> because the page is familiar the second time. Familiarity is not retrieval. Practice testing feels
> harder and worse, and that is partly why it works: you find out what you cannot do while there is
> still time to fix it.
>
> **WHAT A LOW RATING MEANS**
> The ratings are the authors' **judgments of an evidence base**, not results of an experiment they
> ran. **Low** does not mean *never works*. It means the evidence is thin, or narrow, or has not been
> tested across enough materials, learners, tasks and settings. The review says so in each case, and
> it names what is missing.
>
> Most of the studies behind it were done with students learning academic content in their first
> language, not with adults learning a second language for an examination. The review makes no claim
> about language tests, about bands, or about how long any of this takes. And a technique rated high
> can still be used badly: testing yourself on the wrong material is still the wrong material.
>
> Four things the review does not claim: anything about this test · anything about second languages ·
> that low means useless · that high means easy.
>
> *Our summary of a published review. It is long, free and worth an evening.*

### 2 · *-ing* forms as the subject

An *-ing* form can be the **subject** of a sentence, and it takes a **singular** verb.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Testing yourself works.** | not *Test yourself works* |
| **Rereading feels** easier the second time. | singular verb |
| **Spreading your study across four days is** better than one long night. | |
| **Highlighting does not help** as much as students think. | |
| They learned more **by testing themselves**. | after a preposition, always *-ing* |

**2A  Rewrite with an *-ing* subject. Then read each one aloud.**

1 If you test yourself, you learn more. → ______
2 If you reread a page, it feels familiar. → ______
3 If you spread your study out, you remember longer. → ______
4 If you highlight everything, you choose nothing. → ______
5 If you explain a page aloud, you find the gaps. → ______

**2B  Listen and check.** %%AUD PL1-09-09%%

**2C  The mistake this causes.** Students write *Test yourself is better* and *For to learn you must
practice*. An English subject in this pattern is **testing**, and after a preposition the verb is
always **-ing**: *by testing*, *instead of rereading*. **Say your partner four sentences with *-ing*
subjects and let them correct any bare infinitive.**

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

> In many school systems hard work is measured by time at the desk, and a student with a book open
> for four hours is visibly virtuous. The review in this unit measures something else: what you can
> produce later, with the book shut. The two measures disagree, and the disagreement is
> uncomfortable, because the techniques that look like studying are the ones rated low and the
> technique that looks like failing — getting answers wrong on purpose, early — is rated high. **Ask
> your group: in your country, what counts as studying hard? Would a student testing themselves for
> twenty minutes be praised or told to work more?**
