### 2 · Read

%%FIG qa12-f5 | A one-page summary sheet on headed paper: a title, a line saying it is a review of eleven documents with the dates they were consulted, an eleven-row table of the document and the one thing it does not cover, a five-row table of numbers to know by heart, a boxed paragraph headed what no document in this book can do, and a source line in small type at the foot.%%

**2A  Read the page. Then answer the questions in section 3.**

> **MERIDIAN LANGUAGE CENTER — one page for a new student**
> **Subject: everything in Book 1, and the line under each of it**
> **Sources: eleven published documents, consulted between 4 and 11 February. Each is named in full in
> the back of this book. Printed here in our own words.**
>
> **The test.** Four papers. Listening, Reading and Writing in one sitting, in that order, with no
> break; Speaking the same day or within seven days. Listening is about thirty minutes, four parts,
> forty questions, heard once. Academic Reading is sixty minutes, three passages, forty questions, no
> extra time. Writing is sixty minutes, two tasks, Task 2 worth more. Speaking is eleven to fourteen
> minutes, three parts, recorded. *The published format does not say which question types you will
> meet.*
>
> **The scale.** Nine bands with published names. Five numbers on the form: four papers and an overall
> band, which is the average of the four, rounded — .25 up to the next half band, .75 up to the next
> whole band. Nothing is taken away for a wrong answer. *The published raw-score guide is indicative;
> it is not the table used on your paper.*
>
> **The levels.** Six levels, A1 to C2, described in *can do* statements. Bands are mapped onto them
> approximately: 4.0 to 5.0 at B1, 5.5 to 6.5 at B2, 7.0 to 8.0 at C1. *The framework says it is a
> reference and not prescriptive, and it is not a test.*
>
> **The figures.** Mean bands are published every year for all test takers and by first language and
> country. Writing has had the lowest of the four skill means. *Everybody in those tables chose to
> sit, so a group's mean says as much about who applies; and no average can predict one person.*
>
> **Listening.** Played once. Most wrong answers are not vocabulary: they are the question you stayed
> with after the recording left it. *A book about teaching listening is not a book about test
> technique.*
>
> **The rules.** Bring the identification you booked with. Water in a clear bottle, label off. No
> telephone, no watch, no dictionary, no notes. A result can be withheld or canceled. *The notice does
> not define a memorized answer.*
>
> **Vocabulary.** About 8,000 to 9,000 word families for 98 percent of written English, 6,000 to 7,000
> for spoken. The first two thousand do most of the work, and one sense of a common word usually does
> most of that word's work. *Coverage is a count of running words, not of understanding.*
>
> **Studying.** Practice testing and spreading your study out are the two techniques the evidence
> supports best; rereading and highlighting are not. Forgetting is fast and then slow, so a review
> comes before you have lost it, not after. A correction you have never noticed in real English
> changes nothing. *One of those is a review of judgments, one is a single subject on nonsense
> syllables, and one is a contested hypothesis.*
>
> **WHAT NO DOCUMENT IN THIS BOOK CAN DO**
> None of them can tell you what you will get. None of them was written about you. Every one of them is
> worth reading, and every one of them stops somewhere, in a sentence its own authors wrote. Learning
> to find that sentence is the skill this series is actually teaching.
>
> *A review page. Nothing here replaces the eleven documents; read them, and write down the dates.*

### 🌐 CULTURE CHECK

> This book has asked, eleven times, what a document does not cover. In some places that habit reads
> as useful caution; in others it reads as rudeness towards an authority, or as an excuse for not
> trying. It is neither. A document that states its limits is more trustworthy than one that does
> not, and a student who can find the limiting sentence is harder to sell things to. **Ask your group:
> in your country, is it normal to ask what an official document does not say? Who taught you to do
> it, or who taught you not to?**
